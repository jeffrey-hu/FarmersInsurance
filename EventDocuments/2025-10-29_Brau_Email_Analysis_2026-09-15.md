## 2. ⚠️ The sharpest thing in this email is one word

The project has treated the October 10 and October 29 statements as simply "in tension." They are worse than that for Defendant, and the reason is the phrase **"all the information *we can release*."**

That is not "all the information." It is a carve-out. On its face it concedes that information relating to The Claim **existed and was not released**. Set against the October 10 letter:

| | Statement | Implication |
|---|---|---|
| **Oct. 10, 2025** — Zorphlon Miller, Fire Insurance Exchange | "The file does not contain a report on the requested date." | **Nothing exists.** |
| **Oct. 29, 2025** — Nicholas Brau, Fire Insurance Exchange | "All the information **we can release** has already been sent to you." | **Something exists that is being withheld.** |

Nineteen days apart, from two employees of the same entity, on the same request. Defendant will say the second is boilerplate. Perhaps — but it is boilerplate Defendant chose in answer to a specific request for a specific adjuster's inspection record, after telling Plaintiffs that record did not exist.

**This is why the operative set now asks Defendant to admit that, as of October 29, 2025, information relating to The Claim existed that it had not released.** Whichever way that is answered it is useful: an admission contradicts the October 10 letter; a denial commits Defendant to reading "all the information we can release" as meaning "all the information," which is not what it says.

## 3. ✅ The Complaint's dating is correct — both emails exist

The three sentences after the opening line are **identical** to the reply Complaint ¶39 attributes to **October 21, 2025**. That looked at first like a date error. It is not.

`2025-09-18-PostAppraisalEmailChain.md` records an October 21, 2025 exchange — Plaintiffs asking why most of the damaged windows were not covered, and Farmers replying with exactly those three sentences. The **October 29** email repeats them and prepends the "all the information we can release" line.

**This matters because Defendant has already admitted the three sentences.** Its Answer to ¶39 denies only the first sentence (Plaintiffs' characterization that Defendant "refused to address the gap") and **admits the remainder**, which includes the quoted reply. So:

- The three repeated sentences are **already admitted** — do not spend requests on them.
- The **only unadmitted sentence in this email is the first one**, and that is the one the requests target.

---

## 4. A discrepancy worth noting, not yet worth pleading

Brau signs this email **"Claims Spec Rep Prop."** The Complaint (¶16) and Defendant's own Answer both call him **"Senior Claim Adjuster"** as of the September 6, 2024 inspection. Titles change, and the two are thirteen months apart, so this is probably nothing. But if Defendant later argues Brau lacked authority or was not the right custodian, the title history is worth having. Ask it at deposition rather than in a request.

**Also captured:** the thread token **[3CAB117Z3]** in the subject line, and the instruction not to alter the subject line so replies are not misrouted. That token is a usable handle in document discovery — Defendant's system can retrieve every message bearing it, which is a narrower and harder-to-refuse ask than "all communications."
