## 1. Why this document matters more than expected

It was added to resolve a preservation gap — the October 10, 2025 "the file does not contain a report" statement existed in the project only as a family-prepared compilation. **It does resolve that gap, and in a stronger form than the email export that was being chased.** But page 1 is only the first of 75.

Pages 2 through 75 are **the complete September 3, 2024 denial package as Defendant itself re-transmitted it**, including a **100-photograph inspection log** that had not been analyzed in this project. That log refutes, on Defendant's own evidence, the single affirmative factual allegation Defendant volunteered in its Answer.

## 4. ⚠️ The photograph log refutes Answer ¶14

Defendant's Answer to Complaint ¶14 contains the only affirmative factual allegation Defendant volunteered anywhere in its pleading:

> "FIE denies that Plaintiffs cooperated with Defendant's claim-handling process to the extent Plaintiffs attempted to direct the claim-handling process by **adamantly advising Hancock Claims Consultants that the only thing that needed to be inspected was the windows on the inside where they were leaking**."

> ### ⚠️ Correction, September 15, 2026
> An earlier version of this note reported **one** log of 100 photographs. That was wrong. Plaintiffs' page-by-page review found **two** logs, and the numbering **restarts** between them — which a scripted extraction keyed by photograph number silently collapsed, overwriting the first set's 38 entries. The corrected figures below were re-derived per document and match Plaintiffs' manual count exactly.

**Defendant enclosed two photograph logs with its own September 3, 2024 denial letter, containing 138 numbered photographs in total. 132 of the 138 are captioned "Damaged – No" or "Damage – No"; the other six are orientation shots.**

**First Photo Set** — 19 pages, 8/30/2024, photographs 1–38. **All 38 carry "Window Wrap" captions and all 38 end "Damaged – No"** (North 12, South 13, West 13). No caption in this set names siding, downspouts or elevations. ⚠️ That is a fact about *captions only* — "Window Wrap" covers both interior and exterior shots (see the second correction below), so it does **not** establish that this set is exterior-free, interior-only, or a narrower "pass" than the other. The four pages sampled visually (photographs 7–8, 31–32) are interior.

**Second Photo Set** — 50 pages, 8/31/2024, photographs 1–100. Ninety-four captioned "– No," six orientation shots. By subject:

| Subject (Second Photo Set) | Photographs | Caption pattern |
|---|---:|---|
| Window wraps — North 20, South 17, West 13, East 2 | **52** | "Window Wrap [elevation] Damaged – No" |
| Siding — South 15, North 8, East 6, West 5 | **34** | "Siding [elevation] Damage – No" |
| Garage, South | **5** | "Garage South … – No" |
| **Down spouts** — East/Right, South/Front ×2 | **3** | "Down Spouts Damaged … – No" (photographs 98, 99, 100) |
| Orientation — Address, Front Risk, and four Elevation shots | 6 | untagged |

### The caption suffix is a per-label counter — and that is provable

Most captions end not in a bare "No" but in "No_2", "No_3" and so on, **up to "No_20"** (Window Wrap North, Second Photo Set). Only **14** of the 138 end in a bare "No."

Those 14 are exactly the number of distinct damage labels in the two logs, and the suffixes behave accordingly. Checked label by label, **every damage label's suffixes run 1 through N with no gaps and no duplicates**, counting independently within each log:

| Log | Label | Photographs | Suffixes |
|---|---|---:|---|
| First | Window Wrap South / West / North | 13 / 13 / 12 | 1–13, 1–13, 1–12 |
| Second | Window Wrap North / South / West / East | 20 / 17 / 13 / 2 | 1–20, 1–17, 1–13, 1–2 |
| Second | Siding South / North / East / West | 15 / 8 / 6 / 5 | 1–15, 1–8, 1–6, 1–5 |
| Second | Garage South | 5 | 1–5 |
| Second | Down Spouts South-Front / East-Right | 2 / 1 | 1–2, 1 |

Fourteen labels, 132 photographs, every run contiguous. The suffix is a file-naming counter distinguishing repeated captions — it cannot be a different condition or assessment, because then the conditions would have to increment by one, in order, within every label, in both logs.

**This is the proof behind Request for Admission 72.** If Defendant denies that request, this table is how Plaintiffs prove it — which is the Rule 37.03(b) posture: a denial that costs Defendant the expense of the proof.

⚠️ One parsing caution carried forward: the elevation strings extract as "East/ Right" and "North/ Back _2", with spacing that is almost certainly a PDF line-wrap artifact rather than the real caption. **Do not quote elevation strings verbatim in a served document** — the requests deliberately describe subject matter instead and rely on the defined term for the ending.

**An inspector instructed to look only at interior windows where they were leaking does not photograph thirty-four sections of siding, three downspouts, five views of the garage, and four elevations — and does not tag every one of them "Damaged – No."** The exterior scope of the inspection is documented in Defendant's own enclosure.

> ### ⚠️ Second correction, September 15, 2026 — there was ONE inspection, not two
> An earlier version of this note inferred from the 8/30 and 8/31 footer dates that Hancock inspected on one day and "came back" the next. **That inference was wrong**, and Plaintiffs identified why: **both** logs contain photographs taken *inside* the house, and the inspector was only inside on **August 29, 2024**. The footer dates are therefore **document-generation dates, not capture dates** — two reports produced from a single visit.
>
> Confirmed by viewing the pages directly: First Photo Set page 4 (photographs 7–8) and page 16 (photographs 31–32) are interior views — carpet, outlets, wallpaper, a stair banister, a bedroom dresser. Second Photo Set page 5 (photographs 9–10) is likewise interior.
>
> The caption taxonomy never supported the two-visit reading either: **"Window Wrap" captions cover both interior and exterior shots.** Second Photo Set photograph 49, "Window Wrap South Damaged – No_17," is an exterior view across landscaping. Sorting the sets by caption prefix does not sort them by interior/exterior, so no inference about scope or sequence can rest on the prefixes alone.

**The single-visit reading makes the ¶14 point stronger, not weaker.** On **one** visit, on August 29, 2024, Hancock photographed the interior window wraps **and** 34 sections of siding, the garage, three downspouts and four elevations — and tagged every one of them "Damaged – No." There is no "he came back later under different instructions" alternative. One inspector, one visit, one scope, and that scope was the whole house.

This was the hypothesis behind Interrogatory 16 and RFP 42. **It is no longer a hypothesis.**

⚠️ **One further claim in an earlier version of this note was wrong and is withdrawn.** It said the logs were "exterior-only in their damage tagging" with "no interior-water-damage photograph series." **Both logs contain interior photographs, and those interior photographs are themselves tagged "Damaged – No."** That is the opposite of what was written, and it points somewhere more useful: Defendant's September 3, 2024 letter states that "interior water damage around your windows was observed," yet the interior window-wrap photographs in Defendant's own enclosure are tagged **"Damaged – No."** Second Photo Set photograph 10 appears to show staining on the wall covering beside a window and still carries that tag.

**Treat that as a lead, not a finding.** "Window Wrap … Damaged – No" may mean the wrap itself was undamaged rather than that no water damage existed nearby, and the tags may be default captions rather than assessments. Resolve it before pleading it — through the EXIF and native-file production, and by asking at deposition what the "Damaged – No" tag was intended to record and who applied it.

## 5. ⚠️ Defendant's two vendors contradict each other on the downspouts

| Vendor | Date | Downspouts |
|---|---|---|
| **Hancock Claims Consultants** (this log, photographs 98–100) | 8/30–8/31/2024 | "Down Spouts Damaged East/Right – **No**"; "Down Spouts Damaged South/Front – **No**" ×2 |
| **Prieve Engineering**, Photo Appendix A | 10/9/2024 | **Dented** downspouts at A-42, A-92, A-93, A-98, A-99 |

Both were retained by Defendant. Six weeks apart, on the same components, they recorded opposite conditions — and Defendant relied on the second to deny a claim it had already denied on the first.

This bears directly on Complaint ¶22(c): the gutters and downspouts were replaced after September 13, 2021, so a dent on them cannot pre-date late 2021. If Hancock found them undented on August 30–31, 2024 and Prieve found them dented on October 9, 2024, the damage window narrows to a period that contains the July 13, 2024 loss and little else — **subject to confirming whether Hancock's "Damaged – No" tags reflect an actual assessment or a default caption.** That question is worth an interrogatory of its own.

## 7. ✅ The date discrepancy is resolved — the footers are generation dates

The Complaint dates the Hancock inspection to **August 29, 2024**; the two logs carry page-footer dates of **8/30/2024** and **8/31/2024**. That looked like a conflict.

It is not. **Both logs contain interior photographs**, and Hancock was inside the house only on August 29. A log footer-dated August 31 that contains interior photographs cannot be recording an August 31 capture. The footers are when the documents were generated; the photographs are from the single August 29 visit.

**Request for Admission 70 now asks Defendant to admit exactly that**, and Request 71 asks it to admit both sets contain interior photographs — the backstop if Defendant denies 70. If Defendant denies both, it has to say when the photographs were taken, which puts it in a worse place than admitting.

Still worth doing: pull the **EXIF capture timestamps** from the native image files. That converts a sound inference into a documented fact, and it is one more reason the absence of an ESI order requiring native production with metadata intact is a live exposure.
