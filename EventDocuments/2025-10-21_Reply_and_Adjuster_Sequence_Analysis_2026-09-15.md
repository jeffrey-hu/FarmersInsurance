## 2. ⚠️ Three adjusters in nineteen days — and two of them were unknown to this case

The finding is not in any one document. It appears when the three preserved October 2025 communications are laid side by side:

| Date | Signed by | Title | Phone | Thread token |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Oct. 10, 2025** | **Zorphlon Miller** | Senior General Claims Adjuster | (832) 865-4165 — Houston | — |
| **Oct. 21, 2025** | **Jillian Bell** | Senior General Claims Adjuster | (512) 689-0559 — Austin | [HC183T003] |
| **Oct. 29, 2025** | **Nicholas Brau** | Claims Spec Rep Prop | (651) 587-3157 — Minnesota | [3CAB117Z3] |

All three sign for **Fire Insurance Exchange**. All three send from the **same shared address**, `myadjuster@farmersinsurance.com`. The October 21 and October 29 messages carry **different thread tokens**, so they are separate threads rather than one continuing conversation.

**Before today's three documents, neither Miller nor Bell appeared anywhere in this case** — not in the Complaint, not in the Answer, not in any interrogatory or request for admission. Brau, Monson, Dudley and Pietila were the known cast. Two more people were answering substantive claim questions in the same month, from two other states.

**Why it matters.** *Peterson* asks what investigation a reasonable insurer would have conducted, and separately what this insurer actually did. A file answered in one month by three people in three states through a shared queue, with no single owner, is evidence about the second question — and it is a plausible explanation for why the answers do not line up:

- Oct. 10 (Miller): the file contains **no report** on the requested date.
- Oct. 29 (Brau): all the information **we can release** has already been sent — which concedes something was withheld.

Those are easier to reconcile as two people reading the same file differently than as one person changing position. That is not a defense to either statement; it is the mechanism, and it is worth establishing.

**Practical consequences:**

1. **Add Miller and Bell** to the persons-with-knowledge list, to the entity-identity requests, and to the deposition candidates. Interrogatory 13 already reaches "each person known to You to have knowledge," so it captures them — but naming them removes any argument about scope.
2. **The shared mailbox is a discoverable systems fact.** Ask who staffs `myadjuster@farmersinsurance.com`, how messages are assigned, and whether any single adjuster owned this file after the appraisal.
3. **The thread tokens are precise document-discovery handles.** `[HC183T003]` and `[3CAB117Z3]` let Defendant's system retrieve exactly the right threads — a much harder request to resist as overbroad than "all communications."

## 3. ⚠️ Still missing: Plaintiffs' own October 21 question

This PDF preserves **Defendant's reply only**. The email it answers — Plaintiffs' October 21, 2025 message — is still compilation-only:

> "As of now, 2 windows are approved: one 1-sash window at the corner of stairs facing front; and one 3-sash windows in family room facing back. But pretty much all the windows were damaged, as evidenced by the photos submitted through the claim process. Why they are not covered? What do you want us to do?"

**This is the most useful unpreserved document left in the chain**, for a reason unrelated to the refusal narrative: it is **the first document that identifies the two awarded windows in plain language**. The Appraisal Award names them only as "F1.7" and "B1.2" — undefined codes that Plaintiffs cannot decode from the award's face, which is the subject of Request for Admission 18 and of the related production requests.

Plaintiffs' own description is not binding on Defendant. But it was sent to Defendant, Defendant replied the same day, and **Defendant did not correct it** — while Defendant has elsewhere shown itself willing to correct a window description (the September 19, 2025 letter correcting "dining room" to "family room"). An uncorrected description, in a thread Defendant answered, is worth having as an original.

Export it from the sent-mail folder with full headers, alongside the reply.
