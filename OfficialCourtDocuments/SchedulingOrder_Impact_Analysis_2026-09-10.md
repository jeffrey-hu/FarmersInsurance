## 1. Bottom line

The Court adopted Defendant's proposed schedule almost in full, extended two dates modestly, and adopted **none** of Plaintiffs' substantive (non-date) discovery terms.

The Order's opening paragraph is itself a finding on the record: Plaintiffs' plan "proposes deadlines that are more than one year beyond the timeline for similar cases," and the Court "will not fully adopt" it. That sentence sets the baseline against which any future request to extend a deadline will be measured under Minn. R. Civ. P. 16.02 ("A schedule shall not be modified except by leave of court upon a showing of good cause"). Plaintiffs should assume that a generalized "we need more time" motion fails, and that anything they want changed has to be tied to a specific, documented obstruction or a specific event that could not have been anticipated.

The practical effect: **this case now runs on roughly a 6-month fact-discovery clock and reaches a trial block about eight months earlier than Plaintiffs asked for.** The single most urgent consequence is the **November 16, 2026 joinder deadline** — 67 days out — which arrives long before any meaningful document production.

## 3. What the Order does NOT say — and why that matters most

Paragraphs 3, 4, and 6 of Plaintiffs' 9/4 plan asked the Court to build four protections into the Scheduling Order. **None appear anywhere in the Order.** They are not denied; they are simply absent. That means they now have to be obtained by stipulation or by motion, and until they are, the default is Defendant's version.

**(a) ESI form of production — native format, metadata intact, no flattening.** Not ordered. Nothing in the Order stops Farmers from producing the inspection photographs as flattened images or printed PDFs with EXIF stripped. This is not a technicality in this case: the photographs from the three inspections (Hancock Claims Consultants 8/29/2024, adjuster Nicholas Brau 9/6/2024, Prieve Engineering File #241008) carry the timestamps, camera data, and in some cases geolocation that establish what was actually photographed, when, and by whom — and Farmers has given two irreconcilable accounts of whether the 9/6/2024 Brau inspection record exists at all. Original file metadata is the evidence that resolves that. **This should be the first item in a proposed stipulated ESI order.**

**(b) Privilege log under Rule 26.02(f) plus a non-waiver / claw-back order.** Not ordered. Without a court-entered claw-back, an inadvertent production fight is governed by common-law waiver rules, and — as Plaintiffs' plan noted — a mere party agreement does not bind non-parties. Given the volume of non-party discovery contemplated here (Hancock, Prieve, the two appraisers, the umpire), this is worth having on paper.

**(c) Protective order — single tier, no attorneys'-eyes-only, with named access for Jeffrey Hu.** Not ordered. **This is the largest unaddressed exposure in the Order.** Plaintiffs are pro se. If Farmers designates the claims-handling policies, training materials, vendor-retention agreements, or internal communications as "attorneys' eyes only," there is no attorney of record on Plaintiffs' side to receive them — the designation would operate as a complete bar on Plaintiffs' access to the evidence in their own case. The Order gives Plaintiffs no protection against that. It should be resolved by stipulation now, before Farmers has produced anything and has an incentive to hold the designation as leverage.

**(d) Substantial-completion-of-production milestone.** Not ordered. Production will be rolling against a hard 3/15/2027 close, which means depositions and non-party discovery must be scheduled against a moving record — precisely the problem Plaintiffs' plan identified.

**Recommended action:** within the next two weeks, send Baskfield a single proposed **Stipulated Protective, ESI, and Claw-Back Order** covering all four items, with a request for a response by a date certain. If Defendant refuses or does not respond, the ¶4 conference-call process (below) is available and there is ample runway now — there will not be later.

## 4. Structural problems inside the Order

These are internal tensions in the Order itself. Several should be raised and resolved now, while there is time, rather than discovered in April 2027.

### 4.1 The expert schedule collides with the discovery close

¶2 states flatly: **"All discovery must be completed by March 15, 2027."** But ¶3 sets Defendant's expert reports at **4/17/2027** and rebuttal reports at **5/14/2027** — both after discovery has closed.

Read literally, there is **no window in which Plaintiffs may depose Farmers' causation expert.** For a case whose central dispute is expert causation testimony about hail damage to windows, that is not a small problem.

The most likely reading is that ¶2 means *fact* discovery (Defendant's own plan had the same defect — a 2/19/2027 close with Plaintiffs' experts due 3/15/2027 — so the Court inherited it). But "all discovery" is what the Order says, and a self-represented party should not rely on an unwritten reading.

**Recommended action:** ask Baskfield to confirm in writing that expert depositions may be taken after 3/15/2027 and to stipulate to a date by which expert discovery closes (Plaintiffs proposed a separate expert-discovery close; the Order has none). If Defendant will not stipulate, use the ¶4 letter to ask the Court to clarify. Do this in the next 60 days, not next spring.

### 4.2 Plaintiffs' expert report is due the same day discovery closes

¶3(a) requires Plaintiffs' "expert interrogatories, disclosures, and reports" — note that expert *interrogatories* are included — to be complete by 3/15/2027, the same day all discovery closes.

A retained causation expert cannot write a report on 3/15 using documents produced on 3/15. The expert needs the claim file, the three inspection reports and their underlying photographs, the 2020 claim file and Xactimate estimate, and the appraisal panel's materials, with enough lead time to analyze them and draft.

**Practical consequence: Plaintiffs' real document-discovery deadline is not 3/15/2027. It is roughly December 2026 – early January 2027.** Everything in the discovery plan should be back-planned from that date, not from the Order's date. Retaining the expert should happen well before then, so the expert can tell Plaintiffs what to ask for.

### 4.3 Motion practice is compressed and back-loaded

¶5: **all** dispositive and non-dispositive motions must be **HEARD** by 5/14/2027. Under Minn. Gen. R. Prac. 115.03 and 115.04, that back-calculates to:

- **Dispositive motions: serve and file by 4/16/2027** (28 days before the hearing).
- **Non-dispositive motions: serve and file by 4/23/2027** (21 days before the hearing).
- Responses 14 days before hearing; replies 7 days before; 35-page memorandum limit under 115.05.

Three collisions follow:

1. **Dispositive briefing closes the day before Defendant's expert report is due.** A Plaintiffs' summary-judgment motion filed 4/16/2027 is briefed without Farmers' expert opinion on file (due 4/17). Conversely, Farmers can move for summary judgment on its "the September 12, 2025 Appraisal Award is binding" affirmative defense on 4/16 with its own expert not yet disclosed.
2. **Rebuttal expert reports and the last available motion hearing are the same day (5/14/2027).** The expert record is not complete until the last day a motion can be heard.
3. **Hearing dates are the choke point.** Both filing deadlines assume a hearing date on or before 5/14/2027 is actually available on Judge Anderson's calendar. Hennepin County requires the filer to contact judicial staff for scheduling. Dates should be reserved **months** in advance, not weeks. Practically: reserve motion hearing dates in **January–February 2027**.

### 4.4 The § 604.18 bad-faith window is narrow and unmarked

Plaintiffs asked for a separate 11/1/2027 deadline to file the Minn. Stat. § 604.18 motion to amend. The Court set no such deadline. So the motion falls under ¶5's general rule: it is a non-dispositive motion, must be **filed by 4/23/2027** and **heard by 5/14/2027**.

Because § 604.18, subd. 4 bars pleading first-party bad faith in the original complaint and requires a later motion supported by affidavits, and because that showing depends on the discovery record, the working window is:

> **3/15/2027 (discovery closes) → 4/23/2027 (motion must be filed) — about 5½ weeks**

That is 5½ weeks to digest the full claim file, assemble affidavits, and brief both prongs of the *Peterson v. Western National Mutual Ins. Co.* test: (i) objectively, that a reasonable insurer under the circumstances would not have denied benefits — which turns on the level of investigation a reasonable insurer would have conducted; and (ii) subjectively, that Farmers knew of, or recklessly disregarded, the lack of a reasonable basis. Doing that in 5½ weeks, pro se, while also managing dispositive-motion briefing due 4/16, is not realistic.

**The mitigating fact — and it is a real one — is that the Court set NO deadline for amending pleadings.** ¶1 restricts *joinder* only. Rule 16.02 permits a court to limit the time for "joining other parties and amending pleadings"; this Court limited only the first. Amendment is therefore governed by Rule 15.01 and by § 604.18's own procedure.

**Recommended action:** treat the § 604.18 motion as a **February–March 2027** filing, not an April one, and build the discovery plan to produce the prima facie record by then. Filing early also has a tactical benefit: a granted § 604.18 amendment materially changes the settlement calculus going into the June 1, 2027 mediation. Reserve a hearing date early.

### 4.5 The Daubert / Frye-Mack timing is internally inconsistent

¶8 contains a NOTICE: "The Court requires motions to exclude expert testimony and objections to deposition testimony be filed **at least 30 days before the start of trial**" — i.e., roughly **8/21/2027** against a 9/20/2027 block start.

¶5 says **all** motions must be **heard** by 5/14/2027.

These cannot both be satisfied. The repository's existing Frye-Mack / Daubert package (`OffenseStrategies/Motions/Daubert/`) is aimed at excluding Farmers' causation opinion. Under ¶5 it would have to be filed by 4/23/2027 — one week after Farmers' expert report is disclosed (4/17) and three weeks before rebuttal reports. Under ¶8 it would be filed in late August 2027.

The ¶8 NOTICE is the more specific provision and reflects normal practice for expert-exclusion motions, but ¶5 is written in absolute terms. **This should be clarified in writing well before April 2027** — the difference is four months of preparation time and whether the motion can be built on the expert's deposition.

### 4.6 The discovery-dispute protocol adds substantial latency

¶4 imposes a mandatory, multi-step process before any discovery motion:

1. Confer in good faith under Minn. R. Civ. P. 37.01(b) and Minn. Gen. R. Prac. 115.10;
2. If unresolved, **serve and file a correspondence of no more than three pages** describing the dispute and requesting a conference call with the Court;
3. **The Court will not consider any filings on the dispute until the conference call occurs;**
4. Only then may a formal motion be brought — which itself needs a hearing date and 21 days' notice.

Realistically that is **four to eight weeks** from first impasse to any enforceable order. Against a 3/15/2027 discovery close and a 4/23/2027 non-dispositive filing deadline, a dispute that first surfaces in February 2027 is very likely unremediable in time to be useful. This is the exact risk Plaintiffs' 9/4 plan flagged ("A production compelled after expert reports have been served is of little use to the party that sought it"), and the Order does not address it.

**Operating rule: any discovery dispute must reach the ¶4 letter stage no later than mid-January 2027.** Which means written discovery must be served early and deficiencies chased immediately rather than accumulated.

## 5. Most urgent item: the November 16, 2026 joinder deadline (67 days)

Farmers' **first affirmative defense** is that "there exists a lack of jurisdiction over Defendant FIE." Plaintiffs' 9/4 plan asked for a joinder/amendment deadline about thirty days after substantial completion of production, on the ground that the documents bearing on the identity and capacity of the contracting entity — the underwriting file and the attorney-in-fact documentation — sit inside Farmers' own production.

The Court set joinder at **11/16/2026**: fifty-two days after initial disclosures, and before any real production could occur.

Plaintiffs must therefore decide whether to add or substitute a party essentially on the current record. Suggested sequence, starting immediately:

1. **This week — serve a targeted First Set of Interrogatories and Requests for Production directed narrowly at entity identity and capacity.** Which entity issued and underwrote the policy; the subscription/attorney-in-fact agreement between Fire Insurance Exchange and Fire Underwriters Association; which entity employed adjuster Nicholas Brau; which entity made the 9/3/2024 and 11/4/2024 denial decisions; which entity retained Hancock and Prieve; which entity holds the claim file. Responses are due in 30 days (add 3 days if served by mail), which lands in mid-to-late October — in time to act before 11/16.
2. **In parallel, write to Baskfield** and ask her to state, in writing, whether FIE contends it is not the correct party, and if so to identify the correct entity. A refusal to answer is itself useful if an extension motion becomes necessary; an answer is more useful.
3. **If the position is still unclear by about 11/1/2026, file a protective motion** to extend the joinder deadline (or to join an entity in the alternative), supported by the correspondence showing Plaintiffs asked and Defendant would not say. Do not let 11/16 pass on the assumption it can be fixed later — with a first affirmative defense already asserting the point, that assumption is unsafe.

Note: ¶1 by its terms limits *joinder of additional parties*. It does not by its terms bar an amendment correcting a misnomer, and Rule 15.03 relation-back may be available. But that is a fallback argument, not a plan.

## 6. What actually went Plaintiffs' way

Worth recording, because these are the parts of the Order to build the case around:

- **Mediation is scheduled after all three expert rounds.** Mediation must be completed by 6/1/2027; rebuttal reports are due 5/14/2027 and the dispositive-motion cutoff is the same day. So the parties will mediate with **both sides' causation opinions in hand and after the summary-judgment stage has run.** That is precisely the sequencing Plaintiffs argued for in their "Note on sequencing," and the Court gave it to them. Farmers will arrive at mediation knowing whether its appraisal-award defense survived.
- **¶6 and ¶7 compel decision-makers to attend.** Both mediation and the pretrial conference require attendance by "the attorneys who will try the case, the parties involved in the litigation, and **any claims adjuster or other person who has authority to settle the case**." Farmers must produce someone with settlement authority in person — not just outside counsel.
- **The trial setting is a block, not a fixed date.** 9/20/2027 – 10/22/2027, with a comprehensive Trial Order about five weeks before the block opens. More runway than Defendant's proposed fixed 9/1/2027 date.
- **No deadline to amend pleadings** (see §4.4).
- **Discovery close is 24 days later than Defendant proposed.**
- **¶9's bar on contacting chambers applies to "parties represented by an attorney."** Plaintiffs are pro se and therefore may communicate with the Court through the assigned clerk directly — chambers staff at `4thJudgeAndersonStaff@courts.state.mn.us` if the assigned clerk's address is unknown. Every such communication should be in writing and copied to Baskfield, per ¶12(a)(iii).
- **¶12(a) confirms Plaintiffs are excluded from the eFS mandate** and may file by eFS, mail, or hand delivery to Civil Filing (300 South 6th Street, #C-332). ¶12(a)(ii) is a warning worth heeding: documents sent to Judge Anderson rather than Civil Filing are **not filed** and will not be considered.

## 10. Trial-mechanics requirements to build into the exhibit workflow now

These do not bite until 2027 but they change how evidence should be organized from today forward, and retro-fitting an exhibit set is expensive.

- **MNDES is mandatory** (¶16). Register at `www.mncourts.gov/mndes`. Failure to upload and share an exhibit **may result in exclusion**.
- **One exhibit per upload.** Do not combine exhibits into a single PDF (¶16(b)).
- **Plaintiffs' exhibit numbers are 001–499**; Defendant's are 500–999 (¶16(c)). Related exhibits use dashes or decimals (0001-1, 0001.1). Start numbering the evidence set in that range now.
- **Every MNDES "Description" must exactly match the description on the exhibit list** (¶16(b)), and must be neutral and identifiable, with names, dates, and locations.
- **Multi-page exhibits require page or Bates numbers** under Minn. Gen. R. Prac. 16 (¶16(d)). Bates-number the claim file and inspection materials as they come in — not in August 2027.
- **Exhibits upload as "public"** unless law or court order provides otherwise (¶16(e)) — another reason to get the protective order right early.
- **Two paper copies of every document and photographic exhibit** must be brought to trial, with a certification on the record that they match what was uploaded (¶17).
- **Meet and confer on exhibits before trial** to organize lists, eliminate duplicates, and resolve objections; exhibit lists exchanged before the pretrial conference (¶14).
- Do not contact the judge's staff for MNDES help (¶16(j)); MNDES Support is 651-413-8160 / 833-707-2791.
