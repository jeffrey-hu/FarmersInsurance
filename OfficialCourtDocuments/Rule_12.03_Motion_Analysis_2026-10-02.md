## 1. What this document is and is not

FIE asks the Court for three things:

1. **Judgment on the pleadings** under Minn. R. Civ. P. 12.03. In effect, it asks the Court to rule that even if every fact in our Complaint is true, the law gives us no relief.
2. **A stay of all further discovery** until the Court decides the motion.
3. "Such additional relief as the Court shall deem just and proper."

**What it does not contain:** any argument. The notice says the Memorandum of Law "will be submitted in accordance with the Rules." We will not know FIE's actual grounds until the memorandum arrives. Everything in §3 below is an informed prediction, built from FIE's Answer and the law.

**Why now:** Our RFPs (due 10/15), Interrogatories (due 10/20) and RFAs (due 10/21) are coming due. The stay request is aimed at those responses as much as at the merits.

**Rule 12.03 basics (verified text).** The motion may be brought "after the pleadings are closed but within such time as not to delay the trial." The pleadings closed with FIE's 8/10/2026 Answer, so the motion is procedurally proper. If "matters outside the pleadings are presented … and not excluded," the motion becomes a summary judgment motion. In that case "all parties shall be given reasonable opportunity to present all material made pertinent … by Rule 56."

**The standard favors us.** Minnesota rejected the federal *Twombly/Iqbal* "plausibility" test in *Walsh v. U.S. Bank, N.A.*, 851 N.W.2d 598 (Minn. 2014).
- A claim survives if "it is possible on any evidence which might be produced, consistent with the pleader's theory, to grant the relief demanded." *Walsh* quotes this from *N. States Power Co. v. Franklin*, 265 Minn. 391, 122 N.W.2d 26 (1963).
- The Court accepts our factual allegations as true. *Bahr v. Capella Univ.*, 788 N.W.2d 76, 80 (Minn. 2010).
- The Court may also consider documents the Complaint references, such as the policy, the appraisal award and the appraisal demand, without converting the motion. *N. States Power Co. v. Minn. Metro. Council*, 684 N.W.2d 485 (Minn. 2004) [verify pin, likely 490 n.4].

## 3. What FIE will probably argue, and how strong each argument is

### Count I (breach of contract) and Count II (declaratory judgment)

**FIE's likely theory.**
- The award is binding: FIE admits this in Answer ¶48 and Affirmative Defense 2.
- The award's ACV of $6,400 is below the $17,720 deductible, so nothing is owed. FIE pleads this in Answer ¶¶2, 35.
- Therefore no breach is possible as a matter of law.
- Anything more we want is an attack on the "adequacy" of the award, which Minnesota does not allow absent fraud or misfeasance. *Mork v. Eureka-Security Fire & Marine Ins. Co.*, 230 Minn. 382, 42 N.W.2d 33 (1950).

**New adverse authority to expect.** *Jamestown Villas HOA v. State Farm Fire & Cas. Co.*, No. 25-3154 (8th Cir. Sept. 4, 2026), applies Minnesota law and holds:
- "Only ambiguous awards require resubmission."
- When a panel acts within its authority, its decision is "binding."
- Courts "cannot review it for 'adequacy or inadequacy of amount.'"
- "Like kind and quality" (matching) is part of the amount of loss.

It is a federal decision, so it is persuasive only, but it is very recent and on point. Assume FIE's memo leads with it.

**Our strongest answers, in order:**

1. **Components never submitted to appraisal.** This is our strongest ground; it needs no fact-finding.
   - Our 8/3/2025 demand listed only Windows, Siding (repaint) and Siding (R&R). **FIE admitted that in Answer ¶30.**
   - Appraisers decide the "amount of loss" of what is put to them. They "may not construe the policy or decide whether the insurer should pay." *Quade v. Secura Ins.*, 814 N.W.2d 703, 706 (Minn. 2012) [verify pin].
   - An award on windows and siding cannot have decided the downspouts, flashing and sliding-door screen. Complaint ¶49(b) pleads exactly this.
   - Because the demand is referenced in the Complaint and admitted in the Answer, the Court can rely on it without converting the motion.

2. **The award's scope is ambiguous on its face.**
   - "Windows (F1.7 / B1.2)" uses undefined codes and gives one lump sum. There is no umpire report (FIE admits this in Answer ¶34). FIE's own appraiser gave three inconsistent descriptions (Answer ¶36).
   - *Quade* says whether an award "will be conclusive on all issues will depend on … the structure of the appraisal award" [verify pin].
   - The Eighth Circuit, applying Minnesota law, sends ambiguous awards back to the panel for clarification. *Cincinnati Ins. Co. v. Rymer Cos.*, 170 F.4th 1159 (8th Cir. 2026). *Rymer* cites *Herll v. Auto-Owners Ins. Co.*, 879 F.3d 293 (8th Cir. 2018), which remanded an award where "it was unclear whether it covered damage to the windows."
   - *Jamestown* itself confirms the rule that ambiguous awards are resubmitted. Its facts differ from ours: there was one clear dollar figure for a defined scope.
   - Ambiguity about what the panel evaluated is at minimum a fact question, not something to decide on the pleadings. See *Seamon v. Acuity*, No. A11-429 (Minn. App. Dec. 5, 2011) (unpublished; reversing summary judgment where the award left an issue open).

3. **Coverage determinations are for the Court.** If the panel left out the dining-room window because of a "prior claim," that is a coverage or offset decision, which *Quade* reserves to courts. Complaint ¶¶37, 50, 57(h)–(i) plead this.

4. **The deductible is not established as a matter of law.** If any additional covered loss survives, the $17,720 math cannot be decided on the pleadings. The gap is roughly $4,920 on an RCV basis, or more if the deductible is applied against ACV, depending on how the policy applies it.

5. **The implied covenant (¶53).** Minnesota recognizes no independent first-party bad-faith tort. *Morris v. Am. Family Mut. Ins. Co.*, 386 N.W.2d 233 (Minn. 1986), quoting *Haagenson*.
   - The covenant can, however, be pleaded as part of, or alongside, the contract claim. *Columbia Cas. Co. v. 3M Co.*, No. A11-1376 (Minn. App. Mar. 26, 2012) [verify reporter cite, believed 814 N.W.2d 33].
   - Frame it as a contract theory only, with no tort or extra-contractual damages.

**Weak spots to expect in Counts I and II:**
- **Matching and incidental costs on the two awarded windows (¶¶51–52).** *Jamestown* treats matching as part of the amount of loss, so it is decided by the award.
- **"The other ~25 windows."** FIE will say "Windows" means all windows. That argument has to be won on ambiguity (point 2 above), not on the photographs.

**Risk:**
- Counts I and II as a whole: **low-to-moderate.** Under *Walsh*, at least the unsubmitted-components claim and the clarification theory should survive.
- The matching and incidental-cost sub-theories: **elevated.**

### Count III (non-preclusion, modify, remand, or vacate the award)

**Likely FIE argument:** the award is itemized, and no fraud or misfeasance is alleged.

| Sub-theory | Risk | Why |
|---|---|---|
| (a) Not "so itemized" under § 65A.01 subd. 3, so non-preclusive | **High** | The award breaks out Windows, Siding & Paint, and Debris, each with RCV and ACV. A court will likely call that itemized. |
| (b) Modify or correct | **High** | No clear legal vehicle after *Oliver v. State Farm*, 939 N.W.2d 749 (Minn. 2020): appraisal is not arbitration, so the UAA's modification procedure does not apply. |
| (c) **Remand for clarification** | **Moderate. This is the one to fight for.** | *Rymer* and *Herll*: an ambiguous award goes back to the panel. This is also our best answer to *Jamestown*. |
| (d) Vacate under *Mork* | **High** | It requires fraud, misfeasance or wrongdoing, and the award gets "every presumption of validity." A missing umpire report and a contractor-selected appraiser are thin grounds for that standard. |

**Good news on timing:** *Oliver* holds that the UAA's 90-day limit does not apply to appraisal awards. The Court "do[es] not reach" what limit, if any, applies. So timeliness is not FIE's strongest point.

### Count IV (Consumer Fraud Act)

**This is the most exposed count. Be realistic about it.**

- **The "in connection with the sale" problem.**
  - Every act alleged (denial letters, the Prieve weather statement, the engineer-only rule, the award descriptions) happened after the loss, during claim handling.
  - *Force v. ITT Hartford*, 4 F. Supp. 2d 843 (D. Minn. 1998), involved sales-pitch misrepresentations.
  - The one on-point Minnesota decision refused a CFA claim based on a coverage denial because "the real gist of their claim is simply that USAA breached the insurance contract by denying coverage." *Siefferman v. USAA*, No. CX-97-63 (Minn. App. June 17, 1997) (unpublished).
  - Research found no Minnesota case applying the CFA to post-sale claim-handling statements.
- **The statutory bar.** Minn. Stat. § 604.18, subd. 2(b) (verified): "A violation of this section shall not be the basis for any claim or award under chapter 325D or 325F." FIE will argue that Count IV is a bad-faith claim relabeled.
- **No private right under the claims-practices statute.** There is no private action under § 72A.20/.201. *Morris*, 386 N.W.2d at 238 [verify pin].
- **What does help.**
  - § 325F.70, subd. 3 (2023 Minn. Laws ch. 52, art. 19, § 15) does give consumers a private action and declares that such an action "benefits the public." That likely removes the *Ly v. Nystrom*, 615 N.W.2d 302 (Minn. 2000) public-benefit hurdle.
  - But the same subdivision still requires injury "in connection with a sale of merchandise." No court has construed it yet.
- **Attorney fees.** We are pro se, so attorney fees are not recoverable: there are none "incurred." This is persuasive from *Kay v. Ehrler*, 499 U.S. 432 (1991). Costs, disbursements and investigation costs are a separate question.

**Risk: high.** See Decision B in §6.

### A defense FIE has *not* pleaded: the suit-limitation period

- FIE's Answer pleads no limitations defense.
- Rule 8.03 lists "statute of limitations" as an affirmative defense that must be pleaded. A judgment on the pleadings cannot rest on a defense that is not in the pleadings.
- **The latent risk is Minn. Stat. § 65A.26,** a one-year limit for "every policy of insurance against damage by hail." Our action was commenced 7/8/2026: FIE's own 7/28 letter says service under § 45.028 became effective that day. That is within two years of the loss but more than one year after it.
- We found no case applying § 65A.26 to a multi-peril homeowner's policy, and Complaint ¶57(g) already pleads our position and waiver/estoppel.
- **Watch for FIE to raise § 65A.26 in its memo or by a motion to amend its Answer.** If it appears in the memo, our response is:
  1. It is not pleaded, so it is not before the Court on a 12.03 motion.
  2. It is waived.
  3. It does not apply to a § 65A.01-form homeowner's policy.
  4. Waiver and estoppel, per Complaint ¶¶28–29.

## 4. The stay request

**There is no automatic stay.**
- Nothing in Rule 12 or Rule 26 stops discovery because a Rule 12 motion was filed.
- A stay requires a court order on "good cause shown" under Rule 26.03. Until the Court rules, **our deadlines are live.**

**But expect the Court may grant it.** Do not argue that stays are improper.
- Minnesota appellate courts have upheld stays pending dispositive motions, including against pro se plaintiffs: *Smith v. Britton*, No. A13-1039 (Minn. App. Feb. 3, 2014) (unpublished), and *Zhang v. Equity Office Props. Tr.*, No. A05-1094 (Minn. App. Apr. 11, 2006) (unpublished).
- A Hennepin County judge granted a full stay on "judicial efficiency" grounds in *State v. Glock, Inc.*, 27-CV-24-18827 (Hennepin Cnty. June 2, 2025).
- Argue instead that **FIE has not shown good cause on these facts.**

**Our arguments against the stay:**

1. **Procedure.**
   - Scheduling Order ¶4 says no discovery dispute will be heard without conferral and a ≤3-page letter requesting a conference call "before filing a formal motion." FIE skipped both steps.
   - Gen. R. Prac. 115.10 says "No motion will be heard unless the parties have conferred."
   - Ask the Court to decline to hear the stay request.
2. **The motion is unlikely to dispose of the case.** Under *Walsh*, at least part of Counts I and II will survive (see §3). A partial win for FIE does not justify freezing all discovery.
3. **Prejudice to us from the schedule.**
   - The joinder deadline (11/16) falls *before* the hearing.
   - Discovery closes 3/15/2027, and our expert disclosures are due the same day.
   - The Court already refused to extend the schedule (9/8 Order preamble).
   - Any stay running into December or January takes several months out of a roughly six-month discovery period.
4. **Little burden on FIE.** The discovery is already served, targeted at FIE's own claim file, and largely needed for any summary judgment motion FIE will bring later anyway.
5. **A compromise, as a fallback.** If the Court is inclined to stay, ask that the stay:
   - exclude the RFAs and the entity/joinder discovery (RFPs 52–53, Interrogatories 7–8, RFAs 4–8 and 113–115);
   - exclude core claim-file documents;
   - stay depositions only; and
   - extend every scheduling-order deadline by the length of the stay. A motion shell already exists in `Filings/Discovery/PotentialMotions/`.

**What FIE can safely skip while the motion is pending.**
- **Interrogatories and RFPs.** Rule 37.04 (verified) says failure to respond "may not be excused on the ground that the discovery sought is objectionable unless the party failing to act has applied for a protective order as provided by Rule 26.03." FIE's stay request is probably such an application. FIE will likely say it need not answer the Interrogatories and RFPs, and a court is unlikely to sanction it while the request is pending.
- **RFAs.** Rule 37.04 does not cover RFAs. Under Rule 36.01, unanswered RFAs are deemed admitted after 10/21.
  - Courts readily allow withdrawal of admissions under Rule 36.02. Treat any deemed admissions as leverage, not as a win to announce (consistent with the Service Log note).
  - Do not treat anything as admitted before **10/22**.
