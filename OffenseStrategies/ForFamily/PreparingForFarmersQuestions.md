**Loss Date:** July 13, 2024

> **Who this is for.** Yibiao Lu (Bill) and Jie Hu. This is the family's preparation guide for the two channels through which Farmers' lawyers will question you directly: **written interrogatories** (a list of questions you answer in writing under oath) and **depositions** (a live, sworn, recorded oral examination by Farmers' counsel).

> **What this covers.** What Farmers will ask, in both channels. How to prepare. The traps Farmers will try to set, and how not to fall into them. Practical mechanics of how to handle each question.

## 1. The Two Channels Farmers Will Use to Question You

### 1.1 Written Interrogatories (Minn. R. Civ. P. 33)

Farmers will serve you with a written list of up to 50 questions (called "interrogatories"). You and your counsel (if any) draft written answers, sign them under oath, and serve them back within **30 days** (extendable by agreement). Each answer becomes a sworn statement that Farmers can use at deposition and at trial.

**Key features:**
- Answer in writing — you have time to think, consult records, consult counsel.
- Each answer is under oath and binds you.
- You can object to questions that are vague, overbroad, irrelevant, or call for privileged information.
- You have a **continuing duty to supplement** (Rule 26.05) if your answer becomes incomplete or incorrect.

### 1.2 Depositions (Minn. R. Civ. P. 30)

Farmers' counsel will take your sworn oral deposition — typically a full day (7 hours under Rule 30.04(d)). It will be recorded by a court reporter, likely also videotaped, and Farmers will use the transcript at every later stage of the case. You will be deposed individually — Bill and Jie separately.

**Key features:**
- Live, oral questioning.
- You are under oath; the transcript becomes evidence at trial.
- You answer the question asked — no more, no less.
- Your spouse, family members, friends, contractor, etc. cannot coach you during the deposition.
- You can review and correct minor transcript errors afterward under Rule 30.05.

Each channel has different traps. This document covers both.

## 3. Likely Deposition Topics — Both Bill and Jie

The deposition is broader than the interrogatories. Farmers' counsel will have your interrogatory answers in hand, will have read the entire case file, and will probe for inconsistencies. Each topic below identifies (i) what Farmers will ask, (ii) the right way to handle it, and (iii) the traps to avoid.

### 3.1 Background (15–30 minutes)

**Farmers will ask:** Name, age, address, occupation, education, work history, marital status, household composition, prior addresses, prior lawsuits, prior insurance claims, citizenship status, driver's license, any criminal record.

**How to handle:** Answer directly. Routine.

**Traps:** None major in this section, but if there are sensitive personal items (immigration, prior criminal, mental health), pre-discuss with counsel. Do not be evasive — evasion damages credibility.

### 3.2 Educational and Professional Background (15 minutes)

**Farmers will ask:** Degrees, professional certifications, employment history, technical training. Particularly: any engineering, construction, or building-science training (to see whether you can opine on damage).

**How to handle:** Be accurate. Identify your fields of expertise (if any) and disclaim others.

**Traps:** Don't volunteer expertise you don't have. If Farmers later asks you to opine on engineering matters and your prior testimony disclaimed engineering, the disclaimer is your shield.

### 3.3 The Property and the Policy (30 minutes)

**Farmers will ask:** When did you purchase the home? Who lives there? Did you maintain the property (gutters cleaned, roof inspected, windows caulked)? When did you first purchase the Farmers policy? Have you renewed every year? Have you read the policy? Did you read endorsement J6944?

**How to handle:** Use the documented record — `HomePolicy/2014_Application-PolicyDocument-980554965.pdf` and `HomePolicy/2023_Renewal.pdf`. Confirm 10/22/2014 origination; confirm continuous coverage.

**Traps:**
- **Maintenance trap:** Farmers will try to characterize ordinary wear as your fault. Be accurate about maintenance — what you actually did. If you didn't climb to inspect upper windows, say so; that is not unreasonable for a homeowner.
- **"Did you read the policy" trap:** see Interrogatory Q6 above. Truthful answer is best.

### 3.4 Prior Claims (30–45 minutes)

**Farmers will ask:** Tell me about the 2015 claim. The 2020 claim. What damage. What was paid. What was repaired. What remained outstanding. Did you replace the windows that were approved in 2020? Did you read the 2020 settlement letter?

**How to handle:** Use the 2020 Xactimate documents (`EventDocuments/FinalDocument_2020.pdf`). The key facts: 2020 approved exactly 2 casement windows (line items 1–4); the two window sashes and the exterior door were never actually completed before the COVID extension expired (Building Loss Worksheet — "Work Not Completed").

**Traps:**
- **The "you didn't complete the 2020 work" trap:** Farmers will try to argue that any current damage on the unrepaired-2020-scope windows is your problem because you didn't fix it. Counter: the 2020 scope was 2 windows; the current claim is about damage to many other windows.
- **The serial-claimant trap:** Farmers will frame 2014–present as a pattern of claim-seeking. Counter: continuous policyholder for 10 years with two storm-related claims is not unusual.

### 3.5 The 7/13/2024 Storm (15–30 minutes)

**Farmers will ask:** Where were you on 7/13–7/14/2024? Did you hear hail? See hail? Did your neighbors? Did you take any action that day or the next day to inspect the property? Did you photograph the storm? What is your understanding of the storm based on weather records?

**How to handle:** Truthful. Most homeowners do not climb ladders to inspect window wraps after a storm at 1:00 AM. The NWS storm report is the authoritative record. The family's neighbor canvass and City permit records corroborate the storm's effects on the neighborhood.

**Traps:**
- **The "you don't remember" trap:** Farmers will try to make you sound vague on what you observed. Counter: you observed what you observed; the NWS and the appraisal award document the storm.
- **The "if it was so bad why didn't you act" trap:** Window damage is not visible from the ground; nobody noticed until Legacy's professional inspection on 8/13.

### 3.6 Damage Discovery (30 minutes)

**Farmers will ask:** When did you first see damage? Who told you? Why did it take 5 weeks? Why didn't you inspect after the storm? Did you have a roofer come out? How did you decide to call Farmers?

**How to handle:** Mary's 8/9 contact; Jack's 8/13 visit; family opens claim same day. Document support: communication log.

**Traps:**
- **The 5-week-delay trap:** see § 6.1 below — this is one of the principal traps and is addressed at length.
- **The "the contractor was looking for work" trap:** Farmers will argue Legacy had financial motive to identify damage. Counter: Mary's initial contact was about a leak, not about generating a windows claim. Legacy is one of several professionals who later observed damage (Brau, Prieve, Foster, Roos all observed damage).

### 3.7 The Hancock Inspection 8/29/2024 (15 minutes)

**Farmers will ask:** Were you present? What did Hancock do? How long was the inspection? Did Hancock go on the roof? Use a ladder? Discuss findings with you?

**How to handle:** Document what you observed. Hancock did a systematic photo set tagged "Damaged – No" without close-up examination — this is the Hancock methodology gap that the Grimm deposition develops.

**Traps:**
- **"You did not request a different inspector" trap:** Counter: you did not select Hancock; Farmers did. You cooperated with Farmers' chosen vendor.

### 3.8 The 9/3/2024 First Denial (15 minutes)

**Farmers will ask:** Did you receive the denial letter? Read it? Understand it? What did you do?

**How to handle:** Yes received; reviewed; promptly responded with 9/5/2024 photo submission and the claim reopened.

**Traps:**
- **The "the 9/3 denial was clear" trap:** Counter: the denial was procedural; Farmers itself reopened the claim within days. The 9/3 denial is the baseline that Farmers' own subsequent positions walk away from (`offenseStrategy.md` § 5.3).

### 3.9 The 9/6/2024 Brau Inspection (30–45 minutes)

**Farmers will ask:** What did Brau do? What did he say? What did you point out? Did he take photos? Did he take measurements? Did you take notes? Did you discuss the 9/3 denial with him?

**How to handle:** Truthful and detailed. Brau personally observed dents alongside you and measured window dimensions. This is one of the most important factual events for the case — Brau is Farmers' own employee, and his observations contradict Farmers' formal denial three days earlier (`Deposition/1.1Brau_Nicholas.md` § 1).

**Traps:**
- **The "you don't remember exactly what was said" trap:** It's been ~20 months. Be honest about what you remember vs. what you reconstructed from contemporaneous notes.
- **The "you were leading Brau" trap:** Farmers may suggest you "showed him what to look for." Counter: you pointed out damage you had identified; Brau independently inspected and made observations.

### 3.10 The Prieve Inspection and 11/4/2024 Second Denial (30 minutes)

**Farmers will ask:** What did Prieve do on 10/9/2024? Did you give him documentation? Did you discuss his methodology? Did you receive the 11/4 denial? Read it? Understand it? What was your response?

**How to handle:** Document what you observed. You handed Prieve a damage-documentation binder (`ClaimTimeline.md` Missing Documentation #8). The 11/4 denial rests on Prieve's report; the family disputes its factual premises (NWS evidence; City permits; neighbor canvass).

**Traps:**
- **The "you do not have the engineering training to disagree with Prieve" trap:** Counter: you do not need engineering training to point out that NWS records, City permits, and a 14-of-19 neighbor canvass contradict Prieve's "no hail after 2020" assertion. Those are public records, not engineering opinions.

### 3.11 The 2/3/2025 Monson Call (30–60 minutes — CRITICAL)

**Farmers will ask:** What time was the call? Who initiated it? What did Monson say verbatim? What did you say? Did you record it? Did you write it down? When did you write it down? Who else heard it?

**How to handle:**
- This is the centerpiece of the bad-faith case (`offenseStrategy.md` § 5.2). Be precise.
- **Use your contemporaneous notes.** If you wrote down what Monson said on or near 2/3/2025, refer to those notes.
- Identify Monson's exact words to the extent you remember them, particularly the "engineer's report only" statement and the "extruded aluminum windows are easily damaged" statement.
- If you do not remember exact wording, say what Monson said *in substance* — and rely on Brau's 2/10/2025 voicemail (which is in writing) for the corroboration.

**Traps:**
- **The "you don't have a recording" trap:** Farmers may try to suggest the call did not occur as you describe. Counter: Brau's 2/10 voicemail attributes the rule to "my field claim supervisor" — that is Monson. Your account is corroborated independently.
- **The "you might be misremembering" trap:** Counter: you wrote it down at the time; you reported the same content to MN Dept of Commerce on 1/19/2024 [verify date]; the contemporaneous record is consistent with your testimony.

### 3.12 The Appeals — 1/31/2025 and 2/10/2025 (30 minutes)

**Farmers will ask:** Walk me through your 1/31 appeal package. Where did you get the stormersite data? The City permits? The neighbor canvass? How did you select the 14 neighbors? Did the neighbors sign anything? Did you record any of those conversations?

**How to handle:** Document the methodology. The neighbor canvass methodology was disclosed in the 2/10/2025 second response (`EventDocuments/2025-02-10-SecondResponseToFinalReport.pdf`). The City permits are public records. Stormersite is a commercial weather-data service.

**Traps:**
- **The "your neighbor canvass is hearsay" trap:** Counter: the canvass was offered to Farmers to prompt Farmers' own investigation, not as the family's case-in-chief. The witnesses can be subpoenaed for the family's case if needed.
- **The "you didn't follow scientific methodology" trap:** Counter: the family is not an investigative agency; the canvass was a homeowner's good-faith effort to corroborate the storm. Farmers refused to investigate further.

### 3.13 The 5/28/2025 Pietila Meeting and Upload (15 minutes)

**Farmers will ask:** What materials did you bring? What did Pietila do? Did he upload them? Did Farmers respond?

**How to handle:** NWS article and Interactive Hail Maps data. Pietila personally uploaded to the claim portal and requested review. 7/11/2025 Pietila confirms "haven't got any update."

**Traps:** Few. This is a fact-friendly section for the family.

### 3.14 The Appraisal Process (45–60 minutes)

**Farmers will ask:** Why did you demand appraisal? Who is Zach Sexe? Pure Construction? How did you select John Foster? Did you interview him? Did you pay his retainer? What did the appraisal panel do on 9/12/2025? Were you present? Did you speak with the appraisers?

**How to handle:** Truthful. Pure Construction selected Foster, not the family. The family signed the retainer at $2,200. The family did not interview Foster and learned of his prior insurance-industry employment only later. The family was prevented from speaking with the appraisers on 9/12/2025 — directly contrary to the family's prior requests (`EventDocuments/ComunicationLogsWithPureConstruction.md` 7/10/2025 and 9/12/2025 entries).

**Traps:**
- **The "your appraiser agreed with the $12,800" trap:** Counter: Foster was selected by Pure Construction, not by the family; the family did not interview Foster; the family learned of Foster's prior insurance-industry employment only later; the family had no contact with Foster on 9/12/2025; the family's photo record shows damage to many windows beyond what the panel evaluated.
- **The "you signed the Sworn Proof of Loss at $310,000" trap:** Counter: the Proof of Loss is the family's good-faith estimate of total loss; appraisal is a contractual mechanism for resolving "amount of loss," not for setting it at the insurer's preferred figure.

### 3.15 Damages (30–45 minutes)

**Farmers will ask:** What is the $310,000 figure based on? Where did you get the per-window numbers? Have you actually paid for any repairs? What out-of-pocket expenses? What ongoing damages? Who is your expert on damages?

**How to handle:** Refer to the components: full-home re-fenestration; matching to current-production equivalents (Pella Reserve Traditional / Marvin Ultimate); siding repaint; siding R&R; consequential water damage; mold treatment; blinds; exterior repaint. **The expert witness on replacement-cost valuation is the appropriate ultimate authority — defer expert questions to the expert.**

**Traps:**
- **The "you have not actually paid this much" trap:** Counter: replacement-cost coverage under the policy is paid on a replacement-cost basis (net of depreciation held back pending repair) under standard homeowners' policy practice. You have not paid because Farmers has not paid you to repair.
- **The "you should have mitigated" trap:** Counter: you reported active leaks to Farmers on 10/28/2025; Farmers did not respond on the merits. The duty to mitigate does not require you to fund repairs Farmers refuses to cover.

### 3.16 Post-Appraisal Conduct (15–30 minutes)

**Farmers will ask:** Why did you continue corresponding after the appraisal? Did you accept the award? Did you accept the deductible application? What were your communications with Zorphion Miller, Twonda Miller, Jed Jones?

**How to handle:** The family disputes the awarded scope (only 2 windows out of many documented as damaged); Farmers' own descriptions of the awarded scope are inconsistent (9/18, 9/19, 10/14/2025); the umpire produced no methodology document.

**Traps:**
- **The "you accepted the appraisal" trap:** Counter: the family did not accept the award in any binding sense; the family continued to seek explanation and disputed the limited scope.

### 3.17 Preparation and Communications (15–30 minutes)

**Farmers will ask:** How did you prepare for this deposition? Who did you talk to? Did you review documents? Did you have an attorney? When did you start working on this case? Did you and your spouse discuss this case?

**How to handle:**
- **Privileged:** any communication with your attorney is protected by attorney-client privilege; do not waive.
- **Discussions with your spouse:** these are likely protected by marital communication privilege (Minn. Stat. § 595.02 Subd. 1(a)) AND, if you are both plaintiffs represented by the same counsel, by joint-client privilege.
- **Documents you reviewed:** if you reviewed a document specifically to refresh your recollection for the deposition, Farmers may be entitled to see that document (Minn. R. Evid. 612). Be careful what you cite.

**Traps:**
- **The "what did your attorney tell you" trap:** Refuse to answer. Privileged.
- **The "what did your spouse say" trap:** Marital communication privilege applies to confidential spousal communications.
- **The "what documents did you review" trap:** If you reviewed something specifically for the deposition, you may have to identify it — even produce it. Pre-clear what you review with counsel.

### 4.4 Spousal Alignment (Bill and Jie)

**This is critical.** Bill and Jie will be deposed separately. Their accounts must be consistent on key facts. Spend a substantial pre-deposition session (ideally 4–8 hours) ensuring you both agree on:

- The date, time, and substance of the 2/3/2025 Monson call.
- What Brau did on 9/6/2024.
- What Prieve did on 10/9/2024.
- The dates and content of every meeting with Pietila.
- The family's role in the Pure Construction engagement.
- The 9/12/2025 appraisal day (and the Zach instruction not to speak with the appraisers).
- The damages methodology.

**Do not coach each other on testimony.** Discuss what each of you actually observed and remembers. If you remember things differently, say so. Aligned testimony does not mean identical testimony — it means both of you tell the truth and your accounts are mutually consistent because both are accurate.

### 4.5 Photo Organization

Organize the family's photographic record by window, with date stamps where possible. The photos are the strongest single piece of damage evidence.

### 4.6 Communication Log Review

Read through `EventDocuments/Copy of ComunicationLogs.docx.md` at least twice. Know the dates, sequence, and substance of every entry. Farmers' counsel will quote from this; you should be ready to confirm or clarify each entry.

### 4.7 Mock Deposition

If at all possible, conduct a mock deposition 3–5 days before the actual deposition. The mock examiner should:

- Ask the hardest questions Farmers will ask.
- Push back on vague answers.
- Test your ability to say "I don't recall" when truthful.
- Test your ability to stay calm under aggressive questioning.

Ideally retain limited-scope counsel for the mock deposition; if not feasible, ask a family member or friend who is unfamiliar with the case to play the role of Farmers' counsel.

### 4.8 Physical Preparation

- Sleep well the night before.
- Eat a light breakfast.
- Dress professionally (not formal; not casual — business attire).
- Bring water.
- Bring reading glasses if you need them.
- Use the bathroom before each session.
- Plan to take breaks every 60–90 minutes.

## 5. The Traps — What Farmers Will Try To Do

This is the heart of the document. Each numbered trap is a specific tactic Farmers' counsel may use. For each, the entry tells you (i) what the trap looks like, (ii) why Farmers wants you to fall into it, (iii) the right response.

### 5.1 The "5-Week Delay" Trap

**What it looks like:** "The storm was on 7/13. You did not report damage until 8/13. Why did it take you five weeks to notice damage on your own home?"

**Why Farmers wants this:** To suggest (i) the damage might not be from the 7/13 storm, or (ii) you were dilatory in violation of the policy's notice provisions.

**Right response:** Hail damage to window wraps and upper-story window glass is **not visible from the ground or from inside the home**. The family is not a professional roofer or window inspector. The damage was identified by a Legacy contractor on a routine call about a leak (Mary's 8/9 contact; Jack's 8/13 inspection). The family promptly opened the claim on 8/13/2024 — the same day the damage was identified to them by a professional. There was no delay between *identification of damage* and *notice to Farmers*.

Be calm. Do not be defensive about the 5 weeks. Many homeowners never identify hail damage at all without a professional inspection.

### 5.2 The Serial-Claimant Trap

**What it looks like:** "You've had three claims on this property — 2015, 2020, and now 2024. You file a claim every time there's a storm."

**Why Farmers wants this:** To prejudice you in the jury's eyes as someone who treats insurance as a profit center.

**Right response:** You have been a continuous Farmers policyholder for over 10 years. You have filed claims when professionals identified damage caused by covered perils. That is what policies are for. The 2015 and 2020 claims were each processed by Farmers — they paid the 2020 claim (in part). Each claim was based on actual storm damage; you have not filed frivolous claims. The neighbor canvass shows 14 of 19 neighbors had hail-related repairs in the same post-2020 period — the family is consistent with the neighborhood pattern.

### 5.3 The Contractor-Conflict Trap

**What it looks like:** "Legacy identified the damage. Pure Construction selected your appraiser. Both contractors stood to profit if Farmers paid the claim. They had financial motive."

**Why Farmers wants this:** To suggest the damage record is contractor-driven and self-interested.

**Right response:**
- Legacy was called about a leak (a maintenance issue), not to generate a claim. The damage they identified was independent of any payout motive.
- The family did not select Pure Construction's choice of appraiser; the family signed Pure Construction's retainer at $2,200, paid half, and did not interview Foster. The family later learned Foster had prior insurance-industry employment.
- Multiple non-contractor professionals also observed damage: Brau (Farmers' own employee); Prieve (Farmers' own engineer, who catalogued dents at 12+ locations); the appraisal panel including the umpire (who confirmed hail causation).

The damage record does not depend on contractor testimony.

### 5.4 The "You Signed the Sworn Proof of Loss" Trap

**What it looks like:** "You signed the Sworn Proof of Loss at $310,000. Now tell me how you justify that exact figure."

**Why Farmers wants this:** To pin you to a number you may not be able to itemize precisely, then use any imprecision to attack the entire damages case.

**Right response:** The Sworn Proof of Loss is the family's good-faith estimate of total loss for the purpose of demanding appraisal under Minn. Stat. § 65A.01. The figure is built from documented components: full-home re-fenestration based on the Pella Reserve Traditional estimate; matching theory for current-production equivalents; siding work; mold treatment; consequential damages. The expert witness on replacement-cost valuation will provide the ultimate dollar-by-dollar accounting. The family does not need to defend each component dollar at deposition — the experts do.

### 5.5 The "Foster Signed the Award" Trap

**What it looks like:** "Your appraiser, John Foster, signed the appraisal award at $12,800. You can't now claim the award is wrong."

**Why Farmers wants this:** To suggest the family is bound by the award and is barred from challenging it.

**Right response:** Foster was selected by Pure Construction, not by the family; the family did not interview him; the family learned of his prior insurance-industry employment only later; the family had no contact with him or any panel member on 9/12/2025. The award is binding under § 65A.01 Subd. 3 because two of three panel members signed — but the award is also subject to clarification or remand under *Herll v. Auto-Owners Ins. Co.*, 879 F.3d 293 (8th Cir. 2018), and *Cincinnati Insurance Co. v. Rymer Cos.*, No. 24-3356 (8th Cir. Mar. 27, 2026), because the award is facially ambiguous (no itemization; Farmers' own inconsistent post-award descriptions; the umpire produced no methodology document). The family is not trying to *vacate* the award; the family is seeking *clarification* or *remand for additional evaluation* of windows the panel did not address.

### 5.6 The "Prieve Documented Damage" Gambit (Reverse-Use)

**What it looks like:** "You acknowledge Prieve's report. You rely on Prieve's catalog of dents at 12+ locations. You're picking and choosing which parts of Prieve's report to accept."

**Why Farmers wants this:** To make you appear to cherry-pick evidence.

**Right response:** The family does not "rely on" Prieve's report; the family points out that Prieve's own narrative documents damage at 12+ locations, which contradicts Farmers' 9/3 denial finding "no other damages observed." Acknowledging that Prieve's observations are correct does not mean accepting Prieve's flawed conclusions (e.g., "no hail in Eden Prairie after 2020," which is disproven by the NWS, City permits, neighbor canvass, and the 9/12/2025 appraisal panel's hail-causation finding). The damage observations are factual; the causation conclusions are an opinion that is independently disproven.

### 5.7 The Spousal-Inconsistency Trap

**What it looks like:** "Mr. Lu said the Monson call happened at 2:30 PM. But Mrs. Hu said it happened at 3:30 PM. Which is it?"

**Why Farmers wants this:** Even small inconsistencies between Bill's and Jie's testimony are used to question credibility broadly.

**Right response:**
- **Pre-deposition preparation:** Spend the spousal-alignment session ensuring both of you remember the key events the same way.
- **At deposition:** Stick to what you personally remember. If you don't remember exactly, say "I don't remember the exact time" — do not guess. If your spouse said something different, that's because their memory differs slightly, not because anyone is lying. Memory of a phone call 16 months ago is allowed to vary; the substance is what matters.
- **Refer to your contemporaneous record where applicable.**

### 5.8 The "You Have No Engineering Background" Trap

**What it looks like:** "You are not an engineer. You have no training in hail-damage assessment. Yet you are testifying that hail damaged your windows."

**Why Farmers wants this:** To suggest your damage claim rests on lay opinions you are not qualified to give.

**Right response:** You are testifying to what you observed — dents, leaks, marks, damage — not to engineering causation. The engineering causation is established by (i) the NWS storm record, (ii) Farmers' own appraisal panel finding hail causation for the awarded windows, (iii) Brau's contemporaneous observations of dents, and (iv) the family's forensic-engineering expert (per the discovery plan). The family's role at deposition is to testify to observation and timeline; expert opinions come from experts.

### 5.9 The "You Did Not Understand the Policy" / "You Did Understand the Policy" Whipsaw

**What it looks like:**
- If you say you did not read the policy: "You filed a $310,000 claim on a policy you never read?"
- If you say you did read it: "Then you understood endorsement J6944 limits matching, correct?"

**Why Farmers wants this:** No good answer.

**Right response:** Be truthful and precise. Most homeowners review the policy at issuance and renewal for coverage limits and deductibles — not for technical exclusions. Say what you did. If you did not review J6944 in detail, say so; that is not unreasonable. Coverage questions are interpreted by the court under the policy text and *contra proferentem* — your personal understanding of obscure provisions is not the legal standard.

### 5.10 The "Fairly Debatable" Admission Trap

**What it looks like:** "You agree that hail-damage claims can be technically complex, correct? That reasonable experts can disagree on causation, correct?"

**Why Farmers wants this:** To set up the Prong 1 ("fairly debatable") defense to the § 604.18 bad-faith claim. *Sela* requires plaintiffs to show absence of reasonable basis; admissions that the case is "complex" or "debatable" help Farmers.

**Right response:** Do not concede that Farmers had a reasonable basis. Be precise:
- "Hail damage *can* sometimes be technically complex."
- "But in this case, Farmers' own appraisal panel found hail causation."
- "And Farmers' own engineer Prieve documented dents at 12+ locations."
- "Reasonable experts may disagree about specific cases. I am here today because Farmers' own experts agree with us on causation."

### 5.11 The Mitigation Trap

**What it looks like:** "You reported active leaks on 10/28/2025. What have you done since then to prevent further damage? Have you tarped the roof? Replaced any windows yourself? Why not?"

**Why Farmers wants this:** To suggest you failed to mitigate damages.

**Right response:**
- The family is not obligated to fund repairs that the insurer refuses to pay.
- The family has made reasonable efforts: caulking, interior protection, monitoring.
- If specific mitigation steps were taken (sealing, tarping, etc.), describe them.
- The duty to mitigate is reasonable steps under the circumstances — not heroic measures to fund a $310,000 repair out of pocket.

### 5.12 The Recording-Or-Not Trap

**What it looks like:** "You did not record the 2/3/2025 Monson call. You did not record any of your conversations with Farmers. So everything you are testifying to is your unverified recollection."

**Why Farmers wants this:** To undermine your testimony as uncorroborated.

**Right response:**
- Minnesota is a one-party-consent state; you could have recorded but were not required to.
- You documented the call contemporaneously in writing — your communication log entry from 2/3/2025.
- Independent corroboration: Brau's 2/10/2025 voicemail attributes the rule to "my field claim supervisor" (Monson) — that is in writing, in Brau's own voice.
- Many serious legal proceedings rest on unrecorded conversations supported by contemporaneous records. Your testimony is not "unverified."

### 5.13 The "What Did Your Spouse Tell You" Trap

**What it looks like:** "Did your spouse tell you what Monson said on the call?"

**Why Farmers wants this:** To probe spousal communications and to suggest you are repeating your spouse's account.

**Right response:**
- If you personally heard the call, testify to what you personally heard.
- If your spouse heard a different call or part of a call, you can refer to "what I learned from my spouse" — but do not claim personal knowledge of what you did not personally observe.
- Marital communications are privileged under Minn. Stat. § 595.02 Subd. 1(a) — but the privilege applies to confidential spousal communications, not to communications with third parties (Monson) that you both heard.

### 5.14 The "Litigation Strategy" Trap (Privileged Communications)

**What it looks like:** "Did your attorney tell you what to say today? What did you talk about?"

**Why Farmers wants this:** To pierce attorney-client privilege.

**Right response:**
- "I have discussed this case with my attorney. The substance of those communications is privileged."
- Do not waive the privilege under any circumstances. Even if you are pro se, communications with a consulting attorney remain privileged.
- "Yes, I prepared for today" is fine. "My attorney told me X" is not.

### 5.15 The Compound-Question Trap

**What it looks like:** "When you first noticed damage in early August, and you called Legacy who came out two weeks later, did you also call Farmers, and did Farmers tell you to file a claim, or did you decide to file on your own?"

**Why Farmers wants this:** A compound question forces you to answer multiple sub-parts; your single answer is then characterized as agreement to all sub-parts.

**Right response:** Slow down. "Counsel, would you mind breaking that into separate questions? I am happy to answer each one." This is standard practice and is supported by Rule 30.04(c).

### 5.16 The Cumulative-Stress Trap

**What it looks like:** Late in the day. You're tired. You've been answering questions for 6 hours. Farmers' counsel is asking the same question slightly differently each time, getting slightly different shades of answers, building a record of "inconsistencies."

**Why Farmers wants this:** To wear you down and provoke an inconsistent answer.

**Right response:**
- Take breaks. The 7-hour rule is a maximum, not a target.
- "I'd like to take a 10-minute break."
- Drink water; eat a snack.
- When you return, listen carefully and answer slowly.
- If asked the same question repeatedly: "I believe I have answered this question previously. My answer is unchanged."

### 5.17 The "Have You Recovered for This in Another Lawsuit" Trap

**What it looks like:** "Have you previously sued anyone or made any other claim for the damage you are claiming here?"

**Why Farmers wants this:** To probe collateral-source recovery or double recovery.

**Right response:** Be truthful. The 2015 and 2020 prior Farmers claims are on this policy; there are no other lawsuits or claims for this damage. If you have made any homeowners' or other property claims with another insurer at any time, identify them — but be precise that they are for different damage at different times.

### 5.18 The Specific-Memory Trap

**What it looks like:** "Tell me the names of every person at Farmers you have ever spoken to about this claim — in order, with dates."

**Why Farmers wants this:** Impossible recall requirements set up "I don't recall" answers that Farmers later cites as "your memory is unreliable."

**Right response:**
- "I have a contemporaneous communication log. It lists every Farmers contact I'm aware of. May I refer to it?" (Yes — Rule 612 / refresh recollection.)
- "I do not recall every name from memory; my log includes [names you can identify on the spot]."
- "If you have a specific person in mind, I'll answer specifically about that person."

### 5.19 The "Did You Tell Your Doctor" Emotional-Distress Probe

**What it looks like:** "You're not claiming any emotional distress here, are you? Have you sought any therapy or medical treatment for stress related to this claim?"

**Why Farmers wants this:** If you have, your medical records may be discoverable. If you haven't, you may not be able to claim emotional distress.

**Right response:** The family's case is for property damage, breach of contract, statutory bad-faith taxable costs, and possibly punitive damages. The family is not claiming emotional-distress damages as a separate item. Answer accordingly: "We are not seeking emotional-distress damages as a separate compensatory item."

### 5.20 The "You Hired Aggressive Lawyers" Frame

**What it looks like:** "Your lawyers are very aggressive. They've filed multiple motions, asked for vast amounts of documents. This is litigation strategy, not a real damages claim, isn't it?"

**Why Farmers wants this:** To frame the case as plaintiff-overreach.

**Right response:** The family pursued every administrative and informal channel before filing — MN Dept of Commerce complaint, MN Lawyer Referral, AG's office, Self Help Center, the 1/31/2025 and 2/10/2025 appeals, the 5/28/2025 NWS submission, the appraisal demand. Litigation came only after every other channel was exhausted. The discovery requests are scaled to the case, which involves multiple denial letters, a contested appraisal, and disputed conduct. The family is not aggressive; the family is thorough.

## 6. Deposition Mechanics — Basic Rules

### 6.1 Ground Rules at the Outset

Farmers' counsel will state ground rules at the start. Standard ones:

- You are under oath.
- The court reporter records every word.
- If you don't understand a question, ask for clarification.
- If you need a break, ask.
- "Yes" or "no" answers; no nodding (court reporter can't transcribe).
- If you don't know, say "I don't know." If you don't recall, say "I don't recall."
- You can correct prior answers later.

You must agree to these.

### 6.2 The Pause-Listen-Answer Discipline

For every question:
1. **Listen** to the entire question. Don't start formulating your answer halfway through.
2. **Pause** — count to two. This gives counsel time to object if necessary.
3. **Decide** — do I understand the question? If not, ask for clarification.
4. **Answer** the question that was asked, no more, no less.

The pause-and-listen discipline takes practice. Most witnesses fail because they answer too quickly.

### 6.3 The "I Don't Recall" Framework

"I don't recall" is a legitimate, professional answer. It is not the same as "I don't know." Use them precisely:

- **"I don't know"** — the answer is not within your knowledge. Example: "Do you know what Mr. Brau wrote in his inspection notes?" — "I don't know; I never saw his notes."
- **"I don't recall"** — you may have known but cannot now remember. Example: "What time was the 2/3/2025 call?" — "I don't recall the exact time. My communication log entry can help if you want to refer to it."
- **Avoid:** "I think" or "probably." If you don't recall, don't guess.

If pressed: "Sitting here today, that's the answer I can give you."

### 6.4 Asking for Clarification

Compound, vague, or argumentative questions: ask for clarification.

- "Counsel, I'd like to make sure I understand the question. Are you asking about [X] or [Y]?"
- "Could you rephrase that? I want to give you an accurate answer."
- "You are asking about a period of several months. Could you narrow the time frame?"

This is not evasion; this is precision. Court reporter transcripts of well-clarified questions and accurate answers serve everyone.

### 6.5 Taking Breaks

You may request breaks (subject to the rule that you should not consult counsel between a pending question and your answer). Reasonable break requests:

- Every 60–90 minutes.
- After particularly difficult lines of questioning.
- When you feel tired, frustrated, or unable to focus.

"I'd like to take a 10-minute break, please." Counsel must allow this in most circumstances.

### 6.6 Counsel's Objections

Farmers' counsel (defending Farmers, not you) will not object on your behalf. **Your counsel (if represented)** or you yourself (if pro se) can object to:
- Form (compound, vague, argumentative).
- Privilege (attorney-client; marital).
- Beyond the scope of the deposition.

If your counsel objects, you typically still answer (unless instructed not to on privilege grounds). The objection is preserved for trial.

### 6.7 Reviewing the Transcript (Rule 30.05)

After the deposition, you have 30 days to review the transcript and submit corrections. Corrections must be substantive (e.g., reporter transcription errors), not substantive walk-backs.

- Read the transcript carefully.
- Note any errors or required clarifications.
- File the errata sheet within the window.

## 7. Interrogatory Mechanics

### 7.1 The 30-Day Response Window

You have 30 days from service to respond. Extensions are common; ask if you need more time, but do not let the deadline slip without an extension agreement on the record.

### 7.2 Verification Under Oath

Each set of interrogatory answers is verified under oath by signing a verification page. The verification states the answers are true and complete to the best of your knowledge.

### 7.3 Objections

Standard objections you can raise:

- **Vague / ambiguous:** the question is not clear enough to answer.
- **Overbroad:** the question seeks more information than is reasonably needed.
- **Burdensome:** producing the information would cost too much in time / money.
- **Privileged:** attorney-client, marital, work-product.
- **Outside the scope:** not relevant to any claim or defense.
- **Compound:** multiple questions in one.

Raise objections respectfully and answer what you can. "Subject to and without waiving these objections, [witness] responds as follows…" is the standard format.

### 7.4 Continuing Duty to Supplement

Under Rule 26.05, you have a continuing duty to supplement any interrogatory answer that becomes incomplete or incorrect. If new information comes to light, you must update your answer.

Keep a running list of "things to supplement when known."

## 8. The Bottom Line — Three Things to Remember

### 8.1 Tell the Truth

Every untruthful answer is impeachment material. Even small fibs ("yes, I read the entire policy") become big problems on cross-examination. **The truth is always the safe answer.**

### 8.2 Answer Only the Question Asked

The most common mistake is volunteering. "Did you talk to Mr. Brau on 11/8/2024?" — answer: "Yes" or "Yes, I emailed him requesting a meeting." Not: "Yes, I emailed him because Farmers had been ignoring us for weeks and Brau wouldn't even respond to a basic request to discuss the engineer report, and we were getting nowhere…" Volunteering hands Farmers material it would not otherwise have.

### 8.3 You Are Not Trying to Win the Deposition

The deposition is not the trial. You are not trying to convince Farmers' counsel of your case. Your job is to **answer truthfully, accurately, and concisely** — and to **avoid being trapped into helpful-to-Farmers admissions**. Win the deposition by giving Farmers' counsel nothing they can use against you at trial. The arguments are saved for motion practice and trial; the deposition is fact-finding.
