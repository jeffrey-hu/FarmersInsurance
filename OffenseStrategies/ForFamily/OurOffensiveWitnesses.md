## 1. Strategic Framework

### 1.1 Lay vs. Expert Witnesses

| Type | Examples | Role | Compensation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Lay fact witness** | Mary, Jack, Pat (Legacy); Katherine Toomajian (Commerce); the 14 neighbors | Testify to what they personally observed, said, or did. Cannot offer opinions outside personal knowledge. | Rule 45 witness fee ($20 + mileage) at service if subpoenaed |
| **Expert witness** | Forensic hail-damage engineer; window-matching expert; claims-handling expert | Offer opinions on technical matters under Minn. R. Evid. 702 / Rule 26.01(b)(2). Subject to Daubert-style attacks. | Engagement fee + Rule 26.02(b)(2) hourly rate when deposed by Farmers |
| **Records-custodian witness** | City of Eden Prairie permit office; NWS / NOAA records custodian | Authenticate official records under Minn. R. Evid. 803(8) and 902. Limited testimony scope. | Standard witness fee if subpoenaed; often a custodian affidavit is sufficient |
| **Contested / strategic witness** | Zach Sexe; Dan Traxler (Pure Construction) | Mixed loyalty — friendly on some facts, adverse on others. Approach carefully. | Rule 45 witness fee at service |

### 1.2 Which Witnesses Support Which Counts

| Count | Critical offensive witnesses |
|---|---|
| **A1 Breach of Contract** | Bill, Jie (plaintiffs); forensic hail-damage expert; window-matching expert; Legacy contractors (damage discovery); the appraisal panel (per Foster/Roos/Tomes depo plans) |

| **C3 MCFA** | Claims-handling expert; the institutional-pattern witnesses from C3 pattern-of-practice discovery |
| **D1 Punitive Damages** | Claims-handling expert; institutional pattern evidence |

### 1.3 Discovery Deposition vs. Trial Witness

- Some offensive witnesses will be **deposed during discovery** because Farmers wants to test them or because the family wants to preserve their testimony. Mary, Jack, the experts, and possibly Toomajian fall in this category.
- Some witnesses will be **called only at trial** — typically lay witnesses with brief testimony (neighbors). Their depositions, if Farmers wants them, are short and focused.
- Some witnesses' testimony comes in by **affidavit** at summary judgment — particularly the records-custodian witnesses, who do not need to appear in person at trial if the records are authenticated under Minn. R. Evid. 902.

### 1.4 Witness Preparation Discipline

The family preparing offensive witnesses must observe **strict ethical and procedural limits**:

- **Do not coach testimony.** You can ask witnesses what they remember; you cannot tell them what to say.
- **Do not pay non-expert witnesses for testimony.** Beyond statutory witness fees and reasonable travel reimbursement, payment for fact testimony is improper.
- **Document preparation neutrally.** If you meet with a witness before deposition or trial, keep notes that show the meeting was about reviewing documents and refreshing recollection — not about scripting answers.
- **Disclose witness preparation honestly.** When asked at deposition whether the witness met with the family, the witness must answer truthfully.

## 2. Tier 1 — Critical Offensive Witnesses

### 2.1 Mary (Legacy Contracting)

**Who:** Field contact / dispatcher / first-line professional at Legacy Contracting. First contacted the family on **8/9/2024** to check whether recent storms had caused damage to the property; the family then found multiple leaking windows — leading to the 8/13 visit by Jack and the family's same-day claim opening.

**Why she matters:** Mary is the **damage-discovery anchor witness**. Her testimony establishes that an independent professional proactively reached out to check for recent-storm damage and that the leaks surfaced in that process — not that a homeowner went looking to generate a claim. This is the answer to Farmers' "5-week delay" attack (see `PreparingForFarmersQuestions.md` § 5.1).

**Key facts to establish:**
- The 8/9/2024 contact with the family.
- The nature of Legacy's routine business relationship with the family before 8/9.
- Mary's reason for reaching out — a proactive check for damage from recent storms — and the family's discovery of the leaking windows during that contact.
- The decision to dispatch Jack to inspect.

**How to obtain testimony:** Cooperation likely — Legacy worked with the family throughout. Subpoena under Rule 45 if needed for trial. For discovery, an interview, declaration, and possibly a brief deposition.

**Direct-examination topics:**
1. Mary's role at Legacy.
2. The 8/9/2024 contact — date, time, circumstances.
3. What prompted the contact (a proactive check for recent-storm damage, prior service relationship).
4. The substance of the conversation with the family.
5. The decision to send Jack.

**Exhibits:**
- Legacy's contemporaneous records of the 8/9 contact.
- Any text messages or email correspondence.
- Legacy's billing records.

**Anticipated Farmers cross-examination:**
- "Legacy stood to profit if Farmers paid the claim, correct?" — Counter: Mary's initial contact was a proactive, post-storm courtesy check for damage, not a sales pitch for a claim. The leaks were discovered during that contact, and the decision to file a claim came only after Jack's independent inspection found dents; the family decided to file, not Mary.
- "You're a contractor, not an engineer." — Counter: Mary is a damage-discovery witness, not an engineering expert. Engineering opinions come from the family's retained experts.

**Risk assessment:** Low risk. Mary's testimony is short, fact-bounded, and corroborated by the contemporaneous communication log. Even hostile cross-examination cannot undermine the basic fact that Legacy was contacted and identified damage.

## 4. The 14 Neighbors From the Eden Prairie Canvass

### 4.1 Strategic Use

The family's 2/4–2/7/2025 neighbor canvass reached 19 homeowners; **14 confirmed hail damage and insurance-covered repairs in 2023 and/or 2024** (`EventDocuments/2025-02-10-SecondResponseToFinalReport.pdf`). The neighbors are the **most direct corroborating evidence** that Prieve's "no hail in Eden Prairie after 2020" assertion was wrong.

The family is unlikely to need to call all 14 at trial — a representative subset of 3–6 neighbors (including the closest-proximity ones and the ones with the most documented hail repairs) is sufficient.

### 4.2 Initial Steps

1. **Confirm cooperation.** Re-contact each of the 14 neighbors. Confirm:
   - Whether they will testify voluntarily by declaration.
   - Whether they will testify at deposition if subpoenaed.
   - Whether they will testify at trial if subpoenaed.
2. **Obtain written declarations.** Under Minn. R. Civ. P. 56.05, declarations under penalty of perjury are admissible at summary judgment. A short (1-page) declaration from each neighbor stating:
   - Their address.
   - That their home sustained hail damage in 2023, 2024, or both.
   - That insurance-covered repairs were made.
   - That the repair work corresponded to documented hail.
3. **Document the canvass methodology.** A family declaration explaining the canvass — how the 19 homes were selected, what was asked, the 14 affirmative responses, the documentation review.

### 4.3 Direct-Examination Topics (Per Neighbor)

For each neighbor called at trial:

1. Identity, address, length of residence.
2. Awareness of hail events in 2023 and 2024.
3. Damage to their own home.
4. Insurance-covered repairs they obtained.
5. Permit and contractor records.

Keep each direct examination short — 10–20 minutes per neighbor. The cumulative effect is what matters; no individual neighbor's testimony is long.

### 4.4 Exhibits

## 7. Witness Preparation Logistics

### 7.1 Initial Contact Sequence (Per Witness)

For each non-expert witness:

1. **Re-confirm cooperation.** Have the family reach out to each Legacy contact, neighbor, etc. Confirm the witness will participate.
2. **Initial interview.** A brief 30–60 minute call or meeting to refresh the witness's recollection and identify exhibits.
3. **Document review.** Provide the witness with the documents they will be asked about — but **do not script answers**.
4. **Declaration drafting (if used).** Draft a 1-page declaration in plain English. Walk the witness through it; obtain signature.
5. **Pre-deposition prep (if deposed).** 1–2 hour prep session covering deposition mechanics (oath, court reporter, time limits, "I don't recall" answers).
6. **Pre-trial prep (if called).** 2–4 hour prep covering the trial-direct outline and anticipated cross.

### 7.2 Expert Witness Prep

Experts have more complex prep:

1. **Initial engagement and scope discussion.** What is the expert being asked to opine on? What is excluded?
2. **Document and site access.** Provide the full case record. Schedule site inspection.
3. **Report drafting.** The expert drafts; the family / counsel reviews for accuracy and completeness; the expert signs.
4. **Disclosure under Rule 26.01(b)(2).** File the expert disclosure 180 days before trial.
5. **Deposition prep.** 4–8 hours pre-deposition prep with the expert. Walk through the report; anticipate Daubert and methodology attacks; practice cross-examination.
6. **Trial prep.** Pre-trial prep with the expert; coordinate the direct-examination outline; rehearse demonstrative exhibits.

### 7.3 What NOT to Do

- **Do not pay fact witnesses beyond statutory fees and reasonable expenses.** It is improper to pay a fact witness for their testimony.
- **Do not coach testimony.** You can review documents with witnesses and refresh their recollection; you cannot tell them what to say.
- **Do not jointly prep witnesses with conflicting accounts.** If two witnesses remember the same event differently, do not put them together to "reconcile" their accounts. Let each testify to their own recollection.
- **Do not exclude opposing witnesses from your prep.** If Farmers' counsel asks to attend a witness's pre-trial meeting with you, generally you can decline; but do not actively prevent the witness from being interviewed by Farmers.
- **Do not destroy witness-prep materials.** If you take notes or create outlines, those may be discoverable. Be careful what you put in writing.

## 10. Anticipated Defense Cross-Examination Themes

For all the family's offensive witnesses, Farmers will likely pursue these cross-examination themes. Prepare each witness to anticipate them:

### 10.1 "You Are Paid by the Plaintiffs"

Targets: all retained experts.
Counter: Reasonable professional fees; standard engagement; opinion does not change based on who pays. Many witnesses are paid in both plaintiff and defense engagements; the standard is methodology, not paymaster.

### 10.2 "You Have an Interest in the Outcome"

Targets: Legacy contractors; Pure Construction witnesses.
Counter: Legacy (Mary) proactively reached out to check for recent-storm damage and the leaks were found during that contact (not a pitch to generate a claim); Pure Construction's interest does not invalidate the documentary record.

### 10.3 "You Did Not Personally Inspect on July 13, 2024"

Targets: all witnesses except the family.
Counter: No one can personally inspect a roof or window at 1:00 AM during a storm. The damage record is built from professional observation after the storm; the storm itself is independently documented by NWS, City permits, neighbor reports, and the appraisal panel.

### 10.4 "Your Methodology Has Not Been Adopted by Industry Consensus"

Targets: forensic hail-damage expert.
Counter: peer-reviewed scientific methodology under Minn. R. Evid. 702 is the standard, not industry consensus. The HailShapes literature is peer-reviewed.

### 10.5 "You Are Not Familiar With Every Farmers Internal Policy"

Targets: claims-handling expert.
Counter: the expert opines on Minnesota's standard of care under § 72A.201 and Minn. R. 2790, which apply uniformly to all Minnesota insurers. Farmers' internal policies are subject to those standards, not above them.

### 10.6 "Your Records Are Limited / Incomplete"

Targets: Toomajian; records custodians.
Counter: official records are authenticated under Rule 902; "limited" means the witness can testify only to what the records show, which is the appropriate scope.

## 11. Trial Logistics for Witnesses

### 11.1 Subpoenas

Issue subpoenas for **every witness who will testify**, even if cooperation is expected. The subpoena is the formal authority; cooperation is the practical reality.

- Legacy witnesses: 14 days before trial.
- Neighbors: 14 days before trial.
- Records custodians: 30 days before trial (often replaced by Rule 902 affidavits).
- Experts: by retainer terms; trial subpoena as backup.

### 11.2 Witness Sequestration

Minn. R. Evid. 615 permits the court to exclude witnesses from the courtroom so they cannot hear each other's testimony. The family should request sequestration to protect against witness coordination claims and to preserve the credibility of each independent testimony.

### 11.3 Scheduling

Coordinate witness availability with the trial schedule. Experts typically need 1–2 weeks notice; neighbors often need accommodations for work schedules. Have backup witnesses (the other 8–11 neighbors, the family's own observations) in case scheduled witnesses become unavailable.

### 11.4 Travel and Compensation

Out-of-state expert witnesses may need travel expenses paid in advance. Local witnesses typically receive only the statutory $20 + mileage at service. Document all reimbursements; never pay beyond reasonable expenses.
