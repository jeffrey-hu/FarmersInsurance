## 1. Strategy — Why This Deposition

Brau is the **single most important Farmers witness in the case**. He personally inspected the property on 9/6/2024, three days after the 9/3/2024 denial said "no other damages observed." He personally observed dents and measured window dimensions alongside the family. Farmers later stated in writing that "the file does not contain a report" from his inspection. Brau himself later said "all the information we can release has already been sent to you." Those two statements are mutually inconsistent. He signed the 11/4/2024 denial. He left the 2/10/2025 voicemail that confirms in writing the "engineer's report only" rule articulated by Monson on 2/3/2025. He refused to meet with the family after the second denial. He responded to the 7/10/2025 deadline inquiry with boilerplate.

**The deposition has three strategic goals:**

1. **Lock down the missing-notes inconsistency.** Force Brau to choose between (a) "no report exists" (which means Farmers either destroyed records or never created them in violation of § 72A.201 Subd. 4(1) and Farmers' own records-retention policy) and (b) "we already released everything" (which means notes exist or existed and Brau personally observed dents that contradict the 9/3/2024 denial his colleagues issued three days earlier). Either answer fuels the Subd. 4(a) bad-faith motion and the adverse-inference instruction at trial.

2. **Lock in Brau's personal observation of hail damage on 9/6/2024.** Make him admit, on the record, that he personally saw dents on multiple windows and measured those windows. That admission is *Quade*-adjacent evidence — it does not invoke the appraisal preclusion itself, but it places Farmers' own employee in the witness box admitting facts that contradict Farmers' formal denials.

3. **Establish the "engineer's report only" rule as Brau's personally-articulated practice.** The 2/10/2025 voicemail is in his voice, on his phone line, attributing the rule to "my field claim supervisor." Lock in (a) who instructed him to leave that voicemail, (b) what training he received on evidentiary standards, (c) whether the rule is in any written claims-handling document.

**Settlement leverage produced by this deposition:** A deposition that produces clean answers on all three goals materially raises Farmers' settlement valuation because the answers feed (i) the § 604.18 Subd. 4(a) Prong 2 evidence (knowledge/reckless disregard), (ii) the B2 fraud claim (intent and scienter on the "no other damages" misrepresentation), (iii) the Rule 37 spoliation record, and (iv) the centerpiece of the trial-narrative cross-examination.

## 6. Anticipated Objections (and Responses)

Minnesota deposition objections fall into three categories: (i) form objections (preserved by stating "objection to form" on the record); (ii) instruction-not-to-answer objections (limited to privilege, court-ordered limitation, or motion to terminate under Rule 30.04(d)); (iii) general objections (preserved for trial). Defense counsel will likely use:

| Objection | Form | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Compound | Form | Rephrase: break into one fact per question |
| Vague | Form | Rephrase with specifics |
| Calls for speculation | Form | "What did you personally observe, see, or hear" — anchor in personal knowledge |
| Foundation | Form | Lay foundation: time, place, who was present |
| Asked and answered | Form | Move on; you have the answer in the record |
| Argumentative | Form | Rephrase neutrally |
| Mischaracterizes prior testimony | Form | Quote from the transcript or earlier answer |
| Attorney-client privilege | Instruction not to answer | If the question is about substance of communications with counsel, accept and move to facts not protected by privilege. If the question is about facts (when did inspection occur, what did you see), insist that privilege does not shield facts |
| Work-product | Instruction not to answer | If the question is about post-litigation documents prepared in anticipation, accept and re-pose narrowly. If pre-litigation business records (e.g., the 2/10/2025 voicemail content), work-product does not apply |
| Outside the scope of Rule 30.02(f) topics | Instruction not to answer (institutional only) | Does not apply to Brau as a fact witness; mostly relevant to the Rule 30(b)(6) designee |
| "I don't recall" | Witness answer, not objection | Refresh recollection with a document; if still no recollection, lock in "today, sitting here, you don't recall" |
| Counsel speaking objections | Form | "Counsel, please limit your objections to the form" — Minn. R. Civ. P. 30.04(c) prohibits speaking objections that suggest answers |

**Brau-specific anticipated objections:**

- **On the missing-notes sequence:** Defense may object that the question "calls for speculation" if asked "what is meant by 'no report.'" Counter: this is asking the witness about his own statement (10/29/2025) or his employer's statement (10/10/2025) — both are within personal knowledge for an employee who participated in the underlying inspection.

- **On the 2/10/2025 voicemail:** Defense may object that the voicemail is "not authenticated" or "hearsay if offered for truth." Counter: the voicemail is Brau's own statement, admissible under Minn. R. Evid. 801(d)(2)(A) (statement of party-opponent) and 801(d)(2)(D) (statement by agent within scope of employment). Authentication is by the recipient (the family) and by Brau himself.

- **On the engineer-only rule:** Defense may object that questions about pattern of practice are "outside the scope of this witness's personal knowledge." Counter: Brau is a long-tenured claims rep handling Minnesota hail claims; his personal knowledge of other claims he has worked is squarely within his employment.

- **On the personnel file (Exhibit 24):** Defense will object on privacy. Resolve via protective order before the deposition; if it's not produced, move to compel.

**Plaintiff-side technique on persistent objections.** If defense counsel becomes obstructive (repeated speaking objections, frequent instruction not to answer, suspending the deposition), suspend, certify the question for ruling under Minn. R. Civ. P. 30.04(d), and continue with other topics. Move for sanctions after the deposition if the obstruction was unreasonable. The deposition transcript itself becomes Rule 37 evidence.

## 7. Prep Memo (Final Synthesis)

### 7.1 The One Sentence

Get Brau to admit, on the record, that (a) he personally saw dents on multiple windows on 9/6/2024 and measured them; (b) he either did not preserve his notes or Farmers has not produced them; and (c) the 2/10/2025 voicemail he left expressed an "engineer's report only" rule that is not written anywhere in the policy.

### 7.2 The Three Critical Locks

1. **The 9/6/2024 observation lock.** Brau saw hail damage. Brau is Farmers' own employee. Brau's observation directly contradicts the 9/3/2024 denial ("no other damages observed") that his colleague Dudley signed three days earlier.

2. **The records inconsistency lock.** Either "no report exists" (a) is wrong, in which case Brau or Farmers has been concealing records; or (b) is right, in which case Farmers' adjuster created no record of an inspection that produced material observations, violating § 72A.201 and Farmers' own records-retention obligations. Either way, this is a Rule 37 / adverse-inference / B1 Prong 2 fact.

3. **The engineer-only rule lock.** Brau's voicemail confirms the rule in writing — in audio, anyway. He attributes it to "my field claim supervisor." Forced identification of Monson + walkback of any claim that this was Brau's own freelancing makes the rule institutional. Institutional → MCFA pattern-of-practice → § 549.20 deliberate disregard.

### 7.3 Do Not Open These Doors

- Do not ask Brau open-ended questions about Prieve's methodology — that is Prieve's deposition. Brau is not an engineer; do not let him offer engineering opinions.
- Do not let Brau characterize the 2020 claim's Xactimate scope — that is a documents fight, not a memory fight.
- Do not let Brau claim he is just following supervisor orders without locking in *who* the supervisor was. The "Monson did it" answer is the answer the family wants.
- Do not invite Brau to opine on the appraisal panel's scope — that is the Rule 30(b)(6) designee's domain.

### 7.4 The Three Things Brau Will Try to Do

1. **Distance from the 9/3/2024 denial.** "That wasn't my letter — that was Dudley." Response: walk Brau through his pre-9/6 review of Dudley's denial, his agreement or disagreement with it at the time, and his belief about whether the 9/6/2024 inspection contradicted it.

2. **Re-characterize the 2/10/2025 voicemail.** Brau may try to say his voicemail meant something different from what it says, or that he was paraphrasing what Monson told him. Response: play the audio, transcribe slowly on the record, and force Brau to read it back word by word. Then ask whether he agreed with the statement at the time.

3. **"I don't recall."** Particularly on the notes-creation sequence. Response: refresh recollection with email timestamps, calendar entries, and the photographs he took (if produced). Then lock in "you don't recall *today, sitting here* whether you wrote notes during your inspection of the property on 9/6/2024."
