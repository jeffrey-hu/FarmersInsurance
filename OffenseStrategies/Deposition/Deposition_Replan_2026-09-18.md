## 1. Why the old plan no longer works

The old plan assumed a 24-month case with fact discovery running into late 2027. It put the **first** deposition (Brau) at month 5–6 and the last fact depositions at month 10 — roughly **March–July 2027**.

The Court ordered **all discovery complete by March 15, 2027** (Order ¶2). Under the old plan, the first deposition would be taken on about the day discovery closes. Everything has to move up by about six months and fit into a window of about ten usable weeks.

Four other dates squeeze the window from inside:

| Constraint | Date | Effect on depositions |
|---|---|---|
| RFP responses due | 10/15/2026 | No deposition makes sense before the claim file is in hand and reviewed, so realistically none before December |
| ¶4 discovery-dispute process (confer → 3-page letter → conference call → motion) | 4–8 weeks | A deposition that exposes a document gap must happen early enough to run this process. Last practical ¶4 letter: **~1/8/2027** (Impact Analysis § 4.6 says mid-January; this plan builds in a one-week cushion) |
| Last day to serve follow-up written discovery | **2/12/2027** (30-day responses land 3/15) | Depositions that will produce follow-up RFPs/RFAs must be finished by about **2/5** |
| Plaintiffs' expert report due | **3/15/2027** | The expert needs the Prieve and Brau transcripts. Allowing 2–3 weeks for transcripts, those depositions must be done by **late January** |
| § 604.18 motion to amend (target) | Feb.–Mar. 2027 | Needs the Brau and Monson testimony. Another reason to take them in January |

Holidays remove about two weeks (Thanksgiving 11/26, and 12/21–1/1).

**Net: fact depositions happen between roughly December 7, 2026 and March 5, 2027, with the core witnesses in January.**

## 2. What changes in the strategy

1. **Fewer depositions.** The old plan had about 13 fact depositions. A pro se family cannot prepare and take 13 depositions in ten weeks: at 15–30 hours each, that is 200–400 hours on top of written discovery, the expert, and the § 604.18 motion. This plan takes **6 witnesses in 7 sessions** and handles the rest by documents, RFAs, or a deposition only if needed.
2. **Split the 30(b)(6) deposition, and take the first part first.** The old plan held the corporate designee until month 8–9, once the individual record was built. There is no longer time for that. Session 1 (December) covers the *records* topics only. It shows early what exists, what was searched, and where the Brau notes went, so any gap can still go through the ¶4 process. Session 2 (late February) covers the substantive positions, once the individual testimony is in.
3. **Serve the third-party document subpoenas now, not at the deposition.** The old plan paired most Rule 45 document subpoenas with month 9–10 depositions. Prieve's, Roos's and Tomes's files have to be in hand **before** their January–February depositions, so the document subpoenas go out in October.
4. **Book dates early.** Ask counsel for date holds in November for January–February dates. Any witness who needs a Rule 45 subpoena (Tomes, Prieve, Roos) adds a step for issuing it and serving it with the fee.
5. **Plan for Farmers deposing Yibiao and Jie.** FIE's own proposed schedule was even shorter, so expect deposition notices for both Plaintiffs, probably December–February. Offer dates that don't clash with the calendar below.

## 5. Capacity and cost check

- **Sessions:** 7 core, plus up to 2 conditional. That is roughly one deposition a week in January–February.
- **Family time:** about 120–200 hours of preparation, taking and review, spread over mid-November to early March. Preparation for Brau, Monson and Prieve (the three full days) has to start in **November**, not January.
- **Out-of-pocket (rough):** court reporter and transcript run about $800–$2,000 per day; videography about $500–$1,200 per day. **Video only Brau, Monson, Prieve and 30(b)(6) Session 2**, the likely trial clips. Take the others by stenographer only, and remotely where FIE agrees. Rule 45 witnesses: the statutory fee and mileage, plus any fee Prieve or Roos may claim for their time. Confirm the rule before paying anything beyond the statutory fee.
- **Plaintiffs' own depositions:** budget 2 half-days of preparation each. Ask for dates in the **week of 2/8 or 3/1** so they don't collide with the January core witnesses.

## 8. Triggers that change this plan

| If… | Then… |
|---|---|
| FIE's 10/15 production is thin or lacks native photographs | Keep the 12/8 Session 1 date regardless; it becomes the evidence for the ¶4 letter |
| FIE refuses to produce Brau/Monson on notice, or they have left FIE | Rule 45 subpoenas by ~11/13; the January dates hold |
| Prieve objects to the document subpoena | Confer immediately; if unresolved by ~11/20, ¶4 letter. Prieve's deposition slides at most to the week of 2/8, and no later, because of the expert |
| A party question remains open after 10/9 | Consider adding an entity-identity topic to Session 1 and moving it forward to November, ahead of any joinder motion |
| Discovery shows a genuine obstruction (e.g., Brau notes surface late) | That specific event, not general time pressure, is the good-cause basis for a narrow Rule 16.02 / 111.04 request to take a named deposition after 3/15 |
