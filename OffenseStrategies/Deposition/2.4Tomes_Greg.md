## 1. Strategy — Why This Deposition

Tomes was Farmers' party-appraiser on the 9/12/2025 appraisal. He **did not sign the award**. The award is binding under § 65A.01 Subd. 3 because Foster and Roos signed (two-of-three), but Tomes's non-signature is a material fact: Farmers' own appraiser refused to join the panel's hail-causation finding for F1.7 and B1.2.

Tomes's deposition serves four purposes:

1. **Document the reason for non-signature.** If Tomes refused because the award found hail causation that Tomes does not accept, that supports A3 Ground D misfeasance development and reinforces the picture of an institutional Farmers resistance to the panel's finding. If Tomes refused because the award's scope was unclear (the F1.7 / B1.2 issue), that supports *Herll*/*Rymer* remand. If Tomes refused because of a procedural objection (e.g., the panel did not evaluate every window), that supports the scope-limitation argument.

2. **Develop the panel's deliberation record.** Tomes was in the room; he heard Foster and Roos's analysis. His testimony complements Foster and Roos on the methodology, the F1.7 / B1.2 designations, the siding-zero rationale.

3. **Document Tomes's pattern of practice.** How many Minnesota appraisals has Tomes participated in as Farmers' party-appraiser since 2020? In how many did he sign or not sign? Each engagement is C3 pattern-of-practice evidence and supports the disinterestedness inquiry.

4. **Develop the disinterestedness record on Tomes.** § 65A.01 Subd. 3's "competent and disinterested" requirement applies to Farmers' appraiser as well as the insured's. Tomes's relationship with Farmers, fee structure, frequency of engagement, and outcomes inform the institutional picture.

The strategic posture: Tomes did not sign — he is not Farmers' enthusiastic mouthpiece. He may be more candid than a Farmers employee. Approach professionally; let the facts emerge.

## 7. Prep Memo (Final Synthesis)

### 7.1 The One Sentence

Get Tomes to articulate, on the record, the specific reason he did not sign the 9/12/2025 award — and whether that reason supports the family's hail-causation case, the scope-limitation case, or the disinterestedness case.

### 7.2 The Three Critical Locks

1. **The non-signature reasoning lock.** Whatever Tomes says is useful: (a) "the panel was wrong on hail causation" — fact that Tomes minimized damage observations on the record (cross-examined at trial); (b) "the panel got the scope wrong" — supports *Herll*/*Rymer* and A3; (c) "the methodology was unclear" — supports A3 Ground A and Ground D; (d) "I personally disagreed with Foster and Roos on specific items" — illuminates the panel's deliberation and supports A3 Ground D misfeasance development.

2. **The window-by-window observation lock.** Tomes's personal observations of damage on each window — confirming or minimizing what Brau, Prieve, and Foster all variously documented — frame the *Quade* preclusion zone and the unevaluated-windows track.

3. **The pattern-of-practice lock.** Tomes's prior Minnesota Farmers engagements — frequency of signing vs. non-signing, outcomes — anchor the C3 pattern-of-practice case and inform the disinterestedness analysis.

### 7.3 Do Not Open These Doors

- Do not let Tomes claim he refused to sign because the family was unreasonable; the family was not at the deliberation.
- Do not invite Tomes to opine on Farmers' bad faith; that is plaintiff counsel's argument.
- Do not press Tomes on legal conclusions; stay on facts.

### 7.4 Three Things Tomes May Try to Do

1. **Defend the panel's overall result while distancing from the specific finding.** "I didn't sign because of [narrow technical reason], but I agree with the broader outcome." Response: probe whether Tomes agrees with the hail-causation finding. If yes, that strengthens *Quade*. If no, Farmers' own appraiser disputes its own panel's conclusion — usable at trial.

2. **Claim "I just had administrative objections."** Response: force specifics. If Tomes can't articulate, the non-signature was either substantive (helping the family) or arbitrary (helping the family).

3. **Defend the $0 siding outcome as factually correct.** Response: walk through the family's siding-add-on emails and Prieve's A-53, A-90 photos. Force Tomes to confront the documented record.

### 7.5 Coordination With Other Depositions

- Schedule **after** Brau, Monson, Dudley, Rule 30(b)(6), Foster.
- Schedule **before** Roos (so Tomes's reasoning anchors Roos's deliberation account).
- The Tomes transcript feeds the A3 motion, the *Herll*/*Rymer* remand briefing, the Daubert motion (if Tomes minimized damage in a methodology defensible only by Prieve's report), and the C3 pattern-of-practice record.

### 7.6 Post-Deposition Action Items

- Update the A3 motion with Tomes's specific non-signature reasoning.
- Update the *Herll*/*Rymer* remand briefing.
- Cross-reference Tomes's testimony with Foster's and Roos's for inconsistencies that support follow-up motions.
- Use Tomes's testimony on his prior Farmers engagements to expand C3 pattern-of-practice production targets if not already complete.

### 7.7 The Big-Picture Read

Tomes is the only panel member who refused to bless Farmers' favored outcome. His testimony — whatever it says — is useful because it documents that even Farmers' own appraiser would not put his name on the result Farmers ended up trying to defend. That fact alone is a B1 Subd. 4(a) anchor. Beyond that, Tomes's substantive testimony on scope, methodology, and the F1.7 / B1.2 codes supports the family's coverage and remand arguments.
