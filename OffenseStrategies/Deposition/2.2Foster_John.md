## 1. Strategy — Why This Deposition

Foster signed the 9/12/2025 appraisal award alongside umpire Wade Roos; Farmers' party-appraiser Greg Tomes did not. The award found hail causation for "Windows (F1.7 / B1.2)" at RCV $12,800 / ACV $6,400 — a partial win for the family on causation, but only on two windows out of the many the family documented as damaged, and $0 on both siding items (exactly Farmers' pre-appraisal position).

The Foster deposition is **strategically delicate** because:

1. Foster signed the award the family is partially relying on (for the *Quade* hail-causation preclusion on F1.7 and B1.2).
2. Foster signed an award the family wants to challenge for scope (it covers only 2 windows when the photographic record shows damage to most).
3. Foster has reported prior insurance-industry employment that — if undisclosed at the time of his 8/3/2025 retention — bears on the "disinterested" requirement under Minn. Stat. § 65A.01 Subd. 3 and *McQuaid Market House Co. v. Home Insurance Co.*, 147 Minn. 254 (1920).
4. Foster was selected by Pure Construction (Zach Sexe), not by the family.
5. Foster's $0 / $0 outcome on the siding items aligns exactly with Farmers' pre-appraisal denial position on siding.

Strategic goals:

1. **Lock in the hail-causation finding for F1.7 and B1.2.** Make clear that Foster, the insured's appraiser, joined the panel's hail-causation finding. This binds Farmers under *Quade* for those windows and forecloses six exclusions.

2. **Develop the scope-limitation record.** Foster signed an award covering only 2 windows. The family's photo record shows damage to many more. Why? What did the panel evaluate? What was excluded? Foster's answer (or inability to answer) feeds the *Herll*/*Rymer* remand and the A2 declaratory-judgment count.

3. **Develop the disinterestedness record (carefully).** Foster's prior insurance-industry employment is the family's account; the deposition should test it factually without becoming accusatory.

4. **Document the Pure Construction selection process.** Foster was retained through Pure Construction. What was the engagement? Who paid? What was the scope?

5. **Document the methodology — or its absence.** What did the panel inspect? What measurements? What evaluation? How did the panel arrive at $12,800?

6. **Develop the siding-zeroing record.** Foster joined the panel that zeroed siding. Why? On what basis? With what consideration of the family's evidence (Prieve photos A-53, A-90; family-document Figs 1.5.5 and 2.4.5)?

The deposition is uncomfortable but necessary. Approach Foster as a professional witness; document the facts without prejudging.

## 6. Anticipated Objections

### 6.1 Disinterestedness-Privilege Hybrid Objections

Defense may invoke "personal" or "private" employment-history questions. Counter: § 65A.01 Subd. 3 requires the appraiser to be "competent and disinterested"; prior insurance-industry employment is squarely within the scope of disinterestedness inquiry; *McQuaid* expressly recognizes the "fair, impartial, and disinterested" ground for setting aside an award.

### 6.2 Appraiser Privilege / Work-Product

Some courts recognize a limited appraiser work-product protection. Counter: under *Cincinnati Insurance Co. v. Rymer Cos.* (8th Cir. 2026), post-award appraiser testimony is admissible for misconduct purposes. Even broader, the panel's methodology is not privileged — it is the record on which the family's *Herll*/*Rymer* remand request rests.

### 6.3 Beyond Scope of Engagement

Defense may object that questions about specific window evaluations are "beyond the scope of what Mr. Foster was retained to evaluate." Counter: Foster was retained to appraise the loss, which (per § 65A.01 Subd. 3) means "appraise the loss, stating separately actual value and loss to each item." Every disputed item is within scope.

### 6.4 Standard Objections

Form objections (compound, vague, calls for speculation, foundation) per Brau memo § 6. Anticipate:

- "Calls for speculation" on what Tomes was thinking. Reframe: ask about Foster's observations of what Tomes said or did.
- "Argumentative" on the disinterestedness sequence. Reframe factually: "Did you disclose your prior insurance-industry employment to the family at the time of your engagement?"
- "Vague" on "F1.7" and "B1.2." Force a substantive answer — Foster signed the award using those terms.

## 7. Prep Memo (Final Synthesis)

### 7.1 The One Sentence

Get Foster to admit, on the record, that (a) the appraisal panel evaluated only 2 windows out of many the family documented as damaged; (b) the "F1.7" and "B1.2" designations refer to specific physical windows that the panel cannot fully identify or describe; (c) Foster either disclosed or did not disclose his prior insurance-industry employment to the family at the 8/3/2025 retention; and (d) the $0 / $0 siding outcome was joined without meaningful consideration of the family's siding-damage submissions.

### 7.2 The Four Critical Locks

1. **The scope-limitation lock.** The panel evaluated only 2 windows. The photographic record shows damage to many more. Foster cannot explain (or cannot explain consistently) which windows were evaluated and excluded. This is direct *Herll*/*Rymer* remand evidence and A2 DJ-3 declaration evidence.

2. **The F1.7 / B1.2 lock.** Foster cannot define the codes on the record consistently with Farmers' 9/18, 9/19, and 10/14/2025 descriptions. The award's facial non-itemization is preserved.

3. **The disinterestedness lock.** Foster's prior insurance-industry employment is a fact — once on the record under oath, it grounds the A3 Ground E argument under *McQuaid* and § 65A.01 Subd. 3.

4. **The siding-zero lock.** Foster joined an award that zeroed siding without meaningful engagement with the family's siding-damage evidence. This supports the A3 Ground D misfeasance argument and undermines the binding-effect of the siding-portion of the award.

### 7.3 Do Not Open These Doors

- Do not invite Foster to reaffirm hail-causation for windows beyond F1.7 and B1.2 — keep the *Quade* preclusion zone narrow and aligned with what the family wants.
- Do not let Foster speak for Tomes; ask only what Foster observed Tomes do or say.
- Do not let Foster opine on engineering matters beyond his expertise; if pressed, he may concede the panel relied on Prieve's report — which is itself useful.

### 7.4 Three Things Foster Will Try to Do

1. **Defend the scope by claiming it represented the panel's best evaluation.** Response: drill down on which specific windows the panel evaluated and which it did not. Force identification.

2. **Defend the F1.7 / B1.2 designations as obvious to those in the room.** Response: read Farmers' 9/18, 9/19, 10/14/2025 descriptions; ask whether each is accurate. If Foster says yes, lock in. If no, force a correct identification — preferably one that does not match Farmers' descriptions.

3. **Defend the siding-zero by reference to umpire deliberation.** Response: Foster signed the award; he is responsible for the substance, not merely a procedural participant.

### 7.5 Coordination With Other Depositions

- Schedule after Pure Construction's Zach Sexe (if separately deposed) and after Rule 30(b)(6). Zach's testimony anchors the Pure Construction selection record; Rule 30(b)(6) tests Farmers' position on Foster's disinterestedness.
- Schedule before Roos (so Foster's testimony anchors what to ask Roos about deliberation).
- The Foster transcript becomes a centerpiece of the A3 motion and the A2 DJ-3 declaration.

### 7.6 Post-Deposition Action Items

- Update the A3 motion with Foster's specific admissions on scope and the codes.
- Update the A2 DJ-3 paragraph with Foster's testimony.
- If disinterestedness facts are confirmed, prepare an A3 Ground E discussion for the eventual motion.

### 7.7 The Strategic Tension to Manage

Foster's testimony helps the family on the F1.7 / B1.2 hail causation (for *Quade*) and on scope limitation (for *Herll*/*Rymer*). It also potentially supports an A3 Ground E disinterestedness argument that — if pushed — could vacate or modify the award, eliminating *Quade* preclusion. The strategic balance: develop the Ground E record as factual matter (employment history; non-disclosure) without arguing for full vacatur. Reserve the vacatur decision for the motion phase when the full record (Roos, Tomes, Pure Construction) is in.
