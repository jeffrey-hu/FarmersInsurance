## 1. Strategy — Why This Deposition

Roos was the umpire on the 9/12/2025 appraisal. He signed the award; he produced no report, no estimate, no methodology document. Farmers' 9/24/2025 admission is explicit: "Unfortunately, the umpire did not provide a report or estimate. Our only recommendation is to refer you to your appraiser for any additional details he may have in his file."

The Roos deposition serves four highly-leveraged purposes:

1. **Lock in the umpire's "no report" admission.** Roos is the only person who can testify under oath that he did not produce a methodology document for an award binding $12,800 of insurance liability. Farmers' own admission becomes Roos's own admission — the *Herll*/*Rymer* remand foundation is complete.

2. **Force articulation of what the panel evaluated.** Roos's testimony on which windows were evaluated, which were excluded, and on what basis is the centerpiece of the A2 DJ-3 declaration and the A3 Tier 1 remand request. Even if Roos says "Foster handled scope," that itself is a remand-supporting admission.

3. **Test the "F1.7" / "B1.2" designations.** Roos signed the award using those codes. He must define them on the record. If he cannot — or his definition contradicts Farmers' inconsistent post-award descriptions — the ambiguity remand is established.

4. **Develop the disinterested-umpire record.** § 65A.01 Subd. 3 requires the umpire to be "competent and disinterested." Roos's professional background, prior umpire engagements (especially with Farmers or with Farmers' party-appraiser Tomes), fee structure, and selection process all bear on disinterestedness.

5. **Develop (carefully) any A3 Ground D *Mork* misfeasance record.** Roos's testimony may reveal procedural irregularities: did the panel actually evaluate every window the family submitted, or did it delegate scope to one party-appraiser? Did Roos personally inspect, or rely on Foster and Tomes? Was the deliberation a real evaluation or a rubber-stamp? *Mork* requires "clear evidence" — but the deposition is where that evidence is found if it exists.

The *Rymer* opinion is the procedural roadmap: the Eighth Circuit affirmed deposing the umpire on remand and held that the umpire can be questioned to clarify the award's scope. Use *Rymer* explicitly to define the permissible questioning scope at the deposition.

## 6. Anticipated Objections

### 6.1 Post-Award Testimony Scope

Defense will cite *Rymer*'s rule that post-award appraiser testimony cannot expand or alter the award. Counter:
- The family is not seeking to expand or alter — the family is seeking clarification under *Herll*/*Rymer*, and (where applicable) misconduct evidence permitted by *Rymer* itself.
- Roos's testimony on what he evaluated and what he did not is fact-finding, not award expansion.
- *Rymer* expressly permits questioning to determine whether fraud, misfeasance, malfeasance, or wrongdoing occurred — exactly the *Mork* standard.

### 6.2 Umpire-Privilege Hybrid Objections

Some courts recognize a limited umpire-privilege analogous to mediator privilege. Counter:
- No Minnesota court has recognized a § 65A.01 umpire privilege.
- The umpire-privilege analogy fails because the appraisal panel produces a binding award, unlike mediation.
- *Rymer* explicitly authorizes deposing the umpire.

### 6.3 Disinterestedness Inquiry

Defense may object to questions about prior relationships as "irrelevant" or "private." Counter: § 65A.01 Subd. 3 makes disinterestedness a statutory element of a valid appraisal; the inquiry is squarely within scope.

### 6.4 Standard Objections

Form objections per Brau memo § 6. Anticipate:
- "Asked and answered" on the no-report sequence — counter: the family is laying foundation; the no-report fact has multiple dimensions.
- "Calls for legal conclusion" on whether the award is "ambiguous" — reframe factually: ask Roos to define F1.7 and B1.2.
- "Speculation" on Tomes's non-signature — ask what Roos personally observed.

## 7. Prep Memo (Final Synthesis)

### 7.1 The One Sentence

Get Roos to admit, on the record, that (a) he produced no report, estimate, or methodology document; (b) he cannot define "F1.7" and "B1.2" in a way that is consistent with Farmers' inconsistent post-award descriptions; (c) the panel did not evaluate every window the family documented as damaged; and (d) the panel had no documented basis for the $12,800 valuation.

### 7.2 The Four Critical Locks

1. **The no-report lock.** Roos under oath that he produced no methodology document. Already in Farmers' admission; Roos's confirmation makes it unimpeachable. This is the foundation of *Herll*/*Rymer* remand.

2. **The F1.7 / B1.2 ambiguity lock.** Roos either (a) cannot define the codes, (b) defines them inconsistently with Farmers' descriptions, or (c) defines them in a way that limits the award to fewer windows than Farmers claims. Each is good for the family.

3. **The scope-limitation lock.** The panel evaluated only a subset of documented damage. Forced articulation of which windows were evaluated and which were excluded is the *Herll*/*Rymer* clarification record.

4. **The methodology lock.** No quantitative analysis, no written work-product, reliance on party-appraiser walk-throughs — supports A3 Ground D misfeasance development (preserve for later motion decision).

### 7.3 Do Not Open These Doors

- Do not press Roos to confirm causation for windows the family wants to add to the *Quade* preclusion zone — that risks expanding the award (prohibited by *Rymer*) and risks bad answers.
- Do not let Roos opine on engineering matters; he is not an engineer (unless his CV says otherwise).
- Do not commit to a *Mork* vacatur strategy at the deposition; develop the facts.
- Do not antagonize Roos — he is a third-party witness with a reputation; professional fact-finding is more productive than aggression.

### 7.4 Three Things Roos Will Try to Do

1. **Refer to Foster and Tomes for scope details.** "Foster and Tomes evaluated the windows; I served as umpire on disputed items only." Response: That is itself a *Herll*/*Rymer* admission — the umpire delegated scope to party appraisers. Probe further: did the panel ever agree, on the record, which windows it had evaluated.

2. **Defend the $12,800 figure as a synthesis of the party-appraisers' positions.** Response: ask whether Foster's evaluation or Tomes's evaluation produced $12,800; if neither, where did the figure come from.

3. **Claim "this is standard practice for umpires not to produce reports."** Response: § 65A.01 Subd. 3 requires the award to be "itemized." If "standard practice" produces awards that fail the statutory itemization requirement, the standard practice is non-compliant and is the basis for *Herll*/*Rymer* remand.

### 7.5 Coordination With Other Depositions

- Schedule **after** Foster (so Foster's scope testimony anchors what to ask Roos).
- Schedule **after** Tomes (so Tomes's non-signature reasoning is on the record).
- Schedule **before** the A3 motion drafting.
- The Roos transcript is the primary affidavit support for the *Herll*/*Rymer* remand motion.

### 7.6 Post-Deposition Action Items

- Draft the *Herll*/*Rymer* remand motion within 45 days of the transcript.
- Update the A2 DJ-3 declaration with Roos's specific admissions.
- Update the A3 Tier 1 (remand) and Tier 2 (modification under § 65A.01 Subd. 3 itemization) briefing with Roos's testimony.
- Preserve A3 Tier 3 (*Mork* vacatur) as an option but do not commit until the full record is in.

### 7.7 The Strategic Read

Roos is unlikely to be a sympathetic-to-Farmers witness — he is an umpire who must defend his independence. But Roos is also unlikely to be a friend of the family — he signed an award the family wants to challenge. The strategic posture: professional, fact-focused, and *Rymer*-anchored. The goal is not to vilify Roos; the goal is to extract the facts that support a remand under *Herll*/*Rymer*, which produces a clarified or expanded award. A remand is the cleanest pathway to breaking the $17,720 deductible without disturbing the *Quade* causation preclusion.
