## 1. Strategy — Why This Deposition

Monson is the **author of the "engineer's report only" rule**. On 2/3/2025 he told the family by phone that Farmers would not review evidence from the family unless that evidence was provided by an engineer. He also said "extruded aluminum windows are easily damaged." One week later, Brau's 2/10/2025 voicemail confirmed the same rule and attributed it to "my field claim supervisor" — Monson.

Monson is the institutional bridge between Brau (the line-level adjuster) and Farmers' written policies and training (which contain no such rule). He is therefore where the case for **institutional, supervisor-level bad faith** is made or broken. If Monson admits he articulated the rule and that it was applied to refuse review of the family's NWS, City permit, and neighbor-canvass evidence, the § 604.18 Subd. 4(a) motion writes itself.

**Strategic goals:**

1. **Lock in the 2/3/2025 articulation.** Monson said it; the family heard it; Brau confirmed it in writing seven days later. Force Monson to either admit the statement or deny it under oath (and then be impeached by Brau's voicemail).
2. **Identify the source of the rule.** Is it in writing? Is it training? Is it Monson's own protocol? Each answer is bad for Farmers: writing → institutional bad faith; training → institutional; freelance → § 72A.201 violation by the supervisor personally.
3. **Document the rule's application to specific submissions.** The 1/31/2025 appeal, the 2/10/2025 second response, the 5/28/2025 Pietila upload — each was rejected without merits review because it lacked an engineer's report. Lock in the rejection rationale.
4. **Build the C3 pattern-of-practice record.** Monson is a supervisor; he has reviewed many Minnesota hail claims. How many had he applied the rule to? With what frequency? Under what circumstances?
5. **Develop the § 549.20 deliberate-disregard record.** Monson is the highest-rank Farmers personnel who personally communicated with the family. His conduct supports the "supervisor involvement" quantum factor under § 549.20 Subd. 3.

## 6. Anticipated Objections (and Responses)

Standard objection categories apply (see Brau deposition memo § 6). Monson-specific anticipated:

- **"Privileged" objection on communications with counsel after litigation became foreseeable.** Likely after 11/19/2024 (Dept of Commerce complaint) or later. Accept the privilege as to communications with counsel but pursue pre-litigation business communications (anything pre-11/19/2024 substantively claims-handling) and pursue underlying facts.
- **"Beyond the scope of personal knowledge" on the engineer-only rule's institutional status.** Monson is a supervisor — pattern and institutional knowledge are within his role. Reframe: "What is your personal knowledge of how this rule has been applied?"
- **"Calls for legal conclusion" on § 72A.201 violations.** Reframe: ask about Monson's awareness of the regulatory standards and his application of them, not about whether he violated the statute.
- **"Vague" on the term "engineer's report only" rule.** Confront with Brau's voicemail using those exact words; ask whether Monson agrees with the characterization.
- **Speaking objections suggesting answers.** Object on the record under Rule 30.04(c).

If defense counsel becomes obstructive, suspend the deposition, certify questions for ruling, and continue. Move for sanctions afterward; the transcript becomes Rule 37 evidence.

## 7. Prep Memo (Final Synthesis)

### 7.1 The One Sentence

Get Monson to admit, on the record, that on 2/3/2025 he told the family Farmers would not review evidence unless an engineer provided it; that the rule is not written anywhere in the policy or in Farmers' claims-handling manuals; and that the rule was the reason Farmers refused to engage on the merits with the NWS data, City permits, and neighbor canvass.

### 7.2 The Three Critical Locks

1. **The 2/3/2025 articulation lock.** Either Monson admits the statement (which proves the rule) or denies it (which makes him a liar in front of Brau's 2/10/2025 voicemail referring to "my field claim supervisor"). Both outcomes are good for the family.

2. **The "where is it written" lock.** If Monson cannot identify a written claims-handling document containing the rule, the rule is a freelance supervisor-level evidentiary gate found nowhere in the policy — institutional bad faith.

3. **The pattern-of-practice lock.** Force Monson to estimate how many Minnesota hail claims he has supervised in which the rule was applied. Whatever number he gives is the foundation for the C3 pattern-of-practice case and the § 549.20 deliberate-disregard quantum.

### 7.3 Do Not Open These Doors

- Do not let Monson opine on the appraisal panel's evaluation — that is the Rule 30(b)(6) and the appraisal-panel witnesses.
- Do not let Monson testify to "I was just following corporate policy" without identifying the specific corporate policy.
- Do not ask Monson about the engineering or scientific merit of hail-damage assessment — he is not an engineer.

### 7.4 Three Things Monson Will Try to Do

1. **Reframe the rule as "engineering reports are weighted more heavily" rather than "non-engineer evidence is not reviewed."** Response: the family heard "we won't look at it unless an engineer provided it"; Brau confirmed "it's not an engineers report so I believe that's what he wanted to look for from you." Force Monson to reconcile his reframe with the voicemail.

2. **Claim "I don't recall the specific call."** Response: refresh recollection with the family's contemporaneous note, with Brau's 2/10/2025 reference to "my field claim supervisor," with the timing (the 1/31/2025 appeal package had just been submitted), and with any claim-system audit-log entry showing the call.

3. **Distance from Brau's voicemail.** "I didn't instruct Brau to leave that voicemail." Response: even if Monson didn't draft the voicemail, the substantive rule is the same — Brau is reporting Monson's instruction in his voicemail. The rule is Monson's; the audio is just evidence of it.
