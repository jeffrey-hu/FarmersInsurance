## 1. Strategy — Why This Deposition

The Rule 30.02(f) deposition is **the single most strategically important discovery event in the case**, exceeding even the Brau and Monson individual depositions in institutional impact. Under Rule 30.02(f) (and the federal analog applied to its scope), the entity must "make a conscientious good-faith endeavor" to designate witnesses with knowledge of the noticed topics and prepare them to bind the entity on those topics. The entity cannot punt with "I don't know" answers — failure to prepare adequately is sanctionable.

This deposition does five things that individual depositions cannot:

1. **Bind Farmers institutionally on the "engineer's report only" rule.** Individual employees can say "that was just my practice" or "I was following supervisor instructions." The corporate designee speaks for the institution. If Farmers cannot produce a designee who can defend the rule as institutional protocol — or who must admit the rule has no written basis — Farmers' institutional bad faith is on the record.

2. **Lock Farmers into a single, sworn explanation of the missing Brau notes.** Either the records were destroyed (regulatory violation; spoliation), never created (regulatory violation), released to the family (which Farmers can't show), or in a system not searched (in which case Farmers' "no report exists" answer is wrong). One sworn institutional answer; all unfavorable.

3. **Force institutional articulation of the appraisal scope.** The 9/18, 9/19, and 10/14/2025 inconsistent descriptions are a Farmers problem. The Rule 30.02(f) designee must resolve them — and cannot — supporting the *Herll*/*Rymer* remand and the Subd. 4(b) appraisal-bar attack.

4. **Open the C3 pattern-of-practice record.** The designee must speak to Farmers' Minnesota hail-claim handling generally — frequency of denials, retention practice with Prieve and Hancock, application of the engineer-only rule across claims. Institutional pattern-of-practice answers are MCFA gold.

5. **Lay the foundation for adverse-inference instructions.** Each preserved "Farmers has not been able to find this" or "Farmers cannot explain that" answer is jury-instruction foundation at trial.

The notice itself is a multiplicative cost-imposition event. Preparing a designee for 15+ topics requires Farmers to coordinate across departments, retain a corporate-deposition consultant if needed, and spend 40–120 hours of designee-prep time. Defending the deposition itself is 2–5 full days of partner-level defense time.

## 2. Notice Strategy — Designing the Topic List

Under Minn. R. Civ. P. 30.02(f), the notice must "describe with reasonable particularity the matters for examination." Drafting tradeoffs:

- **Particular enough to bind Farmers.** Vague topics ("claims-handling practices") give the designee wiggle room. Specific topics ("the institutional source of the rule articulated in the 2/10/2025 Brau voicemail") force a sworn answer.
- **Not so narrow that defense can object to scope.** A topic must allow Farmers to identify a designee and prep them. "Every internal email mentioning Eden Prairie" is too narrow as a deposition topic (better in document discovery).
- **Reasonably proportional.** Minn. R. Civ. P. 26.02 proportionality applies; defense will object to overbroad topics. Anticipate 15–25 topics that survive.

Each topic below is drafted to balance these tradeoffs.

For a deposition this complex, plaintiffs should conduct two mock examination sessions:

## 7. Anticipated Objections (and Responses)

### 7.1 Topic-Scope Objections

Farmers will object that one or more topics are:
- **Overbroad.** Counter: each topic is specific to a defined subject matter; proportional to the issues in dispute.
- **Vague.** Counter: each topic identifies date ranges, specific documents, and specific personnel where possible.
- **Beyond institutional knowledge.** Counter: each topic is squarely within Farmers' institutional knowledge.
- **Privileged.** Counter: privilege protects communications, not facts; the designee must speak to facts.

Resolve these at the meet-and-confer. If unresolved, raise with the court via Rule 26 conference or Rule 37 motion before the deposition.

### 7.2 Designee-Preparedness Objections

If the designee says "I don't know" on a topic the notice covered:

- **First step:** Ask follow-up questions to determine whether the designee was prepared. "Did you review documents in preparation for testifying on this topic?" "Did you interview Farmers personnel?" "Did you have access to Farmers' claims-handling manuals?"
- **Second step:** If unprepared, certify the question for ruling and continue. Note the unpreparedness on the record.
- **Third step:** Move under Minn. R. Civ. P. 37 for sanctions or to compel a redesignation. Federal Rule 30(b)(6) caselaw (binding by analogy on Minnesota) holds that "I don't know" is not an acceptable answer for a corporate designee; the entity has a duty to prepare.

### 7.3 Privilege and Work-Product Objections

- **Communications with counsel.** Accept privilege for substantive legal advice; pursue the facts the designee learned during preparation.
- **The litigation-hold notice itself.** Mixed authority. Pursue the date, scope, recipients; concede the analytical content.
- **Reserve information.** Pursue if relevant to bad faith; respect protective-order designations.

### 7.4 Speaking Objections

Object on the record under Rule 30.04(c). If persistent, suspend and seek protective order.

## 8. Prep Memo (Final Synthesis)

### 8.1 The One Sentence

Force Farmers, through its corporate designee(s), to give a single sworn institutional answer on each of (a) where the "engineer's report only" rule is written; (b) what happened to Brau's 9/6/2024 inspection notes; (c) what the 9/12/2025 appraisal award actually covers — and to admit that on (a) and (b) the answer is unfavorable to Farmers and on (c) Farmers cannot give a consistent answer.

### 8.2 The Five Critical Locks

1. **The institutional engineer-only rule lock.** Either the rule is in a Farmers document Farmers can identify and produce — in which case institutional bad faith is on the record — or it is unwritten, in which case it is supervisor-level freelancing applied across Minnesota hail claims (pattern of practice + § 549.20 deliberate disregard).

2. **The Brau-notes institutional answer lock.** Farmers must give one sworn answer to "what happened to Brau's notes." Whatever that answer is, it commits Farmers institutionally. Inconsistency between the 10/10/2025 and 10/29/2025 statements must be resolved on the record.

3. **The appraisal-scope inconsistency lock.** Farmers cannot reconcile the 9/18, 9/19, and 10/14/2025 descriptions. The designee's failure to do so is direct *Herll*/*Rymer* evidence and direct Subd. 4(b) appraisal-bar evidence.

4. **The pattern-of-practice lock.** The designee must speak to Minnesota Farmers hail-claim handling generally. Whatever the designee says is the institutional position — C3 gold.

5. **The records-retention / litigation-hold lock.** Failure to articulate a defensible records-retention policy or a timely litigation-hold supports adverse-inference instructions at trial.

### 8.3 Do Not Open These Doors

- Do not allow the designee to opine on engineering matters — pursue Prieve at his deposition.
- Do not allow the designee to defend the appraisal panel — that's Foster/Roos/Tomes.
- Do not get bogged down in factual disputes that belong in individual depositions; the corporate designee speaks to institutional protocols.

### 8.4 Three Things Farmers Will Try to Do

1. **Designate an inadequately prepared witness on the engineer-only rule.** "We didn't have anyone to designate on that topic; it's not really a rule." Response: Demand redesignation; the rule was stated to the family by two Farmers employees seven days apart. Failure to designate a prepared witness is sanctionable.

2. **Compartmentalize the appraisal-scope inconsistency.** "Each of those descriptions came from different people on different days." Response: That is exactly the institutional-bad-faith point. The designee must articulate Farmers' single position; if Farmers has no single position, it cannot defend the Subd. 4(b) appraisal-bar.

3. **Hide the records-retention failure behind "we follow industry standard."** Response: Demand the specific policy, the specific search performed for Brau's notes, the specific result. Generic answers are not sufficient under Rule 30.02(f).

### 8.5 Coordination With Other Depositions

- Schedule Rule 30.02(f) **after** all individual Farmers personnel depositions — Brau, Monson, Dudley, Pietila, Tomes, post-appraisal correspondents. The individual depositions identify the gaps the designee must fill.
- Schedule **before** the Subd. 4(a) bad-faith motion — the institutional answers anchor the motion.
- Schedule **before** the Daubert motion on Prieve — the institutional answer on Prieve's data sources supports the Daubert attack.

### 8.6 Post-Deposition Action Items

- File the Rule 37 motion for sanctions or redesignation on any topic where the designee was unprepared.
- File the § 604.18 Subd. 4(a) motion to amend, supported by institutional admissions.
- File the *Herll*/*Rymer* remand motion, anchored in the institutional inability to describe the awarded scope.
- File the Daubert motion on Prieve, anchored in the institutional confirmation of Prieve's data sources.
- Update the trial-witness designations to include the Rule 30.02(f) designee video clips for the most damaging admissions.

### 8.7 The Two Things Farmers Cannot Win

No matter how Farmers prepares its designee, two facts are institutional. Plaintiffs should drive each:

- **Brau personally observed dents on 9/6/2024.** Either Farmers can produce his notes (which would document the observations and contradict the 9/3/2024 denial) or Farmers cannot (which is regulatory failure and spoliation foundation). There is no third option.
- **The umpire produced no report.** Already conceded in writing on 9/24/2025. The institutional designee must repeat the concession; that supports *Herll*/*Rymer* remand and the Subd. 4(b) attack.

These are guaranteed wins for the family at this deposition. Everything else is a question of how much additional cost the deposition imposes on Farmers' defense team.
