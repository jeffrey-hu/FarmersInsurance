## 1. What the Document Says

Farmers' 9/19/2025 letter forwards the appraiser's description of what the 9/12/2025 award covered, in two versions one day apart:

- **9/18/2025 (original):** "I documented the damage to the windows on the front right, and the rear elevation in the **dining room**. The windows were manufactured by Senco and had manufacturer stamps in the sashes from 1993, 1997, 2008."
- **9/19/2025 (correction):** "The damage to the windows on the rear elevation was in the **family room**. I mistakenly stated the dining room in my report. **The damage on the front windows in the dining room was from the prior claim.** … I documented the damage to the windows on the front right, and the rear elevation in the family room."

Two facts emerge from the appraiser's own words:

1. **Damage on the front dining-room window was observed and documented** — the appraiser does not say the window is undamaged; he says its damage is "from the prior claim."
2. **The dining-room window was excluded from the award on a prior-payment theory**, not on a finding of no hail.

**The family's fact (taken as true per project convention, pending 2020-file confirmation):** the dining-room window has **three sashes**; only **one** sash was damaged and paid in the 2020 hail claim; the **other two sashes carry new dents from the 7/13/2024 storm.** So the appraiser wrote off an entire 3-sash window as "prior claim" when at most one-third of it was prior.

## 2. Why This Is Leverage — Six Uses

### 2.1 The exclusion is a coverage/offset call, not a binding causation finding — the court decides it de novo

Under *Quade v. Secura Ins.*, 814 N.W.2d 703 (Minn. 2012), the panel's authority runs to **causation** ("did hail cause this?"). "This was already paid under the prior claim" is not a causation finding — it is a **prior-payment / offset** determination, which is a coverage question reserved to the court. Under *Mork* (as developed in `06` § 4), a panel statement that ventures into coverage is "mere surplusage," neither final nor binding. The dining-room exclusion can therefore be attacked **without disturbing any binding causation finding**, and the court reviews the prior-payment premise fresh. On that de novo question, the hail-causation evidence — including the `HailShapes` science (`07_HailScience_CausationStandard_Strategy.md`) — is fully admissible to prove the 2 new sashes were hail-damaged in 2024.

### 2.2 The "already paid" premise is factually false for two of three sashes — a "mistake" ground

The 2020 Xactimate covered only a small, sash-level scope (`EngineerReportRebuttal.md` §§ 4.1–4.2: line item "R&R Wood window — casement," qty 2; family's 2/9/2025 statement that "the only dents found in 2020 were for 2 sashes"). New dents on never-paid sashes are, by definition, new damage. An exclusion built on a demonstrably wrong factual premise is the "mistake" / wrong-scope defect that supports **remand under *Herll*/*Rymer*** and **modification under § 65A.01 Subd. 3** (`appraisal_research.MD` Avenue A). The **burden is on Farmers** to prove the prior claim actually paid for these specific sashes; the 2020 file shows it did not.

### 2.3 Demand sash-by-sash itemization — the appraiser's own words make it feasible

The appraiser identified the sashes individually by manufacturer stamp year (**1993, 1997, 2008**) — meaning the panel was examining sashes one at a time and *can* itemize at the sash level. § 65A.01 Subd. 3 requires stating value "separately… to each item." Writing off a 3-sash window as one undifferentiated "prior claim" unit — when two sashes are new — is precisely the non-itemization the Tier 1/Tier 2 motions target. The remand/modification ask should require per-sash treatment: which sash was paid in 2020, the RCV/ACV of each remaining sash, and the stated basis for any exclusion. (Folded into the proposed remand order, `04` § 5, item (a)(vi) and item (e).)

### 2.4 The two new-dent sashes are a clean *Quade* reservation for the court

If the panel did not genuinely evaluate the 2024 damage on those two sashes — it merely assumed "prior claim" — then those sashes were **never evaluated**, making them a reserved coverage question for the court under Count I (`02` § 6, Theory C). This is the cleanest possible reservation candidate because the appraiser's own statement supplies the admission that he treated the window as foreclosed by the 2020 claim rather than evaluating the new damage.

### 2.5 The 9/18 → 9/19 flip is itself ambiguity and unreliability evidence (*Herll*)

The appraiser confused the **dining room (front)** and **family room (rear)** windows and had to issue a same-day correction. This is one of the three mutually inconsistent Farmers scope descriptions (9/18, 9/19, 10/14/2025) already cataloged as the textbook *Herll*/*Rymer* "the parties cannot agree on what the award means" fact. It also impeaches the reliability of the entire scope identification: an appraiser who cannot keep front and rear windows straight is owed no deference on his confident "that one's from the prior claim."

### 2.6 Matching / Senco overlay

Senco is defunct, and the three different stamp years (1993/1997/2008) on a single window corroborate that sashes were replaced at different times — supporting the per-sash damage history and undercutting "the whole window was already handled in 2020." If the two new sashes cannot be matched to the surviving Senco sashes, *Cedar Bluff Townhome Condo. Ass'n v. Am. Family Mut. Ins. Co.*, 857 N.W.2d 290 (Minn. 2014), may push toward full-window (or all-window) replacement. (See `03` § 7.)

## 2.7 The baseline generalizes beyond the dining room — the 2020 file caps "prior" damage home-wide

The sash-by-sash analysis above is the dining-room *application* of a broader fact that should be pleaded in its own right: **Farmers' 2020 inspection documented hail dents on only two sashes and recorded no other dented sash or window anywhere on the home.** Because that record is Farmers' own contemporaneous documentation, it is a party-admission baseline of the property's window condition as of 10/13/2020 — not merely the family's account.

Two consequences follow, and they reach every window, not just the dining room:

1. **Affirmative causation engine.** Any dent the 2024 inspections documented on a sash *other than* the two paid in 2020 is, by Farmers' own baseline, post-2020 damage. The only documented hail event at the property after 10/13/2020 is the 7/13/2024 storm. So the baseline does the family's causation work across the ~19 windows Prieve catalogs: the argument shifts from "prove these are hail dents" to "Farmers' own file proves these dents were not present in 2020."

2. **The "prior claim" basket is capped at two sashes.** Prieve attributes "rounded dents … at sashes" on multiple windows to "a prior hail claim." That basket cannot hold more sashes than Farmers' 2020 record documented — i.e., two. Every additional sash Prieve assigns to "prior" is contradicted by Farmers' own file. This generalizes the § 2.2 "false premise" point from the dining-room window to the whole home and feeds the bad-faith / pretext theory (§ 604.18) and the MCFA count.

**Precision caveat (do not overstate).** Plead the defensible version — "the 2020 inspection documented hail dents *warranting repair* on only two sashes, and the settlement compensated only those two; no other window or sash was *identified as hail-damaged*" — rather than a flat "no dents existed anywhere." The reason is § 4.3 of `EngineerReportRebuttal.md`: Prieve found a trim-board chip (A-53) visible in Farmers' 2020 *photo log* that the 2020 *Xactimate* did not pay. Trim is neither a sash nor a window, so it does not contradict the careful phrasing — but it shows the 2020 photo log and the 2020 payment scope are **not coextensive**. Confirm the exact per-sash scope against the actual 2020 Xactimate + photo log (RFP A.2) before filing. If the photo log shows additional dents, that supports a *separate* 2020-underpayment argument but undercuts a flat "no other dents" allegation.
