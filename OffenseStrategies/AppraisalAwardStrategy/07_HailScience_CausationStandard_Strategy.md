## 1. The One-Paragraph Thesis

The existing appraisal strategy (files `00`–`05`) wins the *procedural* fight: the 9/12/2025 award is ambiguous and not "so itemized," so it can be remanded under *Herll*/*Rymer* or modified under § 65A.01 Subd. 3, and any window the panel never evaluated is a coverage question reserved to the court under *Quade*. That strategy deliberately holds the hail science in reserve. **This document supplies the layer the procedural strategy cannot reach: what happens when the fight turns from "did the panel itemize?" to "was the panel right to find no hail at these windows?"** That is the "evaluated-but-rejected" scenario flagged in `06`. The answer is the 24-source hail-shape record in `HailShapes/`: it proves that hail damage to windows is routinely *elongated, scraped, off-center, and irregular* — not the "smooth, round, scratch-free" signature Farmers' engineer (Prieve) used to deny the claim and that the panel almost certainly inherited. The science cannot, by itself, overturn a binding appraisal causation finding (see § 4). But channeled correctly, it (a) is the family's affirmative causation proof on every window the court decides de novo, (b) shapes the remand order so the panel must expose its standard, (c) recharacterizes "causation" rejections as coverage calls the court decides, and (d) anchors the Daubert attack on Prieve. **Properly deployed, the science is what converts "rejected" windows back into paid windows.**

## 2. What the Scientific Papers Actually Establish (Plain Language)

The `HailShapes/` folder assembles NOAA government authority, peer-reviewed journals, and professional inspection standards (InterNACHI) into five propositions. Stated without the formulas (the formulas are in `HailShapes/KeyDecisionDocument.md` Appendix and should travel with any expert affidavit):

1. **Hailstones are not spheres.** NOAA, *Lin et al.* (2024, *J. Atmospheric Sciences*), and *Farnell et al.* (2022, *Atmospheric Research*) all document that natural hail is "lumpy," "spiked," "lobed," and irregular — formally classified as spherical, irregular, or embryo. A spiked or lobed stone dragging across a surface leaves a scrape *inside* the dent. (Source IDs S-6, S-9, S-8, SP-1.)

2. **Wind makes hail strike at an angle, and angled impacts are elongated, not round.** This is the core of the user's question. The resultant-velocity relationship `vr = √(vw² + (vt + vd)²)` gives an impact angle `θ = arctan(vw / (vt + vd))`. For a typical severe hailstone (terminal velocity ≈ 30 m/s) in a 15 m/s crosswind, hail arrives ≈ 27° off vertical — geometrically guaranteeing an elliptical, dragged mark rather than a clean circle. Wind also *increases* the force: the *Applied Sciences* (2025) regression `Fp = 2.321·Vw + 6375.502·d + 9.487·Vhail − 262.402` shows a 3.2 cm stone delivers ~43.6% more force at 13 m/s wind than in still air. (Source IDs A-1, A-4, A-10.)

3. **Direction governs *where* and *what shape* the damage is.** *Calotescu et al.* (2024, *J. Wind Engineering*) surveyed a real storm and found ~90% of facade damage on the windward side and <5% leeward — and, critically, **windward facades showed mostly circular dents while other orientations showed elongated patterns.** Same storm, same stones, different shapes by orientation. This is the field-evidence answer to Prieve's "east-side damage is impossible because hail comes from the west" argument: storm *track* is not instantaneous *wind direction*, and a single building gets multiple shapes from one storm. (Source ID A-7/Thunderstorm.)

4. **Scratches, spatter, and cracks are recognized hail damage.** InterNACHI's professional standard explicitly lists spatter marks, screen streaking, frame cracking, and varied indentations as legitimate hail signatures, and states "hail fall is random… not likely to leave marks of identical size." Glass fragility research shows the same stone produces different crack patterns on different panes because each pane's microflaw population differs. (Source IDs W-2, A-CIB.)

5. **Material and geometry change the dent.** The dent-depth and dent-diameter formulas (`D = √(α·(m·V²)/(2·t·σf) − …)`, etc.) prove the *same* hailstone dents a thin sash differently than a thick frame, and can reach recessed accessory grooves on an angled trajectory. Damage variability across a single window is the expected physics, not evidence of a non-hail cause. (Source IDs A-9, A-3, A-5.)

**Why this maps onto the family's facts.** The 7/13–7/14/2024 event was an overnight, multi-round system with a **48 MPH gust recorded at Eden Prairie ASOS at 1:00 AM on 7/14** (`Evidence/Significant Wind and Hail in Minnesota July 13-14.pdf`). That is textbook high-wind, oblique-impact, multi-directional hail. Prieve's own Photo Appendix A then documents exactly the signatures the science predicts: dents *with scrape marks* (A-45, A-64, A-107, A-113, A-115–117, A-156, A-159, A-166), dents in *accessory grooves* (A-134, A-149, A-151, A-162, A-163, A-174, A-200, A-206), *elongated/creased* marks (A-99 downspout crease), and **east-side** damage (the entire D-series and the A-208/A-212 siding penetration). The science does not have to be imported from outside — it explains the patterns Farmers' own engineer photographed.

## 3. The Hidden Standard: Prieve's "Smooth/Round = Hail" Test, Inherited by the Panel

The science matters because of a specific mechanism documented in `EngineerReportRebuttal.md` § 6A. In one sentence about the garage window, Prieve revealed his operative diagnostic:

> "Various scrape marks appear in the frame of the window not consistent with hail. **No smooth or rounded dents were observed in the frame or sashes.**"

That sentence is a two-part test: (i) *smooth/round dent → hail*, but (ii) *scrape marks inside a dent → "blunt object other than hail."* He applied part (ii) at least seven times across two pages to wave off dent after dent as "not consistent with hail."

**This is the entire ballgame for the science.** Prieve's test is precisely the proposition the `HailShapes` record disproves: real hail is irregular and wind-driven, so scrape-marked and elongated dents are *normal* hail signatures, not disqualifying ones. Part (i) of his own test even convicts him — he admits "rounded dents" at the front/west window (A-38–41) and "generally smooth" dents at the north porch window (A-179), then hand-waves them to the 2020 claim by presumption rather than analysis.

The strategic inference: **the appraisal panel, working from Farmers' file, very likely applied the same artificially narrow damage definition.** A panel that awarded 2 windows out of ~19 Prieve documented with damage, on a record dominated by scrape-marked and groove dents, behaves exactly as a panel using a "round-and-clean-only" filter would. If that is what happened, the panel did not make a defensible causation finding — it applied a scientifically invalid screening rule. That is the opening the science exploits (see `appraisal_research.MD` Avenue A: the "mistake" was evaluating damage using a wrong, narrow definition).

## 4. The Legal Mechanism — Independently Verified, With the Hard Limit Stated Honestly

The user's instruction is to verify legal theory independently. I did. Here is the analysis, including the part that constrains how aggressively the science can be used.

### 4.1 What is settled (verified against primary and secondary sources)

- **Appraisers decide factual causation.** *Quade v. Secura Ins.*, 814 N.W.2d 703 (Minn. 2012): "amount of loss" necessarily includes determining the cause of loss. Verified via the Minnesota Supreme Court opinion (Justia/FindLaw) and secondary analysis. So an appraisal panel *can* decide "did hail cause this dent?"
- **Courts decide coverage.** The causation/coverage line holds: appraisers find facts (wind vs. hail), but legal coverage questions — exclusions, cosmetic-vs-functional as a *policy* matter, matching — remain for the court. (Zelle; Property Insurance Coverage Law Blog; *Gibbs* line.)
- **Ambiguous awards are remanded, not re-tried.** *Herll v. Auto-Owners*, 879 F.3d 293 (8th Cir. 2018), and now ***Cincinnati Ins. Co. v. Rymer Cos.*, No. 24-3356 (8th Cir. Mar. 27, 2026)** — verified as a real, on-point decision. In *Rymer* a $23,226 "mall roof repair" award was remanded; the panel clarified it meant flashing only; the Eighth Circuit affirmed.
- **The critical *Rymer* holding for us:** appraiser/umpire **testimony "cannot be used to reinterpret or expand an appraisal award"** — its *only* permitted use is to show the award should be vacated for fraud or misconduct.

### 4.2 The hard limit the science runs into

Put those together and the constraint is stark: **the hail science cannot be used to walk into court and ask the judge to overrule the panel's "no hail" finding on a window the panel actually evaluated.** That is a merits re-trial of a binding factual causation finding, and Minnesota courts will not do it (and *Rymer* bars using even the appraisers' own testimony to expand the award). If the panel genuinely evaluated all the windows and rejected seventeen of them on causation grounds, the science alone does not reopen those seventeen — that is the residual risk `06` § 8 states plainly.

**So the science is not a battering ram against the award. It is a precision tool with four legitimate entry points where the law lets it bite.** The strategy is to channel every scientific argument into one of these four, never to argue "the panel was scientifically wrong, so vacate."

### 4.3 The four entry points where the science is admissible and powerful

**Entry 1 — The unevaluated windows (the *Quade* reservation): the court decides causation de novo, and the science is the proof.**
This is the strongest and cleanest use. For every window the panel never evaluated (the base-case theory of `02` § 6, Theory C — roughly 10 window locations plus the never-inspected front-of-garage window in `EngineerReportRebuttal.md` § 6B), there is *no* binding appraisal causation finding. Coverage and amount of loss go to the court under Count I. There, the family bears the ordinary burden of proving hail causation — and the `HailShapes` record plus an independent expert applying it to the actual windows is exactly that affirmative proof. The science is not attacking the award here at all; it is building the family's own case on the territory the award never touched. **This use is fully available regardless of how the "evaluated-but-rejected" question comes out.**

**Entry 2 — Shaping the remand order so the panel must expose its standard.**
`04` § 5 and `06` § 3.5 already propose a remand order forcing per-item causation reasoning. The science is what gives item (d) teeth. The remand order should require the panel, for every window it claims to have evaluated but did not award, to state the *physical observation* and the *specific basis* for finding no hail — cross-referenced to the matching Prieve photograph. Then one of three things happens, all good:
   - The panel can't produce per-window reasoning → it confirms non-evaluation → those windows fall into Entry 1 (court decides, science applies).
   - The panel produces a "round-and-clean-only" rationale → that rationale is now exposed on the record as the scientifically invalid Prieve test, which feeds Entry 3 and the misconduct/mistake record.
   - The panel produces genuine per-window causation analysis → the family is in the hard case of § 4.2, but at least knows it and can target the few windows where the rationale is weakest.

**Entry 3 — Recharacterizing "causation" rejections as coverage calls (the *Mork* surplusage move).**
This is `06` § 4, and the science strengthens it. *Quade* gives the panel causation ("was it hail?"); it does **not** give the panel coverage ("is hail-caused damage barred by the marring / wear-and-tear / cosmetic exclusion?"). The science helps sort each rejection: if a window shows a clear hail-consistent dent (per the `HailShapes` criteria) but was nonetheless rejected, the rejection was probably not really "no hail" — it was a disguised coverage call (cosmetic, marring, pre-existing wear). Coverage calls are *surplusage*, not binding, and the court decides them **de novo** — and on that de novo coverage question the science is fully admissible. The original denial already cited six coverage exclusions (marring def. 16, wear/tear A.7/B.8, inherent vice, corrosion, neglect, faulty construction — `HailShapes/DocumentIndex.md` INS-1), which is the tell that the rejections are coverage-flavored.

**Entry 4 — The Daubert/Frye-Mack attack on Prieve, and the contrast with a real expert.**
In the broader bad-faith case, Prieve's "smooth/round = hail; scrapes = blunt object" test has no methodology, no error rate, no peer-reviewed support, and is applied inconsistently — a clean Daubert/Frye-Mack target (`EngineerReportRebuttal.md` § 6A.4; the Daubert package is in `OffenseStrategies/Motions/Daubert/`). The `HailShapes` literature is the peer-reviewed yardstick against which Prieve's qualitative say-so collapses. Discrediting the standard that produced the denial undermines the foundation the panel built on, supports the "mistake"/inadequate-investigation theory, and supplies the misconduct record that the *flipped vacatur calculus* (`06` § 5) would need if the case ever reaches that last resort.

**Sequencing rule (important):** lead the appraisal motions with the *procedural* arguments (ambiguity, non-itemization) exactly as `04` prescribes. Keep the science out of the opening remand brief except as the benchmark in the proposed order. Deploy the science *after* the panel is forced to commit to a standard, and on the de novo terrain (unevaluated windows, coverage questions, Daubert). Leading with the science invites the court to think the family is asking it to re-try the appraisal on the merits — the one thing *Rymer* forbids.

## 6. Concrete Drop-Ins

### 6.1 Addition to the proposed remand order (insert into `04` § 5 item (d), expanding `06` § 3.5)

> (d)(v) For each window for which the panel awarded nothing, STATE the
>        damage signature the panel treated as indicative of hail and the
>        damage signature the panel treated as inconsistent with hail, and
>        identify the methodology, published standard, or authority on which
>        that distinction rests. If the panel treated the presence of scrape
>        marks within a dent, an elongated (non-circular) dent, or a dent in
>        a recessed accessory groove as indicating a cause other than hail,
>        STATE the basis for that treatment.

Rationale: this forces the panel to either (a) disclaim any such narrow rule (in which case its rejections lack a stated causation basis and collapse toward non-evaluation), or (b) own the Prieve "round/clean only" rule on the record, where the `HailShapes` literature refutes it.

### 6.2 Science-based deposition questions (add to Foster/Roos/Tomes outlines per `06` § 3.2; and to the Prieve outline `2.1Prieve_Nathan.md`)

> - What physical characteristics did you treat as the signature of hail damage to a window sash or frame? Identify every published standard or authority you relied on for that definition.
> - Did you treat a dent containing a scrape mark as evidence *against* hail causation? On what authority?
> - Did you treat an elongated or non-circular dent as evidence against hail? On what authority?
> - Are you aware that NOAA describes hailstones as having "lumps, bumps… and small spikes," and that wind causes hail to fall "at angles or nearly sideways"? How did you account for non-spherical, wind-driven hail in your damage definition?
> - The 7/13–7/14/2024 storm produced a 48 MPH gust at Eden Prairie at 1:00 AM. What impact angle does a 48 MPH crosswind produce on falling hail, and how did you account for it?
> - For each window you found "no hail damage," did you measure or calculate any impact threshold, or was the finding based on visual appearance only?

### 6.3 Independent expert engagement — scope (refines `04` § 2.6 and `appraisal_research.MD` step 10)

The expert is the indispensable vehicle for the science. The report should: (i) state the accepted, literature-based criteria for hail damage to aluminum/aluminum-clad window components, citing the `HailShapes` sources; (ii) apply the wind/angle and dent formulas using the documented 7/13–7/14/2024 storm parameters and the actual window materials (extruded aluminum, Senco/Semco, sash stamps 1993/1997/2008/2014 — verify material first, per `EngineerReportRebuttal.md` § 10); (iii) perform a window-by-window causation opinion cross-referenced to Prieve's Appendix A photos; and (iv) opine specifically that scrape-marked, elongated, and accessory-groove dents are consistent with wind-driven hail. A focused declaration (~$5k) is enough to support the Tier 1/Theory C motion; the full report (~$30k) is for trial (`04` § 9).

## 7. Risks and Honest Limits

1. **The science does not reopen genuinely evaluated-and-rejected windows in court.** Per § 4.2 and *Rymer*, a documented, reasoned panel causation rejection on a window it actually evaluated is binding and not re-tried on the science alone. The science's power is real but channeled through the four entry points — most reliably Entry 1 (unevaluated windows) and Entry 3 (recharacterized coverage calls).
2. **A competent insurer will frame every rejection as "causation"** to stay inside *Quade* and away from de novo coverage review. Entry 3 therefore works window-by-window, not wholesale.
3. **One paper in the folder is retracted** (A-3, *Shock and Vibration* 2019). Do not cite it in any filing or expert report; the conclusions it supports are independently supported by A-9, A-1, and A-4. Drop A-3.
4. **The science is corroborative, not a substitute for the procedural case.** It does not lower the deductible by itself; it expands the *scope* of compensable windows, which is what crosses the $17,720 deductible (see the quantum math in `02` § 5).
5. **Verify the window material before the expert opines.** The dent-threshold argument depends on it (`EngineerReportRebuttal.md` § 10 action item).
