# The 90-Day Deadline Issue — Garlyn, Oliver, and Why It Does Not Bar Us

**Prepared:** May 31, 2026
**Bottom line:** The 90-day deadline to challenge an appraisal award **does not apply to our case.** We are not procedurally barred. The only deadline that actually controls is the policy's two-year suit limitation, expiring **7/13/2026** (≈6 weeks away).

**Disclaimer:** Internal strategy research, not legal advice. *Garlyn* and *Oliver* should be pulled and KeyCited by a Minnesota-licensed attorney before any filing.

---

## The Concern (as raised in `appraisal_vacatur_caselaw.md` § 1.8)

The memo states that *Garlyn Inc. v. Auto Owners Ins. Co.* (Minn. App.) held a court must confirm an appraisal award if no party moves to change, modify, correct, or vacate it within **90 days** — and advises treating **12/11/2025** (90 days after the 9/12/2025 award) as already past. The worry: by not filing a standalone motion to vacate within that window, we forfeited the right to challenge the award.

## The Truthful Evaluation — The Rule Does Not Apply

**1. *Garlyn* is an arbitration case, not an appraisal case.**
*Garlyn* arose from consolidated auto-glass repair claims sent to **arbitration**. The 90-day confirmation deadline it applied comes from Minnesota's arbitration statute (Chapter 572B, the Uniform Arbitration Act / UAA). That deadline lives inside the arbitration framework. Applying it to a § 65A.01 *appraisal* award stretches *Garlyn* beyond what it held.

**2. *Oliver* squarely forecloses the 90-day deadline for appraisals.**
*Oliver v. State Farm Fire & Cas. Ins. Co.*, 939 N.W.2d 749 (Minn. 2020) — already a centerpiece authority in our `03_LegalFramework.md` — holds that appraisal under the Standard Fire Policy is **not** an agreement to arbitrate, so the UAA does not apply. Consequently, the UAA's "ninety day limitation to modify an award does not apply to an appraisal award." *Oliver* postdates *Garlyn* and governs. **There is no statutory 90-day clock on challenging our appraisal award.**

> The memo half-acknowledges this ("After *Oliver*, it is debatable…") but then tells us to assume the deadline passed "out of caution." That is more conservative than the law requires and slightly misstates the legal reality: the 90-day window is **not** a live bar against us.

## Impact on the Case — Minimal, and What Effect It Has Is Favorable

- **Nothing was forfeited on 12/11/2025.** We did not file a standalone motion to vacate within 90 days, but we were never required to.
- **The award has not been judicially confirmed against us.** We remain free to seek remand/clarification under *Herll* / *Rymer*, modification under § 65A.01 Subd. 3, or vacatur under *Mork* — all inside a breach-of-contract action.
- **The controlling deadline is 7/13/2026** (policy two-year suit limitation). That — not the 90-day issue — is what should drive urgency. It is ≈6 weeks out as of today.
- **Strategic conclusion is unchanged, the rationale is corrected.** We still choose the breach-of-contract vehicle (embedding the appraisal attack) — but because it is the stronger play under *Herll* / *Rymer* and avoids the heavy *Mork* burden, **not** because a 90-day clock closed off a standalone challenge.
