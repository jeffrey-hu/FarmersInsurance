## 1. The form carries a no-deposition / no-claims clause — the most consequential find

Printed at the bottom of the award's front page, below the certification block:

> "Appraisal award is subject to all policy terms and conditions including, but not limited to, the deductable(s) and replacement cost provisions. **The deliberation of the Appraisal Panel is confidential and exempt from depositions or suit in the event the agreement is challanged, or a bad faith claim or breach of contract is filed.** Furthermore, to the maximum extent permitted by law, **no party will assert any claims against the named Appraisers or the named Umpire or their respective firms**, seeking to hold them or their firms liable for any act or omission in preforming their duties as an Appraiser or Umpire."

*(Spelling as it appears on the form: "deductable(s)", "challanged", "preforming".)*

**Why this matters immediately.** `OffenseStrategies/Deposition/` contains full deposition plans for Wade Roos (2.3), John Foster (2.2), and Greg Tomes (2.4). This clause is the first thing Defendant will cite to resist all three, and to resist any Count III inquiry into how the panel reached $12,800 on two windows.

**Grounds to answer it — for counsel to evaluate:**

1. **Plaintiffs are not signatories.** The signature lines on the form are Appraiser, Appraiser, and Umpire. There is no insured signature line and no insured signature. A clause reciting that "no party will assert any claims" appears in a document Plaintiffs did not sign and to which they did not assent.
2. **A private form cannot contract away the court's discovery authority.** Discovery scope is set by Minn. R. Civ. P. 26.02 and the Court's orders, not by boilerplate on a vendor's form.
3. ***Rymer* is directly on point and cuts the other way.** *Cincinnati Insurance Co. v. Rymer Cos.*, No. 24-3356 (8th Cir. Mar. 27, 2026) — already relied on elsewhere in this file — holds that post-award appraiser testimony cannot be used to expand or alter an award **but is admissible for misconduct purposes** and to clarify ambiguity on a *Herll*/*Rymer* remand. That is precisely the use contemplated here.
4. **It proves too much.** Read as Defendant will read it, the clause would immunize an appraisal panel from any scrutiny in any bad-faith or breach action — including *Mork* vacatur for misfeasance, which Minnesota law expressly preserves.
5. **Scope.** By its terms it covers "the deliberation of the Appraisal Panel." It does not by its terms reach what each panel member physically inspected, what documents each was given, what methodology each applied, or whether the umpire produced a report — the questions that actually matter here.

**Action items:** raise this in the Rule 26.06 plan or an early conference rather than discovering it at a deposition; and check whether the same clause appears in the Appraisal Demand or the Appraiser Retainer Agreement, which Plaintiffs *did* sign.

## 3. The umpire was appointed the same day the award issued

Both the umpire-appointment block and the certification block are dated **9-12-2025** — the date of the inspection and of the award.

The policy's appraisal condition provides that "[t]he appraisers will choose a competent and disinterested umpire. If the appraisers cannot agree upon an umpire within 15 days, you or we can ask a judge…". A 15-day window implies deliberation; appointment, inspection, and award compressed into a single day does not.

Read alongside two facts already in the record — that Farmers confirmed in writing on September 24, 2025 that **the umpire produced no report or estimate**, and that Plaintiffs were told that morning not to speak with the panel — this supports the Count III itemization and *Mork* misfeasance theories. **Confirm the appointment date against the original**, and add it to the Roos and Tomes deposition outlines.

## 4. The back page confirms the damages arithmetic

Payment summary, Additional Information page:

| Line of Coverage | Dwelling |
|---|---|
| Replacement Cost | $12,800.00 |
| Less: Depreciation | $6,400.00 |
| Actual Cash Value | $6,400.00 |
| Less: Policy Deductible | $17,720.00 |
| **Amount** | **$N/A** |

Two uses:

- **Depreciation is exactly 50%** of replacement cost. This is Defendant's own document, and it is the basis for the interim ACV placeholder proposed at `Filings/Discovery/InitialDisclosure/Proposed_Changes_Initial_Disclosures_2026-09-06.md` P-5 row 2. Note that under *Wilcox v. State Farm Fire & Casualty Co.*, 874 N.W.2d 780 (Minn. 2016), actual cash value is determined under the broad evidence rule and depreciation is a fact question — a flat 50% is a placeholder, not a concession.
- **"Amount: $N/A"** is Defendant's own written confirmation that the award produced no payment.

The award also carries a line item reading **"Permit is to be Paid When Incurred"** — worth noting against the § 65A.10 code-compliance argument (*Great Northwest Insurance Co. v. Campbell* (Minn. July 30, 2025)).

## 5. Reservation-of-rights language on the back page

> "By the writing of this letter, or our issuing payment following the appraisal, Fire Insurance Exchange is not waiving any of the policy coverages, limitations, exclusions, or provisions, all of which are specifically reserved. Further, any activity on our part by way of investigation, estimation of damage, or possible emergency advance payments to you, does not constitute a waiver of our rights. **We understand you are retaining your rights as well.**"

The final sentence is Defendant's own written acknowledgment, in the award transmittal, that **Plaintiffs retained their rights** following the appraisal. That is useful against any argument that the appraisal resolved or extinguished the claim — the position Defendant took on October 21, 2025 ("[t]he claim was settled per the binding appraisal award received").
