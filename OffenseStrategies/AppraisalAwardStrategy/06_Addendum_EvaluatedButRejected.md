# Addendum — The "Evaluated-but-Rejected" Scenario

**What this addendum is.** Files `00`–`05` in this folder build the Count III strategy on one working assumption: that the appraisal panel *did not evaluate* the windows beyond the two it awarded, so those windows remain unevaluated coverage questions reserved to the court under *Quade*. This addendum addresses the scenario that assumption does not cover — where the panel, on remand or in deposition, claims it **did** evaluate every window and simply found hail damage at only the two it awarded. Call it the **evaluated-but-rejected scenario**.

**Scope of this addendum — deliberately narrow.** This is not a catalog of everything the family could try. It keeps only the responses that are strong and likely to move the case, and omits weaker or marginal ones on purpose. The result is two core moves plus one honest strategic note. Read it alongside `00`–`05`; where it conflicts with a base file, this addendum reflects the more current thinking. Each move notes which base file/section it would amend.

> **Disclaimer.** Strategic plan, not legal advice. Every citation should be Shepardized / KeyCited and every proposed filing reviewed by a Minnesota-licensed attorney before use.

---

## 1. The Concern in Plain Terms

The base strategy's lead remedy (Tier 1) is to ask the court to remand the 9/12/2025 award to the panel for clarification under *Herll v. Auto-Owners Ins. Co.*, 879 F.3d 293 (8th Cir. 2018), and *Cincinnati Ins. Co. v. Rymer Cos.*, No. 24-3356 (8th Cir. Mar. 27, 2026). It treats remand as effectively win/win: the panel either (a) expands its itemization upward, or (b) admits it never evaluated the other windows, making them reserved coverage questions for the court.

There is a third response the base files do not work through: **(c) the panel says, "we evaluated all of the windows; we found hail damage at only two; we awarded what we found."** If the panel can make that claim credibly, the family is in real trouble:

- **The *Quade* reservation collapses.** Theory C of `02_AwardedScope_vs_DocumentedDamage_Gap.md` § 6 reserves *unevaluated* windows for the court. If the panel evaluated them, there is nothing left to reserve. An evaluated-and-rejected window is a decided causation question, and under *Quade v. Secura Ins.*, 814 N.W.2d 703 (Minn. 2012), causation findings within the panel's authority bind.
- **\*Rymer\* blocks the cheap fix.** *Rymer* holds that once a clarified award confirms a narrow evaluated scope, appraiser and umpire testimony **cannot be used to "reinterpret or expand" the award** — such testimony is admissible *only* to show the award should be vacated for misconduct. The family cannot simply call Foster, Roos, or Tomes to say the other windows should have been included.
- **\*Mork\* locks in the dollar number.** *Mork v. Eureka-Security Fire & Marine Ins. Co.*, 230 Minn. 382, 42 N.W.2d 33 (1950), gives the award a strong presumption of validity; "mere inadequacy" — even gross inadequacy — will not vacate it. On the present award ($12,800 RCV / $6,400 ACV, net $0 after the $17,720 deductible), that means $0 to the family.

Winning the remand and then losing this branch can leave the family worse off than if it had never moved. The purpose of this addendum is to keep that from happening — primarily by *preventing* the panel from credibly making claim (c) in the first place, and secondarily by having a worked-out response if it does.

---

## 2. Why the Base Strategy Must Be Supplemented (the assumption to retire)

The base files assume non-evaluation because, on the *present* record, there is no evidence the panel performed a window-by-window evaluation: the umpire (Roos) produced no report, estimate, or methodology (Farmers' written admission, 9/24/2025); the award lists only two coded windows against a lump sum; and Farmers cannot consistently describe its own award. That record supports the inference of non-evaluation — but it does not *prove* it, and once a remand is ordered Farmers has every incentive to reconstruct the panel's work as a complete evaluation that simply came out small.

The strategic error to avoid is **letting the panel define the evaluated scope after the fact.** Both moves below exist to take that option away from the panel before it can use it, and to have an answer if it uses it anyway.

## 3. Move 1 — Lock the panel's evaluation account under oath *before* moving for remand (and propose a tight remand order)

*Would amend `05_AppraisalSpecific_Discovery_Deposition.md` (adds a discovery target), the sequencing in `04_MotionTactics.md` § 8, and the proposed remand order in `04_MotionTactics.md` § 5.*

This is the strongest move in the whole scenario, and it is largely a matter of **order of operations**. It is cheap, fully within the family's control, and it determines which world the family is in before it commits to the remand. The danger in claim (c) is that the panel manufactures a full-scope evaluation narrative on remand. The way to defuse it is to fix the panel's story first — under oath, in deposition — so that any later "we evaluated everything" assertion contradicts prior sworn testimony and is impeachable. *Rymer* bars using that testimony to *expand* the award, but expressly permits using it to show *misconduct*; locked contradictory testimony is exactly that.

### 3.1 The factual proposition to lock

For each window Prieve documented with damage but the panel did not award, establish one of two things under oath:

- **Non-evaluation** — the panel did not individually inspect and value that window (the expected answer, given the missing methodology); or
- **If evaluated, on what standard** — the precise causation test each panelist applied, and whether any rejection rested on a policy exclusion rather than pure causation.

Either answer helps. Non-evaluation preserves Theory C. A disclosed-but-flawed standard sets up Move 2 and the strategic note in § 5.

### 3.2 Drop-in — deposition questions (ask each of Foster, Roos, Tomes)

> 1. On September 12, 2025, did you physically inspect each window at 17756 George Moran Dr. individually? Identify every window you personally inspected, and state how long you spent on each.
> 2. Did you create, or rely on, any window-by-window record — field notes, a sketch, a spreadsheet, a photograph index, a measurement log? Produce it. If none exists, confirm that none exists.
> 3. For each window that was not included in the award, state the specific physical observation you made at that window and the specific reason it received nothing.
> 4. For each such window, was the reason a conclusion that hail did **not** cause the marks, or a conclusion that the **policy does not cover** the damage?
> 5. Did you review Prieve Engineering's Photo Appendix A before or during the appraisal? If so, for any window where you concluded there was no hail damage, how did you reconcile that conclusion with Prieve's photograph of a dent at the same window?
> 6. What did you understand the most recent hail event at this property to have been, and on what did you base that understanding?

### 3.3 Drop-in — interrogatory and RFAs (to Farmers)

> **INT-CIII.5.** For each window at 17756 George Moran Dr. that was not included in the September 12, 2025 Appraisal Award, state whether the appraisal panel individually inspected and valued that window; and if so, identify the panelist(s) who did so, the date, and every document reflecting that evaluation.

> **RFA-CIII.13.** The appraisal panel did not prepare any window-by-window written record of its evaluation of the windows at 17756 George Moran Dr.
>
> **RFA-CIII.14.** The appraisal panel did not individually value each window at 17756 George Moran Dr. that Plaintiffs submitted for appraisal.
>
> **RFA-CIII.15.** The September 12, 2025 Appraisal Award does not state, for any window other than those identified as "F1.7" and "B1.2," whether the panel found that hail did or did not cause damage to that window.

### 3.4 The sequencing rule

*Do not file the Tier 1 remand motion until these depositions are complete.* The base timeline in `04_MotionTactics.md` § 8 already places the Foster/Roos/Tomes depositions (month 9) before the Tier 1 motion (month 12). That ordering is load-bearing for this defense, not incidental — if depositions slip past the motion, either move the motion or accept the risk that a clarified award produces an un-impeachable full-scope rejection.

### 3.5 Drop-in — the tight remand order (the operational other half of this move)

If the family wins remand, the proposed order is its main lever to shape what the panel must do. The order should compel the panel, for any window it claims to have evaluated but did not award, to document the evaluation in a way it likely cannot — and, if it can, in a way the family can test against the locked deposition testimony and against Prieve's own photographs. Insert the following between existing items (c) and (d) of the proposed order in `04_MotionTactics.md` § 5 (renumber the existing "PRODUCE the panel's working file" item accordingly):

> (d) For each window, siding component, or other item that the panel
>     DID evaluate but for which it awarded $0 or otherwise did not
>     include in the award, STATE separately, item by item:
>     (i)   that the panel physically inspected the item, and the date
>           and manner of that inspection;
>     (ii)  the specific physical observation the panel made at the item,
>           cross-referenced to any Prieve Engineering Photo Appendix A
>           photograph depicting the same location;
>     (iii) the specific basis for awarding nothing; and
>     (iv)  whether that basis is a finding that the damage was NOT caused
>           by hail (a causation finding) or a finding that hail-caused
>           damage is excluded or limited by a policy provision (a
>           coverage finding), identifying any such provision by
>           endorsement and section.

**Drop-in — anticipated opposition and reply (add to `04_MotionTactics.md` § 6.1):**

> *Opposition:* "Item (d) is unnecessary — the panel obviously evaluated everything and simply found little damage."
>
> *Reply:* Then the panel can readily complete item (d) item by item, and the family is entitled to that itemization to test it. If the panel in fact evaluated every window, stating so per window costs it nothing. Resistance to (d) is itself evidence that no window-by-window evaluation occurred. And the family does not concede the premise: Prieve's own Appendix A documents damage at the very windows Farmers says were found undamaged.

> **Honest limit on the order.** A court may trim an order it sees as micromanaging the panel; *Herll*/*Rymer* remands are framed as "clarify what you meant," not "produce a forensic report." Item (d) is worth proposing because it costs nothing and shapes the outcome, but its force depends on the court adopting it and on Move 1's deposition record giving it teeth.

## 4. Move 2 — Press the causation / coverage line (the *Mork* surplusage escape)

*Would amend `03_LegalFramework.md` § 2 (Quade) and § 3 (Mork); informs the post-remand handling in `04_MotionTactics.md` § 6.*

This is the best *legal* defense if the panel rejects windows on the "merits." *Quade* gives the panel authority over **causation** ("did hail cause this dent?"). It does **not** give the panel authority over **coverage** ("is hail-caused damage to this window excluded or limited by a wear-and-tear, neglect, maintenance, or anti-concurrent-causation provision?"). Under *Mork*, a panel statement that ventures into coverage is "mere surplusage" — neither final nor binding — because coverage is a question of law reserved to the court.

Every rejection sorts into one of two bins, and the family works to push as many as possible into the second:

- **Causation-based rejection** ("these dents are mechanical / not hail-shaped"): within *Quade*, binding. The only realistic attack is the misconduct/mistake record discussed in § 5, which is a long shot.
- **Coverage-based rejection** ("this is wear/tear/age the policy doesn't cover"): *Mork* surplusage — the court decides it de novo, and the window returns to Count I on a clean footing.

This is why Move 1's deposition question 4 and the remand order's item (d)(iv) both force the panel to characterize each rejection. Every rejection the family can show is really a coverage call is a rejection the **court**, not the panel, gets to decide.

> **Honest limit.** A competent panel or insurer will try to frame every rejection as "causation" precisely to stay inside *Quade* and avoid this trap. So this move's reach is window-by-window and fact-specific — it does not sweep the whole rejection aside at once. But where a rejection is genuinely coverage-flavored, this is a clean, well-grounded win on that window.

**Drop-in — brief language (for the motion or post-remand briefing):**

> The appraisal panel's authority under Minn. Stat. § 65A.01 and *Quade v. Secura Insurance*, 814 N.W.2d 703 (Minn. 2012), extends to the amount of loss, including the factual question of causation. It does not extend to coverage, which is a question of law reserved to this Court. *Mork v. Eureka-Security Fire & Marine Insurance Co.*, 230 Minn. 382, 42 N.W.2d 33 (1950). To the extent the panel declined to award any window on the ground that hail-caused damage to that window is excluded or limited by a policy provision, that determination is "mere surplusage," is neither final nor binding, and the coverage question remains for this Court to decide de novo.

## 5. Strategic Note — The Flipped Vacatur Calculus (honestly, a long shot)

*Would amend `04_MotionTactics.md` § 7 (Tier 3 decision logic) and `00_Overview.md` § 4 (Tier 3 framing).*

This is not a strong independent lever, and it should not be sold as one. *Mork* vacatur is hard: the family must show fraud, misfeasance, malfeasance, or wrongdoing by clear evidence, and "the panel used the wrong causation standard" is essentially a wrong-methodology/inadequacy challenge — the kind Minnesota courts are most reluctant to entertain. So treat vacatur as a long shot on the merits regardless of branch.

What changes in the evaluated-but-rejected scenario is the **cost-benefit, not the odds.** The base files hold vacatur in reserve because it would destroy the *Quade* hail-causation preclusion — normally the family's most valuable asset. But if the remand produces a clarified award that evaluates every window and rejects all but two, that preclusion is protecting a finding that pays nothing and shields only the two windows the family already has. Its value falls toward zero. At that point the main reason to avoid vacatur is gone, and the gating question becomes whether the family's forensic-hail-damage expert can re-establish causation independently. If yes, attempting the long-shot vacatur becomes rational despite its difficulty, because the family has little left to lose.

The evidence that would support such a motion — and that is independently useful for the family's causation case and the bad-faith narrative regardless of vacatur — is:

- **The "no hail since 2020" premise is factually false.** The family's downspouts were replaced after 9/13/2021, yet Prieve photographed five dented downspouts in October 2024 (Appendix A: A-42, A-92, A-93, A-98, A-99). Those dents must post-date late 2021 — physical proof of a post-2020 hail event. (See `02_AwardedScope_vs_DocumentedDamage_Gap.md` § 3 and `01_DamageCatalog_PrieveAppendixA.md` § H.)
- **Any rejection contradicts Farmers' own engineer.** Prieve's Appendix A documents 42 distinct window-damage observations across most of the home's windows (`01_DamageCatalog_PrieveAppendixA.md` § E). A panel finding of "no hail damage" at a window where the insurer's own engineer photographed a dent is internally contradictory.

Bottom line: don't plan the case around vacatur, but recognize that a $0 full-scope rejection is the one situation where pursuing it is worth the attempt, and keep the downspout/Prieve evidence developed so the option is real if it is needed.

## 7. Post-Remand Playbook (if the clarified award comes back as a full-scope rejection)

If Tier 1 is granted, the award is remanded, and the panel returns a clarified award asserting full evaluation with only two windows awarded, proceed in this order:

1. **Test compliance with item (d) first.** A clarified award that does not complete item (d) per window has not complied with the order. Move to enforce before treating the rejection as final. A panel that cannot produce per-window reasoning has confirmed non-evaluation, which reopens Theory C.
2. **Impeach against the locked deposition record (Move 1).** A clarified award asserting full evaluation that contradicts prior sworn testimony is, under *Rymer*, admissible to show misconduct.
3. **Sort each rejection causation vs. coverage (Move 2).** Send every coverage-based rejection back to the court as *Mork* surplusage.
4. **Decide whether the flipped vacatur calculus (§ 5) is worth pursuing** — only if the expert can carry causation independently and the misconduct record is real.

## 8. Residual Risk — Stated Plainly

These moves make the evaluated-but-rejected scenario more survivable; they do not guarantee a recovery. If the panel issues a genuinely reasoned, well-documented clarified award that evaluates every window and rejects all but two on articulated causation grounds — and that award survives — the family's amount-of-loss path on those windows is largely closed, and vacatur (a long shot) becomes the main remaining lever. There is no cheap independent backstop that fixes that outcome; the awarded-window valuation/matching question is addressed in the base files (`02` § 5, `03` § 7) and is deliberately not treated as part of this scenario's defense. The mitigation that most reduces the risk is the cheapest and earliest one: **Move 1 — depose the appraisers and lock their evaluation account before moving for remand.** It tells the family which world it is in before it commits to the remand, and it is the only protection whose value is destroyed if it is gathered late.
