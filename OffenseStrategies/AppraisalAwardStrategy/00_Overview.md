**Folder purpose.** This folder houses the family's strategy for Count III of the complaints: the action to *clarify, modify, or correct* (and, as a reserved alternative, to *vacate*) the September 12, 2025 Appraisal Award entered under Minn. Stat. § 65A.01. The strategy is built around a single empirical asymmetry: **Farmers' own engineer (Prieve Engineering, File #241008) photographed and described damage at most of the home's windows and at multiple non-window components, but the appraisal panel awarded only two windows for $12,800 RCV / $6,400 ACV — leaving the family with $0 after the $17,720 deductible.** That asymmetry is the leverage. Prieve's report is, paradoxically, the family's most useful evidence for expanding the appraisal scope.

**Filing context.** This strategy is the implementation layer for paragraphs 50–55 (v1), 50–55 (v2), and 25–26 (v3) of the draft complaints. ## 1. The Problem in One Paragraph

On 9/12/2025, after the family invoked appraisal under Minn. Stat. § 65A.01, the panel — umpire Wade Roos, insured's appraiser John Foster (selected by contractor Pure Construction, not by the family), and Farmers' appraiser Greg Tomes (who did not sign) — issued an Appraisal Award covering three demanded items: Windows, Siding (repaint), and Siding (R&R). The award gave $0 to both siding items and $12,800 RCV / $6,400 ACV to "Windows (F1.7 / B1.2)." After the $17,720 windstorm/hail deductible, the net payment to the family is $0.00. Farmers has since invoked the binding-effect provision of § 65A.01 to refuse any further review, telling the family: "The claim was settled per the binding appraisal award received. The loss is under your deductible. If you need information as to why additional windows were not included in the award then you will need to contact your appraiser to obtain that information" (10/21/2025).

The award (a) does not state actual value and loss "separately to each item" as Subd. 3 requires, (b) is described inconsistently by Farmers across 9/18/2025, 9/19/2025, and 10/14/2025 emails, and (c) does not square with the photographic and engineering record of damage across most of the home's windows and at multiple non-window components — a record that Farmers' own retained engineer created.

## 2. The Opportunity in One Paragraph

**Prieve's Photo Appendix A (110 pages, 217 numbered photographs) is, on its face, the strongest factual brief in support of expanding the appraisal scope that exists in the file.** It catalogs damage observations on the front/west, south, east, and rear elevations across most of the home's windows; on downspouts at three elevations (A-42, A-91–93, A-98–99); on flashing along the deck (A-103, A-128, A-129); on siding (A-25, A-26, A-208, A-212); on trim board (A-53, A-54, A-90, A-148); and on a sliding-door window screen (A-141). The damage Prieve documented far exceeds the two-window scope the appraisal panel awarded. Because the panel's hail-causation finding is binding on Farmers under *Quade v. Secura Insurance*, 814 N.W.2d 703 (Minn. 2012), and because the award does not state actual value and loss separately to each item as § 65A.01 Subd. 3 requires, the appraisal can be remanded for clarification under *Herll v. Auto-Owners Insurance Co.*, 879 F.3d 293 (8th Cir. 2018), and *Cincinnati Insurance Co. v. Rymer Cos.*, No. 24-3356 (8th Cir. Mar. 27, 2026), or modified under § 65A.01 Subd. 3, or — in the further alternative — vacated under *Mork v. Eureka-Security Fire & Marine Insurance Co.*, 230 Minn. 382, 42 N.W.2d 33 (1950). The path Prieve's evidence supports best is remand for clarification: the panel evaluated some windows but did not itemize what it evaluated; expand the itemization and the dollar number will rise dramatically.

## 4. The Three-Tier Framework

Count III is pleaded in three tiers, each with a different remedy and a different risk profile. The tiers are presented in primary-to-alternative order — the family leads with Tier 1 and reserves Tier 3.

### Tier 1 — Remand for Clarification under *Herll* / *Rymer* (PRIMARY)

**Strength rating:** ~70/100 — the best legal hook on the present record.

**The ask.** Remand the 9/12/2025 award to the appraisal panel (or to a successor panel if any member is unavailable) for the limited purpose of (a) identifying each window or item within the scope of the panel's evaluation by location, elevation, sash count, and manufacturer stamp; (b) stating separately the actual cash value and replacement cost value attributable to each such window or item; and (c) identifying any window or item the family submitted for appraisal but the panel did not evaluate, with a brief statement of the reason.

**Why this is the lead.** *Herll v. Auto-Owners Insurance Co.*, 879 F.3d 293 (8th Cir. 2018), holds that an ambiguous Minnesota appraisal award is properly remanded to the panel for clarification. *Cincinnati Insurance Co. v. Rymer Cos.*, No. 24-3356 (8th Cir. Mar. 27, 2026), reaffirms the same posture. The 9/12/2025 award is facially ambiguous: it identifies the awarded windows only by the undefined designations "F1.7" and "B1.2"; the umpire produced no report or estimate (Farmers admitted this on 9/24/2025); Farmers' three written attempts to describe the award scope (9/18, 9/19, 10/14/2025) are mutually inconsistent. Remand preserves the panel's binding hail-causation finding under *Quade* (which is good for the family — see Tier 3 caveat below) while forcing the panel to either expand the itemization or admit it didn't evaluate the rest of the windows on Prieve's photo log.

**How Prieve's evidence supports Tier 1.** The 217-photograph Appendix A catalog is the proof that the award scope and the documented-damage scope diverge sharply. The remand asks the panel to reconcile its $12,800 number against Prieve's record. The panel either expands the itemization (the family wins) or admits it never evaluated the broader set of windows (in which case those windows are coverage questions for the court, the family wins).

### Tier 2 — Modification or Correction under § 65A.01 Subd. 3 (FIRST ALTERNATIVE)

**Strength rating:** ~45/100.

**The ask.** Modify or correct the 9/12/2025 award to comply with Minn. Stat. § 65A.01 Subd. 3's "stating separately actual value and loss to each item" requirement, and to conform the award to the evidence in the record.

**Why this is alternative-to-Tier-1.** § 65A.01 Subd. 3 provides that "an award in writing, **so itemized**, of any two when filed with this company shall determine the amount of actual value and loss." (Emphasis added.) The textual qualifier "so itemized" arguably means an award that is *not* so itemized is not binding under the statute. The 9/12/2025 award lists "Windows (F1.7 / B1.2)" against a single lump $12,800 RCV — that is not item-by-item itemization. A court asked to enforce the award has authority to conform the award to the statute. The relief is judicial modification (or correction); it does not require recalling the panel.

**How Prieve's evidence supports Tier 2.** Prieve's catalog supplies the items into which the award should be split. If the court is willing to modify, the family's brief identifies each window Prieve documented as damaged and proposes RCV/ACV figures derived from the Pella Reserve Traditional / Marvin Ultimate comparable-products workpapers Pure Construction prepared.

### Tier 3 — Vacatur under *Mork* (FURTHER ALTERNATIVE — RESERVED)

**Strength rating:** ~25/100 on the present record; potentially higher after discovery.

**The ask.** Vacate the 9/12/2025 award under *Mork v. Eureka-Security Fire & Marine Insurance Co.*, 230 Minn. 382, 42 N.W.2d 33 (1950), which authorizes vacatur on clear evidence of fraud, misfeasance, malfeasance, or wrongdoing by the panel.

**Why Tier 3 is the *last* alternative, not the first.** Vacating the award destroys the panel's binding hail-causation finding under *Quade*. The hail-causation finding is the family's most valuable single piece of evidence — it forecloses Farmers from re-litigating the six exclusions cited in the 11/4/2024 denial as to the awarded windows. Tier 3 trades that valuable preclusion for a recalculation of the dollar number. The trade is bad on the present record because (a) Tier 1 remand achieves a higher dollar number without losing the preclusion, and (b) the present record on panel misconduct — Tomes did not sign, Roos produced no methodology, Foster was selected by Pure Construction, Plaintiffs requested to speak with the panel but were never afforded any opportunity to do so on 9/12 — is suggestive but not a *Mork*-grade record yet. Pursue Tier 3 only if (i) Tier 1 and Tier 2 are denied or yield insufficient relief, and (ii) discovery (per `05_AppraisalSpecific_Discovery_Deposition.md`) produces a *Mork*-grade misconduct record.

**How Prieve's evidence supports Tier 3.** Indirectly. Prieve's record is the benchmark that exposes the panel's outcome as anomalous — a panel whose award covers ~2% of the documented-damage scope, on a record where Farmers' own engineer photographed dents at most of the windows, is not a panel doing the methodologically defensible work § 65A.01 contemplates. That contextual argument is not by itself *Mork*-grade misconduct, but it frames the inquiry for the panel-member depositions.

## 6. Key Risks and How to Mitigate

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| **Tier 3 destroys *Quade* preclusion.** Vacatur means Farmers can re-litigate the six exclusions on the F1.7 / B1.2 windows. | Plead Tier 3 last; do not file the Tier 3 motion unless Tier 1 and Tier 2 have failed *and* the dollar gain from disturbing $12,800 outweighs the loss of preclusion on those two windows. The decision will turn on whether the family's forensic-hail-damage expert can re-establish causation independently. |
| **Farmers will argue *Quade* + § 604.18 Subd. 4(b) bar this entire count.** Argument: the panel "resolved" the dispute. | Counter with the textual gate in § 65A.01 Subd. 3 — "an award **so itemized**" — and the *Herll* / *Rymer* line. The award is not itemized; nothing to enforce; not a binding resolution. Cross-reference `03_LegalFramework.md` § 4. |
| **Farmers will move to dismiss Count III on the ground that *Herll* / *Rymer* are remand-not-de-novo-review cases and the court has no jurisdiction.** | Counter: the court has equitable jurisdiction to clarify/modify an ambiguous award under Minnesota law; remand to the panel is the exercise of that jurisdiction, not a substitute for it. *Herll* and *Rymer* apply Minnesota law and confirm the remand authority. |
| **The remand panel may simply re-issue the same number with a thin itemization.** | The remand order (see `04_MotionTactics.md` § 5) should require (a) item-by-item RCV/ACV; (b) identification of any item the panel did not evaluate, with reasons; (c) for any unevaluated item, a statement of why; (d) production of the panel's worksheets. A panel that re-issues $12,800 against a thinly-itemized list while disclaiming responsibility for the rest of the windows opens those windows up to *Quade*-preclusion-free coverage litigation under Count I. |
| **Farmers' appraiser Tomes did not sign — Farmers may argue the award is not binding at all and the family's invocation of it is inconsistent.** | The two-of-three signature rule in § 65A.01 Subd. 3 explicitly authorizes a binding award on any two signatures. Tomes's non-signature is evidence for the family (he didn't agree to the panel's hail-causation finding), not against the family. |
| **The family is the one who invoked appraisal — won't a "modify/clarify/vacate" motion look like buyer's remorse?** | No. The family invoked appraisal in good faith and is entitled to the statutory protections the appraisal carries — including the Subd. 3 itemization requirement and the *Quade* preclusion. Asking the court to enforce the statute's text is not buyer's remorse. |

## 7. Sequencing With the Rest of the Case

This Count III strategy is timed to follow, not precede, the main bad-faith discovery cycle:

1. **Months 0–6 (Tranche A and B discovery)** — develop the broader claim record; the appraisal-specific discovery (per `05_AppraisalSpecific_Discovery_Deposition.md`) runs in parallel.
2. **Months 6–10 (depositions)** — Brau, Monson, Dudley, Pietila, Tomes, Foster, Roos, Prieve, Pure Construction. The Foster/Roos/Tomes depositions are the prerequisite to Tier 3 *Mork* analysis.
3. **Month 11–12** — Decision point: file Tier 1 motion. (Strategic timing: file the Tier 1 motion alongside or shortly after the § 604.18 Subd. 4(a) bad-faith motion so the court sees the institutional context.)
4. **Month 13–14** — Tier 1 hearing and ruling.
5. **Month 15–16** — If Tier 1 grants remand, the remand proceedings; if Tier 1 denies, Tier 2 motion.
6. **Month 17+** — Tier 3 only if Tier 1 and Tier 2 fail and the *Mork* record supports it.

## 8. The Single Most Important Principle

**The appraisal award is the family's evidence, not Farmers' shield.** Farmers wants to characterize the 9/12/2025 award as the end of the dispute; the family characterizes it as the start of the next phase. Every motion under this strategy treats the award as (a) confirmation of hail causation (good — preserved through Tier 1 and Tier 2), (b) a non-itemized, ambiguous document that requires further work (good — the basis for relief), and (c) a partial scope that leaves most of the documented damage unevaluated (good — those unevaluated windows are coverage questions for the court that Farmers cannot block with the appraisal-bar defense).

The strategy is therefore not adversarial to the appraisal panel; it is adversarial to Farmers' attempt to use the appraisal's incompleteness to bar the family from recovering on the rest of the damage.
