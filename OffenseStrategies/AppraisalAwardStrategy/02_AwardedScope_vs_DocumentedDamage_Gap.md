# Gap Analysis — Awarded Scope vs. Documented Damage

**Purpose.** Quantify the gap between (a) what the family asked the appraisal panel to evaluate, (b) what the panel actually awarded, and (c) what Farmers' own engineer documented as damaged. The gap is the entire leverage of Count III: the larger and more clearly defined the gap, the stronger the *Herll* / *Rymer* remand argument and the stronger the *Quade* reservation of unevaluated coverage for the court.

## 2. The Inside-Appraisal-Scope Gap (Windows and Siding)

This is the part of the gap the panel had jurisdiction over but did not resolve to the extent the record supports.

### 2.1 Windows — the gap

The family demanded "Windows" with no further qualification. The panel awarded "Windows F1.7 B1.2" at $12,800 RCV.

| Sub-frame | Count |
|---|---|
| Windows on the home (estimated from Prieve's elevation-by-elevation narrative and Appendix A) | ~30+ window units (including bay groups, casements, and single units) |
| Windows Prieve documented with damage observations (sash dents, frame dents/scrapes, drip cap dents, accessory groove dents) | **at least 19 distinct windows (plus a sliding door)** |
| Windows the appraisal panel evaluated and awarded | **2** (F1.7, B1.2) |
| Windows Prieve documented as damaged but the panel did not award | **~17 distinct window locations** |
| Windows with "no hail" findings by Prieve | ~14 window locations |

**The Tier 1 remand argument flows from line 4.** The panel either (a) evaluated and rejected the ~17 unevaluated-but-documented windows (in which case the award should say so and itemize the rejection rationale), or (b) did not evaluate them at all (in which case those windows are coverage questions for the court under *Quade*). Either answer is good for the family. The award as written does neither — it lists two windows against a lump $12,800 without any treatment of the rest.

### 2.2 Siding — the gap

> ⚠️ **REVIEW FLAG (pre-2020 siding penetrations):** The 2 siding penetrations have been reclassified as damage from a hail storm prior to 2020 and removed from this claim's counts. The argument text below (and its reliance on "2 photographed penetrations") still references them and needs your review/rework — left intact per instruction.

The family demanded "Siding (repaint)" and "Siding (R&R)" as separate items. The panel awarded $0 on both.

| Sub-frame | Count |
|---|---|
| Distinct siding penetrations Prieve documented in Appendix A | **0 for this claim** — A-26/A-27 and A-208/A-212 are attributed to a hail storm PRIOR TO 2020 and excluded |
| Distinct trim board damage observations Prieve documented | **3** (A-53 front bump-out — visible in 2020 photo log; A-90 south projection; A-148 east bay) |
| Distinct flashing damage observations Prieve documented (flashing is part of the wall/siding system at the deck interface) | **3** (A-103, A-128, A-129 — all at deck along wall base) |
| Siding amount awarded by the panel | **$0** RCV / **$0** ACV |

**A $0 award on a record containing 2 photographed penetrations, 3 trim chips (one with explicit acknowledgment that it appears in Farmers' 2020 photo log), and 3 dented flashing observations is, on its face, the output of a panel that either (a) was not given a siding repair scope of work to value, or (b) did not consider the engineer's photo record.** Either way, the result is a remand candidate.

The strategic value here is large. Under § 65A.01 Subd. 3 the panel must "appraise the loss, stating separately actual value and loss to each item." A $0 line item is *itemization*, but it is not a substantive appraisal of value if the panel did not actually evaluate the item. The Tier 1 remand should require the panel to identify (i) what siding observations it evaluated, (ii) the basis for the $0 valuation, and (iii) any siding observation it did not evaluate. The likely answer triggers either further valuation work or — if the panel concedes non-evaluation — a coverage question reserved for the court under *Quade*.

## 3. The Outside-Appraisal-Scope Gap (Downspouts, Flashing-Not-Tied-to-Siding, Doors)

This is the part of the gap the panel had **no jurisdiction over** because the family's 8/3/2025 demand listed only three items (Windows, Siding repaint, Siding R&R). Downspouts, gutters, flashing other than wall-base flashing tied to siding, and exterior doors were not in the demand. The panel could not have appraised them.

| Component | Prieve photo observations |
|---|---|
| **Dented downspouts** | A-42 (front, dent at upper edge); A-91 (south side, context); A-92 (south side, dent present); A-93 (south side, adjacent dent); A-98 (east side, dent); A-99 (east side, dent with crease) — **5 distinct dent observations across 3 elevations** |
| **Door window screen dent + scrape mark** | A-141 (sliding door east side at deck) — **1 observation** |

These observations are NOT in the appraisal award and are NOT subject to any "appraisal resolved the dispute" defense. They are reserved coverage questions for the court under Count I (breach of contract), and they are evidence Farmers cannot exclude on appraisal-bar grounds.

**Critical evidentiary point on the downspouts.** The family's gutters and downspouts were replaced after September 13, 2021 (`EventDocuments/2025-01-31-ResponseToFarmer-01-31-25-StormsAfter2020.pdf`). Any dent Prieve photographed on those components on October 9, 2024 must, by simple physical reasoning, have been produced by a hail event between September 13, 2021 and October 9, 2024 — squarely defeating Prieve's own "most recent hail in Eden Prairie reported in 2020" premise. The downspout observations are not just outside the appraisal scope; they are the strongest physical evidence in the file that the 7/13/2024 hail event impacted this specific property, regardless of what Prieve concluded.

## 4. The Itemization Failure — § 65A.01 Subd. 3

Minn. Stat. § 65A.01 Subd. 3 provides:

> "The appraisers shall then appraise the loss, **stating separately actual value and loss to each item**; and, failing to agree, shall submit their differences, only, to the umpire. An award in writing, **so itemized**, of any two when filed with this company shall determine the amount of actual value and loss."

The 9/12/2025 Appraisal Award form contains three lines:

| Line | Item | RCV | ACV |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Siding & Paint | $0 | $0 |
| 2 | Windows F1.7 B1.2 | $12,800 | $6,400 |
| 3 | Siding | $0 | $0 |

The award has three rows. The statute requires "stating separately actual value and loss to each item." The question is whether "Windows F1.7 B1.2" is **one item** stated separately, or **two items lumped together against one lump-sum number**.

The family's position must be the latter, for the following reasons:

1. **The text says "F1.7 / B1.2" — two distinct identifiers.** Farmers' own post-award descriptions treat them as two separate windows: "the windows on the front right, and the rear elevation in the dining room" (9/18/2025). Two windows are two items.
2. **The Sworn Statement on Proof of Loss stated $310,000 whole loss.** Itemizing two windows to "$12,800" without separating the value to each window is not itemization within the meaning of the statute. The insured cannot determine, from the face of the award, what value was assigned to F1.7 vs. what value was assigned to B1.2.
3. **The umpire produced no methodology or estimate.** Farmers confirmed this in writing on 9/24/2025: "Unfortunately, the umpire did not provide a report or estimate." The lack of supporting documentation reinforces that the award is not "so itemized" — there is no source document that records the per-item value the statute requires.
4. **The post-award correspondence shows the codes "F1.7" and "B1.2" are not decodable on the face of the award.** The family had to ask Farmers what F1.7 and B1.2 mean (9/18/2025 email); Farmers gave inconsistent answers (9/18, 9/19, 10/14/2025); even agent Pete Pietila had to ask Farmers for a description (10/13/2025). A statutory award the parties cannot decode without follow-up correspondence is not "so itemized" within the meaning of § 65A.01 Subd. 3.

This is the textual hook for the Tier 2 modification argument and the legal basis for Tier 1 remand.

## 5. The Quantum Gap — Why the Dollar Number Is Wrong on Any Reading

Set the legal arguments aside for a moment and consider the dollar math.

| Calculation | Number |
|---|---|
| Replacement cost per window if the panel valued F1.7 + B1.2 = $12,800 collectively | ~$6,400 per window (RCV) |
| Number of windows Prieve documented as damaged with hail-signature observations (sash dents, frame dents) | 17 |
| Implied RCV if all 17 were valued at the same per-window rate | ~$108,800 RCV |
| Less depreciation at the same ratio the panel applied ($12,800 → $6,400 = 50%) | ACV ~$54,400 |
| Less $17,720 deductible | Net to family ~$36,680 |

Even before the family adds siding repair, downspout replacement, flashing repair, door window screen replacement, exterior repaint, mold remediation, blinds R&R, and the matching-cost premium for the discontinued Senco/Semco windows (each of which has been documented in the Pure Construction file and the family's Pella Reserve workpapers), simple per-window arithmetic on Prieve's own observations puts the family above the deductible. The two-window award is not just under-itemized; it is the source of the $0 net payment.

## 6. The Three Independent Theories the Gap Supports

The same factual gap supports three independent legal theories. Count III pleads all three in the alternative; the brief should be written so each theory stands alone and can be granted without the others.

### Theory A — Remand for Clarification (Tier 1)

The award is ambiguous as to (a) the identity of F1.7 and B1.2, (b) the items the panel evaluated but did not award, and (c) the items the panel did not evaluate. *Herll* and *Rymer* authorize remand to the panel to resolve the ambiguity. The remand order should require:

- Identification of F1.7 and B1.2 by location, elevation, sash count, and manufacturer stamp.
- Per-window RCV and ACV figures.
- A list of every window or item the family submitted for appraisal that the panel did not evaluate, with the reason for non-evaluation.
- The panel's worksheets, methodology, and supporting estimates (i.e., the materials the umpire admitted he did not produce in his 9/12/2025 deliberations).

### Theory B — Statutory Non-Itemization → Modification (Tier 2)

The award is not "so itemized" within § 65A.01 Subd. 3 because:

- "Windows F1.7 B1.2" lumps two windows into one dollar number.
- The two siding lines are zero without identification of any evaluated observation, basis, or methodology.
- The codes F1.7 and B1.2 are not decodable on the face of the award.
- The umpire produced no methodology document.

The court has authority to modify or correct the award to conform to the statute. The proposed modification should itemize by window using Prieve's photo log as the inventory of items and the Pella Reserve workpapers as the valuation methodology.

### Theory C — Unevaluated Items Are Coverage Questions for the Court (Quade Reservation)

Under *Quade v. Secura Insurance*, 814 N.W.2d 703 (Minn. 2012), the appraisal panel's authority extends only to items the panel actually evaluated. Items the panel did not evaluate remain coverage questions reserved to the court — Farmers cannot use the appraisal as a bar.

The family's brief should expressly catalog (using `01_DamageCatalog_PrieveAppendixA.md` § F) the windows Prieve documented as damaged that are outside F1.7 / B1.2, and ask the court for a declaration that coverage and amount of loss for each such window remains a live coverage question for the Court under Count I and Count II. This is the *Quade* reservation theory, and it does not require disturbing the existing award.

---

## 7. The Self-Reinforcing Effect — Why the Three Theories Compound

Each of the three theories above strengthens the other two:

- If the court grants Theory A (remand), the panel must either expand the itemization (good — the dollar number rises) or admit it did not evaluate the rest (good — those windows become Quade-reserved court questions). Theory C is the fallback.
- If the court grants Theory B (modification), the court itemizes on the record using Prieve's photo log. The court is in effect doing what the panel should have done. Theory C remains available for any item the court declines to itemize.
- If the court grants only Theory C (Quade reservation) without disturbing the award, the family proceeds on Count I against the unevaluated windows on a clean record, with Prieve's Photo Appendix A as the principal evidence.

The worst-case outcome under this strategy is that the court grants only Theory C — and even that outcome unlocks the bulk of the damages because it strips Farmers of the appraisal-bar defense on the unevaluated-windows population (the 10+ windows beyond F1.7 / B1.2). The best-case outcome is that all three theories are granted, the panel re-itemizes upward, the court declares additional unevaluated-windows as coverage questions, and Count I damages proceed against the unevaluated population in addition.
