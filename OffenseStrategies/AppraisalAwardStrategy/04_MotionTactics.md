# Motion Tactics — Count III

**Purpose.** Translate the legal framework in `03_LegalFramework.md` and the factual gap in `02_AwardedScope_vs_DocumentedDamage_Gap.md` into the operational steps for drafting, sequencing, and filing the Count III motion(s). The strategy is to lead with a *Herll* / *Rymer* remand motion, hold Tier 2 modification and Tier 3 vacatur in reserve, and use the motion sequence to maximize the dollar number while preserving the *Quade* preclusion the family has already won.

**Disclaimer.** Strategic plan, not legal advice. Each draft motion should be reviewed by a Minnesota-licensed attorney before filing.

---

## 1. The Two-Step Gambit

Count III is pleaded with three tiers in the alternative. At the motion stage, the family proceeds in two sequenced steps:

**Step 1.** File a Tier 1 motion: remand for clarification under *Herll* / *Rymer*. This is the primary motion; it carries the highest strength rating (~70/100), preserves *Quade* preclusion, and frames the case to the court as "we just want the panel to do what the statute required."

**Step 2.** If Tier 1 is denied (in whole or in part), file a Tier 2 motion: modification or correction under § 65A.01 Subd. 3. This motion asks the court to itemize the award on the record using Prieve's photo catalog as the inventory and the Pure Construction / Pella Reserve workpapers as the valuation methodology. Strength rating ~45/100.

**Step 3 (held in reserve).** If both Tier 1 and Tier 2 fail, evaluate Tier 3 (*Mork* vacatur) against the loss of *Quade* preclusion. Do not file Tier 3 unless the family's forensic-hail-damage expert can re-establish causation independently and the dollar benefit of disturbing the $12,800 figure exceeds the cost of losing preclusion.

**Why the two-step gambit (rather than filing all three at once).** Filing all three tiers in a single motion forces the court to engage Tier 3 (vacatur) — which is doctrinally fragile and which destroys *Quade*. A staged sequence lets the court grant the cleanest available relief first and reserves the more invasive remedies for genuine necessity.

## 2. Pre-Motion Record-Building

Before filing the Tier 1 motion, the family should complete the following record-building steps. Each step is itself a discovery instrument; the cumulative record becomes the appendix to the Tier 1 motion.

### 2.1 Decode F1.7 and B1.2

The single most useful pre-motion record is a confirmed identification of what F1.7 and B1.2 actually are. This is the linchpin of the ambiguity argument. Sources:

- **Pure Construction's appraisal-prep file.** Contractor Pure Construction prepared the scope of work that became the appraisal demand. Their file likely contains a window-by-window inventory with the codes the panel later adopted. Subpoena under Rule 45 (see `05_AppraisalSpecific_Discovery_Deposition.md`).
- **Foster's working file.** John Foster, the insured's appraiser, must have used some code system to identify the windows. His working file (notes, sketches, photographs from the 9/12 inspection) is discoverable by Rule 45 subpoena.
- **Tomes's working file.** Farmers' appraiser, Greg Tomes, likely received the same code system. Subpoena.
- **Roos's working file.** Despite Farmers' admission that "the umpire did not provide a report or estimate," any notes Roos took during the 9/12 inspection are subpoenable. He may have used the same code system or developed his own.
- **Farmers' internal communications about the codes.** Per RFP A.12 (correspondence with the appraisal panel), Farmers may have communications discussing what F1.7 and B1.2 refer to.

If the codes turn out to refer to (for example) a building elevation key prepared by Pure Construction's "window expert," the brief should reproduce the key and show that the panel's award covers only 2 of the X windows on the key — which is the ambiguity.

### 2.2 Lock in the inconsistent-description record

Farmers' three written attempts to describe the awarded scope (9/18, 9/19, 10/14/2025) are already in the `EventDocuments/2025-09-18-PostAppraisalEmailChain.md` file and the 9/19 letter is at `EventDocuments/Appraisal Documents/2025-09-19-ResponseFromFarmers.pdf`. Compile them into a single exhibit with a side-by-side comparison. This is the textbook *Herll* "the parties cannot agree on what the award means" fact. The 9/19 correction does double duty: beyond the dining-room→family-room flip, it contains the appraiser's admission that the front **dining-room window** was documented as damaged but excluded "from the prior claim" — a false premise for 2 of its 3 sashes, developed in `08_DiningRoomWindow_PriorClaimExclusion.md`.

### 2.3 Lock in the umpire's missing methodology

Farmers' 9/24/2025 admission ("Unfortunately, the umpire did not provide a report or estimate") is already in the file. Compile as a single-page exhibit with the date and source. Add Roos's working-file Rule 45 subpoena response (or a sworn declaration of non-production) once received.

### 2.4 Compile the Prieve-vs.-award comparison

Use `01_DamageCatalog_PrieveAppendixA.md` as the comparison table. The brief should reproduce the headline totals: 42 distinct window damage observations + 5 downspouts + 3 flashing + 3 trim chips + 1 door window screen → award of 2 windows. (The 2 siding penetrations A-26/A-27 and A-208/A-212 are excluded as damage from a hail storm prior to 2020.) The visual contrast is the most persuasive single page.

### 2.5 Develop the matching cost methodology

The Cedar Bluff overlay requires the family to have a proposed RCV methodology that the panel (on remand) or the court (under Tier 2 modification) can adopt. Pure Construction has already developed the Pella Reserve Traditional / Marvin Ultimate comparable-products estimate (`EventDocuments/CommunicationWithPureConstruction/Reserve-Traditional.pdf`). This is the methodology proposed for the remand.

### 2.6 Develop the forensic-hail-damage expert report

The family's forensic-hail-damage expert (per `OffenseStrategies/Discovery/DiscoveryPlan.md` § 10.1) is engaged for the broader bad-faith case. The expert's report should include a window-by-window causation analysis that aligns with Prieve's photo observations and against Prieve's narrative conclusions. This expert report is the methodological support for the Tier 1 remand briefing.

## 4. The Tier 2 Motion (First Alternative) — Drafting Outline

### 4.1 When to File

Tier 2 is filed only if Tier 1 is denied or yields insufficient relief. "Insufficient relief" means: the court remands the award to the panel but the panel reissues the same number without expanding the itemization, or the panel formally refuses to clarify, or the panel members are unavailable for remand.

### 4.2 Brief Structure

The Tier 2 brief is structurally similar to Tier 1 but pivots from "remand" to "judicial modification or correction." Key differences:

- **Argument III is reframed.** Instead of "remand under *Herll*/*Rymer*," the argument becomes "modification or correction under § 65A.01 Subd. 3." The court has authority to conform the award to the statute's text where the panel has not done so.
- **A proposed modification is included as an exhibit.** Using Prieve's photo catalog as the inventory and the Pella Reserve / Marvin Ultimate estimate as the methodology, the brief proposes a per-window itemization that the court can adopt or modify.
- **Cedar Bluff matching is more central.** Under Tier 2 the court is doing the itemization; the brief must give the court the matching methodology to use.

### 4.3 Risk Profile

Tier 2 asks the court to perform an arithmetic the panel was supposed to perform. Courts are typically reluctant to do this — the statutory scheme contemplates appraisal panels, not judges, valuing windows. The brief must therefore emphasize that the panel has had its opportunity (or, if Tier 1 was denied, that remand was not available), and that the alternative to judicial modification is leaving an award that violates the statute's text in place.

## 5. The Proposed Remand Order — Tier 1 Specifics

The single most important drafting choice in the Tier 1 motion is the proposed order. A poorly drafted remand order produces a thin re-issued award with no expansion of itemization. A well-drafted remand order forces the panel to do the work § 65A.01 Subd. 3 requires.

The proposed remand order should require the panel to:

```
WITHIN 60 DAYS OF THIS ORDER, the appraisal panel composed of John Foster
(insured's appraiser), Greg Tomes (Defendant's appraiser), and Wade Roos
(umpire) — or any successor panel appointed pursuant to Minn. Stat.
§ 65A.01, Subd. 3 if any panel member is unavailable — shall reconvene
and SHALL:

(a) IDENTIFY each window or item within the scope of the panel's
    September 12, 2025 evaluation by:
    (i)   building elevation (north, south, east, west);
    (ii)  story / floor (basement, main, upper);
    (iii) location identifier (e.g., "front/west triple window above main
          entry"; "rear/east family-room three-sash main window");
    (iv)  sash count and sash configuration;
    (v)   manufacturer name and any visible manufacturer stamp date; and
    (vi)  for any multi-sash window, EACH SASH individually, identified by
          position and by its visible manufacturer stamp date (e.g., the
          front dining-room window's three sashes, all stamped 2008, and
          the awarded family-room window's sash stamped 2014).

(b) STATE SEPARATELY, for each window or item identified under (a) — and,
    for any multi-sash window, for EACH SASH identified under (a)(vi) —
    the Replacement Cost Value and the Actual Cash Value, with
    Replacement Cost Value calculated to include the cost of comparable
    current-production material under the policy's "comparable material
    and quality" standard.

(c) IDENTIFY each window, siding component, or other item that
    Plaintiffs submitted to the appraisal panel for evaluation and
    that the panel did not evaluate. For each such non-evaluated item,
    STATE the reason the panel did not evaluate it.

(d) PRODUCE the panel's working file, including:
    (i)   the umpire's notes and methodology document(s) (if any);
    (ii)  each appraiser's notes and methodology document(s);
    (iii) any photographs, sketches, or measurements the panel relied on;
    (iv)  any written communications among the panel members.

(e) For any window, or any sash of any window, that the panel excluded
    from the award on the ground that the damage was attributable to a
    prior insurance claim, STATE separately, sash by sash:
    (i)   the prior claim number and date relied on;
    (ii)  the specific line item(s) in the prior claim's estimate that
          the panel understood to have paid for that sash; and
    (iii) whether the panel independently evaluated that sash for damage
          from the July 13, 2024 loss, or instead treated the prior
          claim as foreclosing further evaluation.
    (This item is directed at the front dining-room window, which the
    appraiser described on September 19, 2025 as documented-but-excluded
    "from the prior claim," where the prior (2020) claim's estimate paid
    for only a subset of the window's sashes. See
    `08_DiningRoomWindow_PriorClaimExclusion.md`.)

If, in the panel's view, no responsive document exists in any
category above, the panel shall file with this Court a sworn statement
attesting to that fact.

PROVIDED, HOWEVER, that nothing in this Order shall be construed to
disturb the panel's September 12, 2025 finding of hail as the cause of
loss for the windows identified by the codes "F1.7" and "B1.2," which
finding shall remain binding on Defendant under Minn. Stat. § 65A.01,
Subd. 3 and Quade v. Secura Insurance, 814 N.W.2d 703 (Minn. 2012).
```

The proviso is critical. It tells the panel and Farmers that the *Quade* preclusion is preserved. That insulates the family from the risk that an aggressive panel response (e.g., "we now find no hail damage") could erase the only causation finding the family has on the record.

## 6. Anticipated Farmers Opposition and the Family's Reply

### 6.1 Likely Farmers oppositions to the Tier 1 motion

| Opposition | Reply |
|---|---|
| "The award is binding and not subject to judicial review." | The award is binding only if "so itemized." The award is not so itemized. *Herll* and *Rymer* both involved binding-on-its-face awards remanded for clarification. |
| "The family invoked appraisal and is bound by the outcome." | *Herll* and *Rymer* both involved invoking parties returning to court. The Eighth Circuit approved that posture. |
| "Two of three panel members signed; the award is final." | Procedural validity (two-of-three signatures) does not satisfy the substantive itemization requirement. The two issues are independent. |
| "Remand is impractical because panel members may be unavailable or Tomes refuses to participate." | The Court has authority to appoint a successor under § 65A.01 Subd. 3. Tomes's non-signature on the original award already shows his disengagement; a successor panelist for Farmers can be appointed under the statute. |
| "The codes F1.7 and B1.2 are clearly defined; the family is fabricating ambiguity." | Farmers' own three written descriptions (9/18, 9/19, 10/14/2025) are mutually inconsistent. Agent Pete Pietila had to ask for a description on 10/13/2025. Farmers itself cannot consistently describe its own binding award. |
| "Prieve's report concluded no hail damage; the panel's award merely settles a quantum dispute." | The panel awarded $12,800. Awarding $12,800 for hail-damaged windows necessarily found hail caused the loss. *Quade* makes that finding binding regardless of Farmers' position. |
| "The remand order is overbroad; the panel cannot be required to produce its working file." | The court has equitable authority to order production in aid of clarifying an ambiguous award. *Herll* and *Rymer* both involved record-development on remand. Production is the only way to determine whether the panel evaluated and rejected items (in which case the rejection rationale should be stated) or did not evaluate them (in which case those items are coverage questions for the court under *Quade*). |

### 6.2 The Daubert / methodology pre-emption

If Farmers attempts to disqualify the family's forensic-hail-damage expert under Daubert, the family's reply should pre-empt by:
- Pointing out that the Tier 1 motion does NOT depend on a battle of expert opinions; it depends on Prieve's own observations.
- Noting that Prieve himself articulated a diagnostic ("smooth or rounded dents") that aligns with the family's expert. See `OffenseStrategies/EngineerReportRebuttal/EngineerReportRebuttal.md` § 6A.
- Reserving the Daubert challenge to Prieve for a separate motion in the broader bad-faith case.

## 7. Tier 3 (Vacatur) — Detailed Decision Logic

Tier 3 is held in reserve. The decision to file it requires affirmative answers to all four of the following questions:

1. **Have Tier 1 and Tier 2 been fully attempted and denied?** Yes → proceed; No → stand down.
2. **Is the misconduct record assembled through discovery (per `05_AppraisalSpecific_Discovery_Deposition.md`) sufficient to meet the *Mork* standard?** *Mork* requires "clear evidence" of fraud, misfeasance, malfeasance, or wrongdoing. Suggestive irregularities (e.g., umpire missing methodology, Foster selected by contractor, family excluded from inspection) are not by themselves enough. Affirmative misconduct evidence is required.
3. **Does the family's forensic-hail-damage expert support re-establishing causation if *Quade* preclusion is lost?** If yes → the *Quade* loss is recoverable; proceed. If no → stand down.
4. **Does the family's dollar gain from disturbing the $12,800 figure exceed the cost of losing preclusion?** Quantify both sides: the gain (anticipated re-award after vacatur, post-vacatur recovery of additional windows the panel didn't evaluate) vs. the cost (losing the binding causation finding on F1.7 / B1.2 and the resulting need to prove causation independently in trial).

If all four answers are yes, the Tier 3 motion may be filed. If any answer is no, do not file Tier 3 — proceed on Count I against the unevaluated-windows population using the *Quade* reservation theory only.

## 9. Practical Pro Se / Limited-Counsel Adaptations

If the family proceeds without counsel (or with limited-scope counsel), the Tier 1 motion is the most achievable of the three because:

- The legal authorities are cleanly cited and well-precedented.
- The factual record is largely contained in documents the family already has.
- The proposed order is a clean drafting exercise.
- The remedy (remand) does not require the court to itemize on the record.

Adaptations for pro se filing:

- **Use the Self Help Center.** The family has already used the Hennepin Government Center Self Help Center (per `EventDocuments/Copy of ComunicationLogs.docx.md` 6/17/2025). Return for assistance with motion form, page limits, and local-rules compliance.
- **Engage limited-scope counsel for the brief.** A Minnesota-licensed attorney can draft the brief at a discrete-task fee that is much lower than full-case representation. The MN Lawyer Referral and Information Service may be able to identify such counsel.
- **Engage the forensic-hail-damage expert for a focused declaration.** The expert's full report is expensive ($30,000+); a focused declaration supporting only the Tier 1 motion's gap analysis is much less expensive (~$5,000) and is sufficient for the motion.
- **Use the Pure Construction file as in-record evidence.** Pure Construction prepared the appraisal scope; their file is already in the family's possession through `EventDocuments/CommunicationWithPureConstruction/`. Authenticate via Yibiao Lu's affidavit.

## 10. The Single Best Defensive Statement

If forced to characterize the motion in one sentence to a skeptical judge, the right framing is:

> "Your Honor, we are not asking the Court to second-guess the appraisal panel. We are asking the Court to remand to the panel so the panel can do what Minnesota law requires — state separately the actual value and loss to each item — and so this Court can then determine the coverage and amount of loss for the windows the panel never evaluated. That posture preserves the panel's hail-causation finding under *Quade*, it preserves the binding effect of the award under § 65A.01 Subd. 3 on the items it actually itemizes, and it complies with the Eighth Circuit's *Herll* and *Rymer* decisions, which expressly authorize remand on this kind of ambiguous-award record."

That sentence is the framing for the oral-argument opening and for the introduction of the brief.
