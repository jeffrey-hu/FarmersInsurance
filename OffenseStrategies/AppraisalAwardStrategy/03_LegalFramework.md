## 1. The Governing Statute — Minn. Stat. § 65A.01, Subd. 3

The Minnesota Standard Fire Insurance Policy is incorporated into homeowners policies under § 65A.01. Subd. 3 contains the appraisal clause:

> "In case the insured and this company, except in case of total loss on buildings, shall fail to agree as to the actual cash value or the amount of loss, then, on the written demand of either, each shall select a **competent and disinterested appraiser** and notify the other of the appraiser selected within **20 days** of such demand. In case either fails to select an appraiser within the time provided, then a presiding judge of the district court of the county wherein the loss occurs may appoint such appraiser for such party upon application of the other party in writing by giving five days' notice thereof in writing to the party failing to appoint. The appraisers shall first select a **competent and disinterested umpire**; and failing for **15 days** to agree upon such umpire, then a presiding judge of the above mentioned court may appoint such an umpire upon application of party in writing by giving five days' notice thereof in writing to the other party. The appraisers shall then **appraise the loss, stating separately actual value and loss to each item**; and, failing to agree, shall submit their differences, only, to the umpire. An award in writing, **so itemized**, of any two when filed with this company shall determine the amount of actual value and loss. Each appraiser shall be paid by the selecting party, or the party for whom selected, and the expense of the appraisal and umpire shall be paid by the parties equally."

(Reproduced from `EventDocuments/Appraisal Documents/AppraisalDemand.pdf` p. 4.)

**Four operative requirements** the family will press against the 9/12/2025 award:

1. **Competent and disinterested appraiser** — each party selects.
2. **Competent and disinterested umpire** — the appraisers select.
3. **Appraise the loss, stating separately actual value and loss to each item.**
4. **An award in writing, so itemized, of any two when filed with the company shall determine the amount of actual value and loss.**

The fourth requirement is the textual gate: an award that is NOT "so itemized" is, by the statute's own terms, not a binding determination of "the amount of actual value and loss." That is the foundation of the Tier 2 modification argument and the textual hook for the Tier 1 remand argument.

### 1.1 What "stating separately actual value and loss to each item" means

The statute uses two terms with distinct meanings:
- **Actual value** = Actual Cash Value (ACV) = the depreciated value at the time of loss.
- **Loss** = Replacement Cost Value (RCV) = the cost to repair or replace with materials of like kind and quality.

The award must state both, separately, *to each item*. Listing "Windows F1.7 B1.2" at $12,800 RCV / $6,400 ACV does not meet this standard if F1.7 and B1.2 are two distinct items — and Farmers' own post-award descriptions treat them as two distinct windows (front-right single sash + rear-elevation three-sash). The Tier 2 brief should put this question squarely to the court.

### 1.2 The "competent and disinterested" requirement — Foster

The family's appraiser was John Foster, retained through Pure Construction at $2,200 (Plaintiffs paid half). The family did not interview or select Foster; Pure Construction's contact Zach Sexe selected him and presented the retainer (`EventDocuments/Appraisal Documents/AppraisalDemand.pdf` p. 2; family's account in `CaseContext/key-facts.md` §11). The family later learned Foster previously worked for insurance companies (`OffenseStrategies/KeyFacts/key-facts-excluding-legal-context.md` Phase 7 Fact #9).

"Disinterested" within the meaning of § 65A.01 Subd. 3 means free from the interests of either party to the dispute. An appraiser selected by a contractor (rather than by the insured) and with a prior insurance-industry background is not a textbook "disinterested" appraiser on the insured side. The Tier 3 *Mork* analysis will revisit this.

### 1.3 The "competent and disinterested umpire" requirement — Roos

The umpire produced no report, no estimate, and no methodology document (Farmers admitted in writing on 9/24/2025: "Unfortunately, the umpire did not provide a report or estimate"). The absence of any methodological record is itself a competence question. A competent and disinterested umpire who appraises $12,800 worth of windows leaves a working file. Roos did not.

## 2. *Quade v. Secura Insurance* — The Binding-Effect Doctrine

**Citation.** *Quade v. Secura Ins.*, 814 N.W.2d 703 (Minn. 2012).

**Holding.** A binding appraisal award under § 65A.01 includes a binding finding on the cause of the loss for items the panel actually evaluated and awarded. The insurer may not, in a later coverage dispute, re-litigate causation as to the awarded items.

**The two-edged nature of *Quade*.** *Quade* is good for the family on the F1.7 / B1.2 windows because the panel's award implicitly finds hail as the cause; Farmers cannot retreat to the six exclusions cited in the 11/4/2024 denial as to those windows. But *Quade*'s preclusive effect extends only to **items the panel actually evaluated and awarded**. For items the panel did not evaluate, the coverage question remains for the court.

**Application to the gap analysis.** Per `01_DamageCatalog_PrieveAppendixA.md` § F:
- The panel evaluated and awarded F1.7 and B1.2 → causation finding binds Farmers on those two windows.
- The panel did not evaluate the other 17 hail-signature window locations Prieve documented → coverage and amount of loss for those windows are reserved court questions under Count I.
- The panel had no jurisdiction over downspouts, flashing (apart from siding-system flashing), and exterior doors → those are reserved court questions under Count I.
- The panel awarded $0 on siding without evident evaluation of the 2 photographed penetrations and 3 trim chips → at minimum, the basis for the $0 should be remanded for clarification under *Herll* / *Rymer*.

**The Quade-vs.-vacatur tension.** Because *Quade* preclusion is the family's most valuable single piece of doctrine, the Tier 3 vacatur motion (which would destroy preclusion) is the last alternative, not the first. (See `00_Overview.md` § 6 and `04_MotionTactics.md` § 4.3.)

## 3. *Mork v. Eureka-Security Fire & Marine Ins. Co.* — Vacatur Standard

**Citation.** *Mork v. Eureka-Security Fire & Marine Ins. Co.*, 230 Minn. 382, 42 N.W.2d 33 (1950).

**Holding.** A Minnesota appraisal award may be vacated on clear evidence of fraud, misfeasance, malfeasance, or other wrongdoing on the part of the panel members. *Mork* is the common-law vacatur standard for Minnesota appraisal awards.

**Application — what the present record supports, what it does not.** Vacatur on the present record is a Tier 3 reserve, not a primary ask. The record contains:

- ✓ Umpire produced no report, estimate, or methodology (Farmers' 9/24/2025 admission). Suggests methodological inadequacy.
- ✓ Farmers' appraiser (Tomes) did not sign — indicating disagreement with the panel's hail-causation finding.
- ✓ Foster (insured's appraiser) was selected by contractor Pure Construction, not by the family, and has a prior insurance-industry background.
- ✓ Plaintiffs requested to communicate with the panel but were never afforded any opportunity to do so on 9/12/2025 — Pure Construction's Zach Sexe told the family there was no need to speak with the appraisers and that he and Jade would handle everything, and the family had no contact with Foster, Tomes, or Roos.
- ✗ No direct evidence yet of fraud, collusion, or financial misconduct.

To upgrade the record to a *Mork*-grade vacatur case, the family needs discovery into Foster's and Roos's prior engagements, the financial relationships between Foster / Pure Construction / Farmers, the umpire's working file (or absence thereof), and any communications among the three panelists. (See `05_AppraisalSpecific_Discovery_Deposition.md`.)

## 4. *Herll v. Auto-Owners Ins. Co.* — Remand for Clarification

**Citation.** *Herll v. Auto-Owners Ins. Co.*, 879 F.3d 293 (8th Cir. 2018) (applying Minnesota law).

**Holding.** When a Minnesota appraisal award is ambiguous as to its scope, calculation, or methodology, the appropriate remedy is remand to the panel for clarification. The court does not substitute its own judgment for the panel's; it asks the panel to clarify its own award.

**Why *Herll* is the centerpiece of Count III.** *Herll* gives the family a remedy that preserves the *Quade* preclusion on the awarded items while forcing the panel to do the itemization the statute requires. It is the strongest doctrinal hook on the present record because:

1. The 9/12/2025 award is facially ambiguous (the F1.7 / B1.2 codes are not decodable on the face of the award).
2. The umpire produced no methodology document (Farmers' 9/24/2025 admission).
3. Farmers' three written attempts to describe the awarded scope (9/18, 9/19, 10/14/2025) are mutually inconsistent.
4. The post-award correspondence shows the parties cannot agree on what the award means even now.

**Application to the gap.** The remand motion should ask the court to remand the award to the panel for:
- Identification of F1.7 and B1.2 by location, elevation, sash count, and manufacturer stamp.
- Per-window RCV and ACV.
- A list of each window or item the family submitted that the panel did not evaluate, with reasons.
- The panel's working file, estimates, and methodology documents (or a sworn statement that none exist).

A panel asked these questions either expands the itemization (the family wins on dollars) or admits non-evaluation (the family wins on *Quade* reservation). Either outcome unlocks recovery.

## 5. *Cincinnati Insurance Co. v. Rymer Cos.* — Reaffirmation of Remand Authority

**Citation.** *Cincinnati Ins. Co. v. Rymer Cos.*, No. 24-3356 (8th Cir. Mar. 27, 2026).

**Holding.** Reaffirms the *Herll* approach: an ambiguous Minnesota appraisal award is properly remanded to the panel for clarification rather than vacated. *Rymer* applies *Herll* to a similar fact pattern of insurer–insured disagreement about the scope of an award and confirms that the federal courts applying Minnesota law treat remand as the default remedy for ambiguity.

**Why *Rymer* matters here.** *Rymer* is recent (2026) and on point. It addresses the recurring pattern Farmers is using in this case — invoking the appraisal's binding effect to refuse further discussion — and confirms that an insured can return to court to clarify the scope without forfeiting the underlying award. The Tier 1 brief should lead with *Herll* + *Rymer* together as a pair.

## 6. *Oliver v. State Farm Fire & Cas. Ins. Co.* — § 65A.01 Is Not UAA Arbitration

**Citation.** *Oliver v. State Farm Fire & Cas. Ins. Co.*, 939 N.W.2d 749 (Minn. 2020).

**Holding.** Appraisal under Minn. Stat. § 65A.01 is not arbitration under the Minnesota Uniform Arbitration Act (UAA). The common-law standard articulated in *Mork* — not the narrower vacatur grounds of the UAA — governs vacatur of an appraisal award.

**Why *Oliver* matters here.** Farmers may argue that the appraisal award is unreviewable except on the narrow UAA grounds (which do not include ambiguity or non-itemization). *Oliver* forecloses that argument. The award is subject to the common-law standard of *Mork* and to the statutory text of § 65A.01 Subd. 3. Both authorize the relief Count III seeks.

**Strategic use.** Cite *Oliver* in the Tier 1 brief preemptively to dispose of the "UAA-only review" argument before Farmers raises it.

## 7. *Cedar Bluff Townhome Condominium Ass'n v. American Family Mut. Ins. Co.* — Matching Doctrine

**Citation.** *Cedar Bluff Townhome Condo. Ass'n v. Am. Family Mut. Ins. Co.*, 857 N.W.2d 290 (Minn. 2014).

**Holding.** When an insurance policy provides for replacement-cost coverage and the damaged property cannot be replaced with material of "like kind and quality" without sourcing matching material for adjacent undamaged property, the matching cost is part of the replacement cost the insurer must pay. *Cedar Bluff* governs the matching question.

**Application to Count III.** The F1.7 / B1.2 windows are stamped Senco/Semco with manufacturer dates of 1993, 1997, and 2008 (per Farmers' 9/18/2025 email; cf. the family's account that Semco Windows and Doors ceased operations in 2019). If the awarded windows cannot be sourced from the original manufacturer, the matching cost for visually consistent replacement using the closest current-production equivalent (Pella Reserve Traditional or Marvin Ultimate per `EventDocuments/CommunicationWithPureConstruction/Reserve-Traditional.pdf`) is part of the replacement cost.

The Tier 1 remand should expressly include matching-cost methodology in the items the panel must address on remand. Endorsement J6944 ("Limited Matching of Undamaged Property") is in play — but J6944 by its own terms applies only to "property which is not damaged by the covered peril," and the awarded windows ARE damaged property. See the matching-doctrine treatment in `LegalReferences/` and in Count II declaration (e) of the draft complaints.

Putting the authorities together, the strongest single sentence for the brief is:

Auto-Owners Insurance Co.*, 879 F.3d 293 (8th Cir. 2018), and *Cincinnati Insurance Co. v. Rymer Cos.*, No. 24-3356 (8th Cir. That sentence frames the lead motion. The body of the brief works through the Prieve catalog to demonstrate the ambiguity, the non-itemization, and the unevaluated-items gap.

## 10. Anticipated Farmers Counter-Arguments and the Family's Responses

| Farmers argument | Family response |
|---|---|
| "The appraisal award is binding under § 65A.01 Subd. 3; the family can't challenge it." | The statute makes binding only an award that is "so itemized." The 9/12/2025 award is not so itemized. The textual gate is unmet; the binding effect is unmet. |
| "The family invoked appraisal in good faith and cannot now disclaim the result." | The family is not disclaiming the result; the family is asking the court to enforce the statute's text. *Herll* and *Rymer* both involved insureds (or insurers) who invoked appraisal and then returned to court to clarify the resulting award. The Eighth Circuit approved that posture in both cases. |
| "The 'binding effect' provision of § 65A.01 Subd. 3 forecloses both Tier 1 (remand) and Tier 2 (modification)." | The binding effect attaches only to an award "so itemized." If the award is not so itemized, the binding-effect clause is not triggered, and the court has authority to remand or modify. If the award IS so itemized, then it is binding on what it explicitly itemizes (Windows F1.7 + B1.2 at $12,800 RCV / $6,400 ACV) but does not extend to items not addressed — which are reserved for the court under *Quade*. Either path, the family wins. |
| "Tomes's non-signature is not relevant; two-of-three signed the award." | True — two-of-three signatures make the award procedurally valid under § 65A.01 Subd. 3. Tomes's non-signature is offered not for procedural invalidity but as evidentiary support: Farmers' own appraiser declined to certify the panel's hail-causation finding. |
| "Appraisal awards are reviewed only under the UAA standard, which does not include ambiguity or non-itemization." | *Oliver v. State Farm*, 939 N.W.2d 749 (Minn. 2020), forecloses this argument. Appraisal under § 65A.01 is not UAA arbitration. The common-law *Mork* standard applies for vacatur, and the statutory text of § 65A.01 Subd. 3 governs itemization and binding effect. |
| "The court should give deference to the panel's expertise; remand is improper." | *Herll* and *Rymer* expressly authorize remand. Remand IS the deference posture — the court is not substituting its judgment; it is returning the matter to the panel to do what the statute requires. |
| "The award contains the codes F1.7 and B1.2 — that is itemization." | Two codes against one dollar number is not item-by-item itemization. The face of the award gives only one dollar figure for two distinct windows. Farmers' own subsequent attempts to describe the windows confirm they are two distinct windows. Lumping them into one number violates "stating separately ... to each item." |
| "The umpire's lack of a methodology document is not a basis for relief." | *Herll* and *Rymer* both list "absence of methodological record" as a factor supporting remand for clarification. The umpire's missing methodology is, in combination with Farmers' own 9/24/2025 admission and the unitemized award face, the textbook *Herll* fact pattern. |

## 11. The Authorities Discussion Should Anchor in This Sequence

When the Tier 1 brief is drafted, the legal section should march in this order:

1. **The statute** — § 65A.01 Subd. 3 in full, with the "so itemized" gate flagged.
2. **The Quade framework** — appraisal panels appraise; courts decide coverage. The two are complementary.
3. **The Herll / Rymer line** — ambiguity remedied by remand. Lead authority.
4. **The Oliver clarification** — common-law standard, not UAA.
5. **The Mork standard** — vacatur reserved; the family is NOT seeking vacatur at Tier 1.
6. **The Cedar Bluff overlay** — matching cost is part of replacement cost; this is part of the items the panel must address on remand.
7. **The factual application** — Prieve's Appendix A as the demonstration of (i) ambiguity, (ii) non-itemization, and (iii) unevaluated items.
