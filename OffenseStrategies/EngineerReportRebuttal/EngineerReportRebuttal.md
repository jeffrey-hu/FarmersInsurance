**Subject report:** "Evaluation of Condition of Windows as Relates to Hail," File #241008, by Nathan E. Prieve, P.E. (License 46203), dated November 4, 2024. The report is attached to the 11/4/2024 Farmers denial letter and is referenced throughout as "the Prieve Report."

**Source PDFs in the project record:**
- `EventDocuments/FarmersDenyLetter-11-4.pdf` — 11/4/2024 Farmers denial letter, Coverage Outcome page, and Prieve narrative report (pp. 4–10 of the combined PDF / pp. 1–7 of Prieve's report).
- `EventDocuments/FarmersDenyLetter-11-4-AppendixA.pdf` — 110-page Photo Appendix A, captions A-1 through A-217.

**Purpose of this document.** The Prieve Report is Farmers' principal substantive justification for the 11/4/2024 denial and its post-appraisal continuing denial posture. This rebuttal catalogs the report's deficiencies in eight independent categories — scope limitation, omitted observations, self-contradictions, contradictions with Farmers' own 2020 file, contradictions with the public record, methodology gaps, biased framing, and reliance gaps — and identifies how each deficiency should be used in (a) the complaint, (b) discovery, and (c) the deposition of Nathan Prieve and the Farmers personnel who retained and adopted his report.

> **Practical note.** Every Prieve assertion called out below is sourced to a specific page of the 11/4 denial-letter PDF or a specific photo number in Appendix A. Where a photo's caption documents damage that the narrative omits, the caption is the impeachment exhibit — Prieve's own client-paid photographer observed it and labeled it.

## 1. Scope Limitation — Prieve Was Not Asked to Evaluate Siding, Downspouts, Flashing, or Doors

### 1.1 What Prieve says about scope

> "Our oﬃce was retained to evaluate the **condition of windows in the home as relates to hail** and to provide recommendations for repairs for purposes of valuation."
>
> "This project scope is limited to the specific claim and/or incident at the subject structure. … All other aspects of the subject structure and property are excluded from this evaluation and report."
>
> — `EventDocuments/FarmersDenyLetter-11-4.pdf` p. 5 (Prieve Report p. 2, BACKGROUND section).

### 1.2 What the family actually reported to Farmers

The family's reported scope was never limited to windows. The record shows the family reported damages to **windows, sidings, and downspouts** — and the Demand for Appraisal listed three items in dispute (Windows, Siding repaint, Siding R&R), not one. *(Sources: `EventDocuments/Copy of ComunicationLogs.docx.md`; `EventDocuments/Appraisal Documents/AppraisalDemand.pdf` p. 1.)*

### 1.3 Why the mismatch matters

- **Farmers framed the engineering question narrowly.** By scoping Prieve to "windows only," Farmers ensured that all observed damage to non-window elements (siding, downspouts, flashing, doors) would fall outside the four corners of Prieve's report and therefore be unaddressed in the denial. This is the engineer-report version of the "engineer's report only" gatekeeping rule documented elsewhere in the case (Monson 2/3/2025; Brau 2/10/2025 voicemail) — Farmers controls what gets in the door by controlling what the engineer is asked.
- **The narrow scope itself is evidence of pretext.** A first-party hail claim involving water intrusion through windows would normally call for an evaluation of the entire wall section around the windows (siding, flashing, drip cap, downspout) because hail damage to those adjacent components is part of the same causation question. Limiting the engineer to "windows only" is an investigation choice, not a methodological constraint.
- **The scope limitation makes Prieve's denial-supporting conclusions affirmatively misleading.** The report's Conclusion 4 (east-siding penetration not from hail) is itself out of scope under the report's stated boundary — Prieve had no contractual mandate to opine on siding — yet Farmers used Conclusion 4 to refuse the siding claim. Either the scope was actually broader than stated (and Farmers should produce the broader engagement letter) or Prieve exceeded his scope and Farmers adopted an unauthorized opinion.

### 1.4 What discovery and deposition should establish

- The actual engagement letter, statement of work, fee schedule, and any scope amendments between Farmers and Prieve. *(DiscoveryPlan.md RFP A.5, A.6.)*
- Who at Farmers decided to scope the engagement to "windows only" and the date that decision was made.
- Whether the scope was narrowed after the family reported siding/downspout damage to Farmers.
- Whether Prieve was instructed to ignore non-window damage observed in the field.
- Whether Farmers asked Prieve to expand scope after the family submitted siding-damage photos through Pure Construction in July 2025.

## 3. Self-Contradictions Within the Prieve Report

The Prieve Report contains three internal contradictions that, standing alone, defeat the report's reliability as a basis for denial.

### 3.1 "No recent hail damage" vs. "Convective storm activity and hail have been reported on multiple occasions"

- **Background section (p. 5 / Report p. 2):** "Convective storm activity and hail have been reported on multiple occasions in the area of the subject structure."
- **Conclusion 1 (p. 10 / Report p. 7):** "It is our opinion that no recent hail damage has occurred to windows in the subject structure related to any recent hail or other storm activity."

If hail has been reported "on multiple occasions in the area" — a statement Prieve makes in his own report — the categorical claim that "no recent hail damage has occurred" is logically inconsistent unless Prieve also asserts (a) the multiple-occasion hail was insufficient in size to damage these specific windows, or (b) the multiple-occasion hail did not actually impact the subject property. Prieve makes neither showing.

### 3.2 "Storms move west-to-east, hail from westerly direction" vs. "Convective storm activity on multiple occasions"

- **Background section (p. 5):** "Convective storm activity and hail have been reported on multiple occasions in the area of the subject structure." Multiple convective storm occasions, by ordinary meteorological understanding, do not all move in one direction.
- **Conclusion 4 (p. 10):** "Recent storm activity in the area generally consists of storms moving from west to east with hail originating from a westerly direction."

The two statements are inconsistent: if storms occur "on multiple occasions," they statistically include rotating supercells, north-to-south frontal passages, and overnight squall lines (like the 7/13–7/14/2024 event with a 1:00 AM 48-MPH gust at Eden Prairie ASOS). The "generally west-to-east" gloss in Conclusion 4 is then used to dismiss east-facing damage without any analysis of the specific 7/13–7/14/2024 storm. And the generalization is squarely contradicted by Farmers' own appraisal award, which awarded a **rear/east** window (B1.2, family room) alongside a **front/west** window (F1.7) — confirming hail damage on the east elevation notwithstanding the supposed westerly origin (see § 5.3).

### 3.3 "No recent hail damage" vs. the photo catalog of dents in Prieve's own appendix

- **Conclusion 1 (p. 10):** "no recent hail damage has occurred to windows in the subject structure."
- **Prieve's own narrative and photos:** Dents and indentations documented at the front/west triple window above the main entry (A-38–41), the upper-west 3-unit bedroom window (A-45), the upper southwest bedroom window (A-64), the east family-room bay projection (A-107, A-113, A-115–117, A-121), the upper-east window above the four-season porch (A-163), the four-season porch SE/E/NE/N windows (A-165, A-166, A-174, A-179), the main-level NE 3-unit window (A-199, A-201), and others. Page 8 of the 11/4 denial PDF alone (corresponding to Prieve Report p. 5) contains **16 separate mentions** of the word "dent" or "dents."

A report whose narrative mentions "dent" or "dents" 16 times on a single page cannot logically conclude "no recent hail damage" without explaining (a) what non-hail mechanism produced those dents, (b) why the mechanism happens to repeat itself at the same window locations the 2020 hail event damaged, and (c) why no contemporary non-hail event (vandalism, construction, contractor activity) is documented in the family's record or in the neighborhood permit data. Prieve makes none of those showings. The "scrape mark inside dent → blunt object other than hail" theory is asserted as an undefended methodological premise, not derived from the evidence.

### 3.4 What discovery and deposition should establish

- Prieve's understanding of the "convective storm activity on multiple occasions" statement in his own report — when, where, what sizes of hail.
- Prieve's reconciliation of his Background statement with Conclusion 1.
- The basis for Conclusion 4's "generally west-to-east" generalization with respect to the specific 7/13–7/14/2024 storm.
- The specific non-hail causation Prieve attributes to each of the 16+ "dent" observations and the evidence supporting each attribution.

## 4. Contradictions With Farmers' Own 2020 Claim File

The Prieve Report's "2020 already paid" theory rests on factual statements that are contradicted by Farmers' own 2020 Xactimate scope.

### 4.1 "2 windows" vs. the 2020 file's actual scope

Prieve Conclusion 1 (p. 10): "We note that prior claim estimates and information include **2 windows** in the home noted for potential hail damage and replacement including the main level window to the left of the main entry and north facing window at the rear four season porch. … Given these 2 windows have already been included and we understand paid for due to prior storm activity, no additional payments in our opinion are appropriate for the 2 windows."

The 2020 Settlement Notice and Xactimate (`EventDocuments/FinalDocument_2020.pdf`, line items 1–4) show what the 2020 claim actually approved:

| Line | Description | Quantity |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | R&R Wood window — casement, 12–23 sf | 2.00 EA |
| 2 | Window blind — horizontal or vertical — Detach & reset | 2.00 EA |
| 3 | Additional charge for a retrofit window, 24–40 sf | 2.00 EA |
| 4 | Scaffolding Setup & Take down — per hour | 11.00 HR |

The line item is a casement window replacement quantity of 2. The family's contemporaneous account (Phase 1, Fact #2 of `OffenseStrategies/KeyFacts/key-facts-excluding-legal-context.md`) records that the 2020 dents were on **sashes**, not whole windows, and the 2020 settlement covered two sashes. The Prieve report treats the 2020 scope as two **whole windows** to support the conclusion that any further work on those same windows is duplicative.

### 4.2 The 3-sash window with dents on all three sashes

The family's 2/9/2025 second response to Farmers states: "The only dents found in 2020 were for 2 sashes as shown at the inspection report from 2020 hail claim" (`EventDocuments/2025-02-10-SecondResponseToFinalReport.pdf`). The current Prieve report observes dents to all three sashes of one of the windows (cf. the triple window at the front/west, A-38–41). On the family's read, two of those three sashes were dented in 2020 and paid for as such; the third sash dent — and any new dents on the same sashes — are post-2020 damage that Prieve's "already paid" theory cannot account for under Farmers' own 2020 record.

### 4.3 Damages Farmers' own 2020 photo log showed but the 2020 estimate did not cover

The Prieve Report itself documents this point. At p. 7 (Prieve Report p. 4), discussing A-53: **"A chip appears in the trim board along the left side of the window bump-out as shown in photograph A-53. We note this chip is visible in prior photo logs from Farmers Insurance dated October 13, 2020."**

So Prieve found a chip in trim that was photographed in Farmers' own 2020 inspection — yet that chip was not addressed in the 2020 Xactimate (which covered only the 2 casement windows). Two possibilities, both bad for Farmers:

- **Option A:** The trim damage existed in 2020, Farmers' own photographer documented it, and Farmers underpaid the 2020 claim by failing to include it. This is direct evidence of a pattern of underpayment that pre-dates the 7/13/2024 storm.
- **Option B:** The "same" chip is actually new damage that resembles the 2020 photo only superficially, in which case Prieve has overstated the temporal continuity of the damage. Either way, the inference Prieve draws — "this was already in 2020, so not new hail damage" — is unsupported.

## 5. Factual Errors Disproven by the Public Record

The Prieve Report's foundational empirical premise is disprovable on the face of public sources Prieve did not consult.

### 5.1 "Most recent hail in Eden Prairie reported in 2020"

Prieve Weather Records section (p. 9 / Report p. 6): "Similar past estimated hail appears in a summary from the NOAA Storm Events Database with most recent hail in Eden Prairie reported in 2020."

This single sentence carries Prieve's entire causation framework. It is contradicted by:

- **The National Weather Service Twin Cities/Chanhassen office report** documenting severe thunderstorms across Minnesota on 7/13–7/14/2024 with hail up to 3.5" in east-central Minnesota and up to 4" near Monticello/Big Lake. Eden Prairie Airport (ASOS) recorded a **48 MPH wind gust at 1:00 AM on 7/14**, with Eden Prairie expressly named in the NWS wind-gust table. *(`Evidence/Significant Wind and Hail in Minnesota July 13-14.pdf`.)*
- **Interactive Hail Maps and stormersite data** showing hail reports in or near Eden Prairie in 2022, 2023, and 2024. *(`EventDocuments/2025-01-31-ResponseToFarmer-01-31-25-StormsAfter2020.pdf`.)*
- **City of Eden Prairie building-permit records** for post-2020 exterior repairs — including permit EP187829 at 17490 George Moran Dr (window replacement, issued 11/15/2023) and permit EP190835 at 17708 George Moran Dr (window replacement, issued 5/9/2024). The permits record work type, address, contractor, and dates and state no cause; the hail causation of this work was confirmed by the affected homeowners in the family's neighbor canvass. *(`EventDocuments/2025-02-10-SecondResponseToFinalReport.pdf`.)*
- **The family's 14-of-19 neighbor canvass** confirming hail-driven repairs in 2023 and 2024 in the immediate neighborhood. *(Same source.)*
- **Farmers' own 9/12/2025 appraisal award** confirming hail as the cause of loss on the same property — entered ten months after Prieve concluded "no recent hail damage has occurred." *(`EventDocuments/Appraisal Documents/Appraisal_Result_Front.png`.)*

Prieve's premise is wrong on the face of independent public sources. The premise also fails on a self-check: Prieve's own Background section says hail "has been reported on multiple occasions in the area of the subject structure." A 2020 hail event is not "multiple occasions" of hail; the only way both statements are simultaneously true is if "multiple occasions" includes events Prieve elsewhere denies occurred.

### 5.2 "Hail at the site was not of sufficient size to dent window frames or the face of sashes"

Prieve Conclusion 2 (p. 10): "Further, it is our opinion that past hail at the site was not of suﬃcient size to dent window frames or the face of sashes."

This is a quantitative claim that requires a quantitative basis:

- A specific hail-size threshold for denting extruded aluminum window frames.
- A specific hail-size threshold for denting the face of sashes (which can be wood, vinyl-clad, or aluminum-clad depending on the window unit).
- The terminal velocity of hailstones at those threshold sizes.
- Material-specific dent-depth modeling tied to the actual material composition of the windows in question.
- The maximum recorded hail size at the subject property's exact location across the relevant period.

The Prieve Report contains none of these. The Weather Records section (p. 9) lists Eden Prairie-area hail at "1.25" on June 19, 2015, 1.75" on July 25, 2017, 1" hail on September 6, 2020, and multiple reports on August 9, 2020 ranging from 1 to 2 inches." 2-inch hail is well above the conventional threshold for denting aluminum and aluminum-clad wood windows — there are peer-reviewed materials on this point (see `HailShapes/DocumentIndex.md`). Prieve's no-quantitative-analysis conclusion that 2-inch hail at this property is insufficient to dent the windows in question is not supported by any methodology shown in the report.

### 5.3 The west-origin directionality fallacy

Prieve Conclusion 4 (p. 10): "Recent storm activity in the area generally consists of storms moving from west to east with hail originating from a westerly direction. The penetration in siding appears at the east side, opposite typical origination of hail in recent storm activity in the area. No repairs in our opinion are warranted to siding as relates to hail."

This is the single weakest analytical statement in the report:

- **Farmers' own binding appraisal award refutes the premise.** The 9/12/2025 appraisal awarded two windows on opposite elevations of the home — **F1.7 on the front (west) elevation and B1.2 in the rear (east) family room.** By awarding the rear/east B1.2 window as a hail loss, Farmers' own appraisal panel confirmed hail damage on the **east** side of the property. Under *Quade v. Secura Ins.*, 814 N.W.2d 703 (Minn. 2012), that causation finding is binding on Farmers. Prieve's premise that hail at this property originates only from the west is therefore contradicted by Farmers' own award — hail demonstrably damaged both the west and the east faces of the dwelling. (This point stands independently of the siding observations, which have been excluded from this claim as attributable to a hail storm prior to 2020.)
- "Generally" is a probabilistic generalization being applied to a specific event without analysis of the specific event.
- The 7/13–7/14/2024 storm was a multi-round, overnight thunderstorm system. The NWS report (`Evidence/Significant Wind and Hail in Minnesota July 13-14.pdf`) documents multiple rounds of severe weather across Minnesota. Multi-round systems include rotating supercells, embedded mesocyclones, and outflow boundaries that produce multi-directional hail.
- **Wind direction changes within a single storm.** Even setting aside the multi-round character of this event, the wind in any one thunderstorm does not blow from a single fixed direction. Surface winds veer and back as the cell approaches, passes, and departs; gust fronts and outflow boundaries reverse the low-level flow; and a rotating updraft drives hail toward different faces of a structure at different moments of the same storm. A "hail generally comes from the west" generalization therefore cannot establish that hail did not strike the east elevation during the 7/13–7/14/2024 storm.
- The 48-MPH gust recorded at Eden Prairie ASOS at 1:00 AM on 7/14 came at the end of an overnight line — wind direction at that moment is a fact, not a generality.
- The conclusion that east-facing damage cannot be hail because hail "generally" comes from the west does not survive contact with the actual storm data — or with Farmers' own east-side (B1.2) window award.

### 5.4 What discovery and deposition should establish

- The specific data sources Prieve consulted for the "no hail after 2020" claim — by source, by query, by date range, by results.
- Whether Prieve consulted: NOAA Storm Events Database (and the actual query results); NWS Twin Cities/Chanhassen reports; stormersite or other commercial subscriptions; Interactive Hail Maps; City of Eden Prairie building-permit records; local newspaper archives; insurance-industry loss databases.
- The peer-review or quality-control process Prieve applied to the Weather Records section.
- The hail-size threshold Prieve uses to determine "sufficient" damage to extruded aluminum or aluminum-clad wood windows, and the source for that threshold.
- The terminal-velocity calculations (if any) underlying Conclusion 2.
- The basis for the "generally west-to-east" generalization in Conclusion 4 — whether it is a generic regional statement or specific to 7/13–7/14/2024.

## 6. Methodology Gaps

A forensic engineering report supporting an insurance denial in a multi-thousand-dollar property loss is expected to include certain core methodological elements. The Prieve Report contains none of them.

### 6.1 No quantitative impact-force analysis

The report contains no calculation of the kinetic energy required to produce the observed dents and no comparison to the kinetic energy of hail at the documented sizes. The "scrape mark inside dent → blunt object other than hail" theory is asserted as an inference rule without any quantitative or peer-reviewed basis.

### 6.2 No terminal-velocity calculations

The report does not calculate terminal velocity for any hail size at the subject location. Conclusion 2's flat statement that past hail was "not of sufficient size" requires terminal-velocity input; the report provides none.

### 6.3 No material-specific dent-depth modeling

The report does not specify the material composition of the windows in question (extruded aluminum, aluminum-clad wood, vinyl-clad wood, etc.), and does not apply material-specific dent-depth thresholds. The family later identified the windows as extruded aluminum and the manufacturer as Senco/Semco, with sash stamps from 1993, 1997, 2008, and 2014. The 2014 sash sits on the awarded family-room window (B1.2) and, per the family, matches that window's other sashes in hardware and appearance; the front dining-room window's three sashes are all stamped 2008. Yet the appraiser's September 18, 2025 description listed manufacturer stamps of only 1993, 1997, and 2008, omitting the 2014 sash — an incompleteness in the description that extends even to a window the panel evaluated and awarded. Extruded aluminum windows have well-documented hail-dent signatures; the report does not engage with them.

### 6.4 No peer-reviewed citations

The report contains no citations to peer-reviewed engineering literature on hail damage, no references to published damage thresholds, no references to academic research on hail-shape effects (see the `HailShapes/` folder of the project), and no industry guideline citations (e.g., NHEMA, NRCA, RICOWI, HAAG).

### 6.5 No statement of error rate or methodology limits

A forensic opinion under FRE 702 / Minn. R. Evid. 702 standards is expected to include a statement of the methodology applied, the error rate, and the standards by which the methodology is generally accepted. The Prieve Report contains a conclusory statement that the opinions are "to a reasonable degree of engineering certainty" but no methodological elaboration.

### 6.6 No analysis of alternative causation hypotheses

The report concludes that observed dents are from "blunt object other than hail" and "impacts other than hail or damage occurring during or following installation of windows at the time of construction of the home." It does not analyze:

- The specific blunt-object event(s) that would have produced the dents.
- Why "blunt object" damage would correlate spatially with the same windows that sustained hail damage in prior claims.
- Why "installation-period" damage would appear at the same window locations across multiple windows of different manufacturer stamp dates (1993, 1997, 2008, 2014).
- Why the family's documented contemporaneous record contains no event consistent with widespread blunt-object damage to a home's exterior.

### 6.7 What discovery and deposition should establish

- Prieve's training and credentials specific to hail-damage analysis on residential window assemblies.
- Prieve's familiarity with the HailShapes peer-reviewed literature.
- The methodology Prieve customarily applies in forensic hail-damage evaluations and how it was applied here.
- Any error-rate or quality-assurance protocol Prieve Engineering uses.
- Whether Prieve has previously testified as an expert and the outcomes of any Daubert challenges to his methodology.
- The full record of Prieve Engineering's Minnesota engagements with Farmers since 2020 (file numbers, claim numbers, hail-causation findings vs. denials) for pattern-of-practice analysis.

## 6A. The Self-Defining Hail Signature — Prieve's Garage-Window Sentence Convicts the Rest of the Report

In one sentence on page 6 of the Prieve report (page 9 of the combined PDF), Prieve admits the operative diagnostic test he applies to determine whether a dent was caused by hail. The sentence appears at the end of the East Side discussion, in the paragraph covering the rear/east garage window:

> "A 2 unit window appears at the rear/east side of the garage adjacent to a shed. Various scrape marks appear in the frame of the window not consistent with hail. **No smooth or rounded dents were observed in the frame or sashes.**"

The sentence operates as a deductive equivalence: Prieve uses the *absence* of "smooth or rounded dents" at the garage window as the reason hail can be ruled out there. The necessary mirror proposition is that the *presence* of smooth or rounded dents would indicate hail. That is Prieve's own implicit diagnostic.

### 6A.1 Prieve's report documents smooth/rounded dents elsewhere — and his own narrative calls them dents

Applied across the rest of the report, the diagnostic Prieve set up at the garage window defeats his own Conclusion 1:

- **Front/west triple window (photographs A-38 through A-41).** Page 4 of the Prieve report (page 7 of the combined PDF): "A triple window unit appears at the main level to the north of the main entry as shown in photograph A-38. **Rounded dents** were observed at sashes of this window unit."
- **North porch four-unit window (photographs A-178, A-179, A-181).** Page 5 of the Prieve report (page 8 of the combined PDF): "Two dents appear at the lower left corner of the left casement **which are generally smooth**."

Both observations match the very signature Prieve uses elsewhere as the marker of hail. Prieve does not deny that these dents are smooth or rounded; he handwaves them away by attributing them to "a prior hail claim," not because the dents themselves differ from a hail signature but because Farmers' 2020 file mentioned those windows. That is a presumption, not a methodology — and it depends on Farmers' 2020 file actually having covered the specific sashes in question, which (per §§ 4.1–4.2 above) it did not.

### 6A.2 The "scrape marks inside dents → blunt object" framework is internally inconsistent with Prieve's own diagnostic

The other observations Prieve repeatedly dismisses as "not consistent with hail" are dents that include scrape marks (e.g., A-45, A-64, A-107, A-113, A-115–117, A-156, A-159, A-166). Prieve's "smooth or rounded" diagnostic implies that a hail dent should be free of scrape marks. There is no peer-reviewed support for that proposition anywhere in his report. The HailShapes literature in the project folder (`HailShapes/DocumentIndex.md` and underlying PDFs) establishes the opposite: real hailstones are rarely perfectly smooth or spherical; they often have lobes, points, and irregular surfaces; and they routinely produce impact signatures that include abrasions and scrape marks, especially in oblique-angle impacts driven by wind. The 7/13–7/14/2024 storm — with a 48 MPH gust recorded at Eden Prairie ASOS at 1:00 AM on 7/14 (`Evidence/Significant Wind and Hail in Minnesota July 13-14.pdf`) — is the textbook example of conditions that would produce wind-driven, oblique-angle hail impacts with scraping signatures.

So Prieve's two-track diagnostic — (i) "smooth/rounded" = hail, but only if Farmers' 2020 file already covered it, and (ii) "scrape marks inside dents" = blunt object other than hail — is constructed to produce a non-hail finding on every observation regardless of what is physically present. That is the structure of an advocacy document, not a forensic evaluation.

### 6A.3 The diagnostic strengthens the *Quade* / appraisal-award argument

The 9/12/2025 appraisal panel awarded "Windows (F1.7 / B1.2)": a single-sash window at the corner of stairs facing front, and a three-sash window in family room facing back. At least one of those — the front/west window — is where Prieve documented "**rounded dents**" in his own narrative. The panel's award of hail causation on that window aligns with Prieve's own implicit diagnostic, not against it. Prieve's report therefore does not just lose to the appraisal panel; it tacitly agrees with the panel's causation finding while reaching the opposite conclusion. That alignment supports the *Quade* binding-effect argument and reinforces the post-award "Farmers cannot consistently describe its own scope" argument.

### 6A.4 The diagnostic is a Daubert ground

A forensic engineer's operative diagnostic test for the central causation question must be supported by methodology, peer-reviewed citation, error rate, and general acceptance. Prieve's "smooth or rounded = hail; scrape marks inside dents = not hail" framework has none of those things on the face of the report. The garage-window sentence is the single cleanest entry point for the Daubert motion: it shows the diagnostic exists, shows it is applied inconsistently, and shows the report contains no methodological defense of it.

### 6A.5 What discovery and deposition should establish

- The peer-reviewed engineering or meteorological literature that supports the "smooth or rounded" = hail / "scrape marks inside dents" = not-hail distinction.
- The hail-impact mechanics under which a smooth or rounded dent forms versus a dent with internal scrape marks.
- Prieve's awareness of the HailShapes peer-reviewed literature.
- Prieve's basis for attributing the front/west "rounded dents" and the north porch "generally smooth" dents to the 2020 storm rather than the 7/13–7/14/2024 storm, given that the 7/13–7/14/2024 storm produced larger hail across the region (up to 3.5–4 inches per NWS).
- The cross-examination sequence at § 4 of `OffenseStrategies/Deposition/2.1Prieve_Nathan.md` (the 7-step garage-window walkthrough).

## 6B. Incomplete Inspection — A Garage Window Prieve Never Inspected

> ⚠️ **Verification flag.** This fact was established by the family on cross-check of the Prieve report against the actual building. Before this point is used in any filing, it should be documented with supporting evidence — a dated photograph of the window and its obstruction, and/or a measured site sketch showing the window's location relative to the documented rear/east garage window (A-213–217), the shed, and the house wall. The factual description below is the family's account pending that documentation.

### 6B.1 The window Prieve documented vs. the window he missed

The Prieve report and Photo Appendix A document **one** garage window: a 2-unit window described in the narrative at page 6 as appearing "at the rear/east side of the garage adjacent to a shed," photographed at A-213 through A-217. The report treats that single window as the complete garage-window population.

In fact there is a **second** garage window that does not appear anywhere in the Prieve report or in the 217-photograph Photo Appendix A:

- A small fixed window, approximately 2 feet by 2 feet, split by a horizontal frame into what appears to be two sashes;
- Located on the **rear** elevation of the garage, immediately **south of** (and closer to the main structure than) the rear/east window Prieve did document (A-213–217);
- Partially obstructed by a shed standing in front of it;
- Accessible only through a narrow gap between the shed and the house wall, or by passing through the shed itself (opening the shed's front and back doors).

Because this window appears nowhere in the report's narrative, nowhere in the Conclusions, and nowhere in the 217-image appendix, the only available inference is that **Prieve did not inspect it.**

### 6B.2 Why a missed window is a distinct and serious defect

This is categorically different from the eight defects in §§ 1–6A above. Those defects concern how Prieve mischaracterized, omitted from his narrative, or drew unsupported conclusions about damage he actually observed. This defect concerns a window he **never observed at all**:

- **It proves the inspection was incomplete even within Prieve's own stated scope.** The report's stated scope is to "evaluate the condition of windows in the home as relates to hail" (p. 2). A windows-only evaluation that omits a window is incomplete on its own terms. Prieve cannot claim the missed window was "outside scope" — windows were the entire scope.
- **It is the cleanest possible unevaluated-window for the *Quade* reservation.** The appraisal panel could not have evaluated a window that Farmers' own engineer never inspected and never documented. Coverage and amount of loss for this window are necessarily reserved to the court under Count I (see `OffenseStrategies/AppraisalAwardStrategy/01_DamageCatalog_PrieveAppendixA.md`).
- **The obstruction explains the omission and indicts the methodology.** The window is blocked by a shed and requires awkward access (a gap between the shed and the house wall, or passing through the shed). The natural inference is that Prieve inspected only what was readily visible and skipped anything requiring effort to reach. That inference, once established, casts doubt on the completeness of the inspection everywhere on the property — including the upper-story windows Prieve reached "via a ladder" and the areas he reported with "no hail dents." If he skipped an obstructed ground-level window, the reliability of his "no damage" findings at less accessible locations is open to question.
- **It compounds the bad-faith record.** An engineer's incomplete inspection, adopted by Farmers as the basis for denial, is evidence that Farmers failed to conduct the reasonable investigation Minn. Stat. § 72A.201 requires and supports the "knew or should have known it had no reasonable basis" element of Minn. Stat. § 604.18.
- **It is a Daubert ground.** A forensic methodology that does not account for all units within the inspection scope is unreliable. An expert who reaches a "no recent hail damage" conclusion about the property's windows without inspecting all of the windows has not applied a reliable methodology to sufficient facts.

### 6B.3 The arithmetic effect

The missed window adds one more window to the count of windows Prieve documented damage on or failed to evaluate, and it is the only window in the entire population that Prieve did not inspect at all. In the damage catalog it should be carried as a separate line — not within the A-213–217 garage-window entry (which is a different, rear/east window) — and added to the unevaluated-windows total that drives the Track 1 *Quade*-reservation breach claim.

### 6B.4 What discovery and deposition should establish

- Confirmation that the Prieve report and the complete camera-download from the 10/9/2024 inspection contain no photograph of the rear-of-garage window south of the documented rear/east window (A-213–217). (The original camera download — not just the curated Appendix A — should be subpoenaed; see `OffenseStrategies/Discovery/DiscoveryPlan.md` RFP A.5.)
- Whether Prieve was aware of the window's existence at the time of the inspection.
- Whether Prieve attempted to access the window and was prevented by the shed, or never identified it at all.
- Prieve's standard practice for inspecting obstructed or hard-to-access windows.
- Whether Prieve's engagement or Farmers' instruction limited the inspection to readily accessible windows.
- How many other windows or components on the property Prieve did not physically access during the 10/9/2024 inspection.
- The family's own photograph and site sketch of the window and its obstruction (to be produced as the verification evidence flagged above).

## 7. Biased Framing — The Report Is Argumentative, Not Investigative

Beyond the specific factual and methodological deficiencies, the Prieve Report reads throughout as an advocacy document rather than a neutral forensic evaluation.

### 7.1 The "not consistent with hail" formula

The narrative repeatedly uses the phrase "not consistent with hail" as a labeling formula attached to specific observations, with no engagement of why the observation is inconsistent with hail. Examples from the narrative:

- "A scrape mark was observed in the face of the bottom corner of a sash **not consistent with hail**." (p. 6)
- "An indentation appears on the left side of the center window accessory groove **not consistent with hail** …" (p. 8)
- "A dent appears in the bottom accessory groove of the upper northeast facing window **not consistent with hail**." (p. 8)
- "A dent appears along the left accessory groove of the southeast facing window **not consistent with hail**." (p. 8)
- "Dents with scrape marks appear at the lower left corner of the sash of the northeast facing window **not consistent with hail**." (p. 8)
- "A dent appears along the bottom accessory groove **not consistent with hail**." (p. 8)
- "A similar dent appears along the left side as shown in photograph A-163 **not consistent with hail**." (p. 8)

The formula appears at least seven times in two pages. In none of those instances does Prieve identify the hail signature he expected, explain why the observation diverges from it, or analyze what mechanism is consistent with the observation. The phrase becomes a label of conclusion rather than a step of analysis.

### 7.2 The "accessory groove" exclusion

Several observations are dismissed as "dents in accessory grooves" — a deflection that treats the location of the dent (a recessed channel on the window frame) as a categorical bar to hail causation. Prieve does not explain:

- Whether hail at the documented sizes (1.25"–2") can deflect off a window frame face and impact the accessory groove.
- Whether downdrafts associated with the overnight storm could deliver impacts to accessory grooves.
- Why a dent in an accessory groove is intrinsically inconsistent with hail when the groove is a directly exposed exterior surface.

### 7.3 "Caulk appears" as wear-and-tear evidence

The narrative repeatedly notes the presence of caulk at corners, mullions, and sashes as evidence of "past attempts to prevent water entry," supporting the wear-and-tear theory. This framing treats normal-maintenance caulking as evidence of pre-existing defect. The caulking observations would not support the wear-and-tear theory unless:

- The caulking is documented to pre-date the current claim period.
- The caulking is documented to have been performed in response to a leak attributable to a non-hail cause.
- The caulking is documented in a quantity inconsistent with normal window-maintenance practice.

The report contains no such documentation. It simply notes that caulk exists and treats that fact as supporting non-hail causation.

### 7.4 Reservation of the right to "supplement or amend the report pending further discovery"

The Conclusions section (p. 10) reserves Prieve's right to amend the report "pending further discovery." That standard reservation is unremarkable on its own, but read with the report's defects, it telegraphs that the report Farmers used to deny the claim is itself an interim work product. Despite the family's submissions on 1/31/2025 (appeal package with new evidence) and 2/10/2025 (neighbor canvass and City permits), Prieve apparently did not supplement or amend.

### 7.5 What discovery and deposition should establish

- Whether Prieve supplemented or amended the report after the 1/31/2025 appeal package was submitted; if so, the supplement; if not, the reason.
- Whether Farmers asked Prieve to review the family's 2/10/2025 City permit data and neighbor canvass; if not, why not.
- The percentage of Prieve Engineering's Minnesota hail-claim opinions for insurer clients (since 2020) that conclude "no hail damage" or substantively similar.
- The percentage of Prieve Engineering's Minnesota hail-claim opinions that have been controverted by subsequent appraisal awards.
- Prieve's annual fees from Farmers in Minnesota since 2020.
