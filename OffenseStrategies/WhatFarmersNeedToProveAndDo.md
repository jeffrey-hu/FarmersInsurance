# What Farmers Needs to Prove and Do — A Defense-Burden Maximization Playbook

**Case:** Yibiao Lu & Jie Hu v. Fire Insurance Exchange (d/b/a Farmers Insurance)
**Policy:** 98055-49-65 | **Claim:** 7007986837-1-1
**Loss Date:** July 13, 2024
**Filing Deadline:** July 13, 2026
**Forum (assumed):** Hennepin County District Court (Minnesota state court)
**Prepared:** May 17, 2026

> **Purpose.** `offenseStrategy.md` answered the question "what must Plaintiffs prove and what offensive moves should we make to win." This document answers the converse, and operationalizes it: **what must Farmers prove or do to win this case, and how does each of those defense burdens translate into defense-attorney hours that Farmers cannot avoid spending?** Every line item below is a place where the defense lawyer's clock starts running — either to produce documents, defend depositions, hire experts, brief motions, prepare witnesses, or respond to plaintiff filings.
>
> **Strategic premise.** In a case where the family is pro se or operating on a tight budget, the family's settlement leverage is not just the merits of the claim — it is the **cost ratio**: the family's marginal cost to keep the case alive versus Farmers' marginal cost to defend it. Each plaintiff move that costs the family 1 hour but costs Farmers' counsel 15+ hours bends the cost ratio in the family's favor. This document catalogs the largest such asymmetries on this record and orders them for maximum effect.
>
> **What this document is not.** It is not a strategy for inflating defense costs through frivolous filings. Every burden identified below maps to a legitimate, supportable claim, motion, or discovery request grounded in the underlying record (see `offenseStrategy.md`, `LegalReferences/LegalArguments/`, and `LegalStrategies/`). The goal is not to harass — it is to make Farmers' litigation accountant calculate, at every decision point, that paying the loss is cheaper than continuing to defend.

## 1. Theory of Defense-Cost Maximization

Defense attorney hours in a Minnesota first-party hail-and-bad-faith case grow on five axes:

1. **Number of distinct legal theories.** Each pleaded count forces a separate motion-to-dismiss section, a separate summary-judgment section, a separate jury instruction set, and (usually) a separate set of facts to investigate. A complaint with five counts is not 1.25× a complaint with four counts in defense hours — it is closer to 1.6×–2.0× because counts interlock and require cross-citation work.

2. **Volume and granularity of disputed facts.** A single contested fact ("did hail damage the house") is one expert deposition. **Forty-plus contested facts** (every individual window's damage status, the panel's evaluation of each, the methodology used, the post-2021 storm record, the manufacturer's operational status, internal communications about the engineer's-report rule, etc.) is a defense engagement that scales linearly with each new fact placed in dispute.

3. **Number of Farmers personnel who must be defended in deposition.** Brau, Monson, Dudley, Pietila, Tomes, Zorphion Miller, Twonda Miller, Jed Jones, Bradlee Monson, plus an institutional Rule 30(b)(6) representative — that is **9 to 12 Farmers-side depositions** before any expert deposition, each requiring 6–15 hours of defense prep + 4–8 hours of defense-of-deposition time.

4. **Documentary-discovery scope.** The full claim file, the full 2020 claim file, the Prieve retention and underlying weather-research file, Hancock workproduct, internal claims-handling policies, the supervisor-training materials, Farmers' litigation hold, the umpire's working files, the reservation-of-rights record, and (under the MCFA count C3) pattern-of-practice records from other Minnesota Farmers claims — every category is a separate Rule 34 response, every objection costs time, and every produced-or-withheld document is potential Rule 37 territory.

5. **Number of expert tracks Farmers must defend.** Each plaintiff expert is one defense expert (or one Daubert motion) that Farmers must mount. With a hail-damage forensic engineer, a window-matching expert, a replacement-cost valuator, a claims-handling expert (for the B1 bad-faith count), and potentially a meteorologist, Farmers' expert side alone is a $50,000–$200,000 line item depending on whom they retain.

This document is organized to maximize each axis simultaneously.

## 2. Substantive Proof Burdens Farmers Must Carry

These are the things Farmers must affirmatively prove (or disprove) on the merits — burdens that the legal authorities reviewed in `LegalReferences/LegalArguments/` and `offenseStrategy.md` place squarely on the insurer. Each entry below states (i) the burden, (ii) the controlling rule placing the burden on Farmers, (iii) what record evidence Farmers needs to develop to carry it, (iv) what plaintiff moves force Farmers to commit time to it, and (v) a rough hour estimate for the defense side.

### 2.1 Each Section I Exclusion Asserted in the 11/4/2024 Denial — Six Separate Burdens

Under *Henning Nelson Construction Co. v. Fireman's Fund American Life Insurance Co.*, 383 N.W.2d 645, 651–52 (Minn. 1986), and *SCSC Corp. v. Allied Mutual Insurance Co.*, 536 N.W.2d 305, 314 (Minn. 1995), the insurer bears the burden of proving any exclusion. The 11/4/2024 denial letter (`EventDocuments/FarmersDenyLetter-11-4.pdf`) cited **six** exclusions: Wear and Tear (§ I.A.7 / I.B.8); Marring (§ I.A.8); Corrosion/Deterioration/Decay/Rust (§ I.A.9 / I.B.9); Inherent Vice or Latent Defect (§ I.A.4 / I.B.6); Neglect/Lack of Maintenance (§ I.B.4); Faulty/Defective Construction (§ I.B.5).

For each exclusion, Farmers must prove its factual application **window-by-window** for every disputed window on the property. The Prieve report does not carry that load — it offers generalized causation theories, not item-by-item exclusion analysis. To satisfy the burden Farmers needs (a) a forensic engineer willing to identify each disputed window and state the operative exclusion as to that window with a defensible methodology; (b) interrogatory answers and Rule 30(b)(6) testimony articulating the factual basis for each exclusion as applied to each window; and (c) policy-language briefing defending against *contra proferentem* construction on the marring/windstorm-restoration seam (§ I.A.8 vs. § I.A.8(b)).

**Plaintiff moves that force this.** Detailed interrogatories asking Farmers to "identify, by window, the specific factual basis for each exclusion you contend applies." Rule 30(b)(6) deposition topic on each exclusion as applied to each window. Requests for admission asking Farmers to admit or deny that the 9/12/2025 panel's *Quade* causation finding forecloses each exclusion for the awarded windows.

**Defense-hour estimate.** Six exclusions × 30+ disputed windows = ~180 individual exclusion-applications to defend. Even at 1 hour per cell of analysis and 0.5 hours of discovery response per cell, that is ~270 hours of defense-side claim-file work, before any motion practice or expert work. A Daubert-eligible exclusion expert is a separate $35,000–$80,000 outlay.

### 2.2 "Fairly Debatable" Defense to B1 Bad Faith — Single Largest Defense Burden in the Case

Once the family files the § 604.18 Subd. 4(a) motion to amend, Farmers' principal Prong 1 defense is that it had a "reasonable basis" to deny — the Prieve report. *Selective Insurance Co. of S.C. v. Sela*, No. 20-2029 (8th Cir. 2021), is the controlling precedent. To carry this defense Farmers must establish:

- That Prieve's methodology was professionally defensible. This requires Farmers to retain a forensic engineering expert willing to defend Prieve's "no quantitative impact analysis," "directional-generalization," and "no-hail-after-2020" approach against the HailShapes peer-reviewed framework.
- That the 9/3/2024 denial — issued before any engineer was engaged — was nevertheless based on a reasonable factual basis. This forces Farmers to put Christian Dudley, the catastrophe claims rep who signed the 9/3 denial, on the stand or in deposition to explain what he believed and why, given only Hancock's photo set.
- That the "engineer's report only" supervisor-level rule articulated by Monson on 2/3/2025 and confirmed in writing by Brau on 2/10/2025 was either (i) a misunderstanding by the family of standard claims-handling protocol or (ii) consistent with Farmers' written claims-handling policies. Either showing forces Farmers to produce internal claims-handling manuals, training records, and supervisor email chains.
- That Farmers' continued denial after the 1/31/2025 appeal, the 2/10/2025 second response, and the 5/28/2025 NWS submission was not reckless disregard but a measured response to evidence that, while voluminous, was claimed-inadequate.
- That the September 2025 appraisal award's hail-causation finding does not retroactively render the prior denials unreasonable — a particularly difficult position given that the panel was Farmers' own.

**Plaintiff moves that force this.** The Subd. 4(a) motion itself, supported by affidavits walking through each of the six documented § 72A.201 violations identified in `B1.md` § 3.1. Discovery requests targeting internal claim-handling policies, supervisor-training records, Prieve's underlying weather-research file, Farmers' acceptance/rejection log for non-engineer submitted evidence. Rule 30(b)(6) deposition on the origin and application of the "engineer's report only" rule.

**Defense-hour estimate.** A § 604.18 Subd. 4(a) opposition with affidavits and exhibits is typically 80–150 attorney hours. Defending the underlying discovery (claims-handling policies, training materials, Prieve file, internal emails) is another 100–250 hours, much of it senior associate or partner time at Minnesota civil-litigation rates of ~$337/hour. Retaining and preparing a claims-handling expert to opine that Farmers' conduct was reasonable typically runs $40,000–$120,000.

This is the single largest defense burden in the case. Plaintiffs' most cost-imposing move is to make sure the Subd. 4(a) motion is filed only when the underlying discovery record is rich enough to require Farmers to fight it on the merits rather than dismiss it on threshold grounds.

### 2.3 Subd. 4(b) Appraisal-Bar Defense to B1 — Forces Farmers to Defend the Award's Scope

Defendant will argue that the 9/12/2025 appraisal "resolved or confirmed" the claim within the meaning of § 604.18 Subd. 4(b) and therefore bars the § 604.18 remedy. To carry this defense, Farmers must affirmatively prove that the appraisal addressed every window the family claims was damaged. That requires Farmers to:

- Establish that the codes "F1.7" and "B1.2" on the award form encompass the full scope of damaged windows the family alleges. Given Farmers' own internally inconsistent post-award descriptions (9/18, 9/19, 10/14/2025 — three different scope statements over four weeks), this is a recursive trap: defending the award's scope requires Farmers to reconcile statements it cannot itself reconcile.
- Produce a methodology document from the umpire — which Farmers admitted in writing on 9/24/2025 does not exist.
- Defend the appraisal panel's competence and disinterestedness against the Foster-was-picked-by-the-contractor and prior-insurance-industry-background facts.
- Distinguish *Herll v. Auto-Owners Insurance Co.*, 879 F.3d 293 (8th Cir. 2018), and *Cincinnati Insurance Co. v. Rymer Cos.*, No. 24-3356 (8th Cir. Mar. 27, 2026), both of which treat facially ambiguous awards as warranting remand for clarification rather than confirmation.

**Plaintiff moves that force this.** The A3 alternative-pleaded count requesting *Herll*/*Rymer* remand, the A2 declaratory-judgment count asking the court to declare the scope of what the panel resolved, and the *Quade* partial-summary-judgment motion locking in causation while preserving scope as an open question.

**Defense-hour estimate.** Opposing a *Herll*/*Rymer* remand request typically runs 40–80 hours of briefing time plus 30–60 hours of underlying record-development. Defending the Subd. 4(b) argument concurrent with the Subd. 4(a) opposition adds 50–100 hours.

### 2.4 The J6944 Matching Endorsement Defense — Expensive Even if Farmers Wins

J6944 (Limited Matching of Undamaged Property) is an exclusion-style endorsement; Defendant bears the burden of proving its application. *Cedar Bluff Townhome Condo. Ass'n v. American Family Mutual Insurance Co.*, 857 N.W.2d 290 (Minn. 2014), supports the matching obligation under "comparable material and quality"; *Noonan v. American Family Mutual Insurance Co.*, 929 F.3d 633 (8th Cir. 2019), is adverse. To carry the J6944 defense, Farmers must:

- Brief the textual scope of J6944's three gates (damage-gate, reason-gate, diminution-gate) against the family's *Cedar Bluff*-based contrary reading.
- Procure a window-matching expert to opine on (a) availability of replacement windows compatible with the existing 1993/1997/2008 Senco/Semco stock, (b) the Pella Reserve Traditional vs. Marvin Ultimate equivalency question identified in the family's 6/12/2025 communications, and (c) the "obsolescence or discontinuation" gate at J6944(1)(iii).
- Defend or controvert the family's "Semco out of business since 2019" factual claim, which requires independent verification work.
- Reconcile the J6944 buy-back election (if any was offered) against the buy-back-election text in the endorsement.

**Plaintiff moves that force this.** The A2 declaratory-judgment count on J6944's scope. Discovery into the J6944 endorsement's drafting history, Farmers' standard claim-handling protocol for J6944 application, and Sierra Pacific Windows' product-availability documentation (subpoenaed under Rule 45). Independent verification of Semco's operational status submitted as evidence at summary judgment.

**Defense-hour estimate.** Window-matching expert retention is typically $25,000–$60,000. Endorsement-interpretation briefing across A1, A2, and possibly A3 is 50–100 hours of motion practice. Sierra Pacific subpoena response will generate either a third-party-driven cost-shift or, if Farmers chooses to participate in third-party discovery, additional defense hours.

### 2.5 Each Misrepresentation Claim in B2 — Three Separate Defense Tracks

The fraud / negligent misrepresentation count (B2 in v2/v3 of the complaint drafts) anchors on three candidate misrepresentations: (a) Prieve's "no hail in Eden Prairie after 2020"; (b) the 9/3/2024 "no other damages observed" representation; (c) the supervisor-level "engineer's report only" rule. For each, Farmers must defeat falsity, scienter, intent to induce, justifiable reliance, and pecuniary damages.

The defense burden on B2 is heavy because:
- **Rule 9.02 particularity** forces Farmers to brief each misrepresentation separately rather than aggregating them — three separate Rule 12 sections, three separate summary-judgment sections, three separate jury instructions.
- **Reliance is contested.** Farmers' strongest defense is plaintiff reliance failure, but the reliance reframe under `B2.md` § 3.4 (appraisal-channel reliance; litigation-delay reliance) requires Farmers to take a deposition of each plaintiff on what they relied on at each step — multiplying deposition prep by the number of plaintiffs (two) and the number of reliance points (three+).
- **Scienter discovery is intrusive.** Defending the scienter element requires Farmers to either produce internal communications about Prieve's draft report and Farmers' review of it, or assert privilege and litigate the privilege. Either route is expensive.

**Plaintiff moves that force this.** Plead B2 with full Rule 9.02 particularity for each misrepresentation. File a § 549.191 motion to amend to add punitive damages tied to B2 — this opens Farmers' financial-condition discovery (net worth, profitability of the hail-claim book), which is among the most expensive single discovery cycles in the case.

**Defense-hour estimate.** B2 Rule 12 briefing: 40–80 hours. B2 discovery (Prieve file, internal review communications, scienter-related ESI search): 60–150 hours. § 549.191 opposition: 40–80 hours. Plaintiff depositions on reliance: 25–50 hours per plaintiff. Defense expert on claims-handling reasonableness as it bears on scienter: $30,000–$80,000.

### 2.6 MCFA "In Connection With the Sale" Defense in C3 — The Deepest Discovery Burden

The Minnesota Consumer Fraud Act claim (C3) presents Farmers with its most expensive single defense to mount, because the "in connection with the sale of merchandise" element is litigated through pattern-of-conduct discovery. *Force v. ITT Hartford Life and Annuity Insurance Co.*, 4 F. Supp. 2d 843 (D. Minn. 1998), supports the continuing-relationship reading that brings post-sale insurance claims-handling within § 325F.69; Farmers will press the *Morris v. American Family Mutual Insurance Co.*, 386 N.W.2d 233 (Minn. 1986), claims-handling/sales-conduct line to distinguish.

To carry this defense, Farmers must:
- Brief the *Force*-vs.-*Morris* line with respect to whether the 10/22/2023 renewal (within 12 months of the loss) and the ongoing premium collection sufficient to bring the conduct within the sales nexus.
- Defeat the causal nexus theory under *Wiegand v. Walser Automotive Groups, Inc.*, 683 N.W.2d 807 (Minn. 2004), and *Group Health Plan, Inc. v. Philip Morris, Inc.*, 621 N.W.2d 2 (Minn. 2001) (no individual reliance required).
- Contest the public-benefit prong under Route 1 (§ 8.31 Subd. 3a) — though the 2023 amendment under § 325F.70 largely neutralizes this for Route 2.

The cost-driver of C3 is **pattern-of-practice discovery**. To defend the MCFA count Farmers must respond to discovery into other Minnesota Farmers hail-claim files where the "engineer's report only" rule was applied. That discovery is bounded only by relevance and proportionality, and the family's counsel can credibly request: (i) all Minnesota Farmers hail-damage denial files from 2020 forward where a Hancock and/or Prieve inspection was conducted; (ii) all internal Farmers communications using the phrases "engineer's report only," "engineer required," "non-engineer evidence," or substantively similar; (iii) supervisor training materials covering hail-claim evidentiary standards; (iv) Farmers' retention agreements with Prieve Engineering across all Minnesota claims.

**Plaintiff moves that force this.** Plead C3 under both Route 1 and Route 2 in the initial complaint. Issue pattern-of-practice Rule 34 requests immediately upon answer. Move to compel under Rule 37.01 on each non-production. Notice a Rule 30(b)(6) deposition on Farmers' institutional practice of requiring engineer-sourced evidence in hail claims.

**Defense-hour estimate.** Pattern-of-practice discovery response: 150–500+ hours depending on the scope of fights over relevance/proportionality. Rule 30(b)(6) prep on institutional practice: 30–60 hours. C3 motion practice (Rule 12; summary judgment; appellate preservation): 80–150 hours. **C3 is the count where Farmers will spend the most defense money relative to the dollar exposure of the count itself** — which is precisely why pleading C3 is recommended in `00_Overview_Complaint_Drafts.md` v2.

### 2.7 D1 Punitive-Damages Defense (Once Added by § 549.191 Motion)

Once the § 549.191 motion to amend is granted, Farmers must defend a punitive-damages exposure under § 549.20. The standard is clear and convincing evidence of "deliberate disregard for the rights or safety of others." The defense burden includes:
- Defeating the clear-and-convincing showing on the deliberate-disregard element.
- Defeating Farmers' financial-condition exposure under § 549.20 Subd. 3.
- Briefing the *State Farm v. Campbell*, 538 U.S. 408 (2003), constitutional ceiling on the punitive-to-compensatory ratio.
- Bifurcated trial preparation under Subd. 4 — effectively two trials in one.

The single largest sub-burden here is **financial-condition discovery**. § 549.20 Subd. 3 permits the jury to consider Farmers' financial condition. That opens discovery into Fire Insurance Exchange's net worth, profitability, reserve practices, hail-claim segment profitability, executive compensation tied to denial rates, and similar internal metrics. Punitive damages are largely uninsurable in Minnesota — the exposure is non-offloadable.

**Plaintiff moves that force this.** File the § 549.191 motion only when the underlying discovery record supports clear and convincing deliberate-disregard. Plead B2 in the initial complaint as the punitive-damages hook (B1 cannot carry punitives per § 604.18 Subd. 3(b); contract claims cannot carry punitives per *Wild v. Rarig*, 302 Minn. 419 (1975)).

**Defense-hour estimate.** § 549.191 opposition: 60–120 hours. Financial-condition discovery response: 80–200 hours. Bifurcated trial preparation: 100–300 hours of additional trial work. Defense expert on claims-handling reasonableness covering both B1 and D1: typically $50,000–$150,000.

### 2.8 Limitations Defense — Small Burden but Cannot Be Skipped

If Farmers asserts the policy's 2-year suit limitation or the § 65A.26 1-year hail-policy limit, Defendant bears the burden. The family's 7/13/2026 filing makes this defense largely academic for the breach claim, but Farmers will likely raise it anyway because (i) failing to raise it waives it and (ii) Farmers' own 7/11/2025 boilerplate response acknowledged the suit-limitation period "may have been extended by statute or case law" — creating *contra proferentem* construction in the family's favor.

**Plaintiff moves that force this.** Plead a *contra proferentem* declaration in A2's prayer for relief. Cite Farmers' own 7/11/2025 acknowledgement at summary judgment.

**Defense-hour estimate.** Limitations briefing: 15–40 hours. Modest, but Farmers cannot skip it.

## 3. Procedural Defense Burdens Farmers Must Carry

These are the procedural moves Farmers must respond to or initiate that are independent of merits proof. Each is a separate timeline event consuming defense hours.

### 3.1 Answer the Multi-Count Complaint

Under v2 (Balanced) of the complaint drafts in `LegalStrategies/`, Farmers must answer A1 + A2 + A3 + B2 + C3. Each count requires count-specific affirmative defenses, count-specific factual denials, and count-specific Rule 8 admissions. Farmers cannot rely on a generic answer.

**Defense-hour estimate.** 25–50 hours for a multi-count answer with carefully drafted affirmative defenses.

### 3.2 Defend Against Rule 12 Pre-Answer Motions (or Decline To File and Pay the Price Later)

Farmers can move to dismiss B2 under Rule 9.02 (particularity), C3 under the "in connection with the sale of merchandise" interpretive question, A3 under the 90-day vacatur window argument, and A2 on the discretionary § 555.06 ground. Each Rule 12 motion is a separate brief, with separate replies. If Farmers files all four, that is ~80–150 hours of motion practice consumed before discovery even opens.

**Plaintiff move that maximizes this.** Plead each count with belt-and-suspenders rigor — full Rule 9.02 particularity for B2; Route 1 + Route 2 alternative pleading for C3; tight DJ-1 through DJ-5 sub-paragraphs for A2; alternative-pleaded *Herll*/*Rymer* + *Mork* tiers for A3 — so that no count gets dismissed in whole or in part on a Rule 12 motion.

### 3.3 Respond to the Litigation Hold Letter and Initial Discovery Package

`offenseStrategy.md` § 4.1 Item 5 contemplates issuing a litigation-hold letter and initial discovery package contemporaneous with filing. The litigation hold itself does not require Farmers action, but the initial Rule 34 RFP set forces Farmers to begin document collection immediately. The initial RFP set should target:

- Full claim file 7007986837-1-1, including all electronic case-notes, all internal status updates, all draft letters, all communications between adjusters and supervisors.
- Full 2020 claim file 3013539499-1.
- Brau's 9/6/2024 inspection notes — or sworn confirmation that none were created, with the supporting records-retention policy.
- Hancock workproduct beyond the photo set in `FarmersDenyLetter_09_03_2024.pdf` pp. 5–74.
- Farmers' written claims-handling policies effective 7/13/2024 forward, including supervisor training materials and the standard evidentiary protocol for hail claims.
- The Prieve Engineering retention agreement and the underlying weather-research file Prieve consulted (including the data sources for the "no hail after 2020" assertion).
- Internal communications regarding the "engineer's report only" rule articulated by Monson on 2/3/2025 and confirmed by Brau on 2/10/2025.
- Any reservation-of-rights letter issued at any time on this claim.
- Farmers' litigation-hold notice and the date it was issued.
- The umpire's working files, notes, and methodology — even though Farmers stated on 9/24/2025 that no umpire report exists, Roos's working notes are still discoverable.
- Greg Tomes's appraiser file, including his non-signature reasoning.
- ESI preservation declaration covering email, internal chat, claims-system audit logs, and physical records.
- Pattern-of-practice discovery for C3 — see § 2.6 above.

**Defense-hour estimate.** Initial RFP response: 80–200 hours just to collect, review for privilege, and produce responsive documents. Each privilege log entry adds 0.5–2 hours. ESI search runs add 40–100 hours of vendor-and-attorney coordination time. Pattern-of-practice discovery alone can double these numbers.

### 3.4 Respond to Targeted Interrogatories Pinning Farmers to Specific Positions

`offenseStrategy.md` § 4.3 Item 12 contemplates interrogatories pinning Farmers to specific positions. Each interrogatory below forces Farmers to commit to a written answer under oath, which then anchors deposition and trial cross-examination:

- Identify each Farmers employee who touched the file 7007986837-1-1, and on what date.
- State the specific factual basis for each exclusion as applied to each contested window.
- State the source (written or oral) of the "engineer's report only" rule and identify any written claims-handling document that contains it.
- State the methodology and data sources Prieve used for the "no hail in Eden Prairie after 2020" assertion.
- State the methodology and data sources the appraisal panel used.
- Identify every appraisal file in Minnesota in the preceding five years in which Greg Tomes served as Farmers' party-appraiser.
- Identify every Minnesota hail claim 2020 forward in which Farmers denied or partially denied based on a Prieve Engineering opinion.
- Identify every Minnesota Farmers personnel who has read, drafted, or applied the "engineer's report only" rule.

**Defense-hour estimate.** Interrogatory drafting (defense side): 30–80 hours. Each set of 25 interrogatories typically generates 20–60 hours of objections, supplemental answers, and meet-and-confer time.

### 3.5 Respond to Requests for Admission

Each RFA forces Farmers to admit or deny a discrete, dispositive fact. The most cost-efficient plaintiff moves include RFAs locking in:

- (a) the policy was in force 7/13/2024;
- (b) hail is a covered peril;
- (c) the 2020 claim approved exactly 2 casement windows;
- (d) Brau personally inspected on 9/6/2024 and observed dents;
- (e) the 9/3/2024 denial was issued before any forensic engineer was engaged;
- (f) the 11/4/2024 denial relied on the Prieve report;
- (g) the umpire produced no report or estimate;
- (h) the policy contains no provision requiring evidence to come from an engineer;
- (i) the 9/12/2025 appraisal award found hail as the cause of loss for the awarded windows;
- (j) Farmers' 9/18/2025, 9/19/2025, and 10/14/2025 written descriptions of the awarded scope are not identical to each other;
- (k) Christian Dudley signed the 9/3/2024 denial;
- (l) Bradlee Monson articulated the "engineer's report only" rule by phone on 2/3/2025;
- (m) Nicholas Brau confirmed the "engineer's report only" rule by voicemail on 2/10/2025;
- (n) the family's NWS, City permit, and neighbor-canvass evidence was received by Farmers in the 1/31/2025, 2/10/2025, and 5/28/2025 submissions;
- (o) Farmers did not reopen the claim between 2/10/2025 and the 8/3/2025 appraisal demand.

**Defense-hour estimate.** RFA responses force Farmers' counsel to coordinate with Farmers' personnel and review the record before answering — 15–40 hours for a careful 30-RFA set. Each "deny" forces Farmers to defend the denial at trial; each insincere "deny" exposes Farmers to Rule 37(c)(2) cost-shifting under Minnesota Rule 36.02. This is one of the highest cost-to-Farmers-per-hour-of-plaintiff-work ratios in the case.

### 3.6 Defend Rule 30(b)(6) Institutional Deposition

The Rule 30(b)(6) notice is among the most expensive single events in the case. Properly noticed topics force Farmers to designate, prepare, and produce a corporate representative to testify on:

- Claims-handling policies and supervisor training in effect 7/13/2024 forward.
- The 2/3/2025 articulation of the "engineer's report only" rule.
- The basis for the 9/3/2024 denial.
- Farmers' retention and management of Prieve Engineering — across this claim and as a general practice in Minnesota.
- The 9/12/2025 appraisal scope and methodology.
- Farmers' position on each exclusion as applied to each contested window.
- Farmers' acceptance/rejection log for non-engineer-sourced evidence in hail claims.
- Records-retention policy for adjuster inspection notes.
- Farmers' financial condition (only if D1 motion is granted).

The Rule 30(b)(6) representative cannot punt on these — Farmers must designate someone to answer fully and make a "conscientious good-faith endeavor" to prepare them. Preparing a Rule 30(b)(6) witness to testify on 10+ institutional topics typically requires 40–120 hours of witness-prep time alone, plus the corresponding amount of defense-counsel time. The deposition itself is typically 1–3 full days.

**Defense-hour estimate.** Rule 30(b)(6) prep + defense of deposition: 80–200 hours. Pattern-of-practice topics (for C3) can double this.

## 4. Compounded Burdens — Where a Single Plaintiff Move Forces Farmers to Defend the Same Fact Multiple Ways

The most cost-efficient plaintiff moves are those where a single legal theory or piece of evidence forces Farmers to defend it in multiple procedural postures and against multiple legal standards. These are the "burden multipliers."

### 4.1 The "Engineer's Report Only" Rule

A single sentence — Bradlee Monson's 2/3/2025 statement that Farmers would not review non-engineer evidence — anchors the following defense burdens for Farmers:

- **B1 Prong 1 ("fairly debatable")** — Farmers must show this rule was either misunderstood or consistent with reasonable claims-handling.
- **B1 Prong 2 (knowledge/reckless disregard)** — Farmers must show the rule was not a supervisor-level deliberate disregard of the policy.
- **B2 fraud** — the rule is one of the three candidate misrepresentations; Farmers must defeat falsity, scienter, intent, reliance, and damages.
- **C3 MCFA** — the rule is the foundation of the "deceptive practice" framing under § 325F.69, requiring pattern-of-practice discovery.
- **D1 punitive damages** — the rule is the centerpiece of the "deliberate disregard" showing under § 549.20.
- **§ 72A.201 violation evidence inside A1** — the rule violates Subd. 6(d) (refusing to consider material evidence).
- **A1 / A2 contract-construction** — the rule has no policy basis, so it is evidence Farmers acted outside the contract.

**One plaintiff fact → seven separate defense lines of work.** Burden multiplier: ~7×.

### 4.2 The Missing Brau 9/6/2024 Inspection Record

A single document gap supports:

- **Rule 37 motion to compel** (and the underlying production fight).
- **Spoliation / adverse-inference instruction** at trial preparation.
- **§ 72A.201 Subd. 4(1) violation evidence** inside B1 (failure to maintain claim records).
- **D3 spoliation factual support** for adverse inferences.
- **§ 549.20 Subd. 3 concealment factor** for punitive damages.
- **B1 Prong 2 (knowledge/reckless disregard)** — Farmers' adjuster observed dents that the same day Farmers had said did not exist.
- **A1 breach of contract** — Brau's observations would have been the primary insurer-side factual baseline that the panel relied on; their absence supports the scope-gap argument.
- **A3 (vacate/modify appraisal)** — the spoliation supports the Ground D *Mork* misfeasance theory.

**Burden multiplier: ~8×.**

The award form's "F1.7" / "B1.2" lump-$12,800 format triggers:

- **A1 Track 1 (unevaluated windows)** — Farmers must prove the panel evaluated every window the family claims.

## 5. Phase-by-Phase Defense-Cost Picture

The defense-cost curve in a Minnesota first-party hail-and-bad-faith case looks roughly like this for Farmers. Plaintiffs' moves at each phase determine how steeply the curve rises.

### 5.1 Filing through Rule 12 (months 0–3)

**Farmers' work.** Engage counsel; conflict check; initial case strategy; intake and assignment to litigation team; Rule 11 / Rule 12 review of complaint; possibly file Rule 12 motions on B2 (Rule 9.02), C3 ("in connection with the sale"), and/or A3 (90-day window); answer the complaint.

**Approximate hours.** 80–250.

**Plaintiff lever.** File v2 of the complaint with belt-and-suspenders pleading. Don't give Farmers an easy Rule 12 dismissal target.

### 5.2 Discovery Opening (months 3–6)

**Farmers' work.** Respond to initial Rule 34 RFPs; respond to interrogatories; respond to RFAs; coordinate ESI vendor; begin privilege log; produce the claim file; produce the 2020 claim file; defend objection-and-motion-to-compel cycles.

**Approximate hours.** 200–500. Higher with pattern-of-practice discovery in C3.

**Plaintiff lever.** Issue all initial discovery on the day of the answer. Don't stagger. The cost-imposition curve is steeper when Farmers is simultaneously responding to RFPs, interrogatories, RFAs, and the Rule 30(b)(6) notice.

### 5.3 Mid-Discovery (months 6–12)

**Farmers' work.** Continue ESI production; respond to supplemental discovery; defend Rule 37 motions; coordinate Rule 45 subpoena responses; prep Farmers personnel for depositions; defend 9–12 individual depositions plus Rule 30(b)(6); engage defense experts (forensic engineer, window-matching, claims-handling, possibly meteorologist); produce expert reports.

**Approximate hours.** 600–1,500. Higher with pattern-of-practice discovery and Rule 37 wins for the plaintiff.

**Plaintiff lever.** File the § 604.18 Subd. 4(a) motion to amend at the optimal point — late enough that the discovery record is rich, early enough that there is still time for substantial post-amendment discovery. This is the single most expensive defense event after pattern-of-practice discovery in C3.

### 5.4 Motion Practice (months 12–18)

**Farmers' work.** Brief and argue summary-judgment motions on B2, C3, and possibly A3; defend the *Quade* partial summary-judgment motion; defend the *Herll*/*Rymer* remand motion; respond to the § 549.191 motion to amend for punitive damages; brief and argue Daubert motions.

**Approximate hours.** 400–1,000.

**Plaintiff lever.** File summary-judgment motions in a defensive cluster — *Quade* preclusion partial SJ + *Herll*/*Rymer* remand + Daubert on Prieve in the same window — so that Farmers' counsel is briefing three large issues simultaneously.

### 5.5 Pre-Trial through Trial (months 18–24)

**Farmers' work.** Final witness preparation; motion in limine practice (typically 5–15 motions per side); jury-instruction conferences; trial preparation; trial itself (5–10 days); post-trial motions.

**Approximate hours.** 600–1,500.

**Plaintiff lever.** Make sure every Farmers personnel witness is on the trial-witness list. Every defense witness Farmers must prepare for trial is an additional 10–30 hours.

### 5.6 Total Defense-Hour Profile

**Conservative estimate.** 1,880–4,750 total defense hours.
**Likely midpoint.** ~3,000 hours.
**At $337/hour Minnesota civil-litigation rate.** **~$1.0 million in defense legal fees** at midpoint, before expert costs.
**Plus defense experts.** $100,000–$400,000.
**Plus appellate work if Farmers loses.** $50,000–$200,000.

**Bottom line for Farmers' settlement calculus.** Defending this case to verdict is a **$1.0–$1.5 million** outlay. The family's $310,000 sworn proof of loss is a smaller number than Farmers' cost of defending it to verdict. **That is the central settlement-leverage fact this document is built to produce.**

## 6. Plaintiff Levers That Maximize Farmers' Hours (Ordered by Cost-Efficiency)

A "cost-efficient" plaintiff move is one that consumes few plaintiff hours but forces Farmers into many defense hours. Ordered roughly from highest to lowest cost-efficiency:

### 6.1 Requests for Admission

**Plaintiff hours.** 4–10 hours to draft a 30-RFA set.
**Farmers' hours.** 15–40 hours to respond carefully (plus future cost-shifting exposure under Rule 36.02 for insincere denials).
**Ratio.** ~4×–10×.
**Why so efficient.** Each well-drafted RFA either locks in a fact the family otherwise has to prove, or forces Farmers to defend a denial of a fact in the record.

### 6.2 Rule 30(b)(6) Deposition Notice

**Plaintiff hours.** 10–20 hours to draft a comprehensive topic list.
**Farmers' hours.** 80–200 hours to prepare and defend.
**Ratio.** ~8×–10×.
**Why so efficient.** A single notice forces Farmers to gather institutional knowledge across multiple departments and prepare a single witness to bind the company on it.

### 6.3 Pattern-of-Practice Discovery in C3

**Plaintiff hours.** 15–40 hours to draft and meet-and-confer.
**Farmers' hours.** 150–500+ hours to search for, review, and produce.
**Ratio.** ~10×–12×.
**Why so efficient.** The MCFA's pattern-of-conduct framing opens discovery into other Farmers files — by far the most expensive single defense burden.

### 6.4 Targeted Rule 37 Motions on Specific Document Gaps

**Plaintiff hours.** 8–20 hours per motion.
**Farmers' hours.** 30–80 hours per motion plus possible fee-shifting.
**Ratio.** ~3×–5×.
**Why so efficient.** Each gap (Brau notes, Hancock workproduct, umpire records, Prieve underlying file) is a separate motion. They stack.

### 6.5 § 604.18 Subd. 4(a) Motion to Amend

**Plaintiff hours.** 30–60 hours to brief.
**Farmers' hours.** 80–150 hours to oppose plus collateral discovery costs.
**Ratio.** ~2×–3×.
**Why so efficient.** The motion itself is one event, but a granted motion materially changes the settlement calculus and opens further punitive-damages discovery.

### 6.6 § 549.191 Motion to Amend for Punitive Damages

**Plaintiff hours.** 30–60 hours.
**Farmers' hours.** 60–120 hours plus financial-condition discovery exposure.
**Ratio.** ~2×–4×.

### 6.7 *Herll*/*Rymer* Remand Motion

**Plaintiff hours.** 20–40 hours.
**Farmers' hours.** 40–80 hours, plus the remand itself if granted forces Farmers' panel to revisit scope (which may produce a higher award).
**Ratio.** ~2×.

### 6.8 *Quade* Partial Summary-Judgment Motion on Causation Preclusion

**Plaintiff hours.** 30–60 hours.
**Farmers' hours.** 50–100 hours.
**Ratio.** ~1.5×–2×.
**Why valuable.** Even if cost-ratio is modest, a granted motion forecloses six exclusions and is the case-defining moment.

### 6.9 Daubert Motion to Exclude/Limit Prieve

**Plaintiff hours.** 30–60 hours.
**Farmers' hours.** 60–120 hours plus rebuttal expert cost.
**Ratio.** ~2×–3×.

### 6.10 § 549.09 Subd. 1(b) Offer of Settlement (Filed at Filing)

**Plaintiff hours.** 3–6 hours.
**Farmers' hours.** 5–10 hours initially, but locks in prejudgment-interest accrual that grows monthly.
**Ratio.** Low on hours; high on monetary exposure.

### 6.11 Subpoenas to Hancock, Prieve, Pure Construction, Sierra Pacific, MN Department of Commerce, AG's Office

**Plaintiff hours.** 8–15 hours total.
**Farmers' hours (indirect via review of returned documents).** 40–100 hours.
**Ratio.** ~5×.

### 6.12 Two Plaintiffs Instead of One

**Plaintiff hours.** Trivial.
**Farmers' hours.** Doubles each plaintiff-related deposition, doubles each plaintiff-witness trial-prep cycle.
**Ratio.** Roughly 2× on plaintiff-witness work; case-wide ~10–15% increase in defense burden.

## 7. The Three Most Expensive Defense Burdens — and How to Force Each

Three defense burdens are categorically more expensive than any others. Plaintiffs should organize the litigation to make sure each one is fully triggered.

### 7.1 Pattern-of-Practice Discovery on the "Engineer's Report Only" Rule (under C3)

This is the single most expensive single discovery cycle in the case. Forcing it requires:

1. **Plead C3 in the initial complaint.** Plead under both Route 1 (§ 8.31) and Route 2 (§ 325F.70).
2. **Frame the "engineer's report only" rule as a deceptive practice in connection with the sale of merchandise.** Cite *Force v. ITT Hartford*. Anchor reliance under *Wiegand* and *Group Health Plan*.
3. **Serve broad pattern-of-practice Rule 34 requests** on day one of discovery — Minnesota Farmers hail-claim files where the rule was applied; internal communications using "engineer's report only" or substantively similar; supervisor-training materials; retention patterns with engineering firms.
4. **Meet-and-confer carefully and move to compel under Rule 37.01** at the first sign of resistance. Each motion-to-compel cycle costs Farmers more than it costs the family.
5. **Notice the Rule 30(b)(6) institutional topic** on the rule's origin and application.

**Defense cost.** 150–500+ hours plus possible defense-expert engagement on institutional claims-handling reasonableness.

### 7.2 § 604.18 Subd. 4(a) Bad-Faith Motion (with Underlying Discovery)

Forcing this requires:

1. **Develop the underlying record before filing the motion.** The six § 72A.201 violations in `B1.md` § 3.1 must each be supported by record citations.
2. **Build the missing-Brau-notes adverse-inference foundation through Rule 37 motions** before filing the Subd. 4(a) motion.
3. **Capture the supervisor-rule discovery** (internal communications and training materials) before filing.
4. **File the Subd. 4(a) motion when the record is rich enough that Farmers cannot dismiss it on threshold grounds.** Typically months 9–15 of the case.

**Defense cost.** Subd. 4(a) opposition: 80–150 hours. Underlying discovery defense: 100–250 hours. Defense expert: $40,000–$120,000.

### 7.3 § 549.191 Punitive-Damages Motion (with Financial-Condition Discovery)

Forcing this requires:

1. **Plead B2 fraud in the initial complaint** with full Rule 9.02 particularity (B1 cannot carry punitives per § 604.18 Subd. 3(b); contract claims cannot carry punitives under *Wild v. Rarig*).
2. **Develop the deliberate-disregard record through B2 discovery** — Prieve underlying file, internal review communications, supervisor-rule discovery, the missing-Brau-notes spoliation.
3. **File the § 549.191 motion when the deliberate-disregard record supports clear-and-convincing showing.** Typically months 12–18.
4. **Open financial-condition discovery on the granted motion.** § 549.20 Subd. 3 makes Farmers' net worth, profitability, and reserve practices discoverable.

**Defense cost.** § 549.191 opposition: 60–120 hours. Financial-condition discovery defense: 80–200 hours. Bifurcated trial prep: 100–300 hours.

## 8. Tactical Sequencing — Order of Plaintiff Moves to Maximize Burden

The sequence in which plaintiffs deploy their cost-imposing moves matters because (i) discovery cycles compound, (ii) the § 604.18 and § 549.191 motions get stronger as the record matures, and (iii) Farmers' settlement calculus inflects sharply on certain events.

The recommended sequence:

**Month 0 (filing).** File v2 complaint. Issue § 549.09 Subd. 1(b) offer of settlement. Serve litigation-hold letter. Issue first Rule 34 RFP set, first interrogatories, first 30-RFA set, Rule 30(b)(6) notice. Serve Rule 45 subpoenas to Hancock, Prieve, Pure Construction, City of Eden Prairie, MN Department of Commerce, AG's office.

**Months 1–3.** Defend any Rule 12 motion. Begin meet-and-confer cycles on initial discovery. Document each Farmers non-cooperation.

**Months 3–6.** First wave of Rule 37 motions on document gaps (Brau notes, Hancock workproduct, Prieve underlying file). Continue serving supplemental discovery.

**Months 6–9.** Conduct Rule 30(b)(6) and individual depositions (Brau, Monson, Dudley, Tomes). Begin to develop the supervisor-rule discovery. Subpoena Foster and Roos.

**Months 9–12.** File the § 604.18 Subd. 4(a) motion to amend. Concurrently file *Quade* partial summary-judgment motion and *Herll*/*Rymer* remand motion. Daubert motion on Prieve.

**Months 12–15.** Once Subd. 4(a) is granted, file the § 549.191 motion to amend for punitive damages, supported by clear-and-convincing deliberate-disregard showing.

**Months 15–18.** Financial-condition discovery on Farmers. Trial preparation begins. Each Farmers witness is on the trial-witness list — forcing Farmers' counsel to prepare 9–12 witnesses for trial.

**Months 18–24.** Trial. Post-trial motions. Appellate preservation if Farmers loses.

**At each phase, plaintiffs should pause and assess Farmers' settlement appetite.** Each phase produces a settlement-conversation moment (see `offenseStrategy.md` § 7). The cost ratio shifts in favor of settlement at: (i) Rule 37 fee-shifting wins; (ii) Subd. 4(a) grant; (iii) *Quade* partial summary-judgment grant; (iv) § 549.191 grant; (v) Daubert exclusion of Prieve; (vi) adverse-inference instructions ordered for trial.

## 9. What Plaintiffs Should NOT Do — Burdens That Don't Pay Off

Not every plaintiff move is cost-efficient. A few moves consume plaintiff hours without commensurate defense burden:

- **Plead B3 (negligent claims handling)** — Foreclosed by *Haagenson*, *Short*, *Pillsbury*. Farmers will Rule 12 it in 20 hours; family will spend 40 hours pleading and defending. Net loss.
- **Plead A4 (unjust enrichment) as a freestanding count** — Foreclosed by valid express contract under *U.S. Fire Insurance Co. v. Minnesota State Zoological Board*, 307 N.W.2d 490 (Minn. 1981); *Holman v. CPT Corp.*, 457 N.W.2d 740 (Minn. App. 1990); *ServiceMaster v. GAB Bus. Servs.*, 544 N.W.2d 302 (Minn. 1996). Plead only as Rule 8.05(2) alternative if at all.
- **Plead C1, C2, C4 as freestanding counts** — Foreclosed by *Morris*. Use only as conduct evidence embedded in A1 and B1.
- **Argue the appraisal is non-binding because Tomes did not sign** — Foreclosed by § 65A.01 Subd. 3 (two-of-three is binding).
- **Pursue A3 Tier 3 (*Mork* vacatur) on the current record** — High bar; current facts support only inferential argument. Preserve in pleading but do not lead with it. *Mork* vacatur also destroys the *Quade* preclusion for the awarded windows — a strategic loss if pursued without misconduct evidence in hand.
- **Front-loaded settlement discussions before discovery wins** — Settlement value compounds with each discovery and motion win. Pre-discovery settlement discussions anchor low.

## 10. The Pro Se / Limited-Counsel Variant

If the family proceeds pro se or with limited-scope counsel, the cost-ratio analysis becomes even more favorable to the family because plaintiff hours have no monetary cost — the only constraint is family time. In that scenario:

1. **Cost-efficient moves remain the same** — RFAs, Rule 30(b)(6), pattern-of-practice discovery, Rule 37 motions, Subd. 4(a) motion.
2. **Pro se does not eliminate the legal sufficiency requirement.** The family must still plead competently, brief competently, and try the case competently. `bowl.md` documents the pre-filing checklist required to file a procedurally adequate complaint.
3. **The Hennepin County Self Help Center, MN Bar Association volunteer-lawyer pool, and limited-scope-representation lawyers** are real assets — see `EventDocuments/Copy of ComunicationLogs.docx.md` for the family's June 2025 outreach. Limited-scope counsel for the § 604.18 Subd. 4(a) motion alone might be a $5,000–$15,000 retainer that captures the single largest defense-cost-multiplier event in the case.
4. **Pro se litigants are entitled to a degree of latitude** but are held to the same Rule 11 standards as represented parties. Every cost-imposing move must be supportable on the merits.
5. **The cost-ratio analysis is the family's primary settlement leverage.** Farmers' defense calculus is the same whether the family is pro se or represented — the defense hours are the same. The asymmetric advantage is that the family's incremental cost approaches zero while Farmers' incremental cost stays at $337/hour and up.
