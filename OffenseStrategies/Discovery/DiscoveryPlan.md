# Discovery Plan — Lu/Hu v. Fire Insurance Exchange (d/b/a Farmers Insurance)

**Case:** Yibiao Lu & Jie Hu v. Fire Insurance Exchange (d/b/a Farmers Insurance)
**Policy:** 98055-49-65 | **Claim:** 7007986837-1-1
**Loss Date:** July 13, 2024
**Filing Deadline:** July 13, 2026
**Forum (assumed):** Hennepin County District Court (Minnesota state court)
**Prepared:** May 17, 2026

> **Purpose.** This is the master discovery plan for the breach-of-contract / declaratory-judgment / appraisal / fraud / consumer-fraud case against Farmers. It catalogs every written discovery instrument the family should serve, organizes them into tranches, identifies the specific records and admissions each is designed to capture, and lays out a 24-month sequencing plan.
>
> **What this plan covers.** Litigation hold and preservation; Rule 26 scheduling conference posture; Rule 26.01 initial disclosures; Rule 34 document requests; Rule 33 interrogatories; Rule 36 requests for admission; Rule 45 third-party subpoenas; expert-discovery framework under Rule 26.01(b); ESI strategy; privilege and protective-order handling; Rule 37 enforcement framework; and the overall discovery timeline.
>
> **What this plan does NOT cover.** Substantive witness-by-witness deposition strategy, deposition topic lists, prep outlines, and cross-examination preparation. Those belong in `OffenseStrategies/Deposition/`. This plan covers deposition *notices, scheduling, and Rule 30.02(f) topic-list design* but defers everything substantive to the deposition folder.
>
> **Strategic context.** This plan operationalizes `offenseStrategy.md` § 4.3 (the discovery-phase strategy section) and `WhatFarmersNeedToProveAndDo.md` (the defense-burden maximization analysis). The unifying premise: every discovery instrument should serve at least one of three goals — (1) produce evidence that supports a count or defeats a Farmers defense; (2) impose cost on Farmers' defense team in a ratio favorable to the family; (3) build the record for the eventual § 604.18 Subd. 4(a) motion and the § 549.191 punitive-damages motion.
>
> **Disclaimer.** Strategic plan, not legal advice or a Rule 11 filing. Each discovery instrument should be reviewed by a Minnesota-licensed attorney (or the family proceeding pro se, against the operative Minnesota Rules of Civil Procedure) before service.

## 1. Discovery Objectives

Every discovery request is designed to land in one or more of these objective buckets. When drafting any individual instrument, ask which bucket(s) it serves; if it serves none, it does not belong in the plan.

1. **Build the breach-of-contract (A1) damages record.** Establish the loss, the unevaluated windows, the matching theory's factual foundation, and the consequential water-intrusion damages.
2. **Lock in the *Quade* preclusion zone.** Force Farmers to admit the 9/12/2025 appraisal award found hail causation for F1.7 and B1.2, and force Farmers to articulate every exclusion's factual basis window-by-window.
3. **Develop the *Herll*/*Rymer* remand record.** Establish facial ambiguity of the award, the umpire's missing report, Farmers' inconsistent scope descriptions, and the panel's methodology gap.
4. **Build the § 604.18 Subd. 4(a) bad-faith record.** Anchor each of the six § 72A.201 violations identified in `B1.md` § 3.1 — premature denial, missing Brau notes, supervisor-level "engineer's report only" rule, refusal to weigh new evidence, falsified Prieve premise, serial silence.
5. **Build the B2 fraud / Rule 9.02 record.** Develop scienter, intent, reliance, and damages for each of the three candidate misrepresentations (Prieve's "no hail after 2020"; the 9/3/2024 "no other damages observed"; the supervisor's "engineer's report only" rule).
6. **Trigger the C3 MCFA pattern-of-practice discovery.** Force Farmers to defend other Minnesota hail-claim files where the engineer-only rule was applied, the Prieve retention pattern, and the institutional acceptance/rejection log for non-engineer evidence.
7. **Develop the spoliation / adverse-inference record.** Document the missing Brau 9/6/2024 notes, the absent Hancock narrative, the umpire's missing report, the produced-wrong-2014 document, Farmers' litigation-hold posture.
8. **Build the D1 punitive-damages record.** Develop deliberate-disregard evidence and (once § 549.191 is granted) financial-condition discovery.
9. **Defeat the Subd. 4(b) appraisal-bar defense.** Establish on the record what the panel *did not* evaluate.
10. **Impose defense-cost asymmetry.** Each discovery cycle should drive Farmers' defense hours up in a multiple greater than the family's investment of hours.

Each instrument below is tagged with the objectives it serves.

### 2.3 Rule 26.06 Conference — Within 30 Days of Initial Answer Due Date

Under Minn. R. Civ. P. 26.06 and General Rule of Practice 111, the parties must confer within 30 days of the initial due date for an answer and produce a written Rule 26.06 report addressing scope of discovery, ESI, claims of privilege, sequencing, and protective orders.

**The family's posture at the conference.** Forward-leaning but proportional. The family should propose:

- **Discovery period.** 12–15 months from the scheduling order — long enough to absorb the C3 pattern-of-practice discovery cycle and the Rule 30(b)(6) prep cycle, but short enough to keep Farmers' calendar pressure on.
- **Phased discovery.** No. Reject any Farmers proposal to phase discovery (e.g., to limit early discovery to A1 only and defer C3/B2 until later). Phasing helps the well-resourced defendant and hurts the family.
- **ESI protocol.** Negotiated terms covering custodians (named above), date range (1/1/2020 forward for C3 pattern discovery, 7/1/2024 forward for the loss-specific discovery), search terms (see § 7 below), production format (TIFF + load file + native for spreadsheets/email), claw-back agreement under Rule 26.02 / Minn. R. Evid. 502.
- **Privilege log standard.** Document-by-document logging for emails and case-notes; categorical logging permissible only for repetitive billing records and routine status communications.
- **Protective order.** A standard two-tier protective order (Confidential / Highly Confidential — Attorneys' Eyes Only) with the family or counsel handling the AEO designations.
- **Limits on discovery.** Resist Farmers' likely request to cap interrogatories at the default 50 (Minn. R. Civ. P. 33.01) — request an enlargement to 75 given the multi-count complaint; cap depositions at the default 10 plus reasonable additions for institutional/foundational witnesses (Rule 30.02 / Minn. Gen. Prac. 111 do not impose a hard cap but courts typically expect a stated reason for exceeding 10).
- **Expert deadlines.** Plaintiff expert disclosure 180 days before trial; defendant expert disclosure 120 days before trial; expert depositions to close 45 days before trial. Aligns with Rule 26.01(b)(2) and gives the family time to develop forensic engineering + matching + claims-handling experts.

**Plaintiff hours.** 8–15 hours to prepare for and participate in the conference.

## 4. Rule 33 Interrogatories — Multi-Tranche Plan

Minnesota Rule 33.01 sets a default limit of 50 interrogatories per party (counting discrete subparts), unless modified by the scheduling order. Negotiate an enlargement to 75 at the Rule 26.06 conference.

### 4.1 Tranche A — Foundational Interrogatories (with Tranche A RFPs)

**INT.1.** Identify every Farmers employee, agent, attorney, or third-party administrator who took any action on claim 7007986837-1-1. For each, state name, title, dates of involvement, role, and current contact information.

**INT.2.** For each of the six exclusions cited in the 11/4/2024 denial letter, state the specific factual basis Farmers contends supports application of that exclusion to each contested window at 17756 George Moran Dr. Identify the documents and witnesses Farmers will rely on to support each.

## 11. Rule 37 Enforcement Framework

Rule 37 motions are the family's principal tool for converting Farmers' non-cooperation into either production or sanctions. Every Rule 37 motion that is granted supports later adverse-inference arguments and feeds the § 604.18 Subd. 4(a) record.

### 11.1 Mandatory Pre-Motion Steps

Under Minn. R. Civ. P. 37.01, every motion to compel must be accompanied by a certification that the movant has in good faith conferred or attempted to confer with the opposing party in an effort to resolve the dispute without court action.

**The family's standard pre-motion practice:**

1. Send a written meet-and-confer letter identifying the specific deficiency and proposing resolution.
2. Allow 7–14 days for response.
3. Conduct a phone or video meet-and-confer; document the conversation with a follow-up email summarizing positions.
4. If unresolved, send a final pre-motion letter giving 5 more days; if still unresolved, file the motion.

This sequence is itself a discovery-cost lever: each cycle forces Farmers' counsel to invest meet-and-confer hours that produce no document yet still bill the file.

## 16. Discovery Dispute Resolution

When a discovery dispute cannot be resolved by meet-and-confer:

1. **First.** File a Rule 37.01 motion to compel. Hennepin County District Court typically requires a Note of Issue / Statement of the Case before motions; check current local rules.
2. **Second.** Request a discovery-issues conference with the court under Rule 26.06 or General Rule of Practice 115.
3. **Third.** If procedural impasse continues, request appointment of a discovery special master under General Rule of Practice 144 (rare in homeowner cases but available). The family pays half the master's fees unless the court reallocates; consider only if discovery has substantially derailed.

Each escalation step is itself a defense-hours driver. Use the meet-and-confer record as the foundation for every escalation.
