## 1. The Strategic Theory of the Motion

### 1.1 Realistic Relief, Not Wholesale Exclusion

A Daubert-style motion under Minn. R. Evid. 702 rarely results in the complete exclusion of an expert. Courts generally permit experts to testify and let cross-examination address weaknesses. The realistic and strategically valuable relief targets **specific, identifiable defects in Prieve's report** and seeks **narrow exclusionary or limiting orders**. The motion's prayer for relief should therefore ask the court to:

1. **Exclude testimony or opinion that no hail occurred in Eden Prairie after 2020** or substantively similar. This assertion is the foundation of Prieve's report and is provably false on the record.
2. **Limit Prieve to factual testimony about observations he personally made** on 10/9/2024 — particularly the dents he catalogued at 12+ window locations. (This is *helpful* to the family; it locks in Prieve's own concession that damage exists.)
3. **Exclude Prieve's causation opinions on individual windows** where his report contains no quantitative methodology, no terminal-velocity calculation, no material-specific dent-depth modeling, and no peer-reviewed support.
4. **Exclude the unidirectional-hail generalization on the east-siding penetration** as unsupported by analysis of the actual 7/13–7/14/2024 multi-round storm event.
5. **Exclude the "wear/tear / 2020-already-paid" causation theory** as unsupported by any window-by-window analysis and contradicted by Farmers' own 2020 Xactimate scope (which approved only 2 casement windows).

This is the achievable relief. Even partial success — say, an order excluding the "no hail after 2020" assertion and the directional generalization — materially weakens Farmers' position.

### 1.2 The "Fairly Debatable" Connection

The Daubert motion is not a standalone evidentiary skirmish; it is **a load-bearing element of the bad-faith case**. Farmers' principal Prong 1 defense to the § 604.18 motion is that it had a "fairly debatable" basis to deny — namely, the Prieve report. If the Prieve report is excluded or substantially limited:

- Farmers cannot point to a coherent technical basis for the 11/4/2024 denial.
- The remaining basis — Hancock's blanket "Damaged – No" photo tags from 8/29/2024 — is itself subject to Daubert / methodology attack via the Grimm deposition.
- Farmers' "reasonable basis" defense collapses for Prong 1.
- Prong 2 follows: continuing to rely on a report Farmers knew (or recklessly disregarded) was foundationally wrong supports knowing or reckless disregard.

The Daubert motion is therefore the single biggest pre-trial event for the § 604.18 case after the Subd. 4(a) motion itself.

### 1.3 The "Foundational Reliability" Frame Under Rule 702

Minnesota Rule of Evidence 702 governs expert testimony. The Minnesota Supreme Court in *Goeb v. Tharaldson*, 615 N.W.2d 800 (Minn. 2000), reaffirmed the **Frye-Mack standard** for novel scientific evidence, but Rule 702 itself applies more broadly to expert testimony. Under Rule 702 as construed in *Doe 76C v. Archdiocese of St. Paul and Minneapolis*, 817 N.W.2d 150 (Minn. 2012), and the surrounding line of cases, expert testimony must satisfy:

1. The witness is qualified by knowledge, skill, experience, training, or education.
2. The opinion is helpful to the trier of fact.
3. **The opinion has foundational reliability** — i.e., the methodology is sound and the data adequate.
4. The opinion is not within the lay knowledge of the jury.

The attack on Prieve focuses primarily on **prong 3 — foundational reliability**. Prieve is qualified as an engineer; his opinions on hail damage are within the topic; the jury would benefit from technical testimony. But the foundational reliability of his specific opinions — the "no hail after 2020" assertion, the methodology used to evaluate individual windows, the directional generalization, the 2020 attribution — is questionable, and Rule 702 requires foundational reliability *of the specific opinions offered*, not merely of the expert's general qualifications.

### 1.4 The Frye-Mack Question

Frye-Mack applies to **novel scientific evidence**. The family's motion does not need to invoke Frye-Mack against Prieve — the issue is not whether his methodology is "novel" but whether his methodology is sufficiently rigorous to support the specific opinions he advances. That is a Rule 702 foundational-reliability question.

The family's own expert (the forensic hail-damage expert per `bowl.md` § 4) applies the HailShapes peer-reviewed scientific framework, which is **well-established** scientific methodology — not novel. So Frye-Mack does not gate the family's expert either. The motion is a clean Rule 702 motion on both sides.

### 1.5 The Daubert / Frye-Mack Procedural Difference

In Minnesota state court:
- The motion is properly styled as a **Motion in Limine** under Minn. R. Civ. P. 16 and the court's scheduling order.
- It is typically filed **30–60 days before trial**, after expert disclosures are complete.
- Some courts allow Rule 702 challenges as part of summary-judgment briefing; others reserve them for the motion-in-limine cycle.
- The court typically resolves the motion by written order after briefing and possibly an evidentiary hearing.

The package in this folder is drafted as a motion in limine.

### 1.6 Why Now (Sequencing)

The Daubert motion should be filed **after** the Prieve deposition (per `Deposition/2.1Prieve_Nathan.md`). The deposition lays the foundation:

- The deposition produces Prieve's own admissions on methodology gaps.
- The deposition produces the data-source admissions on the "no hail after 2020" assertion.
- The deposition produces the reconciliation problem with the 2020 Xactimate scope.
- The deposition transcript becomes the principal evidentiary support for the motion.

Anticipated timing:
- Prieve deposition: Month 9 of discovery.
- Daubert motion filed: Month 12–14 (after motion-in-limine window opens per scheduling order).
- Hearing: Month 14–15.
- Trial: Month 18–24.

If the scheduling order allows earlier filing, the family may want to file the motion concurrently with or shortly after the § 604.18 Subd. 4(a) motion — combining the bad-faith case and the Daubert attack creates more pressure.

### 1.7 The Strategic Trade-Off: Don't Exclude the Useful Concessions

Prieve's report is a **mixed bag** for the family:

- **Useful:** Prieve's catalog of dents at 12+ window locations. This is direct admission that damage exists across most of the home — contradicting Farmers' 9/3/2024 "no other damages observed" finding.
- **Useful:** Prieve's identification of the 2020 claim's scope (2 casement windows) — confirms Farmers' own records.
- **Harmful:** The "no hail after 2020" assertion.
- **Harmful:** The wear/tear and 2020-already-paid causation theories.
- **Harmful:** The directional generalization.

A motion that excludes Prieve *entirely* would also exclude his helpful admissions. The motion is therefore drafted to **exclude the harmful portions while preserving the helpful portions**. Specifically, the prayer for relief asks the court to limit Prieve to:

- Factual observations he personally made on 10/9/2024 (including the catalog of dents).
- Causation opinions only on windows where Prieve performed quantitative analysis with adequate methodology — which, on this record, is none.
- Not to opine on the "no hail after 2020" question or on the 2020 attribution.

This careful framing — exclude the harmful, preserve the helpful — is the strategic core of the package.

## 2. Anticipated Defense Response

Farmers will oppose this motion. Anticipated defense arguments and the family's rebuttal:

### 2.1 "Prieve Is a Qualified Engineer"

Farmers will assert Prieve's qualifications and argue that any methodology question goes to weight, not admissibility.

**Rebuttal:** Rule 702 requires foundational reliability *of the specific opinions offered*. Prieve's qualifications as a general forensic engineer do not establish foundational reliability of his "no hail after 2020" assertion (which is a weather-records question, not an engineering question) or of causation opinions reached without quantitative methodology.

### 2.2 "Weight Not Admissibility"

Farmers will argue that methodological criticism is properly addressed by cross-examination at trial.

**Rebuttal:** Cross-examination is the appropriate vehicle for testing methodological *disagreements among methodologies*. But where an expert's methodology is *missing* — no quantitative analysis at all, no terminal-velocity calculation, no peer-reviewed support — that is a foundational reliability failure, not a methodological disagreement. *Doe 76C* and the Rule 702 line of cases require minimum foundational support; the absence of any quantitative basis falls below that threshold.

### 2.3 "The Methodology is Industry-Standard"

Farmers will argue Prieve's visual-inspection methodology is industry-standard for insurance-defense engineering.

**Rebuttal:** The standard is Rule 702 foundational reliability, not insurance-industry consensus. The HailShapes peer-reviewed scientific framework — which the family's own expert applies — establishes the methodological floor. Prieve's report does not meet that floor on causation; it meets it only on observation. The relief sought reflects this: testify to observation, do not opine on causation without quantitative basis.

### 2.4 "The 'No Hail After 2020' Assertion is Properly a Jury Issue"

Farmers will argue the weather-records question is a credibility question for the jury, not a foundational issue.

**Rebuttal:** The "no hail after 2020" assertion is not a credibility question — it is a factually false statement provable by independent public records (NWS Twin Cities/Chanhassen; City of Eden Prairie permits; the 9/12/2025 appraisal award's hail-causation finding). Allowing Prieve to repeat the assertion at trial would be permitting an expert to mislead the jury on a factual matter the record conclusively resolves. That is precisely the kind of foundational unreliability Rule 702 is designed to prevent.

### 2.5 "The Appraisal Award Does Not Bind Causation Findings for Unevaluated Windows"

Farmers will note that the 9/12/2025 appraisal award's *Quade* preclusion applies only to F1.7 and B1.2 — not to other windows.

**Rebuttal:** True, but irrelevant to the Daubert question. Prieve's specific factual assertions (no hail after 2020; the 2020 attribution; the directional generalization) can be tested for foundational reliability independently of the appraisal preclusion. *Quade* applies to the panel's findings; Rule 702 applies to Prieve's methodology.

### 2.6 "This Is a Disguised Summary-Judgment Motion"

Farmers may argue the Daubert motion is really an attempt to undermine the merits of the case at the in limine stage.

**Rebuttal:** The motion seeks evidentiary exclusion under Rule 702 — a routine pre-trial mechanism. It is not a dispositive motion. The merits of the underlying claim remain for trial; this motion addresses only the evidentiary foundation of one expert's opinions.

## 3. The Three Likely Outcomes — And the Family's Response to Each

### 3.1 Best Case: Substantial Limitation Granted

The court orders Prieve excluded from testifying:
- That no hail occurred in Eden Prairie after 2020;
- To causation conclusions for individual windows without quantitative analysis;
- To the directional generalization on east-siding penetration;
- To the wear/tear / 2020-already-paid theory.

Prieve can testify to his factual observations on 10/9/2024 (including the catalog of dents at 12+ locations) and to general professional background.

**Family's response:** Use the order to (i) anchor the § 604.18 Subd. 4(a) motion's Prong 1 argument that Farmers had no fairly debatable basis after 11/4/2024 when Prieve's report rested on excluded foundations; (ii) compress Farmers' trial-defense theory; (iii) cite the exclusion on summary judgment to lock in *Quade* preclusion for F1.7/B1.2 without Prieve as a competing voice; (iv) press settlement leverage.

### 3.2 Middle Case: Partial Limitation Granted

The court excludes the most clearly foundationally-unsupported items (likely the "no hail after 2020" assertion) but allows Prieve to testify subject to cross on the other elements.

**Family's response:** Use the limited exclusion to focus trial cross-examination. The "no hail after 2020" exclusion alone materially helps because that assertion is the foundation Prieve relied on; without it, his causation opinions lack their factual predicate. Even partial relief is highly valuable.

### 3.3 Worst Case: Motion Denied

The court rules that all of Prieve's testimony is admissible subject to cross.

**Family's response:** 
- Use the motion's preparation to drive trial cross-examination of Prieve.
- The motion itself, even if denied, builds the appellate record.
- The family's own forensic-engineering expert directly rebuts each element of Prieve's report.
- The factual disproof of "no hail after 2020" (NWS report; City permits; neighbor canvass; appraisal award) reaches the jury through direct exhibits.
- The motion serves a cost-imposition purpose regardless of outcome — Farmers must brief substantive opposition and likely retain a methodological-defense expert.

- Retaining a methodological-defense expert: ~$20K–$60K.
- Hearing preparation and argument: ~20–40 hours.

### 4.3 Cost Ratio

Roughly **15×–30×** in the family's favor (the family's pro se cost is ~$2K–$5K; Farmers' defense cost is ~$50K–$120K). This is among the most cost-efficient motions in the family's playbook.

### 4.4 Strategic Risk

The principal risk is that filing and losing a Daubert motion signals to the court that the case is over-aggressive. To mitigate:

- File at the right time (post-deposition; after the underlying record supports the motion).
- File only the substantive arguments that have clear support; do not over-argue.
- Couple the motion with the § 604.18 Subd. 4(a) motion so the court sees a coherent strategic posture.
- Be respectful of Prieve as a person and address only the methodology / foundation issues.

## 6. What Happens at the Hearing

Anticipated procedural flow:

1. **Family's argument** (pro se Bill or limited-scope counsel) — ~20–30 minutes. Focus on:
   - Rule 702 foundational reliability standard.
   - The "no hail after 2020" factual disproof.
   - The methodology gap (no quantitative analysis).
   - The 2020 attribution contradiction.
2. **Farmers' opposition** — ~20–30 minutes. Anticipate: qualifications + weight-not-admissibility + industry-standard methodology + this-is-disguised-summary-judgment.
3. **Family's reply** — ~10–15 minutes. Refocus on Rule 702's foundational reliability requirement.
4. **Court takes the matter under advisement**, issues a written order within 30–60 days.

If the court orders an evidentiary hearing (sometimes called a "Daubert hearing" though Minnesota doesn't formally use that term), the family's forensic-engineering expert would testify briefly to authenticate his / her own methodology comparison with Prieve's. This is a substantial additional preparation step.
