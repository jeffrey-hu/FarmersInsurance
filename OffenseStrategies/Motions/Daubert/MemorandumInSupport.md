## INTRODUCTION

Plaintiffs Yibiao Lu and Jie Hu respectfully submit this Memorandum in Support of their Motion to Limit the Expert Testimony of Nathan Prieve, P.E., of Prieve Engineering LLC, pursuant to Minn. R. Evid. 702.

This Motion seeks not to exclude Mr. Prieve in his entirety, but to narrow his trial testimony to the matters for which his report demonstrates foundational reliability. Specifically, the Motion targets four categories of opinions in Mr. Prieve's report dated November 4, 2024 (Prieve Engineering File #241008) that do not satisfy Rule 702: (i) his weather-records assertion that no hail occurred in Eden Prairie after 2020; (ii) his causation opinions on individual windows reached without quantitative methodology; (iii) his directional generalization dismissing east-facing damage; and (iv) his attribution of observed damage to wear/tear or to the 2020 storm event.

Mr. Prieve will remain free at trial to testify to his professional qualifications, to his factual observations of dents at nineteen or more window locations (plus a sliding door) during his October 9, 2024 inspection, and to any opinions for which his methodology meets Rule 702's foundational-reliability standard. The relief requested is narrowly tailored to exclude only those opinions whose foundation has been demonstrated, through discovery and through the deposition of Mr. Prieve, to be inadequate under controlling Minnesota law.

## II. THE LEGAL STANDARD

### A. Minnesota Rule of Evidence 702

Minn. R. Evid. 702 governs the admissibility of expert testimony in Minnesota courts. The Rule states:

> "If scientific, technical, or other specialized knowledge will assist the trier of fact to understand the evidence or to determine a fact in issue, a witness qualified as an expert by knowledge, skill, experience, training, or education, may testify thereto in the form of an opinion or otherwise. The opinion must have foundational reliability. In addition, if the opinion or evidence involves novel scientific theory, the proponent must establish that the underlying scientific evidence is generally accepted in the relevant scientific community."

Minn. R. Evid. 702 (emphasis added). The "foundational reliability" requirement was added to Rule 702 by the Minnesota Supreme Court in 2006 and is now the controlling reliability standard for all expert testimony in Minnesota state courts. *See Doe 76C v. Archdiocese of St. Paul and Minneapolis*, 817 N.W.2d 150, 168 (Minn. 2012).

### B. The Frye-Mack Standard for Novel Scientific Evidence

The Frye-Mack standard — derived from *Frye v. United States*, 293 F. 1013 (D.C. Cir. 1923), and *State v. Mack*, 292 N.W.2d 764 (Minn. 1980) — controls the admissibility of **novel scientific evidence** in Minnesota. *See Goeb v. Tharaldson*, 615 N.W.2d 800 (Minn. 2000) (reaffirming Frye-Mack). The standard requires that "the scientific principle or technique involved be generally accepted in the particular field." *Goeb*, 615 N.W.2d at 814.

The instant Motion does not invoke Frye-Mack. Mr. Prieve's methodology is not "novel" in the scientific sense — it is conventional visual inspection. The issue is whether his application of that methodology to the specific opinions he offers satisfies Rule 702's foundational-reliability requirement. That is a separate analysis from Frye-Mack.

### C. The Court's Gatekeeping Role

Minnesota courts have repeatedly recognized the trial court's role as gatekeeper of expert testimony under Rule 702. *See, e.g., Doe 76C*, 817 N.W.2d at 168 (court's gatekeeping role applies "regardless of the type of expert testimony"); *Hagenkord v. State*, 100 N.W.3d 526 (Minn. 2004) (similar); *State v. Iennaco*, 829 N.W.2d 65, 69 (Minn. Ct. App. 2013) ("[T]he trial court has 'the responsibility of acting as the gate-keeper of an expert's opinion.'").

The gatekeeping role is not limited to Frye-Mack-style novel-science questions. It extends to whether any expert opinion has the necessary foundational reliability — including whether the methodology, the underlying data, and the application of the methodology to the facts are sufficient to support the opinion offered. *See Doe 76C*, 817 N.W.2d at 170 ("Foundational reliability is the touchstone of Rule 702's reliability inquiry.").

### D. The Burden on the Proponent of Expert Testimony

The proponent of expert testimony bears the burden of establishing foundational reliability. *See Anderson v. Crown Cork & Seal Co.*, No. A06-2126, 2008 WL 467287 (Minn. Ct. App. 2008) (unpublished). Mere recitation of qualifications or general claims to methodology is insufficient. The proponent must demonstrate that the specific opinions offered rest on adequate methodology and data.

### E. Application of Rule 702 to Insurance-Defense Engineering Reports

Although the Minnesota Supreme Court has not specifically addressed the application of Rule 702 to first-party property-insurance engineering reports, federal courts applying Daubert principles in similar contexts have repeatedly held that engineering opinions on hail-damage causation must rest on quantitative or methodological foundation, not on visual generalization. *See, e.g., Herll v. Auto-Owners Ins. Co.*, 879 F.3d 293 (8th Cir. 2018) (applying Minnesota law to ambiguous appraisal awards in hail-damage case); *Cincinnati Insurance Co. v. Rymer Cos., LLC*, No. 24-3356 (8th Cir. Mar. 27, 2026) (similar). The Eighth Circuit's careful treatment of engineering testimony in these cases supports the conclusion that visual-inspection-only opinions on hail causation lack the foundational reliability required under Minnesota's Rule 702.

## III. ARGUMENT

The Court should limit Mr. Prieve's testimony for four independent reasons. Each defect identified below independently warrants the relief sought. Together they require it.

---

### I. The "No Hail in Eden Prairie After 2020" Assertion Lacks Foundational Reliability and Should Be Excluded

Mr. Prieve's foundational premise — that no hail occurred in Eden Prairie after 2020 — is factually false, methodologically unsupported, and disproven by independent public records. The Court should exclude any testimony to this effect.

#### A. The Assertion is Factually False

The assertion that "the most recent hail in Eden Prairie [was] reported in 2020" is disproven on five independent record sources:

**1.** The NWS Twin Cities/Chanhassen Forecast Office storm report for 7/13–7/14/2024 documents hail up to 3.5 inches in east-central Minnesota, hail up to 4 inches near Monticello/Big Lake, and a 48 MPH wind gust at the Eden Prairie ASOS station at 1:00 AM on 7/14/2024. *(Ex. 4.)* The Eden Prairie ASOS station is approximately one mile from the subject property. The NWS storm report places severe-storm conditions at Eden Prairie at the date and time of the loss.

**2.** City of Eden Prairie public building-permit records document two window-replacement permits on properties within walking distance of the subject property, both issued in the period Mr. Prieve characterizes as hail-free; the affected homeowners confirmed the work was hail-related in Plaintiffs' neighbor canvass:
   - EP187829, issued 11/15/2023, for 17490 George Moran Drive (window replacement); *(Ex. 10; Ex. 9.)*
   - EP190835, issued 5/9/2024, for 17708 George Moran Drive (window replacement). *(Ex. 10; Ex. 9.)*

**3.** Plaintiffs' neighbor canvass (2/4–2/7/2025) reached 19 immediate neighbors; 14 confirmed hail damage and insurance-covered repairs in 2023 and/or 2024. *(Ex. 9.)*

**4.** Commercial weather-data services Plaintiffs cited in their 1/31/2025 and 5/28/2025 submissions — Stormersite and Interactive Hail Maps — report hail events in or near Eden Prairie in 2022, 2023, and 2024. *(Exs. 11, 16.)*

**5.** Defendant's own appraisal panel — including neutral umpire Wade Roos — found hail as the cause of loss for windows at the subject property on 9/12/2025. *(Ex. 6.)* That finding cannot be reconciled with Mr. Prieve's assertion that no hail occurred in Eden Prairie after 2020.

#### B. The Methodology Underlying the Assertion is Inadequate

Mr. Prieve's deposition confirmed that the methodology supporting his "no hail after 2020" assertion was limited to two database queries — the Stormersite database and the NOAA Storm Events Database. *(Ex. 7 at [pages].)* He did not consult:

- The NWS Twin Cities/Chanhassen Forecast Office records;
- The Eden Prairie ASOS station observational record;
- City of Eden Prairie building-permit records;
- Local news archives reporting hail events; or
- The HailShapes peer-reviewed scientific literature.

*(Id.)* No competent forensic engineer offering a categorical opinion that hail had not occurred in a specific city after a specific date could rely on two commercial database queries while ignoring the most direct and authoritative public records available — the National Weather Service's own records for that specific location.

#### C. The Foundational Reliability Standard Forecloses the Assertion

A foundationally reliable opinion under Rule 702 must rest on adequate data and adequate methodology. *Doe 76C*, 817 N.W.2d at 170. An opinion that rests on incomplete data — and that is directly contradicted by the data the expert did not consult — fails the foundational-reliability test. The court should not permit Mr. Prieve to repeat at trial an assertion that is shown to be factually false on five independent record sources and that rests on a methodology that ignored the most authoritative available data.

#### D. The Assertion Cannot Be Cured by Cross-Examination

Defendant may argue that any defect in Mr. Prieve's "no hail" assertion goes to weight rather than admissibility. That argument fails for three reasons:

**1.** The foundational-reliability standard does not yield to cross-examination as a substitute for foundation. Where the expert's opinion lacks the basic data and methodology to support it, the opinion is inadmissible, not merely subject to credibility attack. *See Doe 76C*, 817 N.W.2d at 168–70.

**2.** Permitting the "no hail" assertion before the jury — even subject to cross — risks confusing the trier of fact. A jury may give weight to an expert's pronouncement even when subsequent cross-examination demonstrates the pronouncement was foundationally inadequate. Rule 702's gatekeeping function exists precisely to prevent such opinions from reaching the jury in the first place.

**3.** The assertion is not merely an opinion subject to disagreement — it is a factual statement contradicted by independent public records. Cross-examination cannot rehabilitate an assertion the underlying data conclusively disprove.

#### E. The Relief Sought

The Court should exclude any testimony or opinion by Mr. Prieve to the effect that no hail occurred in Eden Prairie at any time after 2020, or substantively similar testimony. Mr. Prieve may, of course, testify to factual observations of physical damage. He may not, however, repeat a factually false categorical statement that the foundational-reliability requirement of Rule 702 forecloses.

### II. Mr. Prieve's Window-By-Window Causation Opinions Lack the Methodological Rigor Required by Rule 702 and Should Be Limited

Mr. Prieve's report makes window-by-window causation opinions — distinguishing observed damage that he attributes to hail from damage he attributes to wear/tear, marring, corrosion, inherent vice, neglect, or faulty construction. The report contains no quantitative analysis to support these distinctions. The Court should limit Mr. Prieve to testifying about his factual observations and exclude his causation opinions unless and to the extent his methodology meets Rule 702's foundational-reliability standard.

#### A. The Methodology Gap

Mr. Prieve's report contains:

**1.** No quantitative impact-force analysis. The HailShapes peer-reviewed literature establishes that hail damage to extruded-aluminum window components can be modeled by reference to (a) hail size; (b) terminal velocity of the hail at the impact location; (c) the modifying effect of wind on terminal velocity; and (d) the material-specific failure modes of the surface impacted. *(Ex. 17, Plaintiffs' Forensic Engineer's Affidavit.)* Mr. Prieve performed none of these analyses.

**2.** No terminal-velocity calculations for hail consistent with the 7/13–7/14/2024 storm event. NWS data establishes that the storm produced hail up to 4 inches in diameter with wind gusts reaching 48 MPH at Eden Prairie ASOS. Standard terminal-velocity formulas yield resultant impact velocities for hail of that size and wind conditions. Mr. Prieve performed no such calculations.

**3.** No material-specific dent-depth modeling for extruded aluminum. The HailShapes literature describes the relationship between dent depth, hail size, and impact velocity on extruded-aluminum surfaces. *(Id.)* Mr. Prieve performed no such modeling.

**4.** No peer-reviewed citations or methodological comparison. The report cites no scientific authority for the visual-only causation methodology applied.

#### B. The Importance of Methodological Rigor for Causation Opinions

Causation opinions on hail damage are not within ordinary lay knowledge. A jury cannot independently distinguish hail damage from wear/tear, marring, corrosion, or other causes by visual inspection alone. Expert testimony on causation is therefore essential — and the foundational-reliability requirement of Rule 702 is essential to protect against unreliable expert testimony reaching the jury.

A purely visual-inspection methodology, without quantitative or peer-reviewed support, cannot reliably distinguish hail damage from non-hail damage with the precision Mr. Prieve's window-by-window opinions purport to provide. The methodology may be sufficient to establish *that* damage exists. It is not sufficient to establish *the cause* of damage with the categorical precision required to attribute damage to wear/tear or to a prior storm rather than to the most recent storm.

#### C. The Family's Forensic Engineer Confirms the Methodological Gap

Plaintiffs' retained forensic-engineering expert applies the HailShapes peer-reviewed scientific framework — quantitative impact-force analysis, terminal-velocity calculations, material-specific dent-depth modeling — window-by-window at the subject property. *(Ex. 17.)* The contrast between Mr. Prieve's methodology and the family expert's methodology is stark. Mr. Prieve's visual-only methodology cannot support causation opinions at the level of certainty Rule 702 requires.

#### D. The Relief Sought

The Court should limit Mr. Prieve to testifying to:

**1.** His factual observations of physical conditions at the subject property on October 9, 2024 — including specifically his catalog of dents, marks, and indentations at the 19+ window locations (plus a sliding door) identified in his report. *(Ex. 1 at pp. 4–9.)*

**2.** Any opinion for which his methodology has been demonstrated to satisfy Rule 702's foundational-reliability standard.

The Court should exclude:

**3.** Any causation opinion on individual windows reached without quantitative impact-force analysis, terminal-velocity calculation, material-specific dent-depth modeling, or other foundationally reliable methodology.

**4.** Any opinion attributing observed damage to wear/tear, the 2020 storm event, marring, corrosion, inherent vice, neglect, or faulty construction, except to the extent supported by such foundationally reliable methodology.

### III. Mr. Prieve's Directional Generalization (Conclusion 4) Lacks Foundational Reliability and Should Be Excluded

In Conclusion 4 of his report, Mr. Prieve invokes a generalized storm direction to dismiss east-facing damage as non-hail: "recent storm activity in the area generally consists of storms moving from west to east with hail originating from a westerly direction. The penetration in siding appears at the east side, opposite typical origination of hail." *(Ex. 1 at p. 10.)* The same west-origin premise underlies Mr. Prieve's treatment of damage on the east elevation of the dwelling generally. (The siding penetration Mr. Prieve referenced has since been excluded from Plaintiffs' claim as attributable to a hail storm prior to 2020; the analysis below addresses the foundational reliability of the directional reasoning itself, which is independently refuted by Defendant's own award.)

This conclusion rests on foundationally unsupported propositions, each independently fatal under Rule 702.

#### A. Defendant's Own Binding Appraisal Award Confirms Hail Damage on the East Elevation

The directional premise is refuted by Defendant's own appraisal. The 9/12/2025 award covered two windows on opposite elevations of the home — **F1.7 on the front (west) elevation and B1.2 in the rear (east) family room.** By awarding the rear/east B1.2 window as a hail loss, Defendant's own appraisal panel confirmed that hail damaged the **east** face of the dwelling. Under *Quade v. Secura Ins.*, 814 N.W.2d 703 (Minn. 2012), that causation finding is binding on Defendant. Mr. Prieve's premise that hail at this property originates only from the west — and therefore cannot have struck the east elevation — is thus contradicted by Defendant's own binding award. An expert opinion whose central factual premise is foreclosed by the proponent's own appraisal award cannot satisfy the reliability requirements of Rule 702.

#### B. Generalized Directional Patterns Cannot Substitute for Storm-Specific Analysis

Mr. Prieve's conclusion treats "recent storm activity in the area generally" as if it were applicable to the specific 7/13–7/14/2024 storm event. It is not. The NWS storm report documents the 7/13–7/14/2024 event as a multi-round series of severe thunderstorms — not a single, unidirectional storm. *(Ex. 4.)* The storm produced large hail across a wide swath of east-central Minnesota with directional variation typical of multi-round convective systems. *(Id.)*

An expert opinion on the cause of damage at a specific location during a specific storm event must rest on analysis of that storm event — not on a generalization about typical storm patterns in the region. Mr. Prieve's report contains no such storm-specific analysis.

#### C. The Generalization is Not Supported by the Record

Mr. Prieve's deposition confirmed that he did not analyze the 7/13–7/14/2024 storm's specific path, hail-producing cells, or wind patterns. *(Ex. 7 at [pages].)* The generalization that "recent storm activity ... generally consists of storms moving from west to east" was not informed by data about the actual storm. It is a regional climatological observation, not a forensic analysis.

#### D. Multi-Round Storms Produce Multi-Directional Impacts

Plaintiffs' forensic engineer's affidavit explains that multi-round convective storm events — like the 7/13–7/14/2024 event documented by the NWS — can produce hail impacts from multiple directions on the same structure. *(Ex. 17.)* Indeed, wind direction is not fixed even within a single storm: surface winds veer and back as a thunderstorm cell approaches, passes, and departs, and gust fronts, outflow boundaries, and a rotating updraft drive hail toward different faces of a structure at different moments of the same event. *(Ex. 17.)* Damage on the east elevation — including the rear/east B1.2 window Defendant's own panel awarded as a hail loss — is consistent with such multi-directional impact patterns. Mr. Prieve's report does not engage with this analysis.

#### E. The Relief Sought

The Court should exclude Mr. Prieve's conclusion that east-facing damage cannot have been caused by hail because of a generalized west-to-east storm direction. Mr. Prieve may testify, if he chooses, to his factual storm-direction observations, but he may not opine that hail could not have caused damage on the east elevation without storm-specific analysis demonstrating foundational reliability — particularly where Defendant's own appraisal award confirms hail damage on that elevation (B1.2).

### IV. Mr. Prieve's "Wear/Tear / 2020-Already-Paid" Causation Theory Lacks Foundation and Should Be Excluded

Mr. Prieve's report attributes the observed damage to wear/tear or to the 2020 storm event. The 11/4/2024 denial letter, relying on Mr. Prieve's report, cites six exclusions on these grounds: wear/tear, marring, corrosion/deterioration/decay/rust, inherent vice or latent defect, neglect/lack of maintenance, and faulty/defective construction. *(Ex. 1 at p. 3.)*

This causation theory fails Rule 702 for two independent reasons.

#### A. The Theory is Mathematically Incompatible with Defendant's Own 2020 Records

Defendant's 2020 Xactimate scope on Claim No. 3013539499-1 approved **exactly two (2) wood-casement windows**. *(Ex. 8, 2020 Final Document, line items 1–4.)* The 2020 settlement payment of $125,044.03 reflects this two-window scope plus roof, gutter, downspout, and exterior door line items.

Mr. Prieve's report documents observed damage at **nineteen or more distinct windows (plus a sliding door)** across the subject property. *(Ex. 1 at pp. 4–9.)* Mr. Prieve was aware, at the time of writing his report, that Defendant's 2020 Xactimate approved only two casement windows. *(Ex. 7 at [pages] (Prieve Dep. Tr.).)*

The mathematical impossibility is plain: if the 2020 storm caused only the two windows Defendant paid for in 2020, the 2020 storm cannot account for the additional seventeen or more damaged windows Mr. Prieve documented. Mr. Prieve's report does not address this discrepancy. He performed no window-by-window analysis distinguishing 2020-attributable damage from later damage. The "2020-already-paid" theory has no foundation.

#### B. The Wear/Tear Theory Lacks Methodological Support

Mr. Prieve's attribution of damage to wear/tear, marring, corrosion, inherent vice, neglect, or faulty construction lacks any window-by-window quantitative analysis. The report does not:

**1.** Identify the specific wear/tear, marring, corrosion, inherent vice, neglect, or faulty construction characteristics attributable to each individual window.
**2.** Quantify the contribution of any such non-hail cause to the observed damage.
**3.** Distinguish, on a window-by-window basis, hail-attributable damage from non-hail damage.
**4.** Compare its conclusions against peer-reviewed scientific literature on the failure modes of extruded-aluminum windows.

A causation theory that attributes a category of damage to one of six possible non-hail causes, without identifying which cause applies to which window, is not a foundationally reliable opinion under Rule 702. It is a conclusory dismissal.

#### C. The Theory Is Contradicted by Defendant's Own Subsequent Findings

Defendant's own appraisal panel — including neutral umpire Wade Roos and Plaintiffs' appraiser John Foster — found hail as the cause of loss for the awarded windows on 9/12/2025. *(Ex. 6.)* That finding is binding on Defendant under *Quade v. Secura Ins.*, 814 N.W.2d 703 (Minn. 2012), for the F1.7 and B1.2 windows. It also independently disproves the categorical "wear/tear / 2020" causation theory for those windows. The Court should not permit Mr. Prieve to testify at trial to causation conclusions that Defendant's own subsequently-procured neutral factfinder has rejected.
