## 3. The Methodology Required for a Foundationally Reliable Hail-Damage Causation Opinion

3.1 A foundationally reliable opinion on whether a specific surface was damaged by hail during a specific storm event must rest on four pillars, each grounded in peer-reviewed scientific methodology:

   **(a) Storm-Specific Data Analysis.** The expert must consult the most authoritative available records for the specific storm event — National Weather Service storm reports, regional ASOS / AWOS observational data, radar reflectivity data, and corroborating public records (building permits, neighbor reports, news archives). General regional climatological observations are not a substitute for storm-specific data analysis.

   **(b) Quantitative Impact-Force Analysis.** The expert must calculate the impact force (and resulting impact energy) for hail of the size documented by the storm-specific record. The calculation must account for hail size, terminal velocity, and the modifying effect of wind on terminal velocity. The result establishes whether the storm was capable of producing the observed damage on the surface in question.

   **(c) Material-Specific Damage Modeling.** The expert must model the relationship between impact force and the failure mode of the specific material struck. For extruded-aluminum window frames, peer-reviewed literature establishes the dent-depth function as a function of impact velocity and aluminum properties. For glass insulating units, the analysis differs and considers stress propagation and seal integrity.

   **(d) Window-by-Window or Component-by-Component Analysis.** The expert must perform the impact-force and material-specific modeling for each individual surface in question — not as a generalized statement applicable to "the house" but as a specific calculation applicable to each window, panel, or component evaluated.

3.2 The four pillars above are well-established in the forensic hail-damage literature. See, e.g., [list peer-reviewed citations from HailShapes literature compilation]. They are not novel methodology; they are the standard of practice in foundationally reliable forensic-engineering work on hail-damage causation.

3.3 My methodology applies all four pillars in evaluating the damage at the subject property. My full report, when delivered, will document each calculation, each model, and each window-by-window result.

## 4. Analysis of the Prieve Report

I have analyzed the Prieve Report against the four pillars described in Section 3 above. Each pillar yields the same conclusion: the Prieve Report does not satisfy the methodological standard required for foundationally reliable causation opinions on hail damage.

### 4.1 Pillar 1 — Storm-Specific Data Analysis: NOT PERFORMED

The Prieve Report's "Weather Records" section relies on two database queries — the Stormersite database and the NOAA Storm Events Database — for the assertion that "the most recent hail in Eden Prairie [was] reported in 2020." *(Ex. 1 at p. 9.)*

The Prieve Report does not reference:

(a) The National Weather Service Twin Cities/Chanhassen Forecast Office storm report for July 13–14, 2024, which documents severe thunderstorms across Minnesota with hail up to 3.5 inches in east-central Minnesota and up to 4 inches near Monticello and Big Lake, and a 48 MPH wind gust at the Eden Prairie ASOS station at 1:00 AM on July 14, 2024;

(b) The Eden Prairie ASOS observational data for July 13–14, 2024;

(c) City of Eden Prairie building-permit records documenting post-2020 window-replacement repairs in the immediate neighborhood, confirmed as hail-related by the affected homeowners in Plaintiffs' neighbor canvass;

(d) Local news archives.

The Prieve Deposition confirms that Mr. Prieve did not consult these authoritative sources for the storm-specific data analysis. *(Prieve Dep. Tr. at [pages].)*

The omission of NWS data — the most authoritative weather record available for a Minnesota storm event — is a fundamental methodological deficiency. No competent forensic engineer offering a categorical statement that no hail occurred at a specific location during a specific period would rely on two commercial database queries while ignoring the official National Weather Service records for that specific location and period.

### 4.2 Pillar 2 — Quantitative Impact-Force Analysis: NOT PERFORMED

The Prieve Report contains no impact-force calculation, no terminal-velocity calculation, no resultant impact-energy calculation, and no analysis of the modifying effect of wind on hail terminal velocity. The Prieve Deposition confirms that Mr. Prieve did not perform such calculations. *(Prieve Dep. Tr. at [pages].)*

The absence of quantitative impact-force analysis means that the Prieve Report cannot demonstrate, on a foundationally reliable basis, that the documented dents at the subject property were *not* caused by hail of the size and velocity documented for the 7/13–7/14/2024 storm event. The methodology is purely visual — and visual observation alone cannot reliably establish hail vs. non-hail causation.

### 4.3 Pillar 3 — Material-Specific Damage Modeling: NOT PERFORMED

The Prieve Report does not model the dent-depth function for extruded-aluminum window frames or the stress-propagation analysis for glass insulating units. The Prieve Deposition confirms that no such modeling was performed. *(Prieve Dep. Tr. at [pages].)*

Material-specific damage modeling is essential because different materials respond differently to hail impact. Extruded aluminum is particularly susceptible to small-deformation denting from hail impact velocities consistent with the 7/13–7/14/2024 storm event. A peer-reviewed methodology would model the expected dent characteristics for the storm conditions and compare those expected characteristics to the observed dents. The Prieve Report performs no such comparison.

### 4.4 Pillar 4 — Window-By-Window Analysis: NOT PERFORMED

The Prieve Report documents dents, marks, and indentations at twenty-five or more distinct windows (plus a sliding door) at the subject property. *(Ex. 1 at pp. 4–9.)* The Report's causation conclusions, however, are stated in generalized form — categorical attributions to "wear/tear," "marring," or the 2020 storm event — without window-by-window quantitative analysis distinguishing hail-attributable damage from non-hail damage.

This omission is particularly significant because:

(a) Defendant's 2020 Xactimate scope approved only two casement windows. The Prieve Report's documentation of damage at twenty-five or more locations (plus a sliding door) cannot be reconciled with the 2020 attribution unless the Report demonstrates, window-by-window, that each documented dent is consistent with the 2020 event and inconsistent with the 7/13–7/14/2024 event. The Report performs no such analysis.

(b) The categorical attribution of damage to "wear/tear" without window-by-window analysis is not a forensic-engineering opinion; it is a conclusory dismissal.

### 4.5 The Directional Generalization in Conclusion 4

The Prieve Report's Conclusion 4 invokes a west-origin storm generalization to dismiss east-facing damage, stating that the east-side observation "appears at the east side, opposite typical origination of hail." *(Ex. 1 at p. 10.)*

This conclusion treats a generalized regional storm-pattern observation as if it were determinative for a specific storm event. It is not. The 7/13–7/14/2024 storm event documented by the NWS was a multi-round convective system. Multi-round storms produce hail impacts from multiple directions on the same structure. Furthermore, wind direction is not fixed even within a single storm: surface winds veer and back as the cell approaches, passes, and departs, and gust fronts, outflow boundaries, and a rotating updraft can drive hail toward different faces of a structure at different moments of the same event. A foundationally reliable analysis of east-facing damage must engage with the actual storm-specific data — including the multi-round nature of the storm, the directional variation of the convective cells, and the localized wind patterns — and would model the expected directional pattern of hail impact at the subject property. The Prieve Report performs no such analysis. Moreover, the directional premise is contradicted by Defendant's own appraisal award, which awarded a rear/east window (B1.2, family room) as a hail loss alongside a front/west window (F1.7) — demonstrating that hail in fact damaged the east elevation of the dwelling.

### 4.6 Summary

The Prieve Report does not satisfy any of the four pillars of foundationally reliable hail-damage analysis. Storm-specific data analysis is incomplete; quantitative impact-force analysis is absent; material-specific damage modeling is absent; window-by-window analysis is absent. The Report's specific opinions — that no hail occurred in Eden Prairie after 2020, that the documented damage is not consistent with hail, that east-facing damage is not hail-caused, and that the damage is attributable to wear/tear or to the 2020 storm event — lack the methodological foundation required for foundationally reliable opinions under Minn. R. Evid. 702.

## 5. The Public Records Disproving the "No Hail After 2020" Assertion

5.1 The Prieve Report's "no hail after 2020" assertion is factually incorrect. The following public records document hail events at or near the subject property after 2020:

   **(a) The NWS Twin Cities/Chanhassen Forecast Office storm report for July 13–14, 2024.** The report documents severe thunderstorms across Minnesota with hail up to 4 inches in diameter and a 48 MPH wind gust at the Eden Prairie ASOS station at 1:00 AM on July 14, 2024. The Eden Prairie ASOS station is approximately one mile from the subject property.

   **(b) City of Eden Prairie public building-permit records.** Permits EP187829 (issued 11/15/2023; 17490 George Moran Drive) and EP190835 (issued 5/9/2024; 17708 George Moran Drive) are public records documenting window replacement at properties within walking distance of the subject property; the affected homeowners confirmed the work was hail-related in Plaintiffs' neighbor canvass. The permits themselves record the work type, address, contractor, and dates and do not state a cause.

   **(c) Plaintiffs' neighbor canvass (February 4–7, 2025).** Fourteen of nineteen neighbors confirmed hail damage and insurance-covered repairs in 2023 and / or 2024.

   **(d) Stormersite database records for the Eden Prairie area** show hail-event reports in 2022, 2023, and 2024.

   **(e) The September 12, 2025 Appraisal Award** by the § 65A.01 panel including neutral umpire Wade Roos found hail as the cause of loss at the subject property.

5.2 Each of these public records, considered independently, contradicts Mr. Prieve's assertion that no hail occurred in Eden Prairie after 2020. Considered together, they conclusively disprove the assertion.

5.3 The omission of NWS data in the Prieve Report is particularly notable because the National Weather Service is the authoritative source of storm-event records for any United States location. A forensic-engineering analysis of a Minnesota storm event that does not consult the NWS records for that event is methodologically deficient. The omission cannot be characterized as a minor oversight; it is the omission of the single most important data source for the storm-specific analysis Mr. Prieve was required to perform.

## 8. Opinions

Based on the foregoing, I offer the following expert opinions:

**Opinion 1.** The Prieve Report's foundational premise — that no hail occurred in Eden Prairie after 2020 — is factually false. The omission of NWS records from the analysis underlying that premise is a methodological deficiency that renders the premise foundationally unreliable.

**Opinion 2.** The Prieve Report's window-by-window causation opinions lack foundational reliability because the underlying analysis omits quantitative impact-force analysis, terminal-velocity calculation, material-specific dent-depth modeling, and window-by-window comparison with peer-reviewed methodological standards.

**Opinion 3.** The Prieve Report's Conclusion 4 (the directional generalization dismissing east-facing damage) lacks foundational reliability because it relies on a generalized regional storm-pattern observation rather than on storm-specific analysis of the actual 7/13–7/14/2024 multi-round storm event, and because its west-origin premise is contradicted by Defendant's own appraisal award confirming hail damage to a rear/east (B1.2) window.

**Opinion 4.** The Prieve Report's attribution of damage to wear/tear or to the 2020 storm event lacks foundational reliability because the Report performs no window-by-window analysis to support the attribution and is mathematically incompatible with Defendant's own 2020 Xactimate scope (which approved only two casement windows).

**Opinion 5.** Each of the four opinions identified in the Prieve Report and discussed above fails the standard of foundational reliability for forensic hail-damage analysis. The Court should limit the trial admissibility of those opinions accordingly under Minn. R. Evid. 702.
