## C. MOTION TO LIMIT EXPERT TESTIMONY OF NATHAN PRIEVE

Plaintiffs Yibiao Lu and Jie Hu (collectively, "Plaintiffs"), pursuant to Minn. R. Evid. 702, Minn. R. Civ. P. 16, and the Court's Scheduling Order, respectfully move the Court for an Order limiting the trial testimony of Nathan Prieve, P.E., of Prieve Engineering LLC, as follows:

### I. Grounds

The Court should limit Mr. Prieve's testimony because specific opinions contained in his report dated November 4, 2024 (Prieve Engineering File #241008, appended to Defendant's November 4, 2024 denial letter — Exhibit 1 to the accompanying Memorandum) lack foundational reliability under Minn. R. Evid. 702. Specifically, four categories of Mr. Prieve's opinions do not satisfy Rule 702's reliability requirements:

1. **The Weather-Records Assertion.** Mr. Prieve's statement that "[t]he most recent hail in Eden Prairie [was] reported in 2020" lacks adequate foundational support. The assertion is factually contradicted by:
   - the National Weather Service Twin Cities/Chanhassen Forecast Office storm report documenting severe thunderstorms across Minnesota on July 13–14, 2024 with hail up to 4 inches in diameter and a 48 MPH wind gust at the Eden Prairie ASOS station at 1:00 AM on July 14, 2024;
   - City of Eden Prairie public building-permit records for permits EP187829 (issued 11/15/2023, cause: hail) and EP190835 (issued 5/9/2024, cause: hail);
   - the September 12, 2025 Appraisal Award, signed by neutral umpire Wade Roos, finding hail as the cause of loss for the awarded windows at the subject property; and
   - additional commercial weather-data records (Stormersite; Interactive Hail Maps) showing post-2020 hail reports in or near Eden Prairie.
2. **Causation Opinions Without Quantitative Methodology.** Mr. Prieve's window-by-window causation opinions are reached without any quantitative impact-force analysis, terminal-velocity calculation, material-specific dent-depth modeling, or peer-reviewed methodological support — the rigor required for a foundationally reliable causation opinion under Rule 702 as construed in *Doe 76C v. Archdiocese of St. Paul and Minneapolis*, 817 N.W.2d 150 (Minn. 2012).
3. **The Directional Generalization.** Mr. Prieve's conclusion that east-facing damage cannot have been caused by hail because "recent storm activity in the area generally consists of storms moving from west to east with hail originating from a westerly direction" rests on a generalization, does not engage with the actual multi-round, multi-directional 7/13–7/14/2024 storm event documented by the National Weather Service, and is contradicted by Defendant's own binding appraisal award — which awarded a rear/east window (B1.2, family room) as a hail loss alongside a front/west window (F1.7), confirming hail damage on the east elevation.
4. **The Wear/Tear and 2020-Already-Paid Causation Theory.** Mr. Prieve's attribution of observed damage to wear/tear or to the 2020 storm event lacks foundational support. Defendant's own 2020 Xactimate scope on Claim No. 3013539499-1 approved only two (2) wood-casement windows. Mr. Prieve's report documents damage at nineteen or more distinct windows (plus a sliding door) across the property. The 2020 scope cannot account for damage at more than the two windows Defendant itself paid for in 2020, leaving Mr. Prieve's attribution of the remaining damage to wear/tear without any window-by-window quantitative or methodological support.

### II. Relief Sought

Plaintiffs respectfully request that the Court enter an Order:

**A.** Excluding any testimony or opinion by Nathan Prieve to the effect that no hail occurred in Eden Prairie, Minnesota after 2020, or any substantively similar assertion about the absence of hail at or near the subject property between the date of the 2020 hail claim and July 13, 2024;

**B.** Limiting Nathan Prieve's testimony on causation to factual observations of physical damage that he personally observed during his inspection of the subject property on October 9, 2024 (including the dents, marks, and indentations he catalogued at the front/west triple window, the upper-west 3-unit bedroom window, the upper southwest bedroom window, the east family-room bay projection, the upper-east window above the four-season porch, the four-season porch SE/E/NE/N windows, the kitchen east window, the main-level NE 3-unit window, the upper-north window above the kitchen, the rear/east garage window, and the east-siding penetration);

**C.** Excluding any opinion by Nathan Prieve attributing the observed damage to causes other than hail, including without limitation attribution to wear/tear, the 2020 storm event, marring, corrosion, inherent vice, neglect, or faulty construction, except to the extent such opinion is supported by quantitative analysis, terminal-velocity calculation, material-specific dent-depth modeling, or other foundationally reliable methodology under Minn. R. Evid. 702;

**D.** Excluding Nathan Prieve's conclusion that east-facing damage cannot have been caused by hail because of a generalized west-to-east storm direction;

**E.** Granting such other and further relief as the Court deems just and proper.

   b. To factual observations of physical conditions at the subject property that he personally observed during his October 9, 2024 inspection;
   c. To opinions on questions for which his methodology has been demonstrated to satisfy Minn. R. Evid. 702.

4. To the extent Defendant offers Mr. Prieve's testimony beyond the scope permitted by this Order, Plaintiffs may object at trial.

## F. Filing and Procedural Notes

1. **Hearing scheduling.** Coordinate with Hennepin County District Court calendar before serving the Notice. Most judges require at least 21 days' notice between filing and hearing for non-emergency motions.
2. **Page limits.** Under Minn. R. Gen. Prac. 115, dispositive motions are typically limited to 35 pages of memorandum unless leave is granted. Daubert motions are technically non-dispositive, but Hennepin County practice often treats them as substantive. Confirm the operative page limit before filing.
3. **Filing fees.** A motion-in-limine filing fee may apply; check the Hennepin County District Court fee schedule.
4. **E-filing.** Hennepin County uses Minnesota's electronic-filing system (MNCIS). Pro se filers can register and file electronically.
5. **Pre-hearing meet-and-confer.** Some judges require a written certification of meet-and-confer before non-dispositive motions. Confirm and include a certification page if required.
6. **Concurrent motions.** Consider whether to combine this Daubert motion with the § 604.18 Subd. 4(a) motion to amend or with other in-limine motions for the same hearing date. Combining motions reduces total court time but can dilute focus; the strategic call depends on the schedule and the judge's preferences.
7. **Reply brief.** Plaintiffs will typically file a reply brief 5–10 days before the hearing. Reserve time in the schedule for it.
