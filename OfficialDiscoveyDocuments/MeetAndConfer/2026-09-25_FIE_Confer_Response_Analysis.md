## 3. The 25-RFA cap

### 3.1 What the rules say

- **No numeric limit.** Minn. R. Civ. P. 36.01 caps nothing. Compare Rule 33.01(a), which limits interrogatories to 50. The drafters capped one device and not the other.
- **Objections go request by request, with reasons.** Rule 36.01: "If objection is made, the reasons therefor shall be stated." An answer must "specifically deny the matter or set forth in detail the reasons why the answering party cannot truthfully admit or deny." A blanket "oppressive/unduly burdensome/not proportional" objection to 116 requests does not state reasons for each one.
- **Remedy for insufficient objections.** Rule 36.01: the requesting party "may move to determine the sufficiency of the answers or objections," and the Court may order an answer served or find the matter admitted.
- **A limit requires a motion.** Rule 26.03 lets the Court protect a party from "oppression, or undue burden or expense," but only "[u]pon motion … and for good cause shown." A responding party cannot give itself a protective order by letter. Secondary authority is to the same effect: courts may limit the number of RFAs on their own initiative or on a motion for protective order. [vLex §36.6 (Washington practice; persuasive only)]
- **Proportionality is real.** Rule 26.02(b) limits discovery to what is "relevant to any party's claim or defense and proportional to the needs of the case." It weighs the importance of the issues, **the amount in controversy**, relative access to information, the parties' resources, importance to the issues, and burden against benefit.

### 3.2 Why FIE's position is weak

1. **FIE's own filed plan.** On 9/1/2026 FIE represented that "[d]iscovery should not be conducted in phases or limited to particular issues" and "[t]he parties do not recommend any limitations on discovery beyond those set forth in the Minnesota Rules of Civil Procedure." Plaintiffs' 9/4 plan said the same. A 25-RFA cap is a limitation beyond the Rules, proposed three weeks later, after the Court entered its order on the basis of those plans.
2. **Requests to admit are the cheapest device to answer.** Admit, deny, or explain. No search, no production.
3. **FIE's letter concedes several requests outright.** RFAs 113–115 ask FIE to admit it does not contend anyone else should be the defendant, be added, or be named differently. The letter's Item 5 answer admits the substance. Several RFAs in A–U track facts FIE's Answer already admits or that its own documents state.
4. **Timing.** FIE's responses are due **Wednesday, October 21, 2026**. If FIE wants relief it has to seek it. An objection served on time avoids deemed admission (Rule 36.01) but leaves Plaintiffs a sufficiency motion.

### 3.3 FIE's "relevant to the appraisal award" framing is wrong on the pleadings

The Complaint pleads (1) breach of contract, (2) declaratory judgment under Minn. Stat. ch. 555 on coverage, (3) non-preclusion, or in the alternative modification or vacatur, of the 9/12/2025 award, and (4) Minnesota Consumer Fraud Act remedies under § 325F.69 / § 325F.70 / § 8.31, subd. 3a. It also reserves a § 604.18 motion. Scope under Rule 26.02(b) is "any party's claim **or defense**," and FIE's own plan says discovery "will be needed on Plaintiff's claims and Defendant's defenses." Expect FIE to run this narrowing theory across all three response sets. Have the answer ready now: **the pleadings define relevance, not FIE's theory of the case.**

### 3.4 Why the proportionality argument could still land

Be realistic:

- On 9/8 Judge Anderson found Plaintiffs' proposed timeline "more than one year beyond the timeline for similar cases." This judge is already watching proportionality in this case.
- The volume is large for one residential hail claim: 53 RFPs, 12 interrogatories, 141 RFAs, and a drafted Second Set of 80 more RFAs. FIE is visibly building a record that Plaintiffs are over-discovering ("we will not continue to 'confirm' …").
- FIE will argue the amount in controversy is small, because the award's $6,400 ACV is under the $17,720 deductible. Plaintiffs will answer with the $310,000 claimed loss and the unappraised components.
- Under Scheduling Order ¶4, the first stop is a **three-page letter and a conference call with the Court**. The judge will form a view quickly. The more reasonable-looking side at that call has the advantage.

## 4. Item 5: the proper-party concession

**What changed.** FIE's counsel wrote that "Fire Insurance Exchange is a proper party to this lawsuit" and that the jurisdiction defense was pleaded only "so as not to waive it." Together with `KeyDecisions.md` Decision 2 (both the 2014 application and the 2023 renewal name FIE as underwriter), the contract-claim joinder risk before **11/16/2026** is now low. The contingency plan (`Filings/Discovery/Joinder_Deadline_Contingency_2026-09-19.md`) can drop from "decision pending" to "monitor."

**What did not change.**

- **The defense is still pleaded.** Affirmative Defense 1 alleges "a lack of jurisdiction over Defendant FIE" and failure to state a claim.
- **Minnesota law on keeping it alive.** In *Patterson v. Wu Family Corp.*, 608 N.W.2d 863 (Minn. 2000), the court held that a defendant who pleads a service or jurisdiction defense in its answer does **not** waive it merely by taking part in discovery. It **does** waive it by "affirmatively invoking the jurisdiction of the district court" to obtain merits relief such as summary judgment without first or simultaneously moving to dismiss. So the defense can sit dormant through discovery. If FIE moves for summary judgment on the award without moving to dismiss, *Patterson* is the answer.
- **Sub-question (c) is unanswered.** FIE did not say whether it would stipulate to amendment without a timeliness objection if its production later shows another entity is proper.
- **The adjuster-employer question is untouched.** Which entity employed Dudley, Brau and Monson still matters to the § 604.18 and CFA theories (RFAs 4–6).

**Recommended.** Ask FIE either to (i) sign a short stipulation, to be filed, that FIE is the proper defendant and withdraws the "lack of jurisdiction" portion of Affirmative Defense 1, or (ii) simply **admit RFAs 113–115 on 10/21**, which is consistent with its letter. Option (ii) is the lower-friction path and costs FIE nothing. Put those three RFAs at the top of any priority list. Keep watching the 10/15–10/21 responses for any "Farmers" collective answer on RFPs 52–53, Interrogatories 7–8, and RFAs 4–8.

## 5. Items 1 and 2: expert discovery. FIE is right on the rule, and that matters.

### 5.1 The rule

Minn. R. Civ. P. 26.02(e) opens: "Discovery of facts known and opinions held by experts, otherwise discoverable pursuant to Rule 26.02(b) and **acquired or developed in anticipation of litigation or for trial**, may be obtained only as follows." Under (e)(1)(A) the default tool is **interrogatories** (identity, subject matter, substance of facts and opinions, and a summary of grounds). Under (e)(1)(B), "[u]pon motion, the court may order further discovery by other means," which includes depositions. Under (e)(3), unless manifest injustice would result, the deposing party pays the expert a reasonable fee and may be ordered to share the other side's expert costs. Unlike federal practice, **there is no automatic right to depose a testifying expert in Minnesota state court.** FIE's letter is correct.

### 5.2 What follows

- **Scheduling Order ¶3 is Minnesota's expert-discovery schedule.** It speaks of expert "interrogatories, disclosures, and reports." Under Rule 26.02(e), that exchange *is* the expert discovery, so the 3/15 close in ¶2 and the ¶3 dates are less contradictory than Item 1 assumed. What cannot happen without an order is an expert deposition.
- **The rule works both ways.** FIE cannot depose Plaintiffs' expert without agreement or an order either. For Plaintiffs, paying for an expert's deposition time is a real cost, and not deposing may be the rational choice.
- **Pre-suit investigators are arguably fact witnesses, not Rule 26.02(e) experts.** The rule reaches only opinions "acquired or developed in anticipation of litigation or for trial." Prieve Engineering (report transmitted with the 11/4/2024 denial), Hancock (8/29/2024) and Brau (9/6/2024) formed their views while handling the claim, before suit. That supports deposing them as **fact witnesses** before 3/15/2027 without a Rule 26.02(e)(1)(B) order: a Rule 45 subpoena for Prieve and Hancock, and ordinary notice or a Rule 30.02(f) organizational deposition for FIE. Expect FIE to argue that the Prieve engagement anticipated litigation, or to designate Prieve as a trial expert on 4/17/2027 and then resist further discovery. **Depose him before 4/17.** This argument is inference from the rule's text and has not been verified against Minnesota case law. Research it before relying on it.
- **Serve expert interrogatories.** Rule 26.02(e)(1)(A) interrogatories to FIE should go out well before FIE's 4/17/2027 date and count toward the 50-interrogatory cap. Budget for them now.

### 5.3 The six-day window (the practical trap)

If Plaintiffs want to depose FIE's retained expert, they need an order under Rule 26.02(e)(1)(B):

- FIE's expert reports arrive **Saturday, April 17, 2027**.
- All motions must be **heard** by **Friday, May 14, 2027** (Order ¶5).
- A non-dispositive motion must be served and filed **at least 21 days before the hearing** (Minn. Gen. R. Prac. 115.04), so by **Friday, April 23, 2027**.
- Order ¶4 requires a conferral, a three-page letter, and a conference call **before** any discovery motion.

That leaves about six days to see the report, confer, write to the Court, get a call, and file. Realistically it cannot be done after the fact. **Options:** (a) raise it with the Court early, in a single short letter that also covers Item 2, asking for leave in advance for limited expert depositions within a set window after 5/14, or for clarification; or (b) decide now that no expert deposition is needed and build the case on fact depositions plus cross-examination at trial. FIE said "any agreement … is premature," so the Court is the only route to (a).

### 5.4 Item 2 (expert-exclusion motions)

The conflict is real. ¶5 says all motions must be heard by 5/14/2027, the same day rebuttal reports are due. The ¶8 NOTICE says motions to exclude expert testimony must be filed at least 30 days before trial (about 8/21/2027). A specific provision ordinarily controls over a general one, which supports ¶8 for exclusion motions. Do not bet on it, though. Put it in the same letter as §5.3 so the Court resolves both at once. FIE has said it has "no concerns," which suggests it would not oppose a clarification.

## 6. Items 3 and 4: ESI and privilege

**Item 3 (ESI).** You already have the protection without FIE's agreement. RFP Instruction 5 (served 9/15/2026) specifies native format with metadata, and native photographs with EXIF. Rule 34 lets the requesting party "specify the form or forms in which electronically stored information is to be produced." The responding party may object to the requested form, but must do so in its response, which is due **10/15/2026**. If FIE neither objects nor complies, that is a Rule 37 issue. RFP 6 (the native Prieve photograph set) is the one to protect. Watch the 10/15 response for any objection to form, or for PDF-flattened photographs.

**Item 4 (privilege).** FIE agreed to logs under Rule 26.02(f)(1) and declined a court-ordered claw-back. Two points:

- FIE's letter slightly misreads Rule 26.02(f)(2). The right to "promptly present the information to the court under seal" belongs to the **receiving** party. FIE's reservation makes sense only if FIE expects to receive privileged material from Plaintiffs, and pro se plaintiffs hold little.
- Minn. R. Evid. 502 (effective 1/1/2019) already makes inadvertent disclosure non-waiving if reasonable steps were taken to prevent it and to fix it, including following Rule 26.02(f)(2). Under Rule 502(d), a party agreement binds only the parties "unless it is incorporated into a court order." That order is what Plaintiffs asked for, to reach the non-party inspection firms and the appraisal panel. Without it, the downside falls mostly on **FIE**, which holds the privileged claim-file material. Not worth a fight. Drop it.
