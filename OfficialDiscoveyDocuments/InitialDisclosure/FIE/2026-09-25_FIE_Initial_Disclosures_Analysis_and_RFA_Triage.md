## 1. Bottom line

1. **The September 6, 2024 Brau inspection record exists, and FIE sent it to the appraisal panel before telling you it didn't exist.** FIE 0687–0737 is a 51-page "Field inspection photos" PDF, "PDF Created on September 6, 2024 at 13:27:35 PM, Claim Number: 7007986837-1," with about 200 photographs and adjuster notes. The identical 51 pages are at **FIE 0456–0506**, inside the **August 13, 2025** packet FIE sent to both appraisers (FIE 0308). Two months later FIE told you "[t]he file does not contain a report on the requested date" (10/10/2025) and "[a]ll the information we can release has already been sent to you" (Brau, 10/29/2025). This is now the strongest fact in the claim-handling part of the case. It also changes how several RFAs read (§4).
2. **Brau's own note gives a third, different causation theory.** FIE 0718: *"Homeowner has some water damage on interior not related to hail no penetrations or openings to cause this water there is somewhere in tear on caulking on the exterior of the windows and there was heavy wind driven rain around these areas."* Hancock recorded no damage. Prieve blamed wear/tear and 2020 hail. Brau blamed torn caulking and wind-driven rain.
3. **FIE's own appraisal letter asked for the itemization your Count III says is missing.** FIE 0309: "We ask that any award be itemized to include the repair or replacement cost, depreciation, and actual cash value of each item or category of damaged property." It also quotes the policy clause requiring the appraisers to state "separately the actual cash value, the repair or replacement cost and a description of the damage and extent of damage **for each item of property**."
4. **The disclosures settle a good part of the entity questions.** FIE itself labels Brau, Dudley, Zorphlon Miller and Twonda Miller as FIE employees. Together with the letter's proper-party concession, about 20 entity and identity RFAs are now low-value. See the triage in §4.
5. **The production is thin and not in native format.** Everything is a PDF re-saved **9/22/2026** (Bates stamping). Photos are downsampled (about 700×526 px) with no capture timestamps. The Prieve set is the curated Appendix A (217 images), **not** the full native set RFP 6 asks for. There is no 2020 claim file, no claim notes, and no Prieve engagement documents. FIE says it holds "the remainder of the claim file not produced herewith." All of that goes to the 10/15 RFP responses.

### 3.1 The Brau record: why it matters and how to use it

- **What it is.** A claim-app export generated on an iPhone at 1:27 p.m. on inspection day. It contains elevation overviews and about 200 close-ups of window frames, sashes, siding and trim, plus four notes: "homeowner pointed these items out" (0719, 0721), "homeowner showed these items to me as well" (0726), and the causation note at 0718.
- **The contradiction, stated carefully.** FIE will argue that photos are not a "report," so the 10/10/2025 letter was accurate. Three answers:
  - Your 10/7/2025 request asked for the "inspection **report/notes**" (RFA 140), and 0718 is a note.
  - Brau's 10/29/2025 "[a]ll the information we can release has already been sent to you" does not depend on the word "report."
  - FIE had packaged the record and sent it to the appraisers on 8/13/2025, so the search could not have missed it.
- **The caveat on your side.** Your own appraiser, John Foster, received the 8/13/2025 packet (it was "[d]elivered by email to: jjlmds@gmail.com"). FIE will say the information reached your side. The answer is that it reached your *appraiser* in a proceeding limited to amount of loss, not *you* in response to your own request. Check Foster's file.
- **Which way it cuts on the merits.** The photos help if they show the dents Brau and you looked at together; that is RFA 29. The 0718 note hurts on causation, but helps on process: it is a coverage opinion from an adjuster who is not an engineer, and it contradicts both Hancock and Prieve. It also undercuts the "we only review engineer evidence" rule (RFA 79), because FIE itself relied on a non-engineer's causation opinion.
- **Next steps.**
  - Ask in the reply letter for the **native** export and original photos with EXIF (RFP Instruction 5), and for any separate notes or measurements. RFA 30 says Brau measured windows, and no measurements appear in the PDF.
  - Add Second Set RFAs: (a) FIE 0687–0737 is a genuine copy of the record Brau created on 9/6/2024; (b) Brau wrote the text on FIE 0718; (c) FIE 0456–0506 was enclosed with FIE's 8/13/2025 letter to the appraisers; (d) FIE 0687–0737 was in FIE's claim file on 10/10/2025 and on 10/29/2025.
  - Update `EventDocuments/Missing_Originals_and_Preservation_Note_2026-09-12.md` and the adjuster-sequence analysis. The theory moves from "the record was lost" to "the record existed and was withheld from the insured."

## 4. RFA triage: which requests to keep, deprioritize, or offer to withdraw

**How to use this.** You are **not** accepting FIE's 25-request cap. This triage has two uses: (1) a good-faith offer in the reply letter, "Plaintiffs will withdraw the following requests, which your production and your 9/25 letter have made unnecessary," and (2) your priority list for a Scheduling Order ¶4 call. Withdraw by letter and leave the gaps; **never renumber**.

**Why a request drops.** Each one below falls into one or more of these groups:
- **(C)** FIE's 9/25 proper-party concession covers it.
- **(D)** FIE's own initial disclosures state the fact.
- **(P)** FIE's production proves it.
- **(S)** The document speaks for itself, and FIE produced the document. Under Minn. R. Evid. 901 a party's own production is ordinarily strong evidence of authenticity.

An RFA admission is binding, which is stronger than a disclosure. But when the fact is already in FIE's own papers, the request buys little and costs credibility in a count-the-requests dispute.
