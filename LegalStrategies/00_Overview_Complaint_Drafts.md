**Policy:** 98055-49-65 | **Claim:** 7007986837-1-1
**Loss Date:** July 13, 2024
**Filing Deadline:** July 13, 2026 (2-year contractual suit limitation)
**Forum (assumed):** Hennepin County District Court (Minnesota state court)
**Prepared:** April 29, 2026

**Purpose.** This folder contains three draft complaints in escalating scope. The drafts implement the recommendations from `LegalReferences/LegalArguments/A1.md` through `E4.md`. Each draft tracks the same factual record but differs in (a) which causes of action are pleaded, (b) how much exposure the family creates for itself by adding marginal counts, and (c) how much fee-shifting and punitive-damages leverage is preserved.

**Disclaimer.** These are drafts for attorney review. They are not filed pleadings. A licensed Minnesota attorney must review and revise before any filing. Several open factual and legal questions (Senco/Semco identification; MN080 endorsement text; Foster's professional history; *Quade* / *Mork* / *Herll* / *Wesser* / *Carlson* current treatment on Westlaw) must be resolved or verified by counsel before the complaint is signed. ## 4. Why v2 Is the Recommended Default

The recommended posture is **v2 — Balanced**. The reasoning:

- **A1, A2, and A3 are non-negotiable.** They are the structural backbone of any first-party hail-claim suit in Minnesota post-appraisal. Every draft includes them.
- **B2 (fraud / negligent misrepresentation) is the single most consequential add-on.** It is the only count in the catalog that (i) preserves the path to § 549.20 punitive damages, (ii) is not subject to the § 604.18, Subd. 4(b) appraisal bar, (iii) does not require an individual justifiable-reliance showing under *Wiegand* / *Group Health Plan* (when paired with C3), and (iv) anchors the bad-faith narrative in a specific factual misrepresentation (Prieve's "no hail in Eden Prairie after 2020"). The cost of pleading B2 is modest — Rule 9.02 particularity is satisfied for at least one candidate misrepresentation, and the *Short v. Dairyland* recharacterization risk is manageable with careful drafting.
- **C3 (Minnesota Consumer Fraud Act) supplies fee-shifting that nothing else in the case provides.** § 604.18's $100,000 attorney-fee cap is a real ceiling; § 8.31, Subd. 3a and § 325F.70's 2023 amendment are uncapped. For a family that has already exhausted the MN Lawyer Referral Service, the fee-shifting potential of C3 is the most economically meaningful remedy in the catalog. The "in connection with the sale" hurdle is the principal interpretive risk; *Force v. ITT Hartford* gives the family a credible reading.
- **A4 (unjust enrichment) and B4 (standalone estoppel) add little value.** A4 is doctrinally weak and largely duplicative of A1; B4 is more powerful as a declaratory question inside A2 than as a separate count. v2 omits both; v3 includes them as alternative pleading (with the trade-off that the complaint becomes longer and more vulnerable to a Rule 12 partial dismissal that creates docket noise).

If the family is filing pro se with no counsel involvement, **v1 — Streamlined** is the safer choice. The reduced count list lowers the chance of procedural missteps that erode the family's credibility with the court.

If the family retains experienced bad-faith counsel and the lawyer is comfortable with the complexity, **v3 — Comprehensive** is the right choice. The additional counts produce broader settlement leverage at the cost of a more complex pleading-stage record.

## 5. What the Drafts Do NOT Address (and Why)

The following arguments are intentionally omitted from all three drafts:

- **B1 (Bad Faith / § 604.18) — pleaded by post-filing motion.** Subd. 4(a) requires this. The drafts do not include B1 as a count and do not pre-claim taxable costs under § 604.18. The factual record supporting B1 is built into the breach narrative of A1 so that the eventual Subd. 4(a) motion can incorporate the same allegations by reference. Drafts of the supporting affidavits should be prepared in parallel — see `bowl.md` Item 12.
- **D1 (Punitive Damages / § 549.20) — pleaded by post-discovery motion under § 549.191.** Same reason. The drafts seek "all available remedies under Minnesota law" in the prayer, which preserves the path to D1 once the § 549.191 motion is filed.
- **B3 (Negligent Claims Handling) — foreclosed.** *Haagenson*, *Short*, *Pillsbury*. Do not plead.
- **Independent tort of spoliation (E in `Possible_Arguments_List.md`'s F section).** Foreclosed in Minnesota. Use the missing-documents facts to support Rule 37 motions and adverse-inference instructions.
- **C1 / C2 / C4 (regulatory-statute counts).** Foreclosed by *Morris* and structural problems with the underlying statutes. The factual material is preserved in the standard-of-conduct allegations in A1.
- **E1 / E2 / E3 / E4 (interpretive doctrines).** These are tools of construction, not causes of action. They appear in the briefing, in the declaratory-judgment count's framing, and in the breach-of-contract count's allegations.

## 6. Drafting Conventions Used Across All Three Versions

- **Plaintiffs.** Yibiao Lu and Jie Hu, jointly. The 2014 application names Yibiao Lu as the named insured; Jie Hu is addressed throughout the Farmers correspondence. The drafts plead both as named plaintiffs subject to confirmation that the policy covers Jie Hu as a named insured (see `bowl.md`).
- **Defendant.** Fire Insurance Exchange, an unincorporated reciprocal exchange. The drafts use "Defendant" and "Farmers" interchangeably in narrative paragraphs; in formal recital paragraphs, "Defendant Fire Insurance Exchange" is used. The reciprocal-exchange status affects diversity jurisdiction and removability — see `bowl.md` Item 7.
- **Forum.** Drafts assume Hennepin County District Court. Eden Prairie is in Hennepin County, and venue under Minn. Stat. § 542.02 attaches to the property's location.
- **Citations.** Case citations in the drafts are formatted for Minnesota state-court practice. All citations require independent Westlaw/Lexis verification before filing — see `bowl.md`.
- **Exhibits.** Each draft references exhibits by letter. The exhibit list in each draft is not reproduced (the documents are already in `EventDocuments/` and `HomePolicy/`). Counsel will need to assemble the exhibit set before filing.
- **Verification.** Minnesota Rule of Civil Procedure 11 does not require verification of a complaint, but several substantive rules (e.g., Rule 9.02 fraud particularity, the § 604.18 Subd. 4(a) affidavit) require sworn statements at later stages. The drafts do not include verification language; the affidavits supporting the eventual B1 motion should be drafted in parallel.
