**Excluded, and why:**

- **B2 (fraud / negligent misrepresentation) — excluded per family decision (6/12/2026).** The reliance element is the count's structural weakness (B2.md: intent-to-induce ~35/100; the family *contested* the denials rather than relying on them, and the reliance-damages theory in v2 ¶ 62 is an uphill recharacterization). **Knock-on effect to accept consciously:** B2 was the only carrier tort for § 549.20 punitive damages (D1). Without it, punitive damages are effectively off the table — breach of contract cannot carry § 549.20, and the MCFA route does not supply a punitive vehicle. Remaining monetary leverage beyond compensatory damages: (i) C3 fee-shifting and costs of investigation, (ii) the post-filing B1 § 604.18 motion (taxable costs up to 50% of excess proceeds, capped at $250,000, plus capped fees), and (iii) prejudgment interest (D2, ~85/100). If counsel is later retained and disagrees, B2 can be added by amended pleading **before** the 7/13/2026 deadline only — adding it after filing as to pre-filing conduct should still relate back, but do not count on it; the decision is functionally being made now.
- **A4 (unjust enrichment)** — ~15/100; doctrinally barred where a valid policy indisputably governs; invites a Rule 12 partial dismissal that costs a pro se filer credibility for zero added recovery.
- **B3 (negligent claims handling)** — ~5/100; foreclosed (*Short*, *Haagenson*, *Pillsbury*). Do not plead.
- **B4 (estoppel/waiver) as a standalone count** — ~25/100 standalone vs. ~55/100 embedded. Lives inside Counts I–II only, and the existing v2 declaration ¶ 48(f) needs **narrowing** (see Count II notes — A2.md open item 12 warns that Minnesota law generally bars estoppel from *expanding* coverage; the narrower "no new denial grounds" rule is unsettled).
- **C1 / C2 / C4 (regulatory counts)** — 8–15/100 standalone; foreclosed by *Morris* (no private right of action). Their factual content is embedded as standard-of-conduct allegations inside Count I ¶ 44 and held for the B1 motion (where C1's record is worth ~70/100 as evidence).
- **B1 (§ 604.18 bad faith)** — procedurally cannot be in the initial complaint (Subd. 4(a)); added by post-filing motion with affidavits. The complaint's job is to build the factual record the motion will incorporate; the Prayer reserves it.
- **D1 (punitive damages)** — § 549.191 motion only, and now without a carrier tort (see B2 above). Not reserved in the Prayer beyond generic "all available remedies" language.
- **E1–E4** — doctrines, not counts. E1 (*Quade*, ~80/100 in its preclusion role) powers Count I Track 1 and Count II (b)/(c); E2 (J6944/matching, 35 affirmative / 58 declaratory) lives in Count I ¶ 43 (alternative) and Count II (e); E3/E4 (interpretive overlays, ~35/~45) appear in Count II (d) and briefing.

**Pro se calibration.** Four counts is the right surface area: the three structural counts plus one statutory count the family has decided to defend. Every additional marginal count multiplies Rule 12 briefing a pro se filer must answer. The complaint should be drafted so that even if Count IV is dismissed at Rule 12, Counts I–III are untouched (no cross-dependencies).

## 2. Count I — Breach of Contract (A1, ~65/100)

**Architecture: two affirmative lanes + one alternative + one backstop, none of which requires disturbing the award.**

1. **Lane 1 (lead, the dollar driver) — the *Quade* reservation for windows the panel did not evaluate.** Asserted **from the face of the award**: undefined codes "F1.7"/"B1.2" against a lump $12,800; Farmers' written 9/24/2025 admission that the umpire produced no report or estimate; Farmers' three mutually inconsistent scope descriptions (9/18, 9/19, 10/14/2025). Frame the burden on Farmers as the party asserting preclusion (flag honestly in briefing: no Minnesota case squarely allocates this burden). Evidence inventory is Prieve's own Appendix A (~10–12 documented-damage window locations beyond the two awarded; `01_DamageCatalog_PrieveAppendixA.md`). **Do not pair this lane with a remand request** — v2 ¶ 42(a) is correct as written; the remand lives only in Count III as a reserved alternative.
2. **Lane 2 — claim components outside the appraisal's jurisdiction.** The 8/3/2025 demand listed only Windows and two Siding items; downspouts (5 dent observations, 3 elevations — A-42, A-91–93, A-98–99), deck flashing (A-103, A-128, A-129), and the door-screen dent (A-141) were never before the panel. No appraisal-bar defense is available. The post-9/13/2021 downspout replacement makes these dents free-standing physical proof of post-2020 hail. v2 ¶ 42(b) carries this; keep verbatim.
3. **Alternative — *Cedar Bluff* matching (E2, Track 2).** Plead, do not lead: J6944 is an express limited-matching endorsement in this policy and *Noonan* is adverse 8th Circuit authority. Predicate facts (Senco/Semco identification; Sierra Pacific parts unavailability) are unverified — Gate V-3.
4. **Backstop — causation vs. coverage (Move 2).** If Farmers asserts the panel evaluated-and-rejected the other windows, plead that any rejection resting on an exclusion or prior-payment theory is a **coverage** determination beyond the panel's § 65A.01 authority — non-binding, decided de novo by the court. (Cite-check the *Mork* "surplusage" label before quoting — Gate V-6.)
5. **Implied covenant ¶ (v2 ¶ 44).** Keep; it is the factual spine the later B1 motion incorporates by reference.

**Gated additions:**
- **Dining-room window (¶ 32a / ¶ 42(a) sentence; from `08_…md`).** Plead on information and belief pending the 2020 per-sash Xactimate confirmation (Gate V-2). **Analytical caution carried over from A2.md § 3.4:** the appraiser's "from the prior claim" can be read as a *temporal causation* finding (2020 vs. 2024 storm) — which would sit *within* the panel's *Quade* authority and bind. Use the dining-room fact primarily as ambiguity/non-itemization evidence in Count III, and as a reserved-coverage allegation in Count I only in the offset/coverage framing; develop the per-sash 2020 record before leaning harder.
- **Front-of-garage window (`EngineerReportRebuttal.md` § 6B).** **Do not put in the filed complaint until documented** (dated photo, measured sketch, attached/detached check — Gate V-1). When verified, it enters Lane 1 as the cleanest unevaluated item. **One caution this overview adds to the existing memos:** the panel conducted its *own* 9/12/2025 site inspection; the inference "Prieve never saw it → the panel never evaluated it" holds only if discovery confirms the panel worked from Prieve's window inventory. Plead it (once verified) as an unevaluated item supported by the absence of any panel record, not as a logical certainty — and aim Move 1 deposition questions at whether any panelist physically reached that window.

**Deductible math (the practical test of every drafting choice).** Recovery requires >$17,720 of established loss. Lane 1 (~17 windows) plus Lane 2 components is the path; the ~$6,400/window extrapolation in `02_…Gap.md` § 5 is illustrative only — A1.md correctly warns the panel plausibly awarded the two *clearest* windows, so per-window value for the rest needs independent valuation (open item; ties to the future expert engagement).

## 4. Count III — The Appraisal Count, Re-Sequenced (A3, ~45/100 blended)

**This is the principal structural change from every baseline draft.** Relief requests ordered by **risk-adjusted value** (downside-protected first), not grant-likelihood:

1. **Lead — declaration that the award is not "so itemized" under § 65A.01 Subd. 3 and therefore does not preclude Count I as to un-itemized scope, while remaining binding on Farmers as to the *Quade* hail-causation finding (~50/100).** Pure statutory text, decided by the court, no route through the panel, no downside if granted. **Plead the narrow partial-award form** — the award binds as far as it went; it is non-preclusive as to what it never itemized. Do not argue the award is wholly void: that would forfeit the causation finding, the family's most valuable asset. Honest weakness to brief around: no Minnesota appellate case squarely on point.
2. **First alternative — modification/correction to comply with Subd. 3 (~45/100).** Courts prefer remand over re-drafting; reached only if the declaratory form does not resolve preclusion.
3. **Reserved — remand for clarification under *Herll*/*Rymer* (~65/100 grant-likelihood, deliberately demoted).** Plead it (it must be in the complaint to be available) but the body and prayer should expressly frame it as relief Plaintiffs request "only in the alternative." **Sequencing rule, load-bearing: no remand motion is filed until Foster, Roos, and Tomes are deposed (Move 1)** and each panelist's window-by-window evaluation account is locked under oath. *Rymer* bars using their testimony to expand an award but permits it to show misconduct; locked contradictory testimony is the insurance policy against a manufactured "we evaluated everything" clarified award. If remand is ever granted, the proposed order must include item (d) (per-item inspection/observation/basis/causation-vs-coverage characterization) and (d)(v) (the damage-signature/methodology question) from `06_…md` § 3.5 and `07_…md` § 6.1, plus sash-level itemization (v2 ¶ 53(d)).
4. **Last alternative — *Mork* vacatur (~25/100 now; ~50 only if discovery produces panel-level misconduct).** Pleaded to preserve it; pursued only under the flipped-calculus conditions of `06_…md` § 5 (a clarified full-scope rejection has already gutted the *Quade* preclusion's value AND the family's own expert can re-establish causation independently). Note A3.md's discipline: the Prieve-report defects are *Farmers/Prieve* conduct, not *panel* misconduct, and do not raise the *Mork* number by themselves.

Keep v2 ¶¶ 51, 51a (siding $0 non-itemization), 51b (dining-room non-itemization, gated by V-2). Rewrite v2 ¶¶ 52–53 and Prayer ¶ C to the ordering above.

**Hail science placement (07_…md, endorsed here):** the science stays **out of the pleading** except the single ¶ 21a(i) clause already in v2 (Prieve's "smooth/round-only" diagnostic contradicts NOAA and peer-reviewed literature). It deploys later at the four entry points: de novo proof on unevaluated windows; shaping any remand order; sorting causation vs. coverage rejections; and the expert-reliability motion — which in Hennepin County is a **Frye-Mack / Minn. R. Evid. 702 foundational-reliability challenge, not "Daubert"** (relabel throughout per `UninspectedGarageWindow_Significance.md` § 3.3; keep the Daubert framing only as the federal-removal contingency).

(2) **Fee-shifting value is muted while pro se** — pro se litigants generally cannot recover attorney fees; the count's near-term monetary value is costs/costs-of-investigation, and its real value is preserving uncapped fee-shifting **if counsel later appears** (a plausible path: a fee-shifting count makes the case more attractive for counsel to take). (3) Damages must be pleaded through the causal-nexus frame, not reliance.
