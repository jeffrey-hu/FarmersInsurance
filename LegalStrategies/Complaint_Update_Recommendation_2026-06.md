## 2. Finding 1 — Hail-Shape Science: Limited Pleading Impact

### 2.1 What the complaint already does well

The draft already pleads the *absence* of science in Prieve's report (¶ 21a(f): "no quantitative impact-force calculation, no terminal-velocity calculation, no material-specific dent-depth threshold … no peer-reviewed citation") and the internal contradiction in his "smooth or rounded dents" diagnostic (¶ 21a(i)). It also pleads the storm facts that make the science relevant — the 48 MPH Eden Prairie gust and 3.5–4″ regional hail (¶ 12). That is the right amount of science for a complaint.

### 2.2 Why not plead more

- **Notice pleading.** The complaint must state a plain claim, not prove causation. The science proves causation; that is the expert's job at summary judgment and trial.
- **Causation is largely already locked by *Quade* for the awarded windows**, and for the *unevaluated* windows (Count I Track 1) the complaint properly rests on Prieve's own photo documentation of damage. The science is how the family will *prove* those documented dents are hail at trial — but the allegation that they are hail-damaged is already pleaded.
- **Tactical caution.** Detailed scientific pleading invites Farmers to litigate methodology at the pleading stage and to frame an early *Daubert* posture. The family wants the science deployed on its own timetable (expert report → Daubert motion D5), not pulled forward.

### 2.3 The one worthwhile addition (misrepresentation counts)

The science *does* sharpen the "knew or recklessly disregarded falsity" element. Recommend a short clause in **¶ 21a(i)** and/or **¶ 60** noting that Prieve's diagnostic is contrary to established authority. Proposed insert (end of ¶ 21a(i)):

> "…without methodological support. Prieve's operative premise — that hail dents are 'smooth or rounded' and that dents containing scrape marks or elongated profiles are 'not consistent with hail' — is contrary to published authority of the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration and peer-reviewed engineering and atmospheric-science literature, which establish that hailstones are commonly irregular and lobed and that wind-driven hail strikes building surfaces at oblique angles, routinely producing elongated, off-center, and scrape-marked impact signatures. Defendant adopted this scientifically unsupported diagnostic as a basis for denial."

Parallel optional clause for **¶ 60** (knowledge/reckless disregard), if counsel wants the science to do double duty in Count IV:

> "Defendant further knew or recklessly disregarded that the damage-causation methodology it adopted from Prieve Engineering rested on a definition of hail damage (round and scratch-free only) contrary to established scientific authority."

Keep it to these. Do not import formulas or source lists into the pleading.

## 3. Finding 2 — The 9/19 Dining-Room Email: Recommended Additions

### 3.1 The gap

Paragraph 32 pleads that Farmers could not consistently describe the award (dining room → family room → dining room). True, but it stops at *inconsistency*. The 9/19 letter contains a stronger, separate fact: the appraiser **affirmatively documented damage on the front dining-room window** and **excluded it from the award only on the ground that the damage was "from the prior claim."** Per the family's account, that window has three sashes; only one was paid in the 2020 claim; the other two carry new 7/13/2024 dents. So the exclusion (a) is a prior-payment/offset determination the panel had no authority to make (a coverage call → *Mork* surplusage → court decides de novo), and (b) is factually false for two-thirds of the window. None of that is currently pleaded.

### 3.2 Proposed new paragraph (insert as ¶ 32a, in Factual Background § I)

> **32a. Defendant's appraiser documented damage to the front dining-room window but excluded it from the award on a prior-claim theory that is incorrect.** In the September 19, 2025 correspondence (correcting the September 18, 2025 description), Defendant's transmittal of the appraiser's position stated that the damage to the front dining-room window "was from the prior claim," and on that basis the dining-room window was not included in the September 12, 2025 award. The dining-room window is a three-sash window. Only one of its three sashes was within the scope of Defendant's 2020 claim payment (claim 3013539499-1); the remaining two sashes sustained new dents from the July 13, 2024 hailstorm that were never evaluated or paid. Whether a prior claim payment bars recovery for new damage to different, previously unpaid sashes is a question of coverage and offset reserved to the Court, not a question of causation within the appraisal panel's authority under *Quade v. Secura Insurance*, 814 N.W.2d 703 (Minn. 2012). To the extent the panel excluded the dining-room window on a prior-payment rationale, that determination is not binding and the coverage and amount of loss for the two unpaid sashes remain live questions for the Court.

### 3.3 Count I (Breach of Contract) — add the dining-room window to the reserved-coverage lane

In **¶ 42(a)** (windows the panel did not evaluate / evaluated but did not resolve on a proper basis), add a sentence:

> "In addition, Defendant's own appraiser documented damage to the front dining-room window but excluded it from the award solely on the ground that the damage was 'from the prior claim' (see ¶ 32a). Because only one of that window's three sashes was within the 2020 claim payment, and because a prior-payment bar is a coverage and offset question outside the panel's causation authority, coverage and amount of loss for the two unpaid, newly damaged sashes remain live questions for the Court that may be adjudicated without disturbing the existing award."

### 3.4 Count II (Declaratory Judgment) — add a declaration

Add **¶ 48(h)**:

> "**(h)** A prior insurance-claim payment that covered one sash of the front dining-room window does not bar coverage for hail damage sustained on July 13, 2024 by other, previously unpaid sashes of the same window; any exclusion of that window from the September 12, 2025 award on a prior-claim basis is a coverage determination outside the appraisal panel's authority and does not bind the Court."

### 3.5 Count III (Clarify/Modify the Award) — non-itemization + sash-level relief

Add **¶ 51b** (non-itemization, parallel to the existing ¶ 51a siding paragraph):

> **51b. Non-itemization and improper exclusion — the dining-room window.** The September 12, 2025 award did not separately state the actual value and loss for the front dining-room window, a window Defendant's own appraiser documented as damaged. Defendant later explained the omission on the ground that the damage "was from the prior claim." The award does not state which sashes of that three-sash window the panel treated as previously paid, the basis for treating any sash as previously paid, or whether the panel evaluated the July 13, 2024 damage to the sashes not within the 2020 claim. An award that silently omits a documented-damaged, multi-sash window — and is explained only by reference to a prior claim that paid for a single sash — is not the item-by-item appraisal § 65A.01, Subd. 3 requires."

And extend the remand request in **¶ 53** by adding, after subpart (c):

> "; and (d) for any multi-sash window, state separately the actual cash value and replacement cost value attributable to each sash, and, for any sash or window excluded on the ground of a prior claim, identify the prior claim, the specific sash(es) the panel understood to have been previously paid, and whether the panel independently evaluated the July 13, 2024 damage to any sash not previously paid."

(This mirrors the remand-order language already added to `OffenseStrategies/AppraisalAwardStrategy/04_MotionTactics.md` § 5, items (a)(vi), (b), and (e).)

### 3.6 Optional: Count IV/V (misrepresentation)

The dining-room exclusion can also be cited as another instance of Farmers asserting a factually unsupported basis (the over-broad "prior claim" attribution). This is optional and lower priority; the strongest homes for this finding are Counts I, II, and III. If counsel wants it in Count IV, add the dining-room over-attribution to the ¶ 66 list of deceptive acts in Count V rather than straining the Rule 9.02 particularity of Count IV.
