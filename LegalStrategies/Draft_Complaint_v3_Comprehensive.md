## I. INTRODUCTION

1. This is an action for breach of an insurance contract; declaratory judgment; clarification, modification, or vacatur of an appraisal award entered under Minn. Stat. § 65A.01; equitable relief in the alternative for unjust enrichment; relief based on waiver and equitable estoppel; common-law fraud and negligent misrepresentation; and statutory remedies under the Minnesota Consumer Fraud Act, Minn. Stat. § 325F.69, enforced through Minn. Stat. § 325F.70 and § 8.31, Subd. 3a.

2. Plaintiffs Yibiao Lu and Jie Hu hold homeowner's Policy 98055-49-65 with Defendant Fire Insurance Exchange. Defendant has denied a hail-damage claim filed for the loss of July 13, 2024 on shifting and incompatible factual theories, has refused to consider three rounds of disproving evidence, has imposed a supervisor-level "engineer's report only" gatekeeping rule found nowhere in the policy, has produced a binding appraisal award that does not state actual value and loss separately to each item, and has paid Plaintiffs nothing for the loss notwithstanding the appraisal panel's confirmation of hail as the cause.

3. Plaintiffs reserve the right to seek statutory taxable costs under Minn. Stat. § 604.18 by post-pleading motion under Subd. 4(a) and punitive damages under Minn. Stat. § 549.20 by post-discovery motion under Minn. Stat. § 549.191.

## IV. CAUSES OF ACTION

### COUNT I — BREACH OF CONTRACT

> **Research notes (remove before filing).**
>
> **Legal basis (A1).** Insurance policies are contracts under Minn. Stat. § 60A.08 and *Bob Useldinger & Sons, Inc. v. Hangsleben*, 505 N.W.2d 323, 327 (Minn. 1993). Elements: formation, performance, breach, damages. *Park Nicollet Clinic v. Hamann*, 808 N.W.2d 828, 833 (Minn. 2011); *Lyon Fin. Servs., Inc. v. Ill. Paper & Copier Co.*, 848 N.W.2d 539, 543 (Minn. 2014); *Short v. Dairyland Ins. Co.*, 334 N.W.2d 384, 387–88 (Minn. 1983).
>
> **Strength rating (revised May 2026): 75/100 on Track 1; 60/100 on Track 2; 65/100 anchor overall.** Track 1 (coverage for unevaluated windows as a *Quade* coverage question) is the dollar driver. Track 2 (matching as a *Cedar Bluff* coverage question with Semco out of business) is the secondary dollar driver. See `LegalReferences/Argument_Strength_Assessment.md` § 10 and `OffenseStrategies/AppraisalAwardStrategy/`.
>
> **Supporting facts.** Same as Counts I in v1 and v2, expanded by the Prieve catalog (at least 19 distinct windows plus 5 dented downspouts, 3 dented flashing observations, 3 trim chips, and 1 door window screen) per `OffenseStrategies/AppraisalAwardStrategy/01_DamageCatalog_PrieveAppendixA.md`.

16. Plaintiffs reallege and incorporate paragraphs 1 through 15 above.

17. Policy 98055-49-65 is a valid and enforceable contract in force on July 13, 2024.

18. Hail caused direct physical damage to insured windows on July 13, 2024. The September 12, 2025 appraisal award is binding under Minn. Stat. § 65A.01, Subd. 3 and confirms hail causation. Under *Quade v. Secura Insurance*, 814 N.W.2d 703 (Minn. 2012), the panel's causation finding is binding on Defendant for the awarded windows.

19. The September 12, 2025 award did not resolve coverage for hail-damaged windows the panel did not evaluate, and the panel had no jurisdiction over claim components Plaintiffs did not submit to it. The breach-of-contract claim therefore proceeds along two distinct lanes that do not require disturbing the existing award:

   **(a) Windows the panel did not evaluate (the *Quade* reservation).** The Prieve Engineering report's narrative and Photo Appendix A document at least nineteen distinct windows, plus a sliding door, on the home, including the upper-west three-unit bedroom window (A-43–46), the upper southwest bedroom window (A-59–65), the upper east window above the porch (A-118–124), the northeast-facing family room window (A-125–135), the southeast window at deck (A-136–141), the upper bay window group at the central quadrant (A-147–160), the upper east window above porch (A-161–166), the four-season porch windows (A-167–175), the north-facing porch window (A-176–183), the kitchen corner windows (A-184–195), the main-level east window at the north quadrant (A-196–204), the upper-north window above the kitchen (A-205–206), and the rear/east garage window (A-213–217). Under *Quade*, the panel's authority extends only to items it actually evaluated. For each unevaluated window, coverage and amount of loss remain live court questions; Defendant's failure to pay is a breach the Court may adjudicate without disturbing the existing award. Defendant's own 2020 claim file reinforces this: that file (claim 3013539499-1) documents hail dents on only two sashes and identifies no other dented window or sash on the home, so any dent the 2024 inspections documented on a different window or sash is, on Defendant's own baseline, new post-2020 damage for which no prior payment was made (see paragraph 9a above). The front dining-room window is a further instance: Defendant's own appraiser documented its damage but excluded it from the award as "from the prior claim," when, on information and belief, only one of that three-sash window's sashes was within the 2020 claim payment; whether a prior payment bars new damage to the two previously unpaid sashes is a coverage and offset question for the Court, outside the panel's causation authority.

   **(b) Claim components outside the appraisal panel's jurisdiction.** Plaintiffs' August 3, 2025 Demand for Appraisal listed only Windows, Siding (repaint), and Siding (R&R). The Prieve report's Photo Appendix A documents 5 dented downspouts at three elevations (A-42, A-91 through A-93, A-98 and A-99), 3 dented flashing observations at the deck (A-103, A-128, A-129), and 1 door window screen dent (A-141) — all outside the appraisal panel's jurisdiction. The post-9/13/2021 gutter and downspout replacement makes any dent on those components direct physical evidence of post-2020 hail. Coverage and amount of loss for each non-window component is a live court question; the appraisal cannot bar these claims because the panel never had jurisdiction over them.

   **(c) The matching question (the *Cedar Bluff* track).** The policy's "comparable material and quality" replacement-cost obligation is a coverage question reserved to the Court under *Quade*; where the original-equipment manufacturer is no longer in operation, Endorsement J6944 does not bar replacement of damaged property and does not relieve Defendant of the matching obligation recognized in *Cedar Bluff Townhome Condominium Ass'n v. American Family Mut. Ins. Co.*, 857 N.W.2d 290 (Minn. 2014).

20. Defendant has further breached the implied covenant of good faith and fair dealing as detailed throughout the factual background, including by scoping the Prieve Engineering engagement to "windows only" while reporting damage to other components had been claimed; by adopting and transmitting a report whose own Photo Appendix A documents non-window damage that the report's narrative and Conclusions ignore; by adopting and transmitting a report whose foundational "no hail in Eden Prairie after 2020" premise was objectively disprovable on public record before the denial issued; by imposing a supervisor-level "engineer's report only" rule found nowhere in the policy; by issuing two formal denials on incompatible factual theories; by advancing a "prior damage" and "already paid in 2020" rationale that Defendant's own 2020 claim file contradicts, in that the 2020 file documents hail dents on only two sashes and identifies no other dented window or sash on the home; and by failing to maintain or produce a written investigation record from Defendant's own September 6, 2024 inspection.

21. Plaintiffs have suffered damages in excess of $50,000.

> **Research note (remove before filing).** "In excess of $50,000" is a pleading convention, not a damages estimate. Minn. R. Civ. P. 8.01 bars stating a specific dollar amount for unliquidated damages above $50,000 and requires pleading only that "reasonable damages in an amount greater than $50,000" are sought (a specific figure is stated only when the claim is under $50,000). The phrasing also keeps the case in District Court (above the conciliation-court limit) and clears the § 549.09 interest breakpoint (~10% at $50,000+ vs. ~4% below). Actual damages — appraisal-award shortfall, full-home matching/replacement cost, consequential water-intrusion damage, out-of-pocket reliance costs, interest, and any punitive claim — are proven later by evidence and expert testimony.

Cleanup pleading purpose: hedge against the procedural scenario in which Defendant's "settled per the binding appraisal award" position is credited as a complete bar to the contract claim. ## DRAFTING NOTE — STRUCTURAL TENSIONS WITHIN v3

This draft includes alternative-pleaded counts (A4 and B4 as a standalone count) that introduce structural tensions with the primary contract theory. Counsel should be aware:

1. **A4 (Unjust Enrichment) is doctrinally weak where the contract is valid.** *U.S. Fire Ins. v. Minn. State Zoological Bd.* and *Holman v. CPT Corp.* are squarely against this claim. The pleading is a Rule 8.05(2) hedge, not a recovery vehicle. A motion to dismiss is likely to succeed; A4 is included here as a procedural reservation against a scenario in which Defendant takes the position that the appraisal "fully discharged" the contract and the contract claim is barred.

2. **B4 as a standalone count adds little beyond what Count II declaration (f) already accomplishes.** The cleaner posture is to plead B4 as a declaratory question inside A2 (as v2 does). Including B4 as a standalone count creates a pleading risk that the court will treat the same factual material twice, and the doctrine itself has no independent damages component.

3. **Tiered alternative pleading discipline is essential.** The complaint must clearly state which counts are pleaded in the alternative under Rule 8.05(2) (A4 here, and the alternative tiers within A3). The drafter should ensure the prayer for relief reflects the alternative posture cleanly so the court is not invited to treat alternatives as cumulative damages.

4. **Counsel may prefer v2.** The honest assessment is that v2 captures the value of v3 with cleaner doctrinal posture. v3 is provided here for completeness; v2 is the recommended default. See `00_Overview_Complaint_Drafts.md` § 4.
