## I. INTRODUCTION

1. This is an action for breach of an insurance contract, for declaratory judgment construing that contract, and to clarify, modify, or correct an appraisal award entered under Minn. Stat. § 65A.01. Plaintiffs Yibiao Lu and Jie Hu are homeowners whose dwelling at 17756 George Moran Dr., Eden Prairie, Minnesota was damaged by hail on July 13, 2024. Plaintiffs are insured under Defendant Fire Insurance Exchange's homeowner's Policy 98055-49-65, which has been continuously in force since October 22, 2014. Defendant has denied coverage twice, on incompatible factual theories, and has paid Plaintiffs nothing for the loss notwithstanding a binding § 65A.01 appraisal award entered on September 12, 2025 confirming hail as the cause of loss to Plaintiffs' insured windows.

2. Plaintiffs seek monetary damages, declaratory relief, clarification or modification of the September 12, 2025 appraisal award, prejudgment and postjudgment interest, costs and disbursements, and such further relief as the Court deems appropriate.

2a. Plaintiffs reserve the right to seek statutory taxable costs under Minn. Stat. § 604.18 by post-pleading motion under Subd. 4(a) of that section, and punitive damages under Minn. Stat. § 549.20 by post-discovery motion under Minn. Stat. § 549.191, both upon the showings those statutes require.

## IV. FACTUAL BACKGROUND

### A. The Policy

8. On October 22, 2014, Plaintiff Yibiao Lu and Defendant entered into Policy 98055-49-65, a Farmers Next Generation Homeowners Policy Minnesota, 2nd Edition, insuring the dwelling and personal property at 17756 George Moran Dr., Eden Prairie, Minnesota. The policy has been continuously renewed since that date.

9. The policy in force on July 13, 2024 provides Coverage A (Dwelling) of $886,000, classifies the property as Owner Occupied (Primary Residence), applies a "New Roof" discount, and imposes a 2% windstorm/hail deductible of $17,720. Hail is an enumerated covered peril under Section I (Loss or Damage Insured) of the policy. Endorsements active on the loss date include J6898 (Amending General Conditions), J6944 (Limited Matching of Undamaged Property), MN079, and MN080.

10. Plaintiffs paid all premiums due under the policy through and after the loss date.

### B. The July 13, 2024 Hailstorm

11. On July 13–14, 2024, severe thunderstorms with hail struck the Twin Cities metropolitan area, including Eden Prairie. The National Weather Service Twin Cities/Chanhassen office documents hail up to 3.5 inches in east-central Minnesota and up to 4 inches near Monticello and Big Lake. Eden Prairie Airport (ASOS) recorded a 48 MPH wind gust at 1:00 AM on July 14, 2024, with Eden Prairie expressly named in the NWS wind-gust table.

12. The July 13–14, 2024 hailstorm caused direct physical damage to the windows of Plaintiffs' insured dwelling.

### C. Discovery of Damage and Claim Opening

13. On August 9, 2024, Mary of Legacy contractor contacted Plaintiffs about a leaking window. On August 13, 2024, Jack of Legacy visited the property, identified dents on the windows, and assisted Plaintiffs in opening a claim with Defendant. Defendant assigned the claim number 7007986837-1-1.

14. Plaintiffs cooperated with Defendant's claim-handling process throughout. Plaintiffs called Defendant for a status update on August 19, 2024 and proactively contacted Hancock Claims Consultants — Defendant's chosen inspection vendor — on August 20, 2024 to provide Legacy's contact information.

### D. The Three Inspections

15. **First inspection (August 29, 2024).** Hancock Claims Consultants, through Jayden Grimm, inspected the property at Defendant's direction. Hancock produced a systematic photo set of every window wrap, each tagged "Damaged – No."

16. **Second inspection (September 6, 2024).** Defendant's own Claims Specialist Representative — Property, Nicholas Brau, personally inspected the property. During the inspection, Mr. Brau identified dents on multiple windows alongside Plaintiffs and measured window dimensions. Defendant has subsequently asserted that "the file does not contain a report on the requested date" for Mr. Brau's September 6, 2024 inspection. Mr. Brau personally refused to release inspection notes when Plaintiffs asked.

17. **Third inspection (October 9, 2024).** Defendant retained Prieve Engineering, LLC, and engineer Nathan Prieve inspected the property. The Prieve Engineering report (File #241008) documents dents, marks, and indentations across at least twelve distinct window locations on the home but concluded that the damage was not consistent with hail. The report's Photo Appendix A separately documents — in captions Prieve's own office created — dented downspouts at three elevations of the home (photographs A-42 at the front, A-91 through A-93 at the south side, and A-98 and A-99 at the east side), dents in flashing at the deck (photograph A-103), additional siding and trim board observations (photographs A-25, A-26, A-53, A-54, A-90, and A-148), and a dent with a scrape mark in the screen of a door window (photograph A-141). None of those non-window observations is addressed in the narrative of the Prieve report and none is addressed in the report's Conclusions.

### E. The Two Denials

18. **First denial (September 3, 2024).** Five business days after the Hancock inspection and before any forensic engineer had been retained, Defendant denied the claim by phone and by formal written letter signed by Catastrophe Claims Representative Christian Dudley. The September 3, 2024 letter affirmatively stated that "interior water damage around your windows was observed. There were no other damages observed. There was no opening created by the direct force of wind or hail that could have occurred and allowed water to enter and cause immediate damage."

19. **Second denial (November 4, 2024).** Defendant issued a second formal denial citing six exclusions: Wear and Tear (§ I.A.7 / I.B.8); Marring (§ I.A.8); Corrosion, Deterioration, Decay, or Rust (§ I.A.9 / I.B.9); Inherent Vice or Latent Defect (§ I.A.4 / I.B.6); Neglect or Lack of Maintenance (§ I.B.4); and Faulty or Defective Construction (§ I.B.5). The November 4, 2024 denial relied on the Prieve Engineering report's premise that "the most recent hail in Eden Prairie reported in 2020." That premise was and is objectively false.

20. **The two denials rest on incompatible factual theories.** The September 3, 2024 letter says no exterior damage exists; the November 4, 2024 letter acknowledges damage exists but blames aging and prior storms. Both cannot be correct.

### E.1 The Prieve Engineering Report's Independent Deficiencies

20a. **Scope limitation.** The Prieve report on its face states: "Our office was retained to evaluate the condition of windows in the home as relates to hail." Defendant scoped the engineering engagement to "windows only," excluding from the engineer's mandate the siding, downspouts, flashing, and exterior doors that Plaintiffs had reported as damaged. The scoping decision was made by Defendant; Plaintiffs were not informed of it; and Defendant subsequently used the report (whose stated scope was "windows only") as the basis for denying the entire claim, including the components the report was not authorized to evaluate.

20b. **Observations documented in the report's own photo log but omitted from the report's narrative.** The Photo Appendix A captions identify dented downspouts at three different elevations of the home (front: A-42; south: A-91, A-92, A-93; east: A-98, A-99), dents in flashing at the base of the wall along the deck (A-103), and chips in trim board (A-90, A-148), among other non-window damages. Plaintiffs' gutters and downspouts were replaced after September 13, 2021, such that any dent on those components physically cannot pre-date late 2021 and is direct physical evidence of post-2020 hail activity at the property. The Prieve report's narrative and Conclusions do not address any of those observations and the report concludes — flatly inconsistent with its own photo log — that "no recent hail damage has occurred."

20c. **Internal contradictions in the report.** In the Background section the report states, "Convective storm activity and hail have been reported on multiple occasions in the area of the subject structure." In Conclusion 1 the report states, "It is our opinion that no recent hail damage has occurred to windows in the subject structure related to any recent hail or other storm activity." The two statements cannot both be true unless the report also explains why hail-on-multiple-occasions did not impact the subject windows — an explanation the report does not provide. Page 8 of the engineer's narrative (corresponding to the report's east-side discussion) contains sixteen separate uses of the word "dent" or "dents." A document with sixteen catalogued dent observations on a single page concluding "no recent hail damage has occurred" requires methodological support the report does not provide.

20c-1. **The report's own diagnostic for hail damage convicts its conclusion.** At page 6 of the Prieve report (page 9 of the combined denial-letter PDF), in the discussion of the rear/east garage window, the report states: "No smooth or rounded dents were observed in the frame or sashes." That sentence operates as Prieve's implicit diagnostic for hail causation — the *absence* of smooth or rounded dents at the garage window is the stated reason hail is ruled out there. Applied across the rest of the report, that same diagnostic defeats Conclusion 1: page 4 of the report (front/west triple window) states that "**Rounded dents** were observed at sashes of this window unit," and page 5 of the report (north porch four-unit window) states that "Two dents appear at the lower left corner of the left casement **which are generally smooth**." By Prieve's own diagnostic, those observations are hail damage. Prieve handwaves them away by attributing them to "a prior hail claim," but Defendant's own 2020 Xactimate (paragraph 20d below) does not establish that the specific sashes at issue were paid for in 2020. The report's framework — (i) "smooth or rounded" dents are hail only if Farmers' 2020 file already covered them, and (ii) "scrape marks inside dents" rule out hail without any peer-reviewed support for that distinction — is constructed to produce a non-hail finding on every observation regardless of what is physically present. That distinction is also contrary to established scientific authority: the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration and peer-reviewed engineering and atmospheric-science literature establish that hailstones are commonly irregular and lobed and that wind-driven hail strikes building surfaces at oblique angles, routinely producing elongated, off-center, and scrape-marked impact signatures.

20d. **Contradiction with Defendant's own 2020 claim file.** Conclusion 1 of the Prieve report treats Defendant's 2020 hail claim as having approved and paid for "2 windows … already included and paid for." Defendant's own 2020 Building Loss Worksheet and Xactimate (claim 3013539499-1) reflect the approval of two casement windows at line items 1–4. Plaintiffs maintain that the 2020 dents were limited to two sashes (not two whole window units), and that the current Prieve report documents dents at additional sashes on the same windows that Conclusion 1 treats as "already paid in 2020." The "already paid" defense cannot logically extend to sashes or windows that were not in the 2020 scope. In addition, at page 7 of the report Prieve himself acknowledges that the chip he observed at trim board photograph A-53 is "visible in prior photo logs from Farmers Insurance dated October 13, 2020" — meaning either (i) the chip existed in 2020 and Defendant's 2020 estimate underpaid the claim by failing to include it, or (ii) the chip is new and the "same chip" identification is incorrect.

20e. **Methodology gaps.** The Prieve report contains no quantitative impact-force calculation tied to the documented hail sizes (the report's own Weather Records section lists Eden Prairie-area hail at 1.25" on June 19, 2015, 1.75" on July 25, 2017, and "multiple reports on August 9, 2020 ranging from 1 to 2 inches"); no terminal-velocity calculation; no material-specific dent-depth threshold for extruded aluminum or aluminum-clad wood windows; no peer-reviewed citations; and no stated error rate or methodological standard. The conclusion that "past hail at the site was not of sufficient size to dent window frames or the face of sashes" is asserted without supporting calculation.

20f. **Directional fallacy on the east-side siding penetration.** Conclusion 4 of the Prieve report dismisses the siding penetration on the east side of the home on the ground that "recent storm activity in the area generally consists of storms moving from west to east with hail originating from a westerly direction." The 7/13–7/14/2024 event was a multi-round overnight thunderstorm system documented by the National Weather Service to include multiple rounds of severe weather across Minnesota, including a 48 MPH wind gust at Eden Prairie ASOS at 1:00 AM on 7/14. The "generally west-to-east" generalization is not supported by an analysis of the specific 7/13–7/14/2024 storm. Conclusion 4 also exceeds the report's stated scope ("windows only").

20g. **Activities-section omissions.** The Activities section of the Prieve report does not list, as inputs to the evaluation, (i) the Hancock 8/29/2024 inspection workproduct, (ii) Defendant's 9/3/2024 first denial letter, (iii) any record of Defendant's own adjuster Nicholas Brau's 9/6/2024 observations, or (iv) the photographic submissions Plaintiffs made on September 5, 8, and 10, 2024 that prompted Defendant to reopen the claim and retain Prieve in the first place.

### F. Plaintiffs' Repeated Submissions of Disproving Evidence

21. Beginning on September 5, 2024 and continuing through May 28, 2025, Plaintiffs submitted four separate rounds of evidence disproving the factual premises of Defendant's denials:

   a. **Photographic record** submitted on September 5, 8, and 10, 2024 documenting damage across most of the home's windows.

   b. **Minnesota Department of Commerce complaint** filed on November 19, 2024 (Complaint ID 93547), processed and escalated to Director-level review, which produced no compulsory action.

   c. **Written appeal of January 31, 2025** with stormersite.com hail data showing reports near Eden Prairie in 2022, 2023, and 2024, plus evidence that Plaintiffs' gutters and downspouts were replaced after September 13, 2021 and now bear hail damage.

   d. **Second written response of February 10, 2025** with City of Eden Prairie public permit records EP187829 (17490 George Moran Dr., issued 11/15/2023, cause: hail) and EP190835 (17708 George Moran Dr., issued 5/9/2024, cause: hail), plus a 14-of-19 neighbor canvass confirming hail-driven repairs after 2020.

22. Defendant did not retract the denials, did not reopen the claim, and did not engage substantively with any of these submissions.

### G. The "Engineer's Report Only" Gatekeeping Rule

23. On February 3, 2025, Defendant's field claim supervisor, Bradlee Monson, told Plaintiffs by telephone that Defendant would not review any evidence or documents from Plaintiffs unless they were provided by an engineer.

24. On February 10, 2025 at 11:06 AM, Defendant's adjuster Nicholas Brau left a voicemail confirming the rule: "Brad reached out to me on the field claim supervisor wanted me to let you know that he did receive the information that you just sent in recently but it's not an engineers report so I believe that's what he wanted to look for from you."

25. The policy contains no provision requiring evidence to come from an engineer. The rule was used to refuse review of the family's neighbor canvass, City of Eden Prairie permit records, stormersite weather records, and the National Weather Service materials.

### H. The Appraisal Process

26. On August 3, 2025, Plaintiffs filed a formal Demand for Appraisal under Minn. Stat. § 65A.01, naming claim 7007986837-1-1, loss date July 13, 2024, and three items in dispute: Windows, Siding (repaint), and Siding (replace and remove). Plaintiffs filed a Sworn Statement on Proof of Loss with the demand stating a total claimed loss of $310,000 against Coverage A of $886,000 with a $17,720 deductible.

27. The appraisal proceeded under § 65A.01. Plaintiffs' appraiser was John Foster (retained through contractor Pure Construction at $2,200, half paid by Plaintiffs). Defendant's appraiser was Greg Tomes. The umpire was Wade Roos.

28. **The September 12, 2025 Appraisal Award.** The award form, signed by umpire Wade Roos and by Plaintiffs' appraiser John Foster, awarded "Windows (F1.7 / B1.2)" with a Replacement Cost Value of $12,800 and an Actual Cash Value of $6,400. Both siding items received $0. Defendant's appraiser Greg Tomes did not sign the award.

29. **The award confirms hail as the cause of loss.** The panel's award of dollars to Plaintiffs' windows is a binding finding under Minn. Stat. § 65A.01, Subd. 3 and *Quade v. Secura Insurance*, 814 N.W.2d 703 (Minn. 2012), that hail caused the damage to the awarded windows.

30. **The award does not state actual value and loss separately to each item.** The award identifies the awarded windows only by the undefined codes "F1.7" and "B1.2" against a single lump-sum $12,800 RCV. Plaintiffs cannot determine from the face of the award which windows are covered. Defendant has been unable to consistently describe the awarded scope.

31. **The umpire produced no report or estimate.** On September 24, 2025, Defendant confirmed in writing: "Unfortunately, the umpire did not provide a report or estimate. Our only recommendation is to refer you to your appraiser for any additional details he may have in his file."

32. **The net payment to Plaintiffs is $0 because the $17,720 deductible exceeds the $6,400 ACV** awarded.

### I. Post-Appraisal Refusal to Engage

33. On September 18, 2025, Defendant described the awarded windows as "front right, and the rear elevation in the dining room." On September 19, 2025, Defendant said the dining-room reference was a mistake. On October 14, 2025, Defendant repeated the original "dining room" description. Defendant has been unable to consistently describe its own binding award.

33a. **Defendant's appraiser documented damage to the front dining-room window but excluded it on a prior-claim theory that is incorrect.** In the September 19, 2025 correspondence correcting the September 18, 2025 description, Defendant's transmittal of the appraiser's position stated that the damage to the front dining-room window "was from the prior claim," and on that basis the dining-room window was not included in the September 12, 2025 award. The front dining-room window is a three-sash window. On information and belief, only one of its three sashes was within the scope of Defendant's 2020 claim payment (claim 3013539499-1); the remaining two sashes sustained new dents from the July 13, 2024 hailstorm that were never evaluated or paid. Whether a prior claim payment bars recovery for new damage to different, previously unpaid sashes is a question of coverage and offset reserved to the Court, not a question of causation within the appraisal panel's authority under *Quade v. Secura Insurance*, 814 N.W.2d 703 (Minn. 2012). To the extent the panel excluded the dining-room window on a prior-payment rationale, that determination does not bind the Court, and coverage and amount of loss for the two previously unpaid, newly damaged sashes remain live questions for the Court.

34. On October 21, 2025, Plaintiffs wrote to Defendant: "As of now, 2 windows are approved … But pretty much all the windows were damaged, as evidenced by the photos submitted through the claim process. Why they are not covered? What do you want us to do?" Defendant's same-day reply: "The claim was settled per the binding appraisal award received. The loss is under your deductible. If you need information as to why additional windows were not included in the award then you will need to contact your appraiser to obtain that information."

35. On October 28, 2025, Plaintiffs reported active window leaks on a rainy day to Defendant. Defendant did not respond on the merits.

## VI. CAUSES OF ACTION

### COUNT I — BREACH OF CONTRACT

> **Research notes (remove before filing).**
>
> **Legal basis (A1).** Insurance policies are contracts under Minn. Stat. § 60A.08 and *Bob Useldinger & Sons, Inc. v. Hangsleben*, 505 N.W.2d 323, 327 (Minn. 1993). The elements are (1) formation, (2) performance by plaintiff, (3) breach by defendant, and (4) damages. *Park Nicollet Clinic v. Hamann*, 808 N.W.2d 828, 833 (Minn. 2011); *Lyon Fin. Servs., Inc. v. Ill. Paper & Copier Co.*, 848 N.W.2d 539, 543 (Minn. 2014); *Short v. Dairyland Ins. Co.*, 334 N.W.2d 384, 387–88 (Minn. 1983).
>
> **Strength rating (revised May 2026): 75/100 on Track 1; 60/100 on Track 2; 65/100 anchor overall.** Track 1 (coverage for windows the panel did not evaluate, as a *Quade* coverage question) is the dollar driver and the highest single argument in the case — see `LegalReferences/Argument_Strength_Assessment.md` § 10 and `OffenseStrategies/AppraisalAwardStrategy/01_DamageCatalog_PrieveAppendixA.md` §§ F–H. Track 2 (matching under *Cedar Bluff* with Semco out of business) is the secondary dollar driver. Track 3 (modification of the awarded amount under *Mork*) is reserved for Count III as a further alternative.
>
> **Supporting facts.** Continuous policy since 10/22/2014 and renewal in force on the loss date (HomePolicy/2014_Application-PolicyDocument-980554965.pdf; HomePolicy/2023_Renewal.pdf); NWS-confirmed July 13–14, 2024 hailstorm with 48 MPH gust at Eden Prairie ASOS (Evidence/Significant Wind and Hail in Minnesota July 13-14.pdf); 9/12/2025 binding appraisal award confirming hail causation under *Quade* (EventDocuments/Appraisal Documents/Appraisal_Result_Front.png); Prieve report's own catalog of dents at ~12 window locations plus 5 dented downspouts, 3 dented flashing observations, 2 siding penetrations, 3 trim chips, and 1 door window screen (EventDocuments/FarmersDenyLetter-11-4.pdf pp. 4–9; EventDocuments/FarmersDenyLetter-11-4-AppendixA.pdf; OffenseStrategies/AppraisalAwardStrategy/01_DamageCatalog_PrieveAppendixA.md); 2020 Xactimate scope of exactly 2 casement windows (EventDocuments/FinalDocument_2020.pdf line items 1–4); $0 net payment to date (EventDocuments/Appraisal Documents/Appraisal_Result_Back.png).

37. Plaintiffs reallege and incorporate paragraphs 1 through 36 above.

38. Policy 98055-49-65 is a valid and enforceable contract between Plaintiffs and Defendant. The policy was in force on July 13, 2024.

39. Plaintiffs performed all conditions precedent required by the policy as set forth in paragraph 36 above.

40. Hail is an enumerated covered peril under Section I (Loss or Damage Insured) of the policy. Hail caused direct physical damage to insured windows on July 13, 2024.

41. The September 12, 2025 appraisal award is binding under Minn. Stat. § 65A.01, Subd. 3 and confirms hail as the cause of loss to insured windows. Under *Quade v. Secura Insurance*, 814 N.W.2d 703 (Minn. 2012), the panel's causation finding is binding on Defendant. Defendant is therefore precluded from asserting in this action that the windows the panel evaluated were damaged by wear and tear, marring, corrosion, deterioration, decay, rust, inherent vice, latent defect, neglect, lack of maintenance, or faulty or defective construction.

42. **The September 12, 2025 award did not resolve coverage for hail-damaged windows the panel did not evaluate, and the panel had no jurisdiction over claim components Plaintiffs did not submit to it.** The breach-of-contract claim therefore proceeds along two distinct lanes that do not require disturbing the existing award:

   **(a) Windows the panel did not evaluate (the *Quade* reservation).** The award identifies the awarded windows only by the undefined codes "F1.7" and "B1.2." Defendant has acknowledged that the umpire produced no report or estimate. The Prieve Engineering report itself documents dents, marks, and indentations across approximately twelve distinct window locations on the home — a scope materially broader than the two windows the award identifies. The unevaluated windows include, without limitation, the upper-west three-unit bedroom window (photographs A-43 through A-46), the upper southwest bedroom window (photographs A-59 through A-65), the upper east window above the porch (photographs A-118 through A-124), the northeast-facing family room window (photographs A-125 through A-135), the southeast window at deck (photographs A-136 through A-141), the upper bay window group at the central quadrant (photographs A-147 through A-160), the upper east window above porch (photographs A-161 through A-166), the four-season porch windows (photographs A-167 through A-175), the north-facing porch window (photographs A-176 through A-183), the kitchen corner windows (photographs A-184 through A-195), the main-level east window at the north quadrant (photographs A-196 through A-204), the upper-north window above the kitchen (photographs A-205 and A-206), and the rear/east garage window (photographs A-213 through A-217). Under *Quade v. Secura Insurance*, 814 N.W.2d 703 (Minn. 2012), the panel's authority extends only to items it actually evaluated. For each hail-damaged window the panel did not evaluate, coverage and amount of loss remain live coverage questions for the Court, and Defendant's failure to pay is a breach of the policy that the Court may adjudicate without disturbing the existing award. In addition, Defendant's own appraiser documented damage to the front dining-room window but excluded it from the award solely on the ground that the damage was "from the prior claim" (see paragraph 33a above). Because, on information and belief, only one of that window's three sashes was within the 2020 claim payment, and because a prior-payment bar is a coverage and offset question outside the panel's causation authority, coverage and amount of loss for the two previously unpaid, newly damaged sashes remain live questions for the Court that may be adjudicated without disturbing the existing award.

   **(b) Claim components outside the appraisal panel's jurisdiction.** Plaintiffs' August 3, 2025 Demand for Appraisal listed three items: Windows, Siding (repaint), and Siding (R&R). The Demand did not include downspouts, flashing other than wall-base flashing tied to siding, or exterior doors. The appraisal panel therefore had no jurisdiction over those components, and the September 12, 2025 award could not and did not address them. The Prieve report's Photo Appendix A nevertheless documents (i) dented downspouts at three elevations of the home — photographs A-42 (front), A-91 through A-93 (south side), and A-98 and A-99 (east side); (ii) dented flashing at the base of the wall along the deck — photographs A-103, A-128, and A-129; and (iii) a dent with scrape mark in the screen of a sliding-door window — photograph A-141. Plaintiffs' gutters and downspouts were replaced after September 13, 2021, such that any dent on those components is direct physical evidence of post-2020 hail at the property. Coverage and amount of loss for each of these non-window components is a live coverage question for the Court under Count I, and Defendant cannot raise the binding effect of the appraisal award as a bar to those questions because the panel never had jurisdiction over them.

43. **The policy's "comparable material and quality" replacement-cost obligation is a coverage question reserved to the Court under *Quade*.** The original-equipment manufacturer of the awarded windows is, on information and belief, no longer in operation, such that windows of the same manufacturer and product line cannot be sourced. Under *Cedar Bluff Townhome Condominium Ass'n v. American Family Mut. Ins. Co.*, 857 N.W.2d 290 (Minn. 2014), the policy's "comparable material and quality" standard governs replacement cost. Endorsement J6944 ("Limited Matching of Undamaged Property") does not bar replacement of property that itself sustained direct hail damage and does not bar payment of the replacement cost where matching is impossible.

44. **Plaintiffs' performance has been undermined by Defendant's claim-handling conduct.** Defendant denied the claim on September 3, 2024 within five business days of the first inspection and before any forensic engineer had been retained, asserting "no other damages observed" — a finding directly contradicted three days later by Defendant's own adjuster's personal observations on September 6, 2024. Defendant denied the claim a second time on November 4, 2024 on a wholly different theory ("wear and tear / 2020 already paid") that rested on Prieve Engineering's objectively false weather premise. Defendant adopted that denial notwithstanding (i) the Prieve report's narrow scope-of-engagement (limited to "windows only" but used to deny non-window components), (ii) the multiple non-window damage observations the report's own Photo Appendix A documents in its captions but omits from its narrative — including dented downspouts at three elevations of the home, dents in flashing, and chipped trim board — and (iii) the report's internal contradictions and its objectively false "no hail in Eden Prairie after 2020" premise. Defendant subsequently imposed an "engineer's report only" evidentiary rule found nowhere in the policy and used it to refuse review of Plaintiffs' supplemental evidence. Defendant has been unable to consistently describe the scope of the binding appraisal award. Defendant's own adjuster's September 6, 2024 inspection has no produced written record. These facts demonstrate a pattern of conduct inconsistent with the implied covenant of good faith and fair dealing each party owes under Minnesota contract law.

45. ### COUNT II — DECLARATORY JUDGMENT (Minn. Stat. Ch. 555)

> **Research notes (remove before filing).**
>
> **Legal basis (A2).** Minnesota Uniform Declaratory Judgments Act, Minn. Stat. §§ 555.01–555.16. Insurance coverage disputes are "particularly amenable to declaratory relief." *Seaway Port Auth. of Duluth v. Midland Ins. Co.*, 430 N.W.2d 242, 246 (Minn. App. 1988); *Onvoy, Inc. v. ALLETE, Inc.*, 736 N.W.2d 611, 617–18 (Minn. 2007).
>
> **Strength rating (revised May 2026): 70/100 overall; declaration (c) (coverage for unevaluated windows under *Quade* reservation) is now the highest-value declaration in the count and the declaratory companion to Count I Track 1 (paragraph 42(a) above).** Procedurally excellent; bedrock Minnesota law. Does not require disturbing the appraisal award.
>
> **Doctrines invoked inside this count.** E1 (*Quade* preclusion); E2 (J6944 scope limits, *Cedar Bluff* matching); E3 (*Atwater* reasonable expectations on the Definitions § 16 "marring includes denting" / § I.A.8(b) windstorm-restoration interaction); E4 (*contra proferentem*); B4 (estoppel of denial rationales).
>
> **Supporting facts.** Two formal denials on incompatible theories; $0 net post-appraisal; unevaluated-windows photographic record; defunct manufacturer; J6944 textual gates; the Definitions § 16 "denting" gloss within "Marring"; the § I.A.8(b) windstorm/hail restoration; Defendant's inconsistent post-award scope descriptions (9/18, 9/19, 10/14/2025).

46. Plaintiffs reallege and incorporate paragraphs 1 through 45 above.

47. A justiciable controversy exists between Plaintiffs and Defendant regarding the rights, duties, and legal relations of the parties under Policy 98055-49-65 with respect to the July 13, 2024 hail loss. Defendant has issued two formal denials, has paid $0 net to Plaintiffs after a binding appraisal award confirming hail causation, and has formally taken the position that the claim is "settled per the binding appraisal award received" and "under your deductible." Plaintiffs dispute each of those positions.

48. Plaintiffs request a judicial declaration, pursuant to Minn. Stat. §§ 555.01–.16, that:

   **(a) Coverage.** The July 13, 2024 hail loss is a covered loss under Section I (Loss or Damage Insured) of Policy 98055-49-65, subject only to the $17,720 windstorm/hail deductible.

   **(b) Preclusion of denial-letter exclusions for the awarded windows.** Under *Quade v. Secura Insurance*, 814 N.W.2d 703 (Minn. 2012), and the binding effect of the September 12, 2025 appraisal award under Minn. Stat. § 65A.01, Subd. 3, Defendant is precluded from asserting in this action that the windows identified by the codes "F1.7" and "B1.2" in the September 12, 2025 award were damaged by wear and tear, marring, corrosion, deterioration, decay, rust, inherent vice, latent defect, neglect, lack of maintenance, or faulty or defective construction. The exclusions cited in Defendant's November 4, 2024 denial letter cannot defeat coverage for those windows.

   **(c) Coverage for unevaluated windows.** The September 12, 2025 appraisal award did not resolve coverage as to hail-damaged windows the panel did not evaluate. For each such window, the question of coverage and amount of loss remains a live coverage question for the Court, including any defense Defendant may assert under any policy exclusion. Plaintiffs are entitled to coverage for any window the Court finds was damaged by the July 13, 2024 hail event.

   **(d) Marring exclusion and windstorm/hail restoration.** Section I.A.8 of the policy excludes "marring" of covered property; Section I.A.8(b) restores coverage for marring "directly caused by, arises out of or results from … windstorm or hail"; and the policy's Definitions § 16 includes "denting" as an enumerated example of "marring." Read together, the marring exclusion does not defeat coverage for hail-caused dents to insured windows. To the extent the policy's text is reasonably susceptible to more than one interpretation, the ambiguity is resolved in favor of coverage under Minnesota's settled rule that ambiguities in insurance contracts are construed against the drafter. *Hammer v. Investors Life Ins. Co. of N. Am.*, 511 N.W.2d 6, 8 (Minn. 1994); *Nathe Brothers, Inc. v. American Nat'l Fire Ins. Co.*, 615 N.W.2d 341, 344 (Minn. 2000).

   **(e) Endorsement J6944.** Endorsement J6944 ("Limited Matching of Undamaged Property") does not bar coverage for the cost to repair or replace windows that themselves sustained direct hail damage. Subparagraph (1) of the endorsement applies, by its own terms, only to "property which is not damaged by the covered peril." Where windows are dented by hail, they are damaged by the covered peril and fall outside the endorsement. Subparagraph (2) of the endorsement bars only "actual or perceived loss in value" and does not modify Defendant's obligation to pay the replacement cost of damaged windows under the policy's "comparable material and quality" standard. Where the original-equipment manufacturer of insured windows is no longer available to supply matching material, Endorsement J6944 does not relieve Defendant of the matching obligation recognized in *Cedar Bluff Townhome Condominium Ass'n v. American Family Mut. Ins. Co.*, 857 N.W.2d 290 (Minn. 2014).

   **(f) Estoppel of denial rationales.** Defendant has issued two formal denials on incompatible factual premises (September 3, 2024 — "no other damages observed"; November 4, 2024 — "wear and tear / 2020 already paid"). Plaintiffs have relied on each successive Farmers theory in turn, developing the administrative record around Defendant's stated grounds. Defendant is estopped from asserting in this action any denial rationale not stated in its written denial letters and is bound, with respect to the windows identified in the September 12, 2025 appraisal award, by the panel's hail-causation finding.

   **(g) Suit-limitation period.** This action is timely. The two-year contractual suit-limitation period in the policy controls; any shorter statutory text (including the language of Minn. Stat. § 65A.26 to the extent applicable to a multi-peril homeowner policy) is superseded by the policy's express terms or by Minn. Stat. § 65A.01, Subd. 3.

   **(h) Prior-claim payment does not bar new-sash damage.** A prior insurance-claim payment that covered one sash of the front dining-room window does not bar coverage for hail damage sustained on July 13, 2024 by other, previously unpaid sashes of the same window. Any exclusion of that window from the September 12, 2025 award on a prior-claim basis is a coverage determination outside the appraisal panel's authority and does not bind the Court.

49. The requested declarations will substantially terminate the controversy between the parties as required by Minn. Stat. § 555.06 and will streamline the determination of damages on Count I.

### COUNT III — ACTION TO CLARIFY, MODIFY, OR CORRECT THE APPRAISAL AWARD

> **Research notes (remove before filing).**
>
> **Legal basis (A3).** Minn. Stat. § 65A.01, Subd. 3 (statutory itemization requirement: "stating separately actual value and loss to each item"); *Mork v. Eureka-Security Fire & Marine Ins. Co.*, 879 F.3d 293 (8th Cir. 2018) (remand for clarification of ambiguous award); *Cincinnati Ins. Co. v. Rymer Cos.*, No. 24-3356 (8th Cir. Mar. 27, 2026) (same); *Oliver v. State Farm Fire & Cas. Ins. Co.*, 939 N.W.2d 749 (Minn. 2020) (appraisal not UAA arbitration; *Mork* common-law standard governs).
>
> **Strength rating (revised May 2026):** Tier 1 (*Herll*/*Rymer* remand for clarification): ~70/100 — the primary motion; Tier 2 (modification/correction under § 65A.01 Subd. 3): ~45/100; Tier 3 (full vacatur under *Mork*): ~25/100 on current facts. Lead with Tier 1; reserve Tier 2 as first alternative; reserve Tier 3 only if Tier 1 and Tier 2 fail and the *Mork* misconduct record (developed through discovery of Foster, Tomes, Roos, and Pure Construction) supports it. See `OffenseStrategies/AppraisalAwardStrategy/04_MotionTactics.md` for the tier-by-tier motion strategy.
>
> **Important:** Tier 3 (full vacatur) is in tension with E1 (*Quade* preclusion) — full vacatur would destroy the *Quade* causation binding, which is the family's most valuable single piece of doctrine. Pursue Tier 3 only if discovery delivers clear evidence of misconduct and the dollar gain from disturbing the $12,800 figure outweighs the loss of *Quade* preclusion.
>
> **Supporting facts.** Award lists only "F1.7" / "B1.2" against $12,800 lump RCV with no itemization (EventDocuments/Appraisal Documents/Appraisal_Result_Front.png); two-line $0 siding award without identified evaluation methodology against Prieve-documented siding penetrations (A-26, A-27, A-208, A-212) and trim chips (A-53, A-90, A-148); umpire produced no report or estimate (EventDocuments/Copy of ComunicationLogs.docx.md (9/24/2025 entry)); Defendant cannot consistently describe scope (9/18, 9/19, 10/14/2025); Foster selected by Pure Construction not by Plaintiffs; no on-site speaking with appraisers permitted on 9/12/2025; Tomes did not sign.

50. Plaintiffs reallege and incorporate paragraphs 1 through 49 above.

51. **Statutory non-itemization — the windows lump.** Minn. Stat. § 65A.01, Subd. 3 requires the appraisers to "appraise the loss, stating separately actual value and loss to each item," and provides that "an award in writing, so itemized, of any two when filed with this company shall determine the amount of actual value and loss." The September 12, 2025 award identifies the awarded windows only by the undefined designations "F1.7" and "B1.2" against a single lump-sum $12,800 Replacement Cost Value and $6,400 Actual Cash Value. The award does not state actual value and loss separately to each item. The award is not "so itemized" within the meaning of § 65A.01, Subd. 3.

51a. **Statutory non-itemization — the $0 siding line.** The September 12, 2025 award lists two siding-related items at $0 Replacement Cost Value and $0 Actual Cash Value. The award does not identify which siding observations the panel evaluated; whether the panel evaluated the documented siding penetrations photographed at A-26, A-27, A-208, and A-212; whether the panel evaluated the documented trim board chips photographed at A-53 (which the Prieve Report itself acknowledges is "visible in prior photo logs from Farmers Insurance dated October 13, 2020"), A-90, and A-148; or whether the panel evaluated the documented dented flashing at A-103, A-128, and A-129. An award of $0 on items the Prieve Report's own Photo Appendix A documents in its captions, without identification of any methodology, valuation basis, or even which observations the panel considered, is not the substantive appraisal of value § 65A.01, Subd. 3 contemplates. The siding lines are likewise not "so itemized" within the meaning of the statute.

51b. **Non-itemization and improper exclusion — the front dining-room window.** The September 12, 2025 award does not separately state the actual value and loss for the front dining-room window, a window Defendant's own appraiser documented as damaged. Defendant later explained the omission on the ground that the damage "was from the prior claim." The award does not state which of that three-sash window's sashes the panel treated as previously paid, the basis for treating any sash as previously paid, or whether the panel evaluated the July 13, 2024 damage to the sashes not within the 2020 claim. An award that silently omits a documented-damaged, multi-sash window — explained only by reference to a prior claim that, on information and belief, paid for a single sash — is not the item-by-item appraisal § 65A.01, Subd. 3 requires.

52. **Ambiguity warranting remand for clarification.** The award's facial structure, Defendant's mutually inconsistent post-award descriptions of the awarded scope on September 18, 19, and October 14, 2025, the umpire's failure to produce any report or estimate as confirmed by Defendant in writing on September 24, 2025, and the gap between the two-window awarded scope and the photographic record of damage across most of the home together render the September 12, 2025 award ambiguous as to scope and methodology. Under *Herll v. Auto-Owners Insurance Co.*, 879 F.3d 293 (8th Cir. 2018) (applying Minnesota law), and *Cincinnati Insurance Co. v. Rymer Cos.*, No. 24-3356 (8th Cir. Mar. 27, 2026), the appropriate remedy for an ambiguous appraisal award is remand to the panel for clarification of its own scope and methodology.

53. **Plaintiffs' primary requested relief is remand for clarification under *Herll* and *Rymer*.** Plaintiffs request that the Court remand the September 12, 2025 award to the appraisal panel (or to a successor panel if any panel member is unavailable) for the limited purpose of (a) identifying each window or item within the scope of the panel's evaluation by location, elevation, sash count, and manufacturer stamp; (b) stating separately the actual cash value and replacement cost value attributable to each such window or item; and (c) identifying any window or item that the family submitted for appraisal but the panel did not evaluate, with a brief statement of the reason; and (d) for any multi-sash window, stating separately the actual cash value and replacement cost value attributable to each sash, and, for any sash or window excluded on the ground of a prior claim, identifying the prior claim, the specific sash or sashes the panel understood to have been previously paid, and whether the panel independently evaluated the July 13, 2024 damage to any sash not previously paid.

54. **Alternative request — modification or correction.** In the alternative, Plaintiffs request that the Court modify or correct the September 12, 2025 award to comply with Minn. Stat. § 65A.01, Subd. 3's "stating separately actual value and loss to each item" requirement and to conform to the evidence in the record.

55. **Further alternative — vacatur under *Mork*.** In the further alternative, and only to the extent the Court determines that clarification or modification is unavailable or insufficient, Plaintiffs request that the Court vacate the September 12, 2025 award under *Mork v. Eureka-Security Fire & Marine Insurance Co.*, 230 Minn. 382, 42 N.W.2d 33 (1950). The grounds Plaintiffs preserve for this alternative request include: (i) the absence of any "competent and disinterested" umpire methodology evidenced by the umpire's failure to produce any report or estimate; (ii) the absence of statutory itemization; and (iii) any further misconduct or misfeasance discovery may reveal. This alternative request is preserved without prejudice to Plaintiffs' primary request for *Herll*/*Rymer* clarification, which Plaintiffs respectfully submit is the appropriate remedy on the present record.
