# V2 vs. V4 — What Changed and Why (Plain-Language Two-Pager)

**Prepared:** June 12, 2026. Written for readers without a legal background. V2 = `Draft_Complaint_v2_Balanced.md` (April 29, 2026). V4 = `Draft_Complaint_V4.md` (June 12, 2026, the operative draft, including today's edits).

---

## What these documents are

Both are drafts of the **complaint** — the document that starts the lawsuit. It tells the court who the parties are, what happened, what laws Farmers allegedly violated (each violation is a "count"), and what the family wants. V2 was the recommended draft as of late April. V4 supersedes it. About 80% of the factual story is the same in both; the differences are in strategy, count selection, and new facts learned in May–June 2026.

## What stayed the same

The core of the case is unchanged. Both versions tell the same story: a documented hailstorm on 7/13/2024, two denials by Farmers built on contradictory explanations, an engineer's report whose own photos undercut its conclusions, an "engineer's report only" rule found nowhere in the policy, and an appraisal that confirmed hail caused the damage but awarded only two windows — netting the family $0 after the deductible. Both versions keep the same first two counts: **Count I (breach of contract)** — Farmers didn't pay for a covered loss — and **Count II (declaratory judgment)** — asking the judge to formally settle what the policy covers. Both demand a jury, both seek interest, and both reserve the "bad faith" claim (a special Minnesota statute) for a later motion, because the law does not allow it in an opening complaint.

## The five main changes

**1. Who files it.** V2 was written assuming a lawyer would sign and file it — it has a signature block for an attorney and research notes sprinkled through the text for counsel to review. V4 is built for the family to file **pro se** (on their own): both family members sign, the research notes are pulled out of the body, and two internal appendices were added — a pre-filing verification checklist (gates V-1 through V-8) and an exhibit list — both to be removed before filing.

**2. Five counts became four — the fraud count was dropped.** V2 included a count for common-law fraud / negligent misrepresentation (built on Farmers adopting the engineer's false "no hail in Eden Prairie after 2020" statement). It was always rated weak (~25/100) because fraud requires proving the family *believed and relied on* the false statement — and the family famously did the opposite, immediately disproving it with weather data and neighbor canvassing. By family decision on 6/12/2026, V4 drops it. The trade-off: dropping the fraud count also closes the path to **punitive damages** (punishment money requires an underlying tort), which is why V4 contains no punitive-damages reservation. What's kept instead is the Consumer Fraud Act count (V2's Count V, now V4's Count IV), which targets the same conduct but does **not** require proving the family believed the false statements — and it carries the case's main hope of recovering **attorney fees**.

**3. The appraisal count (Count III) was re-ordered — the biggest strategy change.** Both versions attack the appraisal award's vagueness (it names two windows only by the codes "F1.7" and "B1.2" against one lump sum, with no umpire report, while the statute requires itemizing value "to each item"). The difference is what each version asks for *first*:

- **V2's first ask:** send the award back to the appraisal panel to clarify it ("remand").
- **V4's first ask:** a declaration that because the award wasn't properly itemized, it **does not block** the family's contract claim for anything it never itemized — while still keeping the part that helps the family (the binding finding that hail caused the damage).

The reason for the flip is a risk identified in May–June strategy work: remand hands the pen back to the panel that awarded $0. The panel could come back saying "we evaluated every window and found hail on only two" — converting a vague award the family can work around into a clarified, binding award that shuts the door. So in V4, remand is demoted to a fallback the court must specifically want, vacatur (throwing the award out entirely) stays last because it would also destroy the helpful hail finding, and a new "backstop" paragraph adds: even if the panel did evaluate the other windows, any rejection based on policy exclusions or "already paid in 2020" was outside the panel's legal authority, so the court decides those questions fresh. A related ground rule (recorded in the appendix): the three appraisers get deposed under oath *before* any remand motion is ever filed.

**4. New facts and protections added since April.** V4 incorporates material that did not exist or was not developed when V2 was written: the **never-inspected garage window** — a window on the rear of the garage, behind the shed, that appears nowhere in the engineer's 217 photos, pleaded as the cleanest example of damage no one ever evaluated (must be photographed and documented before filing — gate V-1); the **deadline-protection paragraphs** — the family's July 2025 written question to Farmers about deadlines, Farmers' non-answer, and Farmers' continued participation in the appraisal, pleaded so Farmers cannot spring a shorter (one-year hail) deadline later — a risk verified as real on 6/12/2026 (see the § 65A.26 note at gate V-4; getting the MN080 endorsement text is now the most urgent task in the case); a **missing-records paragraph** preserving sanctions arguments over the adjuster's vanished inspection notes; and refined treatment of the **dining-room window** (excluded from the award as "from the prior claim" even though, per the family, only one of its three sashes was ever paid in 2020).

**5. Sharpened legal positions.** V2's broad declaration that Farmers is "estopped" (barred) from raising any new denial reasons was removed — research showed Minnesota law does not let that doctrine expand coverage, so keeping it invited an easy loss. V4 replaces it with narrower, better-grounded declarations. V4 also fixes party details (Jie Hu pleaded as spouse-insured rather than named insured), names Farmers' attorney-in-fact in the caption, and — as of today's edits — corrects the venue statute, softens a sentence that overstated how "binding" the award is, neutralizes who authored the post-award window descriptions, and replaces a wrong administrative-rule citation with the correct statute.

## Bottom line

V2 was a lawyer-ready draft that led with sending the appraisal back to the panel and kept a weak fraud count alive mainly to preserve punitive damages. V4 is the leaner, pro se filing: four counts instead of five, the appraisal attack restructured so its strongest move carries no downside, new evidence and deadline protections folded in, and the known citation and wording errors fixed. The open items before filing are the ¶ 49(b) flashing inconsistency (deferred by user decision) and the family-action gates — above all obtaining the complete certified policy with the MN080 endorsement text.
