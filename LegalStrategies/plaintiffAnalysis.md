# Plaintiff Line-Up Analysis — Yibiao Lu Only vs. Yibiao Lu and Jie Hu

**Case:** Yibiao Lu & Jie Hu v. Fire Insurance Exchange (d/b/a Farmers Insurance)
**Policy:** 98055-49-65 | **Claim:** 7007986837-1-1
**Loss Date:** July 13, 2024
**Filing Deadline:** July 13, 2026 (2-year contractual suit limitation)
**Prepared:** April 29, 2026

> **Question.** How significant is it to have Yibiao Lu as the only plaintiff vs. having both Yibiao Lu and Jie Hu as plaintiffs? What are the pros and cons of each posture?
>

## 1. Bottom Line

**Include both. The policy text resolves the question in favor of Jie Hu.** The "you" / "named insured" definition in `HomePolicy/2014_newBusiness_policy.pdf` (page 4, Definitions section) provides:

> "Throughout this policy, **'you' and 'your' mean the 'named insured' shown in the Declarations or renewal notice and your spouse or registered domestic partner under applicable state law if a resident of the same household with you.** Any domestic partner must be registered prior to the date of a loss."

The policy's "Insured" definition (Definition 14, page 5) further provides:

> "14. Insured — means: a. you; b. permanent residents of your household who are: (1) your relatives; or (2) other persons under the age of 21 and in the care of any person named above..."

Read together, the policy form expressly extends "you" to the named insured's spouse (or registered domestic partner under Minnesota law) so long as they reside in the same household. Because Yibiao Lu and Jie Hu are spouses residing at 17756 George Moran Dr. (the insured premises designated as "Owner Occupied (Primary Residence)"), Jie Hu is a "you" under the policy by operation of policy text — i.e., she is a co-named insured for all purposes the policy uses "you." Even setting aside her spousal status, Jie Hu would qualify as an "insured" under Definition 14(b)(1) as a relative who permanently resides in the household.

**Two factual predicates to confirm before filing** (not contained in the policy itself):
1. Marital / registered-domestic-partner status of Yibiao Lu and Jie Hu;
2. Household residence at 17756 George Moran Dr. on the loss date (July 13, 2024).

Both are strongly indicated by the existing record (Farmers' consistent dual addressing throughout the claim; the project's references to "the Lu/Hu family"; the "Owner Occupied (Primary Residence)" classification) but should be formally confirmed by counsel before the complaint is signed.

The **standing risk previously flagged at § 3.1** is therefore essentially eliminated by the policy text. The decision to include both Plaintiffs as co-named insureds is correct.

## 2. Pros of Having Both Yibiao Lu and Jie Hu as Plaintiffs

### 2.1 Farmers itself treated them as joint insureds throughout

The November 4, 2024 denial letter, the January 31, 2025 appeal, the Department of Commerce complaint correspondence, and several other communications address both. Suing as both matches the posture Farmers already set and weakens any later argument that Jie Hu was not Farmers' "insured."

### 2.2 Captures both spouses' insurable interest

Minnesota recognizes a resident spouse's insurable interest in the marital home regardless of which spouse is on title or on the application. The Farmers Next Generation Homeowners Policy form almost certainly defines "you" or "named insured" to include the spouse residing in the household — that is industry standard for homeowner policies. If that definition is in this policy, Jie Hu *is* a named insured, and excluding her from the suit would mean leaving a co-named insured's claims on the table.

### 2.3 Captures Jie Hu's individual reliance and out-of-pocket damages

The reliance-based damages under B2 (fraud) and C3 (Minnesota Consumer Fraud Act) include the family's costs of pursuing the appraisal channel, the Commerce complaint, the neighbor canvass, and the consequential water damage. To the extent Jie Hu personally incurred those costs (e.g., she was the one who took the 2/3/2025 phone call with Bradlee Monson, she signed the Pure Construction retainer, she paid out of her own funds), those damages are individual to her. Without her as a plaintiff, those individual claims are not recoverable through Yibiao Lu alone.

### 2.4 Avoids a Minn. R. Civ. P. 19 necessary-party challenge

If Jie Hu holds title to the property as a joint tenant or tenant in common with Yibiao Lu, she has a real-property ownership interest in the damaged dwelling. A defendant can move under Rule 19 to require joinder of an absent necessary party — and the resulting motion practice is a distraction. Naming both up front eliminates this avenue.

### 2.5 Stronger factual narrative on the shifting-rationales theme

Many of the Farmers conduct facts that drive the bad-faith narrative — the circular Pete-Prieve-Brau refusal to discuss the report; the 2/3/2025 Monson phone call; the day-of-appraisal blocking by Pure Construction; the deadline-question non-answer of 7/10–7/11/2025 — involved both Plaintiffs as participants. Having both testify as plaintiffs is more powerful than having one testify as a plaintiff and the other as a third-party witness.

### 2.6 Privileged communications are easier to manage

With both as plaintiffs represented by the same counsel under joint representation, intra-family communications about the case are protected by the joint-client privilege. With only one plaintiff, communications between Yibiao Lu and Jie Hu about the case may be discoverable as conversations with a non-party witness.

### 2.7 Settlement authority is shared but not split

Both plaintiffs must consent to any settlement, which sounds like a complication but in practice is a feature for a married couple sharing the same household and the same loss — it ensures neither is bound to a settlement the other rejects.

## 3. Cons of Having Both

### 3.1 Standing risk for Jie Hu if she is not a named insured

If the policy text defines "you" narrowly to exclude a spouse not on the application, Defendant can move to dismiss Jie Hu under Minn. R. Civ. P. 12.02(e) or for lack of standing. The motion would not kill the case (Yibiao Lu remains), but it creates docket noise and a partial dismissal that defense counsel can later cite against the broader narrative.

### 3.2 Doubles the discovery exposure

Both Plaintiffs are subject to deposition, interrogatories, requests for admission, and document production. Two depositions instead of one. Pre-litigation communications between the spouses become potential discovery targets if they were not made in anticipation of litigation.

### 3.3 Adds verification and signature complexity

The complaint, sworn proof of loss, § 604.18 affidavits, and § 549.191 motion affidavits all require both Plaintiffs to sign in their individual capacities.

### 3.4 Joint-representation conflicts must be managed

If at any point Plaintiffs' interests diverge — settlement valuation, allocation between them, divorce, etc. — counsel must navigate joint-representation rules under Minn. R. Prof. Conduct 1.7. Unlikely on these facts but a real consideration.

## 4. Pros of Having Only Yibiao Lu

### 4.1 Cleanest standing

Yibiao Lu signed the application on October 22, 2014. He is unambiguously a named insured. No standing issue. No Rule 12 or Rule 19 motion to defend.

### 4.2 Simplified pleading and discovery

One Plaintiff, one set of damages, one deponent, one verification.

### 4.3 Cleaner privilege

Attorney-client privilege runs between counsel and a single client. No joint-client framework to manage.

### 4.4 Single decision-maker for settlement

Yibiao Lu controls offers, counteroffers, and the eventual settlement decision.

---

## 5. Cons of Having Only Yibiao Lu

### 5.1 Loses any individual recovery for Jie Hu

If Jie Hu has a personal insurable interest, has incurred individual reliance damages, or holds title to the property, those claims are not pursued.

### 5.2 Inconsistent with how Farmers treated the claim

Farmers addressed both throughout. Suing as only Yibiao Lu invites the optical question of "why is Jie Hu not here if Farmers treated her as an insured?"

### 5.3 Jie Hu becomes a third-party witness rather than a plaintiff

Her testimony — which is central to several conduct facts — is structurally less powerful as a witness than as a plaintiff. Her testimony about the 2/3/2025 Monson phone call, for example, becomes a Farmers-can-impeach-her-as-third-party scenario rather than a co-plaintiff testifying about her own treatment.

### 5.4 Risk of Rule 19 motion if Jie Hu is a necessary party

As above. Excluding her does not avoid this risk; it invites it.

### 5.5 Property-interest ambiguity

If Jie Hu holds any real-property interest in the dwelling, the loss is partially hers. Recovering the full loss through Yibiao Lu alone is technically possible but invites valuation arguments.

## 7. Verifications

### 7.1 Policy "Definitions" section — RESOLVED

✅ **Verified against `HomePolicy/2014_newBusiness_policy.pdf` page 4 (Definitions section, immediately following the "Agreement" provision).** The policy expressly defines "you" and "your" to include the named insured's "spouse or registered domestic partner under applicable state law if a resident of the same household with you." This is the standard industry definition and it covers Jie Hu by operation of policy text.

### 7.2 Marital / registered-domestic-partner status — TO CONFIRM

Counsel should confirm before filing that Yibiao Lu and Jie Hu are married (or in a registered domestic partnership under applicable Minnesota law). The project record strongly indicates marriage (`CLAUDE.md` references "the Lu/Hu family"; both addressed in Farmers correspondence; both at 17756 George Moran Dr.). A marriage certificate or domestic-partnership registration filing should be in counsel's file before the complaint is signed.

### 7.3 Household residence on the loss date — TO CONFIRM

Counsel should confirm both Plaintiffs resided at 17756 George Moran Dr. on July 13, 2024. The Declarations classify the property as "Owner Occupied (Primary Residence)," and the entire factual record proceeds on the assumption that both Plaintiffs lived there throughout the relevant period. Confirmation should be straightforward.

### 7.4 Hennepin County Recorder title check — STILL HELPFUL

Although no longer dispositive (Jie Hu's standing is now established by policy text), pulling title is still worthwhile because it (a) confirms whether Jie Hu has a real-property interest independent of the policy and (b) forecloses any later valuation argument that her interest in the loss is partial.

### 7.5 If Jie Hu turns out NOT to be a spouse or registered domestic partner

The narrow exclusionary scenario: if Jie Hu is not in fact married to Yibiao Lu and not registered as a domestic partner, the policy's "you" definition does not reach her for first-party property coverage purposes. In that scenario:

- She would still qualify as an "Insured" under Definition 14(b)(1) as a relative who permanently resides in the household — but only for Section II (Liability) purposes, not Section I (Property).
- The cleaner pleading is Yibiao Lu only, with declaratory relief sought under Count II (A2) seeking a declaration that Jie Hu is covered.
- This scenario is unlikely on the existing record but should be ruled out before the complaint is signed.

## 8. Recommendation

**Include both Yibiao Lu and Jie Hu as Plaintiffs.** With the policy text now verified, the recommendation is settled:

- **Policy text resolves standing.** The "you" definition expressly includes the named insured's resident spouse / registered domestic partner. The Declarations name only Yibiao Lu, but the policy form's "you" definition makes Jie Hu a co-named insured for all purposes the policy uses "you."
- **Farmers treated both as insureds throughout.** Their conduct is consistent with the policy's "you" definition; suing as both matches that record.
- **Captured damages and narrative power are real.** Jie Hu's individual reliance damages, her testimony as a co-plaintiff (rather than third-party witness) on key conduct facts (the 2/3/2025 Monson phone call, the day-of-appraisal blocking by Pure Construction, the deadline-question exchange), and any property-interest she holds are all preserved.
- **Standing risk is essentially eliminated.** A defense Rule 12 motion targeting Jie Hu individually would have to argue against the policy's own "you" definition — a textually losing posture.
- **Rule 19 necessary-party exposure is eliminated** by joining both at the start.

The remaining open items (marital status and household residence on the loss date) are factual confirmations counsel can complete in a single sitting.

### 8.1 Hybrid alternative

If counsel wants to be extra cautious, a hybrid posture is available: name both as Plaintiffs, but plead in the alternative that "to the extent Jie Hu is not a named insured under the Policy, Yibiao Lu sues individually for the full amount of the covered loss." That language preserves both routes without committing to either if standing later becomes an issue. Sample phrasing for the complaint's parties section:

> "Plaintiffs Yibiao Lu and Jie Hu are named insureds under Policy 98055-49-65, husband and wife, residing at the insured premises. To the extent Jie Hu is determined not to be a named insured under the Policy, Plaintiff Yibiao Lu sues individually for the full amount of the covered loss and prays in the alternative for a declaration under Minn. Stat. Ch. 555 that Jie Hu is covered under the Policy as a resident of the named insured's household."

### 8.2 Decision timing

This decision should be made before the complaint is finalized for filing — not deferred to a later motion. Once filed, changing the plaintiff line-up requires a Rule 15 motion to amend or, in the case of dropping a plaintiff, a Rule 41(a) voluntary dismissal as to that plaintiff. Both are routine but add procedural noise the family does not need.
