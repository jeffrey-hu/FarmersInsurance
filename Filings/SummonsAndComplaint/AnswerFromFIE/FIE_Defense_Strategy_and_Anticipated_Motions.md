# FIE's Defense Strategy — Analysis & Anticipated Motions

**Case:** *Yibiao Lu and Jie Hu v. Fire Insurance Exchange* — Court File No. 27-CV-26-11606
**Court:** Hennepin County District Court, Fourth Judicial District (Judge Jamie Anderson)
**Re:** Reading of Defendant's Answer (dated Aug. 10, 2026) for defense strategy; what to watch
**Prepared:** August 11, 2026

> **Not legal advice.** I am not a lawyer. This is a strategic reading of the Answer to help you prepare and to discuss with a Minnesota-licensed attorney. Verify every rule, deadline, and case citation before relying on it.

---

## Executive summary

FIE's Answer is built around a single strategy: **treat the September 12, 2025 appraisal award as a settlement of the entire claim, then use the $17,720 deductible to drive the payment to zero.** Everything else — the flat denials, the "speaks for itself" non-answers, the preserved exclusions, the jury demand — supports or protects that one move. The strategy has a structural weakness FIE is trying to paper over: it only works if the appraisal panel evaluated *all* of the damaged windows and components, and FIE will not actually commit to that, because its own record (a two-window award, a missing umpire report, an unsigned appraiser, and a 217-photograph engineering appendix cataloguing 25+ damaged windows) shows the panel did not.

Your job is to keep the case out of the "it's all settled and under the deductible" box by proving **scope** (covered hail damage the panel never evaluated) and **value** (enough covered loss to clear $17,720). FIE's counter-strategy is to stop you on either one.

---

## FIE's core theory: the award as a global settlement

FIE admits the award is "binding" (¶48; Affirmative Defense #2), admits the panel found **hail** caused the two awarded windows (¶¶31–32), and repeatedly frames the claim as "settled per the binding appraisal award received." Because the award's ACV ($6,400) is below the deductible ($17,720), FIE says nothing is owed.

The critical tactical read is that FIE wants the award to cut **two inconsistent ways at once**:

- **Binding and dispositive** when that produces $0 (the claim is "settled").
- **Uncommitted as to scope** the moment you point out the panel evaluated only 2 of 25+ damaged windows — at which point FIE retreats to "the Appraisal Award speaks for itself" and "without sufficient information."

FIE cannot have both. Its global-settlement theory requires that the panel evaluated everything, and **as the party asserting the award as a preclusive bar, FIE bears the burden of proving the panel actually evaluated the other windows and components.** Its own missing records make that showing hard. This tension is the center of the case — press it everywhere.

---

## Five tactical patterns to recognize

**1. The "speaks for itself / without knowledge" wall.** A large share of the specific factual paragraphs drew non-committal answers. This deflects admissions on the damaging specifics (the two inconsistent denials, the Prieve inconsistencies, the missing Brau notes) and forces you to prove everything. Weakness: FIE may not plead "without knowledge" about facts within its own control — its adjuster's notes, its claim file, its 2020 records. That is both a discovery pressure point and a Minn. Stat. § 72A.201 claim-file-documentation problem.

**2. A causation denial aimed at the *unevaluated* damage.** FIE denies generally that the July 2024 hail caused damage (¶12) while admitting hail on the two awarded windows. The denial is really aimed at the other windows and the non-window components — because conceding hail broadly would exceed the deductible. Expect an engineering expert to label the other dents as cosmetic, marring, wear, or pre-2020.

**3. Exclusions held in reserve — outside the two awarded windows.** FIE concedes (via *Quade*) it cannot reassert exclusions on the awarded windows, but wear-and-tear, marring, corrosion, prior-payment, and cosmetic-damage remain available against everything else. The **marring / cosmetic-damage** theory is its cleanest argument against dents; watch for it.

**4. The deductible wall — won quietly through ACV and depreciation.** Even if you prove additional covered windows, FIE still wins if total *covered ACV* stays under $17,720. Its quiet plan: minimize the covered-window count, push valuation to depreciated ACV rather than replacement cost, and attack your numbers. **FIE can lose the causation fight and still win on valuation.** Watch the ACV-vs-RCV framing and exactly how the policy applies the deductible (to the loss, per occurrence).

**5. A thin Answer plus a jury demand.** Only three boilerplate affirmative defenses, and — notably — **no statute-of-limitations/suit-limitation defense, no failure-to-mitigate, no setoff, no cosmetic-exclusion defense pleaded.** Either minimalism with a plan to amend after discovery, or under-pleading. Either way, expect a possible **motion to amend the Answer**. The jury demand raises cost and complexity for a pro se plaintiff and signals FIE will go the distance; remember the § 604.18 bad-faith and Consumer Fraud Act fee questions are decided by the **judge**, not the jury.

## What FIE conceded — lock these in and use them offensively

These are admitted or effectively admitted and should be cemented through Requests for Admission and used affirmatively:

- The policy was valid and in force; the deductible is $17,720.
- The award is **binding**, and the panel found **hail** caused the two awarded windows (*Quade* causation locked; FIE cannot relitigate wear/marring/etc. on those windows).
- **The policy contains no provision requiring evidence from an engineer** (¶26) — this guts the "engineer's report only" gatekeeping rule.
- The umpire produced **no report or estimate**; appraiser Greg Tomes **did not sign** the award; the award uses undefined codes "F1.7"/"B1.2" and a single lump sum.
- FIE has paid **$0**.

The award's ambiguity and thin support are a double-edged sword FIE created: they undercut FIE's ability to prove the award "settled everything."

## Priorities — what to watch most closely

1. **The scope fight decides the case.** FIE's global-settlement theory vs. your *Quade*/unevaluated-windows theory determines whether you reach the merits at all. Nail down, window-by-window, which units the panel evaluated. FIE's "speaks for itself" answers cannot survive a direct interrogatory.
2. **The deductible math and valuation.** Line up RCV/valuation evidence and an expert so FIE cannot quietly keep you under $17,720 even if you win causation on more windows.
3. **Watch the pleading-amendment deadline.** Hold FIE to the scheduling-order deadline for amending its Answer; preserve the **waiver** argument against any late limitations defense.
4. **The missing Brau notes.** If FIE cannot produce its own adjuster's September 6, 2024 inspection record, that supports an adverse-inference request and undercuts FIE's "we investigated adequately" posture.
5. **The single biggest event to prepare for:** FIE's likely early **summary-judgment motion** that the binding award resolved the claim below the deductible. See the prep outline below.

## Prep outline — defeating FIE's binding-award summary-judgment motion (Motion A)

This is the motion most likely to end the case if unanswered. Treat defeating it as the organizing goal of discovery.

**1. The legal standard works in your favor.** Summary judgment is proper only if there is **no genuine issue of material fact** and the movant is entitled to judgment as a matter of law; the court views the evidence in the light most favorable to the non-moving party (you) and does not weigh credibility. You do not have to *win* the scope question on this motion — you only have to show a **genuine factual dispute** about it.

**2. Frame FIE's motion as depending on a factual premise it cannot establish.** FIE's argument reduces to: *the award resolved the entire claim.* That is true only if the panel **evaluated every damaged window and component**. Whether it did is a question of fact. Because FIE is the party asserting the award as a **preclusive bar**, it bears the burden of proving the panel's scope — and it has produced no umpire report, an unsigned appraiser, and an award identifying only two windows by undefined codes.

**3. Anchor the *Quade* rule.** Under *Quade v. Secura Insurance*, 814 N.W.2d 703 (Minn. 2012), the appraisal panel's authority extends to determining the amount of loss (including causation) **for the items it evaluates**. It does **not** convert an award on two windows into a binding resolution of windows and components the panel never looked at. For unevaluated items, coverage and amount of loss remain **live questions for the court**. Reinforce with the remand-for-clarification and appraiser-testimony limits recognized in *Cincinnati Ins. Co. v. Rymer Cos.*, No. 24-3356 (8th Cir. Mar. 27, 2026): an ambiguous award can be remanded, and appraiser testimony cannot be used to expand or "explain" an award into something it does not say.

**4. Assemble the counter-evidence now (the affidavit record you will file in opposition):**

- **The Prieve Photo Appendix A**, cataloguing dents/marks across at least twenty-five distinct windows plus a sliding door, and non-window damage (dented downspouts A-42, A-91–A-93, A-98, A-99; dented flashing A-103; chipped trim A-54, A-90, A-148) — proof the damage the award ignored exists and was documented by FIE's own engineer.
- **FIE's interrogatory answers and RFA responses** pinning down which windows the panel evaluated (your First Set already forces this window-by-window).
- **The 2020 claim file** showing the 2020 payment reached at most two sashes — foreclosing the "already paid in 2020" theory for the rest.
- **A retained engineer's affidavit** opining that the July 13–14, 2024 hail caused damage to the broader set of windows/components and rebutting Prieve's methodology.
- **The award itself** (undefined F1.7/B1.2, lump sum, no umpire report, Tomes unsigned) — showing on its face it does not resolve the whole claim.
- **The appraisal demand** listing only Windows, Siding (repaint), and Siding (R&R) — proof the panel had no jurisdiction over downspouts, flashing, and doors.

**5. Make three independent arguments, any one of which defeats the motion:**

- **(a) Scope / *Quade*.** The panel evaluated only two windows; coverage and amount of loss for the rest are live questions the award did not decide. Fact dispute → deny.
- **(b) No jurisdiction over unsubmitted components.** The demand never submitted downspouts, flashing, or doors, so the award cannot bar those claims regardless of scope. Pure legal point in your favor.
- **(c) Valuation / deductible not established as a matter of law.** Because covered loss beyond the two windows is disputed, FIE cannot show as a matter of law that the total covered loss is under $17,720. Fact dispute → deny.

**6. Convert their own theory against them.** FIE's motion needs the award to be both binding *and* comprehensive. Quote FIE's own hedging answers ("speaks for itself," "without sufficient information") back at the court: FIE will not say what the award covers, yet asks the court to hold it covers everything. That is the definition of an unresolved factual dispute.

**7. Preserve the alternative relief.** Even in the worst case, Count III asks the court to declare the award **non-preclusive** as to what it did not itemize, or to **remand** it for clarification (*Herll*; *Rymer*), or to **modify** it. Argue these in the alternative so a scope ruling for FIE still does not end the case.

**8. Housekeeping that wins motions.** Get your engineering expert retained early (an opposition affidavit is far stronger than argument); serve the discovery now so FIE's scope answers are on the record before the motion; and if FIE moves before you have had a fair chance at discovery, invoke the summary-judgment-continuance mechanism (Minn. R. Civ. P. 56.04) to ask the court to defer ruling until you complete the scope and valuation discovery.

## One-line takeaways

- FIE's case is the **deductible plus a global-settlement reading of the award**; your case is **scope plus valuation**.
- The award's **ambiguity and missing support** are FIE's own problem — use them.
- Prepare the **anti-summary-judgment record** (engineer affidavit + scope discovery) as the first priority.
- Hold the line on the **amendment deadline** and the **waived limitations defense**.
