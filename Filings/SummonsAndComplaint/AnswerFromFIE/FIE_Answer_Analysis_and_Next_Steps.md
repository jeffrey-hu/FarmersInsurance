## Bottom line up front

FIE's Answer is **narrow and defensive**, and in a few important respects it is **thin**. Its entire theory reduces to one sentence: *the September 12, 2025 appraisal award is binding, it valued the loss below your $17,720 deductible, so nothing is owed and the claim is "settled."* Almost everything else is a general denial or a "the document speaks for itself" non-answer.

The most consequential thing about the Answer may be **what is missing from it**: FIE pleaded only three boilerplate affirmative defenses and did **not** plead any statute-of-limitations or suit-limitation defense — the very defense your Complaint spent pages pre-empting. Under Minnesota law an unpleaded affirmative defense is generally waived. That is favorable to you.

Your path to recovery has not changed and the Answer largely confirms it: because the two awarded windows alone ($6,400 ACV) fall under the deductible, **you win money only if you prove additional covered hail damage** — the ~25 other windows, siding, downspouts, flashing, and doors that the appraisal panel never evaluated — sufficient to push the covered loss above $17,720. FIE's evasive answers preserve that as a live factual fight, which is exactly where discovery and an engineering expert come in.

## The strategic reads that matter most

**1. No limitations defense was pleaded — likely waived.** Your Complaint (¶¶28–29, 57(g)) worked hard to defeat a suit-limitation defense (waiver/estoppel; two-year period under § 65A.01 rather than a one-year § 65A.26 hail period). FIE's Answer raises **no** limitations defense at all. Under Minn. R. Civ. P. 8.03, statute of limitations is an affirmative defense that must be affirmatively pleaded, and defenses omitted from the answer are generally waived. **Watch for FIE to try to amend** its answer to add it; if it does, be ready to oppose on waiver, prejudice, and the merits (your waiver/estoppel facts). Do not raise it for them — just preserve your objection.

**2. The jurisdiction defense is weak — but it has not been conceded away.** *(Corrected September 11, 2026. The original text of this item said FIE admitted maintaining a registered agent and had thereby conceded personal jurisdiction. That was wrong.)*

Complaint ¶5 alleged six things: that Defendant is a reciprocal insurance exchange; organized under California law; authorized to transact insurance in Minnesota; **acting by and through its attorney-in-fact, Fire Underwriters Association**; operating under the trade name Farmers Insurance and licensed by the Department of Commerce; and that it **maintains a registered agent for service of process in Minnesota**.

FIE's answer to ¶5, in full:

> "Admit that Defendant is a reciprocal insurance exchange licensed to conduct business in Minnesota. The remainder of paragraph 5 includes statements and conclusions of law to which no response is required in this Answer."

So FIE admitted **only** that it is a reciprocal exchange licensed in Minnesota. It did not admit the California organization, the attorney-in-fact relationship with Fire Underwriters Association, the Farmers trade name, or the registered agent. And on Complaint ¶7 (personal jurisdiction under Minn. Stat. § 543.19) it answered only that the paragraph "contains of a statement or conclusion of law which requires no response."

**What the defense actually is.** Affirmative Defense 1 bundles two defenses with opposite characters under Minn. R. Civ. P. 12.08. *Lack of jurisdiction over the person* is waived unless raised by motion or in the responsive pleading — so pleading it was necessary to preserve it, and that is the work the sentence is doing. *Failure to state a claim* cannot be waived by omission; it "may be made in any pleading permitted or ordered pursuant to Rule 7.01, or by motion for judgment on the pleadings, or at the trial on the merits." Pleading that half was unnecessary.

**Assessment.** A genuine personal-jurisdiction challenge is very unlikely to succeed: FIE is a licensed insurer that issued a policy on Minnesota property to Minnesota residents and handled the claim here, which is squarely within Minn. Stat. § 543.19. The more plausible reading is that the defense is a placeholder for a **capacity or proper-party** argument — that a reciprocal exchange is an unincorporated association that must be sued in a particular form, or that the entity named is not the contracting entity. Note the posture: FIE says there may be no jurisdiction over it while declining to say which entity is the right one.

**Why this now has a deadline.** The September 8, 2026 Scheduling Order sets **joinder of additional parties at November 16, 2026**, and the Court did not adopt Plaintiffs' request to place the amendment deadline after substantial completion of document production. If there is a proper-party problem, it must surface before that date. Do not chase the defense — make FIE define it. Interrogatory 15 asks the factual basis of each affirmative defense; the targeted follow-up is the entity-identity interrogatory proposed as item **I-13** in `Filings/Discovery/FirstSetDiscovey/Proposed_Changes_Interrogatories_First_Set_2026-09-11.md`, which asks FIE to state under oath which entity issued the policy, employed the adjusters, made the denial decisions, retained the vendors, and holds the claim file.

**3. *Quade* is doing a lot of work — for you.** *Quade v. Secura Insurance*, 814 N.W.2d 703 (Minn. 2012), holds that determining the "amount of loss" necessarily includes determining **cause**, so the appraisal panel's causation finding is within its authority and binds the insurer for what it decided. FIE admits the panel found **hail** caused the two awarded windows, so FIE **cannot re-litigate** that those windows were wear-and-tear, marring, corrosion, etc. But *Quade* cuts the other way on scope: the panel's authority reaches **only the items it actually evaluated**. For the windows and components the panel never looked at, causation and amount of loss remain **open questions for the court** — which is your Count I lane.

**4. The whole case is the deductible math.** Award = $12,800 RCV / $6,400 ACV for two windows. Deductible = $17,720. To recover a dollar on breach of contract you must establish covered hail loss above $17,720. That means proving up the additional damaged windows (Prieve's own Photo Appendix A catalogs dents across 25+ windows plus a sliding door), the siding, and the non-window components (downspouts, flashing, door) that the appraisal demand did not cover and the panel had no jurisdiction over. **This is an evidentiary/expert problem, not a pleading problem.**

**5. FIE's own record is your best evidence.** Prieve's report and photo log, FIE's 2020 claim file, and Brau's missing inspection notes are, on your telling, internally inconsistent and incomplete. FIE's repeated "speaks for itself" answers invite you to put those documents in front of the factfinder. The 2020 file (dents on only two sashes) is the linchpin that forecloses the "already paid in 2020" theory for everything else.

## Recommended next steps, in priority order

**1. Do not file a reply to the Answer.** Under Minn. R. Civ. P. 7.01, a reply is required only when the answer contains a **counterclaim** (or the court orders one). FIE's Answer has affirmative defenses but **no counterclaim**, so its new matter is automatically deemed denied and you file nothing in response. (Filing an unnecessary reply is a common pro-se misstep — avoid it.)

**2. Calendar the case-management/scheduling track now.** In Minnesota state court, once the case is at issue the parties are expected to confer and the court sets a scheduling order under Rule 16; Hennepin County uses an **Informational Statement (Gen. R. Prac. 111 / Form 111.02)** to do this. Expect (or ask the court/clerk about) a deadline to file that statement and to meet-and-confer on a discovery plan under Rule 26.06. Get the trial track (expedited/standard/complex), discovery cutoff, expert-disclosure dates, and dispositive-motion deadline fixed in writing. Missing these is how good cases get hurt.

**3. Open discovery aggressively and target the gaps you already identified.** This is the highest-value work you can do. Prepare and serve:

- **Requests for Production (Rule 34)** for: the complete claim file for claim …6837-1-1 and the 2020 claim …9499-1; the **full 2020 Xactimate and photo log** (line-item level); **Nicholas Brau's September 6, 2024 notes/sketches** (or a verified explanation of their absence); Hancock's complete inspection workproduct; all versions of the Prieve engagement letter and scope; all internal claim notes, activity logs, and communications; the umpire's file; and any documents on the "engineer's report only" rule.
- **Interrogatories (Rule 33, max 50)** asking FIE to state, window-by-window, which units the panel evaluated, the basis for each non-award, and whether each non-award rested on causation vs. a coverage ground (this forces the *Quade* line into the open).
- **Requests for Admission (Rule 36)** to lock down the internal inconsistencies (e.g., that the 2020 file documents dents on only two sashes; that no engineer report exists for Brau's inspection; that Prieve was scoped to "windows only").
- Plan a **Rule 30(b)(6) deposition** of FIE and depositions of Brau, Monson, and the Prieve engineer later in the schedule.

**4. Send a litigation-hold / preservation letter immediately (if not already done).** Put FIE on written notice to preserve Brau's notes, the full 2020 file, Prieve's native files and photos, claim-system logs, and all claim communications. You already reserved **Rule 37 sanctions and an adverse-inference instruction** and cited the claim-file documentation duty under **Minn. Stat. § 72A.201**. A clean preservation record now is what makes a spoliation/adverse-inference argument work later if records are "missing."

**5. Line up an engineering (and likely meteorology) expert — this is the linchpin.** Because you must beat the deductible with damage beyond the two awarded windows, you will very likely need a **retained forensic engineer** to (a) opine that hail caused the broader set of window/siding/component damage on July 13–14, 2024, and (b) rebut Prieve's methodology (your Complaint already lays out the attack: no impact-force/terminal-velocity calculation, internally inconsistent "dents" language, unsupported "smooth/rounded only" hail signature, false "no hail after 2020" premise). Budget for this early; expert disclosure deadlines come fast and a causation case without an expert is very hard to win at summary judgment or trial.

**6. Hold the appraisal-scope theory as your primary merits engine.** Your strongest, cleanest argument is the *Quade* scope point plus the **panel-had-no-jurisdiction** point for siding/downspouts/flashing/doors (items never submitted to the panel). The recent Eighth Circuit decision, *Cincinnati Insurance Co. v. Rymer Cos.*, No. 24-3356 (8th Cir. Mar. 27, 2026), reinforces two things useful to you: courts may **remand an ambiguous award to the panel for clarification** (your alternative Count III relief), and **appraiser testimony can only support fraud** — it cannot expand or "explain" an award. That supports both your remand alternative and your position that FIE can't use the umpire/appraiser to enlarge the two-window award into a "the panel settled everything" bar.

**7. Treat the bad-faith and Consumer Fraud pieces as later-stage leverage, and mind their mechanics.**
   - **Minn. Stat. § 604.18 (first-party bad faith):** you correctly reserved it for a post-pleading motion. The two-prong standard is (1) no reasonable basis to deny, and (2) the insurer knew or recklessly disregarded that. Recovery is **taxable costs = the lesser of one-half the proceeds above what FIE offered pre-trial or $250,000, plus up to $100,000 in attorney fees**, decided by the **court after** the factfinder rules on the policy claim (Gen. R. Prac. 119). One wrinkle to research and be ready for: the statute's costs are generally **unavailable for claims "resolved through appraisal"** — FIE will argue that bars you; your answer is that your claim was *not* resolved by the appraisal (that is the whole premise of Count I). Frame accordingly.
   - **Consumer Fraud Act (§ 325F.69, via the private-AG statute § 8.31, subd. 3a):** this is your **fee-shifting** vehicle and the reason a contingency lawyer may find the case attractive. Be ready for FIE to challenge the "causal nexus" (*Group Health v. Philip Morris*) and whether claims-handling counts as conduct "in connection with the sale" of insurance (*Force v. ITT Hartford* helps you).

**8. Seriously consider retaining policyholder-side counsel — ideally on contingency.** You have done sophisticated work, but the next phase (expert practice, a Rule 30(b)(6) deposition, summary-judgment briefing, and a **jury trial** FIE has now demanded) is where represented parties pull ahead. The CFA fee-shifting and § 604.18 exposure make this the kind of case some first-party property/bad-faith firms take on contingency or hybrid terms. At minimum, a paid strategy consult before the scheduling conference would be money well spent.
