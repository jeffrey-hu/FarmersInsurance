## 1. Why this is being filed now, and the one thing to check first

**Minn. Gen. R. Prac. 104** requires the party filing a non-family civil case to notify the court administrator, **"at the time of filing,"** of basic case information on **Form CIV117**. No Civil Cover Sheet appears in `OfficialCourtDocuments/` or in `Filings/SummonsAndComplaint/Court/`, and none appears among the MCRO documents downloaded to date. On that record it was missed on July 8, 2026.

**Before filing, confirm on MCRO that no CIV117 is already on the docket.** If one is there, this draft becomes unnecessary — or, if Defendant filed one, Rule 104 lets a party file a **supplemental** cover sheet within 7 days of being served with the initial one, and the framing changes. Two searches settle it: the MCRO register of actions for 27-CV-26-11606, and the July 8, 2026 eFiling receipt.

**Timing consequences, so they are not a surprise:**

- Rule 104's own remedy for late information is mild: the rule directs that information not known at the time of filing "shall be provided to the court administrator in writing by the filing party **within 7 days of learning** it." The rule prescribes no sanction for a late cover sheet, and the form is administrative rather than substantive.
- **Gen. R. Prac. 111.03** says the court enters its scheduling order "no sooner than the due date of the last civil cover sheet under Rule 104." That sequencing point is **moot** — the Scheduling Order issued September 8, 2026. Filing the cover sheet now does not reopen it, and the draft does not ask the Court to.
- The draft is written so that it **records** the case as the Court has already ordered it, rather than proposing anything different. Items 5, 7, 8 and 14 track the Scheduling Order exactly. That is deliberate: after the Court's finding that Plaintiffs' proposed schedule ran "more than one year beyond the timeline for similar cases," a cover sheet proposing different dates would invite an unnecessary fight over a ministerial form.

**Two decisions to make before filing.** Neither is mine to make:

- **Whether to file at all.** Filing cures the gap and costs little. Not filing leaves an administrative gap no one has raised. A third option is a one-paragraph transmittal letter to Civil Filing noting the cover sheet was inadvertently omitted at filing and is submitted now — which puts the correction on the record in Plaintiffs' own words.
- **Service.** Kennedy Law Firm appeared July 28, 2026, so this must be served on counsel (Minn. R. Civ. P. 5.02(a)) — Kevin J. Kennedy and Mary J. Baskfield, 7616 Currell Blvd., Suite 270, Woodbury, MN 55125. eFiling through eFS serves registered eFilers automatically; filing by mail or hand delivery to Civil Filing (300 South 6th Street, #C-332) does not, and needs a certificate of service. Say the word and I'll draft one to the project's standard.

**⚠️ Item 2 is the one entry with real downside risk, and the draft is written defensively.**

`Filings/Discovery/README.md` warns: the Rule 26.06(a) conference deadline runs 30 days from the **initial** answer due date; FIE's counsel fixed that at July 29, 2026 (asserting § 45.028 service became effective when the Affidavit of Compliance was filed July 8), which put the conference deadline at August 28 — the day it was actually held, with no slack. **If an earlier operative service date such as July 1 were adopted, the conference becomes retroactively late.**

So Attachment A ¶ 2 recites all three dates as facts and expressly takes no new position: served on the Commissioner of Commerce July 1, 2026; Affidavit of Compliance filed July 8, 2026; Defendant treats service as effective then and the Court's July 28 order proceeded on that footing; corrected Complaint served on CSC July 20, 2026. **Read that paragraph closely before signing** — it is the one place where a careless word could cost something.

The same care applies to "Date Case Filed." The draft states July 8, 2026, consistent with Plaintiffs' position, and Attachment A ¶ A notes the pending Rule 11.05 relation-back motion. It neither concedes July 20 nor argues the motion.

**On item 4 — why "No" rather than "Yes."** Rule 146.02(c) provisionally designates certain categories complex, including construction-defect claims and insurance coverage claims related to those categories.
