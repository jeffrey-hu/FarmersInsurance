## 2. Why the RFAs — not the interrogatories — are the sharpest tool in this case

All verified against the primary source on September 15, 2026. The asymmetry against the interrogatory set is large and the file does not exploit it.

| | Interrogatories | **Requests for Admission** |
|---|---|---|
| **Numerical cap** | **50 total per party, cumulative** across every set, counting "each subdivision of separate questions" — Rule 33.01(a) | **NONE.** Rule 36 contains no cap. |
| **If ignored** | Nothing happens; you must move to compel | **Automatically deemed admitted.** Rule 36.01: *"The matter **is admitted** unless within 30 days... the party... serves... a written answer or objection."* Self-executing — no motion. |
| **If admitted** | An answer, contestable at trial | **Conclusively established.** Rule 36.02: *"Any matter admitted pursuant to this rule is **conclusively established** unless the court on motion permits withdrawal or amendment."* Removed from dispute, not merely admissible. |
| **"We don't know"** | Available in practice | **Barred without inquiry.** Rule 36.01: may not plead lack of knowledge *"unless the party states that a **reasonable inquiry** has been made and that the information known or readily obtainable by the party is insufficient."* |
| **"That's for trial"** | — | **Expressly barred.** Rule 36.01: a party *"may not, **on that ground alone**, object to the request."* |
| **Enforcement** | Rule 37.01(b) motion to compel, after the ¶4 protocol | **Rule 36.01 motion to determine sufficiency** — a distinct, cleaner vehicle: *"Unless the court determines that an objection is justified, it **shall** order that an answer be served."* Mandatory phrasing. |
| **Cost of a wrong denial** | None | **⚠️ Fee-shifting.** Minn. R. Civ. P. **37.03(b)**: if a party fails to admit and the requesting party later proves the matter, the requesting party may recover *"the reasonable expenses incurred in making that proof, including reasonable attorney fees."* And *"The court **shall** make the order unless"* one of four exceptions applies. |

**Three practical consequences.**

**(a) There is no reason to be economical.** Every compound request in this set should be split into one proposition per request. A compound request is the easiest thing in the world to deny — one inaccurate element justifies denying the whole — and splitting costs literally nothing under Rule 36.

**(b) ⚠️ Rule 37.03(b) belongs in the instructions.** It is not there. Its exceptions are the reason precision matters: the court must award expenses **unless** it finds *"(1) the request was held objectionable pursuant to Rule 36.01... (3) the party failing to admit had reasonable ground to believe that the party might prevail on the matter."* **A misquoted request supplies FIE with exception (1) or (3) and destroys the fee-shifting lever on that request.** That is what makes §3 below more than a proofreading note.

**(c) ⚠️ Know the withdrawal rule before relying on a deemed admission.** Rule 36.02 lets the court permit withdrawal *"when the presentation of the merits of the action will be subserved thereby and **the party who obtained the admission fails to satisfy the court** that withdrawal or amendment will prejudice that party."* **The burden of showing prejudice falls on Plaintiffs, and silence loses.** If FIE blows the deadline and later moves to withdraw, be ready to document concrete merits-based prejudice — lost evidence, an unavailable witness, expired discovery — not merely that Plaintiffs relied on the admission. Do not build the case on a deemed admission alone where the same fact can also be proved from FIE's own documents.

## 3. ⚠️ The defect that must be fixed before service: misquoted source text

I checked the quoted and characterized language in this set against the source documents. **The pattern is clear: the requests that quote verbatim are unanswerable; the requests that paraphrase or characterize are easy denials.**

### RFA 13 — three of the six named provisions are wrong

RFA 13 asks FIE to admit its November 4, 2024 letter *"cited the following policy provisions"* and then lists six. Verified against `../../Discovery/Expert/Expert_Record_Packet/04_Denial_Letter_2024-11-04_transmitting_Prieve.pdf`:

| RFA 13 says | The letter actually says | |
|---|---|---|
| "Wear and Tear" | I.A.7 "Wear and tear **or mechanical breakdown**"; I.B.8 "Wear and Tear, **Deterioration** or Mechanical Breakdown" | ⚠️ |
| "Marring" | I.A.8 "Marring" | ✓ |
| "Corrosion, Deterioration, or Rust" | I.A.9 / I.B.9 "Corrosion, Deterioration, **Decay** or Rust" | ⚠️ **omits "Decay"** |
| "Inherent Vice or Latent Defect" | I.A.4 / I.B.6 "Inherent Vice or Latent Defect" | ✓ |
| "Neglect or Lack of Maintenance" | I.B.4 "Neglect or Lack of Maintenance **or Failure to Make Repairs**" | ⚠️ |
| "Faulty or Defective Construction" | I.B.5 "Faulty, **Inadequate**, Defective **or Incomplete Planning, Zoning, Maintenance, Repairs**, …" | ⚠️ **no such provision by that name** |

As drafted, FIE denies RFA 13 truthfully and looks reasonable doing it — and under Rule 37.03(b)(3) it has "reasonable ground to believe it might prevail" on the denial, which forecloses fee-shifting.

**Fix: split RFA 13 into six requests, one per provision, each quoting the letter's own words verbatim with its section number.** Six unanswerable requests instead of one deniable one, at zero cost under Rule 36. This also matters beyond the RFA set: Interrogatory 2 asks FIE to justify each of these provisions, and the two documents should name them identically.

### RFA 18 — the A-91 problem, already flagged and still unfixed

`../../Discovery/README.md` notes that RFA 18 cites photograph A-91 as depicting a dented downspout when its caption shows only a downspout location. The request bundles five photographs (A-42, A-91 through A-93, A-98, A-99); **one bad element justifies denying all five.**

### Two more, lower risk but worth correcting while the document is open

The report says Prieve was *"retained to evaluate the condition of windows in the home as relates to hail."* **Requote it exactly** and the request becomes unanswerable; the "only" inference is then argued from the quoted scope rather than conceded away in a denial.

### The one that is already right — use it as the model

**RFA 10** quotes the September 3, 2024 letter as *"There were no other damages observed."* Verified word-for-word against the letter. That is the template: **a verbatim quotation of FIE's own writing, one proposition, no characterization.** Every request in this set should look like RFA 10.

## 5. The ranking

### TIER 1 — The deductible cluster. These decide the case.

FIE's whole defense is arithmetic: the award is $6,400 ACV, the deductible is $17,720, therefore nothing is owed (Answer ¶¶2, 35), and the award is binding (Affirmative Defense 2). These seven requests attack that arithmetic at its two joints — **the award does not itemize**, and **the 2020 claim cannot absorb 25 windows.**

| Rank | № | Why it is first |

| **1** | **9** | ***The highest-value request in the set.*** The award states a single lump-sum $12,800 RCV and does **not** separately state actual cash value and loss per awarded window. **Minn. Stat. § 65A.01, subd. 3** requires appraisers to *"appraise the loss, **stating separately actual value and loss to each item**,"* and that *"An award in writing, **so itemized**,"* determines value and loss. ## 6. ⚠️ The gap: four requests that should exist and do not

These are worth more than most of what is in the set. All four are attachment-free.

**(a) The affirmative preclusion request — the single most valuable admission available, and it is missing.**

> *Admit that The Appraisal Award does not state an actual value or a loss for any window at The Property other than the two windows designated "F1.7" and "B1.2."*

RFA 9 establishes that the award is **not itemized**. This establishes what follows from that: **the award determines nothing about the other windows.** That is the proposition Affirmative Defense 2 has to survive, and it is the proposition that clears the way past the $17,720 deductible. It is a question about the face of a document FIE holds, so there is no lack-of-knowledge escape and no genuine-issue objection. **Add it, and serve it immediately before or after No. 5** so the two are read together.

**(b) Entity identity — and the interrogatory restructure depends on it.**

The companion interrogatory review recommends moving Interrogatory 12's six lettered subparts out of the interrogatory set (they consume 7 of a capped 50) and into RFAs, because the **November 16, 2026 joinder deadline is 62 days out** and FIE's **first affirmative defense** asserts *"lack of jurisdiction over Defendant FIE."* The reason is mechanical: an objected-to interrogatory **self-defers** under Rule 33.01(c) until the objection is resolved — pushing it past November 16 with no motion required from FIE — while an unanswered RFA is **automatically admitted**. Against a hard deadline that asymmetry decides the question.

Add one request per proposition, each in RFA 10's verbatim style:

> *Admit that Fire Insurance Exchange issued and underwrote The Policy.*
> *Admit that Nicholas Brau was employed by Fire Insurance Exchange at the time of his September 6, 2024 inspection of The Property.*
> *Admit that Fire Insurance Exchange made the coverage decision communicated in Your November 4, 2024 letter.*
> *Admit that Fire Insurance Exchange retained Prieve Engineering, LLC.*
> *Admit that Fire Insurance Exchange maintains the claim file for The Claim.*
> *Admit that Fire Underwriters Association acts as attorney-in-fact for Fire Insurance Exchange pursuant to a written instrument.*

**These are the most time-critical additions in this review.** Everything else here can slip a week; these cannot.

**(c) Bare receipt of the three post-denial submissions.**

> *Admit that You received Plaintiffs' January 31, 2025 submission.* *(and the same for February 10, 2025 and May 28, 2025)*

FIE's Answer ¶27 dodged the substance. **Receipt** is not deniable, and it is the predicate for Interrogatory 15 — which asks what FIE then *did* with each, and is the cleanest *Peterson* prong-2 question in the discovery package. Establish receipt by admission so the interrogatory cannot be answered around.

**(d) The uninspected rear-garage window.**

> *Admit that no person acting for You inspected the window described in paragraph 38 of the Complaint.*

FIE answered ¶38 with lack of knowledge. An RFA closes that door: Rule 36.01 bars lack of knowledge *"unless the party states that a reasonable inquiry has been made."* Either FIE admits an entire insured window was never inspected, or it must swear it looked and found otherwise.

## 7. Two instruction changes

**(a) Add Rule 37.03(b) to the instructions.** The set currently tells FIE what happens if it stays silent (Instruction 3) but not what happens if it denies wrongly. Add:

> **Cost of an unfounded denial.** If Defendant fails to admit any matter requested and Plaintiffs thereafter prove that matter, Plaintiffs will apply for an order requiring Defendant to pay the reasonable expenses incurred in making that proof, including reasonable attorney fees, pursuant to Minn. R. Civ. P. 37.03(b).

A deterrent that costs one paragraph. ⚠️ Note honestly: a self-represented party generally cannot recover attorney fees for their own time, so the realistic recovery is out-of-pocket proof costs — expert time, records charges. That is still real money in this case, and the rule's mandatory framing (*"The court **shall** make the order unless"*) gives the paragraph weight with opposing counsel regardless.

**(b) Instruction 3's citation is right — leave it.** It cites Rule 36.01 for the deemed-admitted default and Rule 36.02 for conclusive establishment. Both verified correct. Worth noting for future drafting that Minnesota's Rule 37 lettering diverges from the federal scheme: the evasive-answer rule is **37.01(c)** (not (b)), and failure-to-admit fees are **37.03(b)** (not 37(c)(2)). Parts of this project's files have these inverted.
