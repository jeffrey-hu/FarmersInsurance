**REQUEST FOR ADMISSION NO. 13:** Admit that the Appraisal Award attached as Exhibit C identified the item Windows F1.7 B1.2 to have a replacement cost value of $12,800 and actual cash value of $6,400 for damage caused by hail.

**RESPONSE:** Plaintiffs object to Request No. 13 as compound and as resting on a disputed premise. By referring to "the item Windows F1.7 B1.2," it assumes that two windows are a single "item" within the meaning of Minn. Stat. § 65A.01, subd. 3, which requires the appraisers to state "separately actual value and loss to each item." Whether the Appraisal Award is so itemized is a central issue in this action (Complaint ¶¶ 33, 60). Subject to and without waiving this objection: Admit that the Appraisal Award states, on a single line reading "Windows F1.7 B1.2," a combined Replacement Cost Value of $12,800 and a combined Actual Cash Value of $6,400 for two windows, and that it identifies hail as the cause of the damage to those windows. Deny that the Appraisal Award states an actual value or a loss for either window separately. Further answering, the Appraisal Award does not state what "F1.7" or "B1.2" denotes and does not identify the location of either window. Except as expressly admitted, denied.

**RESPONSE:** Admit.

**RESPONSE:** Admit.

**REQUEST FOR ADMISSION NO. 15:** Admit that the Appraisal Award attached as Exhibit C is binding on Plaintiffs and FIE as to the amount of the loss to the Windows identified on Exhibit C.

**RESPONSE:** Plaintiffs object to Request No. 15 as resting on a disputed premise: Exhibit C does not identify any window except by the undefined codes "F1.7" and "B1.2" (see Response to Request No. 13). Subject to and without waiving this objection, and subject to the alternative relief pleaded in Count III of the Complaint:

Admit that the Appraisal Award binds Plaintiffs and FIE as to what it actually determined for the windows designated "F1.7" and "B1.2": a combined Replacement Cost Value of $12,800 and a combined Actual Cash Value of $6,400.

Deny that the Appraisal Award states an actual value or a loss for either of those windows separately, as Minn. Stat. § 65A.01, subd. 3 requires.

Deny that the Appraisal Award determines the amount of loss for any window or sash other than those two, or for any component the Appraisal Award does not list.

Deny that the Appraisal Award determines any question of coverage, including the cost of matching (Complaint ¶ 51) and the incidental costs of replacement (Complaint ¶ 52), neither of which the Appraisal Award itemizes or states that it includes. FIE instructed the appraisal panel in writing that "whether any particular loss or damage to covered property is in fact insured under this policy may not be determined" in appraisal (FIE 0309), and reserved all of its coverage positions after the Appraisal Award (FIE 0673–0676).

Except as expressly admitted, denied.

**REQUEST FOR ADMISSION NO. 16 (first):** Admit that the Appraisal Award attached as Exhibit C is binding on Plaintiffs and FIE as to the cause of the loss/damage to the Windows identified on Exhibit C.

**RESPONSE:** Admit that the Appraisal Award's determination that hail caused the loss to the windows designated "F1.7" and "B1.2," which Exhibit C identifies only by those codes, is binding on Plaintiffs and FIE. FIE has confirmed that determination. Its Answer to Plaintiffs' Interrogatory No. 5 states that "the appraisers identified the two windows they determined were damaged from hail." Plaintiffs' Request for Admission No. 20 asked FIE to "Admit that You are bound by The Appraisal Award's determination that hail was the cause of loss to the windows designated 'F1.7' and 'B1.2,'" and FIE responded: "Admit that both Plaintiffs and Defendant are bound by the Appraisal Award." FIE also admitted that the windstorm/hail deductible is the only reason it has made no payment for those windows (FIE's Response to Plaintiffs' Request for Admission No. 22). FIE therefore may not assert, as to those windows, the exclusions cited in its November 4, 2024 denial (Complaint ¶¶ 48, 57(b), (f)).

Deny that the Appraisal Award contains any determination of the cause of loss or damage to any other window, sash, or item.

Except as expressly admitted, denied.

**REQUEST FOR ADMISSION NO. 16 (second):** Admit that the Appraisal Award attached as Exhibit C is binding on both Plaintiffs and Defendant as to the amount of loss and the cause of loss identified therein.

**RESPONSE:** Plaintiffs object to Request No. 16 (second) as compound: it combines the amount of loss and the cause of loss, for every line of the Appraisal Award, in a single request. Subject to and without waiving this objection:

As to the cause of loss: Admit that the cause-of-loss determinations stated in the Appraisal Award are binding on Plaintiffs and Defendant, including, as stated in Plaintiffs' Response to Request No. 16 (first), its determination that hail caused the loss to the windows designated "F1.7" and "B1.2." The Appraisal Award identifies "Hail" as the "Loss Cause."

As to the amount of loss for the windows designated "F1.7" and "B1.2": Plaintiffs incorporate their Response to Request No. 15 and admit only to the extent stated there.

As to the amount of loss for "Siding & Paint" and "Debris": Deny that the Appraisal Award's statement of $0 for each is binding to the extent it rests on any policy exclusion or other coverage ground. The "Siding & Paint" line combines the two siding items submitted in the Demand for Appraisal, and neither line states a basis (Complaint ¶¶ 50, 57(j), 61).

Except as expressly admitted, denied.

**REQUEST FOR ADMISSION NO. 17:** Admit that Plaintiffs, in their Complaint, admitted that the Appraisal Award is binding.

**RESPONSE:** Plaintiffs object to Request No. 17 because it mischaracterizes the Complaint by removing its context. It asks Plaintiffs to admit that the Complaint "admitted that the Appraisal Award is binding," without stating on whom, as to what, or to what extent. Every reference to the Appraisal Award as "binding" in the Complaint is limited to the Appraisal Award's hail-causation finding or to what the Appraisal Award actually determined. The request also disregards that Count III is pleaded in the alternative under Minn. R. Civ. P. 8.05. The Complaint speaks for itself. Subject to and without waiving this objection:

Deny that the Complaint admits that the Appraisal Award is binding without limitation. The Complaint states Plaintiffs' position as follows:

- Paragraphs 2 and 56 refer to the Appraisal Award as binding in describing its confirmation of hail as the cause of loss.
- The panel's hail-causation finding "is binding on Defendant" (¶ 32; Prayer for Relief ¶ C).
- The Appraisal Award is binding "as to the matters it determined, including the panel's finding that hail caused the loss to the windows it awarded" (¶ 48).
- The Appraisal Award "remains binding on Defendant as far as it went"; "Plaintiffs do not contend the award is void; they contend it is a partial determination that binds as to what it itemized and decided, and is non-preclusive as to everything it did not" (¶ 63).
- The Appraisal Award does not determine the amount of loss for any window or sash other than those designated "F1.7" and "B1.2," or for any component it does not list, and does not determine any question of coverage (¶¶ 49–50, 60–63).
- Count III, including each of its alternatives, is pleaded under Rule 8.05 and is not waived (¶ 59).

Except as expressly stated, denied.

**REQUEST FOR ADMISSION NO. 18:** Admit that Plaintiffs filed a complaint with the Minnesota Department of Commerce as related to Defendant's handling of Plaintiffs' 2024 Claim.

**RESPONSE:** Admit. Plaintiffs filed their complaint with the Minnesota Department of Commerce on November 19, 2024 (Complaint ID 93547).

**REQUEST FOR ADMISSION NO. 19:** Admit that The Minnesota Department of Commerce did not take any action against Defendant as a result of Plaintiffs' complaint to the Minnesota Department of Commerce.

**RESPONSE:** Plaintiffs object to Request No. 19 because it implies that the Department of Commerce reviewed Plaintiffs' complaint and found no wrongdoing by Defendant. It did not. In closing its file on February 3, 2025, the Department stated that its authority "does not extend to resolving questions of fact or determining who is correct when two sides disagree on the determination of causes of loss to property, such as hail vs wear-and-tear. This authority rests solely with a court of law," and that "[o]ur decision in this matter is not meant to imply that the parties involved have no civil liability for the alleged actions." Plaintiffs further object under Minn. R. Evid. 401–403. Subject to and without waiving these objections: Admit that the Department closed its file on February 3, 2025 without, so far as Plaintiffs know, issuing any order or penalty against Defendant, stating that it lacked jurisdiction to resolve the parties' dispute over the cause of loss. Deny that the Department took no action on Plaintiffs' complaint. It opened an investigation, requested Defendant's written response and claim file notes (FIE 1040–1042), and received Defendant's response (FIE 1044–1045). Except as expressly admitted, denied.

**REQUEST FOR ADMISSION NO. 20:** Admit that Plaintiffs filed a complaint with the Minnesota Attorney General as related to Defendant's handling of Plaintiffs' 2024 Claim of alleged hail/wind loss.

**RESPONSE:** Admit. On June 18, 2025, Plaintiffs submitted a Consumer Assistance Request to the Minnesota Attorney General's Office concerning Defendant's handling of the 2024 Claim (File No. PHW/2025/5428546/C).

**REQUEST FOR ADMISSION NO. 21:** Admit that the Minnesota Attorney General did not take any action against Defendant as a result of Plaintiffs' complaint to the Minnesota Attorney General.

**RESPONSE:** Plaintiffs object to Request No. 21 on the grounds stated in their Response to Request No. 19. The Attorney General's Office does not resolve private insurance disputes. Its August 4, 2025 letter closing its file stated that it "cannot provide legal advice or legal representation to individual constituents," recommended that Plaintiffs consult a private attorney, and identified the Department of Commerce as "the State agency with the authority to license and regulate insurance companies." Subject to and without waiving these objections: Admit that, so far as Plaintiffs know, the Office issued no order or penalty against Defendant. Deny that the Office took no action. It wrote to Defendant on Plaintiffs' behalf, obtained and forwarded to Plaintiffs Defendant's July 11, 2025 written response (FIE 1127–1128), and added Plaintiffs' report to its complaint database. Except as expressly admitted, denied.
