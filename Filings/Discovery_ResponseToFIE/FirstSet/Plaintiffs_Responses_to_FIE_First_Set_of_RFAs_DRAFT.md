**RESPONSE:** Admit. Further answering, the storm that caused the loss began on the evening of July 13, 2024 and continued into the early morning of July 14, 2024. **RESPONSE:** Plaintiffs object to Request No. 2 as compound and as resting on a false premise: that the amount of loss was the only matter in dispute. The parties also disputed coverage: FIE had denied the entire claim in writing on September 3 and November 4, 2024, relying on policy exclusions (FIE 0051–0124; 0677–0686), and stated that coverage could not be addressed in appraisal (FIE 1588–1589). Subject to and without waiving this objection: Admit that Plaintiffs served a Demand for Appraisal dated August 3, 2025 invoking the appraisal clause. Deny that the parties' disagreement concerned only the amount of loss. Except as expressly admitted, denied.

**REQUEST FOR ADMISSION NO. 3:** Admit that attached as "Exhibit A" is a true and correct copy of Plaintiffs' Demand for Appraisal, dated August 3, 2025.

Mr. Sexe provided all of its typed entries; Mr. Lu added the handwritten entries. Except as expressly admitted, denied.

**RESPONSE:** Plaintiffs have never seen the original Appraisal Award. **REQUEST FOR ADMISSION NO. 13:** Admit that the Appraisal Award attached as Exhibit C identified the item Windows F1.7 B1.2 to have a replacement cost value of $12,800 and actual cash value of $6,400 for damage caused by hail.

**RESPONSE:** Plaintiffs object to Request No. 13 as compound and as resting on a disputed premise. By referring to "the item Windows F1.7 B1.2," it assumes that two windows are a single "item" within the meaning of Minn. Stat. § 65A.01, subd. 3, which requires the appraisers to state "separately actual value and loss to each item." Whether the Appraisal Award is so itemized is a central issue in this action (Complaint ¶¶ 33, 60). Subject to and without waiving this objection: Admit that the Appraisal Award states, on a single line reading "Windows F1.7 B1.2," a combined Replacement Cost Value of $12,800 and a combined Actual Cash Value of $6,400 for two windows, and that it identifies hail as the cause of the damage to those windows. Deny that the Appraisal Award states an actual value or a loss for either window separately. Further answering, the Appraisal Award does not state what "F1.7" or "B1.2" denotes and does not identify the location of either window. Further answering, FIE sent the Appraisal Award to Plaintiffs as the last page of its four-page September 16, 2025 settlement notice (FIE 0673–0676). That notice states a "Policy Deductible" of $17,720.00 against the Appraisal Award's figures, and states that FIE "is not waiving any of the policy coverages, limitations, exclusions, or provisions, all of which are specifically reserved." Except as expressly admitted, denied.

**REQUEST FOR ADMISSION NO. 15:** Admit that the Appraisal Award attached as Exhibit C is binding on Plaintiffs and FIE as to the amount of the loss to the Windows identified on Exhibit C.

**RESPONSE:** Plaintiffs object to Request No. 15 as resting on a disputed premise: Exhibit C does not identify any window except by the undefined codes "F1.7" and "B1.2" (see Response to Request No. 13). Subject to and without waiving this objection, and without prejudice to the alternative relief pleaded in Count III of the Complaint:

Admit that the Appraisal Award binds Plaintiffs and FIE as to what it actually determined for the windows designated "F1.7" and "B1.2": that hail caused the loss to those windows, a combined Replacement Cost Value of $12,800, and a combined Actual Cash Value of $6,400.

Deny that the Appraisal Award states an actual value or a loss for either of those windows separately, as Minn. Stat. § 65A.01, subd. 3 requires.

Deny that the Appraisal Award determines the amount of loss for any window or sash other than those two, or for any component the Appraisal Award does not list.

Deny that the Appraisal Award determines any question of coverage, including the cost of matching (Complaint ¶ 51) and the incidental costs of replacement (Complaint ¶ 52), neither of which it itemizes or states that it includes. FIE itself told the appraisers in writing that coverage could not be determined in appraisal (FIE 0309).

Except as expressly admitted, denied.

**REQUEST FOR ADMISSION NO. 16 (first):** Admit that the Appraisal Award attached as Exhibit C is binding on Plaintiffs and FIE as to the cause of the loss/damage to the Windows identified on Exhibit C.

**RESPONSE:** Admit that the Appraisal Award's determination that hail caused the loss to the windows designated "F1.7" and "B1.2," which Exhibit C identifies only by those codes, is binding on Plaintiffs and FIE.

Deny that the Appraisal Award contains any determination of the cause of loss or damage to any other window or sash.

Except as expressly admitted, denied.

**REQUEST FOR ADMISSION NO. 16 (second):** Admit that the Appraisal Award attached as Exhibit C is binding on both Plaintiffs and Defendant as to the amount of loss and the cause of loss identified therein.

**RESPONSE:** Plaintiffs object to Request No. 16 (second) as compound: it combines the amount of loss and the cause of loss, for every line of the Appraisal Award, in a single request. Subject to and without waiving this objection, and without prejudice to the alternative relief pleaded in Count III of the Complaint:

As to the cause of loss: Admit that the cause-of-loss determinations stated in the Appraisal Award are binding on Plaintiffs and Defendant.

As to the amount of loss for the windows designated "F1.7" and "B1.2": Admit only as stated in Plaintiffs' Response to Request No. 15, which Plaintiffs incorporate.

As to the amount of loss for "Siding & Paint" and "Debris": Deny that the Appraisal Award's statement of $0 for each is binding to the extent it rests on any policy exclusion or other coverage ground. Neither line states a basis, and the "Siding & Paint" line combines the two siding items submitted in the Demand for Appraisal (Complaint ¶¶ 50, 57(j), 61).

Except as expressly admitted, denied.

**REQUEST FOR ADMISSION NO. 17:** Admit that Plaintiffs, in their Complaint, admitted that the Appraisal Award is binding.

**RESPONSE:** Plaintiffs object to Request No. 17 because it mischaracterizes the Complaint by removing its context: it does not state on whom, as to what, or to what extent the Complaint treats the Appraisal Award as binding, and it disregards that Count III is pleaded in the alternative under Minn. R. Civ. P. 8.05 (¶ 59). Subject to and without waiving this objection:

Admit that the Complaint alleges that the Appraisal Award is binding under Minn. Stat. § 65A.01, subd. 3 "as to the matters it determined, including the panel's finding that hail caused the loss to the windows it awarded" (¶ 48), and that the panel's hail-causation finding is binding on Defendant (¶ 32; Prayer for Relief ¶ C).

Deny that the Complaint admits that the Appraisal Award is binding without limitation. The Complaint alleges that the award "remains binding on Defendant as far as it went" and that "it is a partial determination that binds as to what it itemized and decided, and is non-preclusive as to everything it did not" (¶ 63); that it does not determine the amount of loss for any window, sash, or item it did not separately itemize, or any question of coverage (¶¶ 49–50, 60–63); and that any coverage-based determination by the panel is not final or binding (¶ 50).

Except as expressly admitted, denied.

**REQUEST FOR ADMISSION NO. 18:** Admit that Plaintiffs filed a complaint with the Minnesota Department of Commerce as related to Defendant's handling of Plaintiffs' 2024 Claim.

**RESPONSE:** Admit. Plaintiffs filed their complaint with the Minnesota Department of Commerce on November 19, 2024 (Complaint ID 93547).

**REQUEST FOR ADMISSION NO. 19:** Admit that The Minnesota Department of Commerce did not take any action against Defendant as a result of Plaintiffs' complaint to the Minnesota Department of Commerce.

**RESPONSE:** Plaintiffs object to Request No. 19 because it implies that the Department of Commerce reviewed Plaintiffs' complaint and found no wrongdoing by Defendant. It did not. In closing its file on February 3, 2025, the Department stated that only a court could resolve the parties' dispute over the cause of loss, "such as hail vs wear-and-tear," and that "[o]ur decision in this matter is not meant to imply that the parties involved have no civil liability for the alleged actions." Plaintiffs further object under Minn. R. Evid. 401–403. Subject to and without waiving these objections:

Admit that, so far as Plaintiffs know, the Department issued no order or penalty against Defendant.

Deny that the Department took no action. It opened an investigation, requested Defendant's written response and claim file notes (FIE 1040–1042), received Defendant's response (FIE 1044–1045), and on February 13, 2025, after Plaintiffs' follow-up, forwarded the matter to the Department's director for additional review.

Except as expressly admitted, denied.

**REQUEST FOR ADMISSION NO. 20:** Admit that Plaintiffs filed a complaint with the Minnesota Attorney General as related to Defendant's handling of Plaintiffs' 2024 Claim of alleged hail/wind loss.

**RESPONSE:** Admit. On June 18, 2025, Plaintiffs submitted a Consumer Assistance Request to the Minnesota Attorney General's Office concerning Defendant's handling of the 2024 Claim (File No. PHW/2025/5428546/C).

**REQUEST FOR ADMISSION NO. 21:** Admit that the Minnesota Attorney General did not take any action against Defendant as a result of Plaintiffs' complaint to the Minnesota Attorney General.

**RESPONSE:** Plaintiffs object to Request No. 21 on the grounds stated in their Response to Request No. 19. The Attorney General's Office does not resolve private insurance disputes. Its August 4, 2025 letter closing its file stated that it "cannot provide legal advice or legal representation to individual constituents," recommended that Plaintiffs consult a private attorney, and identified the Department of Commerce as "the State agency with the authority to license and regulate insurance companies." Subject to and without waiving these objections: Admit that, so far as Plaintiffs know, the Office issued no order or penalty against Defendant. Deny that the Office took no action. It wrote to Defendant on Plaintiffs' behalf, obtained and forwarded to Plaintiffs Defendant's July 11, 2025 written response (FIE 1127–1128), and added Plaintiffs' report to its complaint database. Except as expressly admitted, denied.
