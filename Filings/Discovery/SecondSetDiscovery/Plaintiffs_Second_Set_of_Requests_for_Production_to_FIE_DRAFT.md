## Why this set is held

The vendor-pattern request below is defensible on relevance — Complaint ¶72 pleads that Defendant's conduct "reflects institutional claim-handling practices affecting numerous Minnesota homeowners," which is a pleaded element of the Consumer Fraud Act count, not a bad-faith theory awaiting a § 604.18 amendment. But relevance is not the issue. **Sequence is.**

Served in the first round, it is the request Defendant builds a protective-order motion around, and it makes the narrowed vendor requests that *did* go out — First Set Nos. 9 (Prieve engagements) and 28 (Hancock engagements) — look like the same overreach in smaller type.

Served second, after Defendant has answered whether it performed any independent review of the vendor reports, it stops being a fishing expedition and becomes targeted follow-up on an established fact.

## Trigger conditions — serve only after all three

1. **Interrogatory 11** (First Set — **numbered 17 before the September 12, 2026 renumbering**) is answered: Defendant's policies and practice for retaining, reviewing, evaluating, and accepting or rejecting third-party inspection and engineering reports, including who reviews them and what independent analysis is performed.
2. **Request 32** (First Set) is responded to: documents reflecting Defendant's review, evaluation, approval, or adoption of the Hancock and Prieve reports.
3. **Requests 9 and 28** (First Set) are responded to: the narrowed Minnesota residential hail engagement histories for Prieve and Hancock.

**What to look for in those answers.** If Defendant states or shows that it performed no independent review of the vendor reports — or produces an engagement history showing a lopsided causation-finding pattern — this set becomes follow-up on Defendant's own admission. If Defendant instead documents a genuine review process, reconsider whether this set is worth the fight at all.

### Open decision before service: serve as drafted, or narrowed?

P-21 flagged this text as likely to draw an overbreadth objection and offered a narrowed alternative. **That narrowing was never approved** — the text above is preserved exactly as it stood. Decide at service time:

**Option A — as drafted above.** Broadest reach. Highest objection risk.

**Option B — narrowed:**

> Documents sufficient to show the vendor-selection criteria, performance metrics, and service agreements applicable to Hancock Claims Consultants and Prieve Engineering, LLC for Minnesota property claims from January 1, 2020 forward, together with the annual volume of assignments to and payments made to each.

**Option C — as drafted, but conditioned on what the First Set produced.** If Defendant's answers establish no independent review, Option A is far easier to defend, because the request is then aimed at a documented practice rather than a suspected one.

If either option is served, add the protective-order and third-party-redaction offer used in First Set Nos. 9 and 28 — it removed the confidentiality objection there and should do the same here.
