### B. Subjects on Which Discovery Will Be Needed (Rule 26.06(c)(2))

- The cause, extent, and location of the July 13, 2024 hail damage to the windows and other exterior components of the Property, including all components the appraisal panel did not evaluate;
- The three inspections (Hancock, Brau, and Prieve) — their scope and methodology, including the decision to limit the Prieve engagement to “windows only”;
- Defendant’s handling of the claim, the two denials (September 3 and November 4, 2024), and its “engineer’s report only” evidentiary rule;
- The 2020 claim and file (Xactimate and photo log) and Defendant’s “prior payment” and “no hail after 2020” theories;
- The September 12, 2025 appraisal award — its scope and itemization, and the umpire’s and appraisers’ files;
- Defendant’s internal communications and recorded calls concerning the claim and its handling (email, messaging, and telephone);
- Defendant’s institutional claims-handling policies, training, and procedures in effect since July 13, 2024, including its treatment of non-engineer evidence and its practice for reviewing and adopting third-party inspection and engineering reports;
- Defendant’s retention and oversight of its inspection and engineering vendors (Hancock and Prieve), including in other Minnesota hail claims from 2020 forward;
- Defendant’s claim file and audit trail, its record-retention practices, and the existence or loss of the Brau September 6, 2024 inspection records (Minn. Stat. § 72A.201; preservation/spoliation); and
- Defendant’s response to Plaintiffs’ November 19, 2024 complaint to the Minnesota Department of Commerce (Complaint ID 93547).

### C. Phasing, ESI, Privilege, Confidentiality, and Limits (Rule 26.06(c)(1),(3)-(6))

1. **Phasing.** Plaintiffs propose that discovery proceed without formal phasing, with fact discovery preceding expert discovery on the schedule set out in Part I.E.

2. **Electronically stored information.** ESI shall be produced in native format with all metadata intact, or in another reasonably usable form as the parties agree; the searchability and metadata of ESI shall not be degraded. The parties shall address preservation, custodians, and data sources at the Rule 26.06 conference.

3. **Privilege.** A party withholding materials on privilege grounds shall serve a privilege log under Rule 26.02(f). The parties shall enter a non-waiver/claw-back agreement providing that inadvertent production does not waive privilege and permitting the Court to approve the agreement so that its protections bind non-parties.

4. **Discovery limits.** Absent agreement or leave of court, the parties will observe the presumptive limits on written discovery and depositions. Plaintiffs propose that the parties confer on any needed adjustments, including deposition hours for Rule 30.02(f) organizational depositions.

5. **Confidentiality and protective order.** Plaintiffs anticipate that Defendant will seek protection for portions of its claim file and related business records. Plaintiffs are prepared to enter a stipulated protective order and propose the following terms:

    (a) *A single tier of protection.* Material may be designated CONFIDENTIAL. Plaintiffs do not agree to an "Attorneys' Eyes Only" tier or any equivalent counsel-only restriction. Plaintiffs appear pro se and have no counsel of record; a counsel-only tier would not limit the *use* of evidence in this case but would operate as a complete bar on Plaintiffs' access to evidence in their own case.

    (b) *Standard for designation.* Designations shall be limited to material the designating party in good faith believes contains trade secrets or confidential proprietary business information, or private personal or financial information. Material shall not be designated solely because it was produced from a claim file, nor to shield material from scrutiny or to impose burden.

    (c) *Persons permitted access.* Material designated CONFIDENTIAL may be disclosed to: the parties; the Court and its personnel; court reporters and videographers; any person retained by a party as an expert or consultant in this action; Jeffrey Hu, who assists Plaintiffs in this litigation; deposition and trial witnesses to the extent reasonably necessary for their examination; and, as to Defendant, its counsel of record, counsel's staff, and its employees and representatives with a need to know. Before receiving designated material, each person under this subparagraph other than the parties, the Court and its personnel, and counsel of record and counsel's staff shall first sign a written undertaking agreeing to be bound by this Order and submitting to the jurisdiction of this Court for its enforcement.

    (d) *Burden and challenge procedure.* A party may challenge a designation by written notice identifying the material at issue. The parties shall confer within 14 days of the notice. If the dispute is not resolved, the designating party bears the burden of establishing that the material is entitled to protection and shall move the Court within 21 days after the conferral; material as to which no timely motion is filed shall lose its designation.

    (e) *Use, return, and destruction.* Designated material shall be used solely for purposes of this action. Within 60 days after final resolution of this action, each party shall return or destroy designated material, except that a party may retain one archival copy of pleadings, motion papers, deposition and trial transcripts, and its own work product.

    (f) *No effect on admissibility or public access.* A designation does not render material admissible or inadmissible and does not by itself authorize filing under seal. Any request to file designated material under seal shall be made by separate motion under the Minnesota Rules of Public Access to Records of the Judicial Branch.

    If the parties are unable to agree on the terms of a protective order, Plaintiffs request that the Court resolve the disputed terms at or promptly following the Rule 26.06 conference so that document production is not delayed.

### E. Proposed Scheduling Deadlines

The following dates are proposals. They are keyed to the July 29, 2026 initial answer due date and the August 28, 2026 discovery conference. The schedule reflects the demands of a document-intensive first-party insurance case — a multi-year claim file, three separate inspections, non-party discovery from the inspecting firms and the members of the appraisal panel, and competing expert causation testimony — while setting a date certain for the substantial completion of document production so that depositions and expert work may proceed on a predictable timetable. Plaintiffs further propose that ADR be completed after the close of fact discovery but before expert disclosures, so that the parties can evaluate settlement on a complete factual record without first incurring the cost of expert reports. All dates are subject to the parties' conferral and the Court's scheduling order:

**Note on the § 604.18 deadline.** Minn. Stat. § 604.18 bars pleading a first-party bad-faith claim in the original complaint; the claim may be added only by a later motion to amend supported by affidavits making a prima facie showing. That showing depends on the fact-discovery record, so Plaintiffs propose a separate, later deadline for the § 604.18 motion (November 30, 2027, after the close of fact discovery and Plaintiffs' expert disclosures) rather than the general January 15, 2027 pleading-amendment deadline. The award of § 604.18 taxable costs, if the claim is added, would be determined by the Court after the fact-finder's verdict under Minn. Gen. R. Prac. 119.
