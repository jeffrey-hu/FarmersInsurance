# [PROPOSED] STIPULATED PROTECTIVE ORDER

> **Working draft — ready reserve; not legal advice.** This is Plaintiffs' proposed form of stipulated protective order. Because Defendant is the party seeking to protect its own records, Defendant is expected to propose the form of order first; Plaintiffs do **not** circulate this out of the gate. Hold it to (1) counter or redline Defendant's proposed order from a reasonable baseline, or (2) offer it if Defendant uses the absence of a protective order to delay producing the claim file. Confirm every term with a Minnesota-licensed attorney before circulating or filing. The bracketed and italicized notes are drafting flags, not part of the order.

The parties, seeking to facilitate the exchange of documents and information in discovery while protecting legitimately confidential material, stipulate to the following protective order under Minn. R. Civ. P. 26.03. The Court, having reviewed the stipulation, **ORDERS** as follows:

## 1. Single tier of protection; no "Attorneys' Eyes Only."

Material may be designated **CONFIDENTIAL**. There is a single tier of protection under this Order. The parties do not agree to, and this Order does not create, an "Attorneys' Eyes Only," "Highly Confidential," or any equivalent counsel-only tier. Plaintiffs appear pro se and have no counsel of record; a counsel-only tier would not limit the *use* of evidence in this action but would operate as a complete bar on Plaintiffs' access to evidence in their own case.

## 2. Standard for designation.

A party may designate material CONFIDENTIAL only where the designating party in good faith believes it contains trade secrets or confidential proprietary business information, or private personal or financial information. Material shall not be designated CONFIDENTIAL solely because it was produced from a claim file, nor for the purpose of shielding material from scrutiny or imposing burden or expense on another party. A party shall not designate material more broadly than necessary.

## 3. Manner of designation.

*[Drafting note: this operational clause was not in the original plan text; it is added because a working order needs a designation mechanic. Adjust to the parties' agreed method.]*

A party designates documents by marking each page "CONFIDENTIAL" (or by an equivalent cover designation for native-format or bulk productions). Deposition testimony may be designated on the record or by written notice within 14 days after the transcript becomes available; the transcript is treated as CONFIDENTIAL in its entirety during that 14-day period.

## 4. Persons permitted access.

Material designated CONFIDENTIAL may be disclosed only to: the parties; the Court and its personnel; court reporters and videographers; any person retained by a party as an expert or consultant in this action; Jeffrey Hu, who assists Plaintiffs in this litigation; deposition and trial witnesses to the extent reasonably necessary for their examination; and, as to Defendant, its counsel of record, counsel's staff, and its employees and representatives with a need to know. Before receiving designated material, each person under this paragraph — other than the parties, the Court and its personnel, and counsel of record and counsel's staff — shall first sign the written undertaking attached as **Exhibit A**, agreeing to be bound by this Order and submitting to the jurisdiction of this Court for its enforcement.

## 5. Burden and challenge procedure.

A party may challenge a designation by written notice identifying the material at issue. The parties shall confer within 14 days of the notice. If the dispute is not resolved, the designating party bears the burden of establishing that the material is entitled to protection and shall move the Court within 21 days after the conferral; material as to which no timely motion is filed shall lose its CONFIDENTIAL designation. Material retains its designation while a timely challenge is pending.

## 6. Use, return, and destruction.

Designated material shall be used solely for purposes of this action. Within 60 days after final resolution of this action, each party shall return or destroy all designated material, except that a party may retain one archival copy of pleadings, motion papers, deposition and trial transcripts, and its own work product. This Order survives the conclusion of the action, and the Court retains jurisdiction to enforce it.

## 7. No effect on admissibility or public access.

A designation under this Order does not render material admissible or inadmissible and does not by itself authorize filing the material under seal. Any request to file designated material under seal shall be made by separate motion under the Minnesota Rules of Public Access to Records of the Judicial Branch. This Order does not restrict a party's use of its own material.

## 8. Inadvertent failure to designate.

*[Drafting note: added as a routine practical term; optional.]*

A party's inadvertent failure to designate material as CONFIDENTIAL does not waive the designation, which may be corrected by prompt written notice; the receiving party need not treat material as CONFIDENTIAL before it is notified. (Inadvertent production of **privileged** material is governed by the parties' separate non-waiver/claw-back agreement, not this Order.)
