## 1. The gap the first shortlist left open

The August list is strong on two axes and silent on a third.

| Axis | August list | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Storm meteorology | Bunkers (SD), McGinnis (WI), Bryant (TN) | Covered — but all out of state |
| Roof / general hail engineering | Standohar-Alfano, AET, Cerci, Densmore, Arete | Covered |
| **Windows and glazing specifically** | **nobody** | **Open** |

That gap matters more than it looks. Read candidate A's own SEAK keyword list: "Wind, Storm Damage, Tornado, Hail, Snow, Precipitation, **Roof**." The hail-expert market is built around roofs, because that is where the claim volume is. **This case is not a roof case.** It is about impact damage to **aluminum-clad wood and extruded-aluminum window assemblies** — a fenestration problem with its own standards, its own test methods, and its own impact literature.

Farmers' expert will very likely be a roof-trained generalist too. An expert who can speak to *fenestration* standards rather than roofing rules of thumb is a differentiator, not a luxury.

The second gap: **every meteorologist on the August list is out of state** (Rapid City, Waukesha, Memphis). §3 fixes that.

## 2. NEW — Window and fenestration capability

### ⭐ Encompass, Inc. — Minnetonka, MN *(the best new find on this list)*

- **Contact:** (952) 854-4511 · 5435 Feltl Road, Minnetonka, MN 55343 · encompassinc.com
- **What it is:** a Minnesota consulting engineering firm founded in 1979 — structural, civil, mechanical and architectural — whose stated specialty is "forensic analysis of building failures."
- **Why it is the standout:** Encompass holds **AAMA accreditation as a testing laboratory for windows and curtain wall systems**, and performs **window and door testing — water penetration, air infiltration, and operability testing**. AAMA (now FGIA) is the fenestration standards body. This is a lab that tests windows to the industry's own standards, thirteen miles from the property.
- **Litigation:** the firm states it "provides expert witness services for all phases of litigation."
- **Client base:** its site describes serving **owners, architects, builders and developers** — not insurers. That matters for the cross-examination in §7.
- **Bench:** principals **Curt Isernhagen, P.E.** and **Kent Jones, P.E.**, plus associate principals Mark Blazevic P.E., Nate Willson P.E., Mike McIntire P.E., and a deep roster of P.E.s and S.E.s. Individual bios do not name a forensic-window specialist, so the first call is: *which of your engineers would handle a hail-impact window claim?*
- **Why it fits this case specifically:**
  1. **The never-inspected rear-garage window.** An accredited window lab can inspect *and test* it. That is first-hand data.
  2. **Rule 703(b).** Their own inspection and test results are independently admissible. They do not depend on a proprietary hail database the jury will never see.
  3. **Foundational reliability under Rule 702.** Testing to AAMA/FGIA methods is a documented, repeatable methodology — precisely what the Prieve report lacks (no impact-force calculation, no terminal-velocity calculation, no stated hail-size threshold).
  4. **Local.** No travel cost on a claim this size, and easy re-inspection.
- **Open questions:** whether they take single-family residential insurance work (their profile leans commercial/institutional); whether they have testified in a first-party coverage case; whether any Farmers/FIE relationship exists.

- **Credentials:** **FenestrationMaster Professional Certification (FMPC®)** — the fenestration industry's own professional credential — plus a mechanical engineering M.S. and a P.E.

**Christine Standohar-Alfano, PhD, PE, CCM (ICC Forensics, Minneapolis).** Her SEAK profile now states she has been **deposed or testified 75 times in the last four years**, and describes her as the only person in the country holding both a PE and a CCM. Practice keywords: Wind, Storm Damage, Tornado, Hail, Snow, Precipitation, **Roof**. Phone 612-542-8210.

Three things follow, and they are the most consequential diligence items on this list:

1. **That 75-case list is discoverable and you will serve it.** Rule 26.01(b)(2)(E) requires a list of every case in which the witness testified as an expert at trial or by deposition in the previous four years, attached to the report due **3/15/2027**. Seventy-five is a large number and it goes to Kennedy either way.
2. **Combined with the Haag Global history already flagged** in the August memo (~7 years, until 2024, at a firm that does substantial insurer-side work), the composition of those 75 is the whole question. **Ask for the list, then ask for the plaintiff/insurer split within it, before any substantive discussion.** If it is heavily insurer-side, that is Farmers' cross-examination written by your own witness. If it is balanced, her dual PE+CCM is genuinely efficient — one expert covering both disciplines.
3. **"Roof," not windows.** Even at her best she is a storm-damage generalist. Ask specifically what fenestration work she has done.

None of this is disqualifying. It is the reason the criteria memo says to pull (D), (E) and (F) *before* retention rather than discovering them in March 2027.

## 6. Licensure — not verified here, and how to verify

Engineering causation opinions in Minnesota implicate the professional-engineering licensure statutes. **I did not verify any individual's Minnesota PE licence.** The Board's roster is searchable by last name or licence number, is updated daily, and shows active, expired, suspended, revoked and retired status:

> **https://roster.aelslagid.state.mn.us** (linked from https://mn.gov/aelslagid/license-lookup/)

Check, before retaining: **Standohar-Alfano** (she is reported licensed in ~10 states — confirm Minnesota is one), and whichever **Encompass** or **WJE** engineer is actually assigned. The Board also publishes Current and Archived disciplinary Orders separately — worth a look for any finalist. For meteorologists there is no licence; the credential is the **CCM**, verifiable in the AMS directory.

## 7. The pre-retention packet — send before any substantive conversation

Same email to every candidate. Items 1–4 track Rule 26.01(b)(2)(D)–(F) and exist because these answers become discoverable anyway.

1. **CV**, and for engineers the **Minnesota PE licence number**.
2. **All publications from the previous 10 years** — Rule 26.01(b)(2)(D).
3. **Every case testified in, by deposition or at trial, in the previous 4 years** — Rule 26.01(b)(2)(E) — **and the plaintiff-versus-insurer split within it.**
4. **Fee schedule and compensation terms** — Rule 26.01(b)(2)(F). Hourly, report, deposition and trial rates; retainer. **No contingent or outcome-based fee.**
5. **Conflict statement** naming: Fire Insurance Exchange; Fire Underwriters Association; Farmers Insurance and affiliates; **Kennedy Law Firm** and Katherine Baskfield; **Prieve Engineering** and Nathan Prieve; **Hancock Claims Consultants**; adjuster Nicholas Brau.
6. **Has any court ever excluded or limited your testimony?** (Rule 702 foundational-reliability or Frye-Mack challenge.)
7. **Methodology:** can you quantify hail-impact thresholds for extruded-aluminum and aluminum-clad wood window assemblies, and state the terminal velocity and impact energy required to damage them?
8. **Rule 703(b) question — ask it exactly this way:** *"For each input to your opinion, what is the source, and is it a record we can authenticate and offer into evidence?"* Minnesota requires underlying expert data to be independently admissible on direct. An expert leaning on a proprietary hail-verification database may hold the opinion but be unable to show the jury its basis.
9. **Site inspection:** will you inspect the property, **including the rear garage window that no one has examined**, and review Prieve's native photographs and metadata when produced?
10. **Schedule:** written commitment to a **draft report in February 2027** and a signed final report by **3/15/2027**, plus availability for a deposition after that date.
11. **Understanding that Minnesota gives drafts no protection.** Minnesota has not adopted the federal shields for draft reports and attorney–expert communications. Every email and draft is discoverable. Confirm they work accordingly.

## 8. Recommendation — pair two specialists rather than hunting for one generalist

The August memo's instinct was to find a single dual-credentialed expert. Having looked at the market, I think that is the second-best structure here, for one reason: the dual-credentialed candidates are **roof**-trained storm generalists, and the disputed building components are **windows**.

**Primary recommendation — a two-expert pairing:**

| Role | Candidate | What they prove |
|---|---|---|
| **Fenestration causation** | **Encompass, Inc.** (Minnetonka) | That hail of the size and velocity established by the meteorologist damages these window assemblies — grounded in their own inspection and AAMA-standard lab testing, including the rear garage window |
| **Storm reconstruction** | **Susie Martin, CCM / Praedictix** (Eden Prairie / Minneapolis) | Hail size, wind and severity **at this address** on 7/13/2024, from radar and archived primary observations — defeating Prieve's "no hail since 2020" premise |

Why this pairing:

- **Both are local.** Both can inspect. No travel billed on a residential claim, and the site is reachable again if the record changes.
- **Both generate first-hand, independently admissible data** — lab results and primary weather records — which is the Rule 703(b) answer.
- **Encompass's client base is owners and builders, not carriers.** On a Rule 26.01(b)(2)(E) list that reads better than a career of insurer retentions.
- **Division of labour matches the two things that must be proved.** Prieve's report fails on both axes; attacking it with two focused experts is cleaner than one witness stretched across both.
- **Cost is not obviously higher.** Two local experts with no travel may cost less than one out-of-state dual-credentialed expert flying in for an inspection and a deposition.

**Alternative — single-expert route:** **Standohar-Alfano**, *if and only if* the four-year testimony list in §7 item 3 comes back reasonably balanced. Her PE+CCM genuinely does cover both disciplines, which is efficient and gives the jury one witness to follow. Pair her with Encompass or Tenace for the window-specific opinion if the fenestration question needs its own voice.

**Reserve:** WJE (if budget allows and the firm clears conflicts), Brian Tenace (fenestration standards), AET, Bunkers or McGinnis (meteorology backup), GCI Consultants.
