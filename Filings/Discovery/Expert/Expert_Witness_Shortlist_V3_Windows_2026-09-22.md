## 1. One correction to the premise — and it makes the case for a window expert stronger, not weaker

"The appraisal panel already stated the causation is hail" is true, and narrower than it sounds.

Per `../../../OffenseStrategies/AppraisalAwardStrategy/03_LegalFramework.md`, *Quade* preclusion reaches only **items the panel actually evaluated and awarded**. The panel awarded **two windows** (codes F1.7 / B1.2). Prieve's own Appendix A documents sash or frame dents at **at least 25 distinct windows**, plus 5 downspouts, 3 flashing observations and 3 trim chips.

So the record splits:

| | Causation | Who bears the burden |
|---|---|---|
| The 2 awarded windows | **Settled — hail.** Farmers cannot retreat to the 11/4/2024 exclusions | Farmers is bound |
| The other ~23 windows and components | **Open.** Reserved court question under *Quade* | **You** |

The entire remaining fight is on the second row, and it is per-window. That is not a storm question at all — a meteorologist cannot help with it. **It is a damage-signature question about specific window assemblies, which is exactly the expertise you have just asked for.** Your instinct and the case file point the same way.

### The reframe worth taking to the expert

Because the panel already designated the awarded items "Hail," you are not starting from zero. You have a **conceded reference standard** on your own building. The question becomes:

> **Do the dents and marks at the other window locations share the damage signature of the two windows the panel found were hail-caused?**

That is a comparative analysis on a single structure, on the same elevation exposures, on the same window product, from the same date. It is a far more tractable expert assignment than "prove hail caused this," and it is much harder for Farmers to attack — arguing the signatures differ requires their expert to explain what else, on one house, on one day, dented twenty-three windows in the same way.

**Make this the framing in the engagement letter.** It also narrows the scope, which lowers the fee.

**Two conditions would bring meteorology back. Watch for both:**

1. **Farmers puts hail size in issue** — i.e. argues that whatever fell on 7/13/2024 was too small to damage these assemblies. Then the engineer's threshold opinion needs a sourced size input, and the NWS record may not be granular enough at your address.

Practical handling: mention to Praedictix that timing is uncertain, and ask them to hold a no-obligation file. Do not retain now.

## 3. The methodology that actually answers the question — and why it matters under Rule 702

This is the most useful thing in this memo. Two established, documented methods apply, and Prieve used neither.

### (a) Glass fractography — for any cracked or chipped panes

Fractography reads the fracture surface itself. Under a stereo microscope a fracture shows **mirror, mist and hackle** regions, and the **hackle lines point back toward the origin**; from the origin you can determine whether the fracture began at an impact, at an edge flaw, or from thermal stress, and estimate the stress at failure. Practitioners support it with SEM and EDS, and with flexural testing of comparable specimens under ASTM C1161. ASTM C1322 is the standard practice for fractography and characterising fracture origins.

**Why this matters here:** it is a peer-reviewed, standardised, repeatable method that produces a *located, identified origin* — the opposite of Prieve's "smooth and rounded = hail, anything else = not hail" visual heuristic. Under Minn. R. Evid. 702 the opinion must have **foundational reliability**; a fractographic origin determination has it and a visual generalisation does not. It converts your expert's opinion into the kind of showing the `../../../OffenseStrategies/Motions/Daubert/` package says Prieve's lacks.

### (b) Dent-depth / impact-energy analysis — for the sash and frame dents

Most of the damage is **denting of metal**, not broken glass. There is a developed literature relating hailstone diameter, terminal velocity and kinetic energy to dent depth in metal sheet, and your own folder holds it. An expert who can run that calculation for aluminium-clad and extruded-aluminium sections produces exactly the quantitative foundation the repository documents as absent from Prieve's report (no impact-force calculation, no terminal-velocity calculation, no stated hail-size threshold).

### What your own literature folder already supports — and one caution

## 7. Two things to do this week regardless of which expert you pick

1. **Preserve physical evidence.** If any damaged window or sash is ever removed or replaced, **keep it, labelled by location**. A retained unit is testable; a photograph is not, and a lab-tested exemplar is the strongest Rule 703(b) input available. This is cheap now and impossible later.
2. **Annotate the retraction at the point of use.** `../../../HailShapes/DocumentIndex.md` lines 94 and 260–265 summarise paper A-3 without flagging the retraction there. The warning exists elsewhere in the repository; it should also sit next to the summary, so nothing is lifted from it into a report.
