### 1.1 What Rule 26.01(b) requires

Verified text: a party "must disclose to the other parties the identity of any witness it may use at trial to present evidence under Minnesota Rule of Evidence 702, 703, or 705." The disclosure "must be accompanied by a written report — prepared and signed by the witness — if the witness is one retained or specially employed to provide expert testimony in the case or one whose duties as the party's employee regularly involve giving expert testimony." The report must contain: **(A)** a complete statement of all opinions and the basis and reasons for them; **(B)** the facts or data considered; **(C)** exhibits used to summarize or support them; **(D)** the witness's qualifications, including publications from the previous 10 years; **(E)** a list of other cases in which the witness testified in the previous 4 years; and **(F)** a statement of compensation.

For a witness **not** required to give a report, the disclosure states only **(A)** the subject matter and **(B)** a summary of the facts and opinions.

Default timing is 90 days before trial, and a rebuttal disclosure within 30 days after the other party's disclosure — both superseded here by Scheduling Order ¶3.

⚠️ **The Prieve problem.** Prieve Engineering was retained to evaluate the claim, not to provide testimony (which is the point of RFAs 52–53 and of the Second Set's ordinary-course request). If FIE designates Nathan Prieve as a trial expert, FIE may argue he is **not** "retained or specially employed to provide expert testimony in the case," so no signed report is owed — only a subject matter and a summary. Counters, in order of strength:

1. **Scheduling Order ¶3(b)** requires Defendant's "expert interrogatories, disclosures, **and reports**" by April 17, 2027. Read naturally, the Order requires a report from Defendant's experts whatever Rule 26.01(b) would allow on its own.
2. **The expert-discovery stipulation** (`../Expert_Discovery_Stipulation_Letter_to_Kennedy_2026-09-12.md`) is the place to fix this cheaply — add a term that any testifying expert provides a report with the Rule 26.01(b)(A)–(F) contents.
3. **The ordinary-course documents substitute.** RFP 5 already demands the engagement letter, every draft, all peer reviews, all communications with FIE, and every impact-force, terminal-velocity or hail-threshold calculation — which is most of what a report would contain, and is discoverable precisely because the engagement was not in anticipation of litigation.

### 1.2 The expert interrogatory — the only expert discovery available as of right

Rule 26.02(e)(1)(A) lets a party by interrogatory require another party to identify each expert it expects to call at trial and to state the subject matter, the substance of the facts and opinions, and a summary of the grounds for each opinion. That is one interrogatory if drafted as one question tracking the rule; an adversary may argue the rule's elements make it four.

### 1.3 Depositions are not automatic in Minnesota

Rule 26.02(e)(1)(B): "Upon motion, the court may order further discovery by other means…" There is no Minnesota analogue to the federal practice of noticing an expert deposition as of right. That is the whole reason for the September 12, 2026 stipulation letter, which also flags that the Scheduling Order closes fact discovery (3/15/2027) **before** FIE's expert is even disclosed (4/17/2027), with all motions heard by 5/14/2027. **If no stipulation or order is obtained, there is effectively no route to depose FIE's expert.** Rule 29 supplies the authority for the parties to agree; ¶3(a)'s sweep of "Plaintiffs' expert interrogatories" into the 3/15/2027 date is the ambiguity the stipulation resolves.

### 1.4 Fees, and non-testifying experts

Rule 26.02(e)(3): the court "shall require the party seeking discovery to pay the expert a reasonable fee for time spent in responding to discovery" absent manifest injustice. Budget for FIE's expert's hourly rate for deposition time. Rule 26.02(e)(2): facts and opinions of an expert retained in anticipation of litigation but **not** expected to testify are discoverable only on a showing of exceptional circumstances — which is why it matters that Prieve was retained in the ordinary course of adjusting the claim, not for this litigation.

⚠️ **Asymmetry to respect.** Minnesota has **not** adopted the federal protections for draft expert reports and attorney-expert communications (Fed. R. Civ. P. 26(b)(4)(B)–(C)). Nothing found today shields Plaintiffs' own expert's drafts and correspondence from discovery. Assume everything sent to or from Plaintiffs' expert is discoverable, and keep instructions factual.

## 2. Attacking the opinion itself: Rule 702, not Daubert

**Minn. R. Evid. 702, verbatim:** "If scientific, technical, or other specialized knowledge will assist the trier of fact to understand the evidence or to determine a fact in issue, a witness qualified as an expert by knowledge, skill, experience, training, or education, may testify thereto in the form of an opinion or otherwise. **The opinion must have foundational reliability.** In addition, if the opinion or evidence involves **novel scientific theory**, the proponent must establish that the underlying scientific evidence is generally accepted in the relevant scientific community."

- **Minnesota rejected Daubert.** *Goeb v. Tharaldson*, 615 N.W.2d 800 (Minn. 2000). The two-prong Frye-Mack test comes from *State v. Mack*, 292 N.W.2d 764 (Minn. 1980) and *State v. Roman Nose*, 649 N.W.2d 815 (Minn. 2002).
- **Frye-Mack applies only where the evidence is both novel *and* scientific.** Engineering reconstruction and causation opinions of this kind are ordinarily neither, so the attack runs through Rule 702's foundational-reliability requirement, not through a Frye-Mack hearing.
- **The analysis is substantially the same either way.** *Doe v. Archdiocese of Saint Paul & Minneapolis*, 817 N.W.2d 150 (Minn. 2012).
- **Practical consequence.** A motion labeled "Daubert" is mislabeled in this forum. `../../../OffenseStrategies/Motions/Daubert/` should be read as a Rule 702 foundational-reliability motion; the folder's `Frye-Mack-OnePager.md` has the right frame, and `UninspectedGarageWindow_Significance.md` §3.3 already flagged the naming problem.
- **What foundational reliability turns on here** — and note how the served discovery already targets each: no quantitative impact-force calculation (RFA 62), no terminal-velocity calculation (RFA 63), no hail-size threshold (RFAs 105–106), no identified alternative cause (RFA 103), a scope that omitted a window inside the engagement (RFAs 80–81), and a weather premise contradicted by the NWS page (RFAs 59–61). Those are reliability facts, established by admission rather than by expert opinion.

## 3. What "rebuttal expert interrogatories, disclosures, and reports" means

Scheduling Order ¶3(c) sets **May 14, 2027**. Under Rule 26.01(b), a rebuttal disclosure is one **intended solely to contradict or rebut evidence on the same subject matter** identified by another party's expert disclosure, and by default it is due within 30 days after that disclosure. So:

- **The three-step sequence:** Plaintiffs disclose (3/15/2027) → FIE discloses (4/17/2027) → either side's **rebuttal** disclosure (5/14/2027), limited to the same subject matter the other side's expert addressed.
- **It is a response, not a second chance.** A rebuttal expert cannot be used to add affirmative opinions Plaintiffs should have disclosed on March 15. Plan the case-in-chief expert accordingly.
- **"Expert interrogatories" in ¶3** means the Rule 26.02(e)(1)(A) interrogatory — which is why ¶3(a)'s wording matters: read literally it would require Plaintiffs to have served their expert interrogatory by 3/15/2027, a month before FIE designates anyone. The stipulation's term 3 fixes this by allowing service within 21 days after each disclosure.
- **The 27-day squeeze.** FIE's disclosure on 4/17/2027 leaves 27 days to rebuttal. Without the stipulation there is no time for an expert interrogatory, a motion to depose, and a rebuttal report inside that window.

## 4. Consequences for the interrogatory budget (F-9)

### 4.1 The window for expert follow-ups does not currently exist

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| **3/15/2027** | All discovery must be **completed** (¶2), and Plaintiffs' expert disclosures and reports are due (¶3(a)) |
| **4/17/2027** | FIE's expert disclosures and reports (¶3(b)) — **one month after discovery closed** |
| **5/14/2027** | Rebuttal disclosures (¶3(c)); last day any motion may be **heard** (¶5) |

An interrogatory about FIE's expert cannot be answered after 3/15/2027, and FIE need not name anyone until 4/17/2027. **So there is no post-disclosure follow-up window at all unless the stipulation creates one** (Bill's observation, 9/17/2026). An earlier draft of this note recommended reserving 5 interrogatories for expert work; that reserved capacity for a window that may never open.

### 4.2 What still works without a stipulation

Serve the **Rule 26.02(e)(1)(A)** interrogatory **before the close** — early **February 2027** — asking FIE to identify each person it "expects to call" as an expert and to state the subject matter, the substance of the facts and opinions, and a summary of the grounds. FIE may answer that no expert has been retained. Two things then operate:

- **Rule 26.05** requires seasonable supplementation once FIE designates.
- **Instruction 3** of the First Set, as amended 9/17/2026 (item I-9), requires FIE to state expressly that an answer rests on an incomplete search or review, to identify what remains, and to supplement on completion.

The result is a sworn, continuing obligation rather than a single snapshot — and it is the reading that makes sense of ¶3(a), which sweeps "Plaintiffs' expert interrogatories" into the 3/15/2027 date. What it does **not** provide is a deposition, or any opportunity to test a report first seen on 4/17/2027. That gap is what the stipulation exists to close.
