## 1. How the 70-mile search was actually run

The registry's distance selector offers only **10, 15, 30 and 50 miles** — there is no 70-mile option. So the ring was covered in two passes:

| Pass | Centre | Radius | Results |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | **55347** (Eden Prairie) | **50 miles** — the maximum available | 7 pages, **≈66 inspectors** |
| B | **56301** (St. Cloud) | 30 miles | 35 entries — sweeps the NW ring |
| B | **56001** (Mankato) | 30 miles | 6 entries — sweeps the SW ring |
| B | **55901** (Rochester) | 30 miles | 6 entries — sweeps the SE ring |

Combined coverage is roughly the 70-mile band you asked for. **Total: about 80 certified inspectors.**

## 2. The headline finding is unchanged — and now much better tested

**Across roughly 80 certified inspectors in a ~70-mile radius, there is still not one engineering firm.** No company name in the entire set contains "Engineering." Seven times the search area, six times the people, same answer.

That is now a solid conclusion rather than a small-sample impression: **the Haag-certified population in this region is roofing contractors and insurance-company vendors, not professional engineers.** Haag certification is a damage-assessment credential, not a PE licence, so nobody on this list can supply the Rule 702 engineering causation opinion due 3/15/2027.

What the widening *did* produce is something more valuable than another name — see §3.

**Two consequences.**

**(a) Conflict screening — immediate and concrete.** Nobody from Hancock can be your expert or your pre-retention second opinion, and the same goes for the other carrier-side entries in the pool:

- **Seek Now** (a national carrier inspection vendor) — Kolcz, Christopher (Plymouth); Gerasimov, Alexander (Rosemount); Bagley, Jackson (Rochester)
- **American Modern Insurance Group (AMIG)** — Cobb, Ken (Minneapolis)

Add "Hancock Claims Consultants" and "Seek Now" to the conflict-check list you send every candidate — the V2 packet §7 item 5 list currently names Hancock but not Seek Now.

**(b) Discovery intelligence, which may matter more.** Eight certified inspectors across the Twin Cities, St. Cloud and southeastern Minnesota is not an occasional vendor relationship — that is a standing regional operation. It supports the inference that Hancock is a routine, high-volume Farmers vendor rather than a one-off retention, which is exactly the premise behind the vendor-retention-agreement requests already served (RFPs on vendor retention agreements, and the Hancock document requests in the First Set). If Farmers resists producing the Hancock retention agreement or volume data, this public registry is a free, citable data point showing the scale of the relationship.

Worth flagging to whoever handles the Brau and Dudley depositions: the question "how many Farmers inspections does Hancock perform in Minnesota annually" now has an obvious floor.

## 5. What this changes about the plan

**Nothing in the recommendation — and that is the useful result.** Widening from 10 to ~70 miles tested the conclusion properly and confirmed it: the hail-certified population near you does not contain the expert you need, because that credential and that expert are different things.

The route in `Local_Hail_Expert_Search_2026-09-24.md` §§5–7 stands:

1. **Livgard & Lloyd PLLP** — (612) 825-7777 — ask for an engineer referral. Still the best single call.
2. **American Engineering Testing** — St. Paul HQ, seven Minnesota offices, lists windows among envelope components assessed after hail.
3. **Encompass, Inc.** — Minnetonka, already contacted; the only candidate who can test the assemblies.
4. **Kenneth Udoibok** — (612) 808-6031 — appraisal-dispute practice.

**One thing to add to the plan:** send the Hancock and Seek Now names to whoever is preparing the Brau/Dudley depositions, and add **Seek Now** to the conflict list in every expert inquiry.

**Optional, cheap:** if you want a quick local second opinion on the windows before the engineer is retained, one of the §4 consultancies or an unaffiliated Eden Prairie inspector could look at them for a modest fee. Treat that as reconnaissance, not expert work — and remember that anything you send a person you later designate as a testifying witness is discoverable (`Expert_Communications_Privilege_Research_2026-09-19.md`).
