# Expert Witness Research — Shortlist & Vetting Guide

**Case:** *Yibiao Lu and Jie Hu v. Fire Insurance Exchange* — Court File No. 27-CV-26-11606
**Purpose:** Candidate experts to prove hail causation/scope and rebut the Prieve report, plus how to vet and retain them.
**Prepared:** August 2026

> **Important — please read.** I am not a lawyer and I cannot personally vouch for, endorse, or guarantee the reliability of any expert below. These are **real, credentialed candidates surfaced from public expert-witness directories** — a starting list to investigate, not a recommendation. An expert becomes "trustable" only after *you* (ideally with a Minnesota attorney) run the vetting checklist at the end: a clean conflict check, genuine independence, solid credentials, and a clean testifying record. Contact details change — confirm them on each firm's own website before relying on them.

---

## 1. What kind of expert(s) you need

Your case needs opinion testimony in **two disciplines** — which can come from two experts or, ideally, one dual-credentialed expert:

- **Forensic engineer (structural / building-envelope).** To opine that the July 13–14, 2024 hail caused the damage to the additional windows and components, to quantify hail-impact thresholds for extruded-aluminum and aluminum-clad wood windows, and to **rebut Prieve's methodology** (the "smooth/rounded only = hail" premise, the missing impact-force calculation, the internal contradictions).
- **Forensic meteorologist.** To establish, from radar/ASOS/storm data, the **hail size, wind, and severity at your specific address** on the loss date — and to disprove Prieve's "most recent hail in Eden Prairie reported in 2020" statement.

A single expert who is **both a PE and a Certified Consulting Meteorologist (CCM)** can cover much of both — which is why the first name below is worth a hard look.

## 6. Vetting checklist — this is what makes an expert "trustable"

Run this before retaining anyone:

- **Conflict check (do this first).** Confirm the expert/firm has **no** current or past work for Fire Insurance Exchange / Farmers or for Kennedy Law Firm, and no relationship with Prieve Engineering. Ask directly.
- **Independence / balance.** Ask their approximate **plaintiff-vs-insurer split**. Someone who works both sides (or leans neither way) is harder to paint as biased than a pure hired gun. Beware anyone who works *only* for insurers.
- **Credentials.** Engineering opinions should come from a **licensed PE** (ideally licensed in Minnesota, or able to obtain comity licensure); meteorology from a **CCM**. Ask for the CV and license numbers.
- **Testifying record.** Ask for a **list of prior testimony** (depositions/trials) and whether the expert has ever been **excluded or limited** by a court (a Frye-Mack/Daubert challenge). Ask for two attorney references.
- **Methodology fit.** Can they (a) quantify hail-impact thresholds for extruded-aluminum and aluminum-clad wood windows; (b) rebut Prieve's "smooth/rounded only" hail signature and its "no hail after 2020" weather claim; and (c) work within the appraisal/scope framework of this case?
- **Willingness to inspect.** Confirm they will **inspect the property** (including the rear-garage window no one has looked at) and review Prieve's **native photos and metadata** once produced.
- **Engagement terms in writing.** Fee schedule, retainer, and an engagement letter.

## 7. Minnesota admissibility standard (so the opinion survives challenge)

Minnesota applies the **Frye-Mack** standard (general acceptance of the scientific technique **plus** foundational reliability under Minn. R. Evid. 702) — **not** the federal *Daubert* test. *See Goeb v. Tharaldson Motors*, 615 N.W.2d 800 (Minn. 2000). Standard forensic hail-causation engineering and meteorology are generally accepted, so a challenge will usually target **foundation and methodology**, not the science itself. Pick an expert who documents a rigorous, repeatable method (impact-force analysis, address-specific weather data) — the very things Prieve's report lacks. *(Confirm the current standard with counsel.)*

Two recent developments worth flagging to your expert: courts have grown skeptical of causation opinions resting on **automated/aggregated weather-database reports** (relevant to attacking Prieve's reliance on such data), while properly-founded **engineering hail-impact opinions** have been admitted. Your expert should ground opinions in primary radar/ASOS data and hands-on analysis.

## 8. Rough cost expectations (confirm with each expert)

General market ranges, not quotes: forensic engineers and CCM meteorologists commonly bill **~$250–$600+/hour**; an initial review + site inspection + written report often runs from a **few thousand dollars to $10,000+** depending on scope; **deposition and trial testimony** are billed hourly, frequently at a higher rate, and a **retainer** is typical. Get a written fee schedule up front. Note the litigation-cost tie-in: your Consumer Fraud Act count (§ 8.31/§ 325F.70) and § 604.18 provide fee/cost-shifting vehicles, and you already reserved investigation costs — keep every expert invoice.
