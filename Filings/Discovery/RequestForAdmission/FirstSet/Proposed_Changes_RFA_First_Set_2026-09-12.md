## Why this file exists

Requests for admission have become the natural home for a good deal of what was originally drafted as interrogatories, for one structural reason: **Rule 36 has no numeric limit, and Rule 33.01(a) caps interrogatories at 50 cumulatively.** Rule 36.02 also makes an admission *conclusively established* for the action, which is stronger than an interrogatory answer (evidence) or a document production (authentication still required).

Items are numbered **A-1** onward, to stay distinct from the **P-** items (Requests for Production) and **I-** items (Interrogatories).

## A-6. The defense-universe lock — move it here from Interrogatory 15

*Added September 12, 2026. Paired with item **I-33** in the interrogatory proposal, which proposes dropping Interrogatory 15.*

### Why this belongs in the RFA set and not in an interrogatory

The most consequential feature of FIE's Answer is what it does **not** plead. There is no statute-of-limitations or contractual suit-limitation defense — the very defense the Complaint spent pages pre-empting — and no failure to mitigate, setoff, prior payment, or cosmetic-damage/marring defense. Under Minn. R. Civ. P. 8.03 an unpleaded affirmative defense is generally waived, and `FIE_Defense_Strategy_and_Anticipated_Motions.md` anticipates a motion to amend the Answer.

**The Scheduling Order sets no pleading-amendment deadline.** It fixes joinder at November 16, 2026 (¶1) and says nothing else about amending pleadings. So nothing in the schedule prevents FIE from moving to add a limitations defense months from now; the protections are Rule 8.03 waiver and prejudice.

An interrogatory is the wrong instrument for locking this down, because Rule 33 practice tolerates the standard dodge — *"Defendant reserves the right to assert additional defenses as discovery proceeds."* **Rule 36 does not.** Rule 36.01:

> "The answer shall specifically deny the matter or set forth in detail the reasons why the answering party cannot truthfully admit or deny the matter. … An answering party may not give lack of information or knowledge as a reason for failure to admit or deny **unless the party states that a reasonable inquiry has been made** and that the information known or readily obtainable by the party is insufficient to enable the party to admit or deny."

Admit, deny, or certify a reasonable inquiry and explain. "We reserve the right" is not among the options. And under Rule 36.02 an admission is **conclusively established** unless the court permits withdrawal.

**Note also what a reservation does not do.** Rule 8.03 waiver operates on the *pleadings*. A reservation in a discovery response does not amend the Answer — FIE would still need leave under Rule 15.01. The language is rhetorical, not procedural.

### Proposed requests

> **REQUEST FOR ADMISSION NO. [41]:** Admit that You do not contend this action is barred, in whole or in part, by any statute of limitations.
>
> **REQUEST FOR ADMISSION NO. [42]:** Admit that You do not contend this action is barred, in whole or in part, by any contractual suit-limitation or time-to-sue provision in The Policy.
>
> **REQUEST FOR ADMISSION NO. [43]:** Admit that You do not contend Plaintiffs failed to mitigate their damages.
>
> **REQUEST FOR ADMISSION NO. [44]:** Admit that You do not contend that any payment made on The 2020 Claim operates as a setoff, credit, or prior payment reducing any amount owed on The Claim.
>
> **REQUEST FOR ADMISSION NO. [45]:** Admit that You do not contend Plaintiffs failed to comply with any condition precedent under The Policy, including any notice, proof-of-loss, or cooperation provision.
>
> **REQUEST FOR ADMISSION NO. [46]:** Admit that You do not contend any statement made by Plaintiffs in connection with The Policy or The Claim was false, fraudulent, or a material misrepresentation.
>
> **REQUEST FOR ADMISSION NO. [47]:** Admit that, other than the defenses asserted in Your Answer, You are not presently aware of any fact supporting any additional defense, avoidance, exclusion, or limitation barring or reducing Plaintiffs' recovery.

**Why each is framed "You do not contend."** An admission is present-tense and answerable today; a request about what FIE might contend in future is not. A **denial** is equally useful — it flushes the defense out now, while there is still time to take discovery on it, rather than at summary judgment.

### Why No. 45 is on the list

FIE's Answer contains an unprompted swipe at cooperation: it "denies that Plaintiffs cooperated with Defendant's claim-handling process to the extent Plaintiffs attempted to direct the claim-handling process by adamantly advising Hancock Claims Consultants…" That is not pleaded as an affirmative defense, but it signals the theory exists. No. 45 forces FIE either to disclaim it or to assert it now.

### What to expect, and why each answer helps

| FIE's answer | Consequence |
|---|---|
| **Admits** | Conclusively established under Rule 36.02. The defense is gone. |
| **Denies** | The defense is disclosed now, with the whole discovery period left to meet it — and FIE has effectively pleaded a defense not in its Answer, which is itself useful on a Rule 8.03 waiver argument. |
| **"Cannot admit or deny pending discovery"** | Only available with a certification that reasonable inquiry was made. For Nos. 41, 42 and 46 that certification is hard to make honestly — a suit-limitation defense turns on **The Policy**, FIE's own document since 2014, and on the filing date, a public record. Nothing in discovery changes either. A refusal to answer those is impeaching if FIE later moves to amend. |

## A-7. Loss settlement and the deductible — lock FIE's reading of its own policy

*Added September 12, 2026. Replaces the withdrawn interrogatory item **I-15**.*

### Where this comes from

`HomePolicy/2014_newBusiness_policy.pdf`, page 24 of 37, "How we settle covered loss":

> **5.a.(1)** Settlement for covered loss or damage to the dwelling "will be settled at **replacement cost, without deduction for depreciation**," but for no more than the smallest of the stated limit, "the replacement cost of that part of the dwelling … for equivalent construction with materials of like kind and quality," the amount actually and necessarily spent to repair or replace, or the loss to the insured's interest.
>
> "When the cost to repair or replace damaged property is more than $2,500, **we will pay no more than the actual cash value of the loss until actual repair or replacement is completed**."
>
> **8. Deductible clause.** "We pay for loss or damage when **a covered loss exceeds the deductible(s)** amount applicable to the property. The deductible(s) applies separately to each loss or damage event."

On that language, loss is **settled** at replacement cost, and actual cash value operates as a limit on the **timing** of payment until repairs are done — not as the measure of the loss against which the deductible is applied.

**Why it matters, and why it is not decisive.** FIE's Answer to Complaint ¶35 admits "no payment was due … because the amount awarded … did not exceed the Plaintiffs' deductible," measuring $6,400 against $17,720. On the replacement-cost reading the two awarded windows contribute **$12,800** — which narrows the gap Plaintiffs must close from roughly $11,320 to roughly **$4,920**. It does not by itself produce a payment, because $12,800 is still below $17,720. It changes the size of the hill, not whether there is one.

### Proposed requests

> **REQUEST FOR ADMISSION NO. [48]:** Admit that under The Policy, covered loss or damage to the dwelling is settled at replacement cost, without deduction for depreciation, subject to the limits stated in the policy.
>
> **REQUEST FOR ADMISSION NO. [49]:** Admit that the policy form applicable to The Claim provides: "When the cost to repair or replace damaged property is more than $2,500, we will pay no more than the actual cash value of the loss until actual repair or replacement is completed."
>
> **REQUEST FOR ADMISSION NO. [50]:** Admit that You do not contend that the amount of covered loss, for purposes of applying the windstorm and hail deductible, is limited to actual cash value.
>
> **REQUEST FOR ADMISSION NO. [51]:** Admit that the policy form in effect on July 13, 2024 contains the same "How we settle covered loss" and "Deductible clause" provisions as the form issued to Plaintiffs in 2014.

**Why these are requests for admission and not an interrogatory.** FIE cannot answer any of them with "the appraisal panel determined that figure," and cannot answer them with "there is no other damage" — they are about the meaning of FIE's own policy language, not about the extent of the loss. Rule 36.01 requires admit, deny, or a detailed statement of why FIE cannot truthfully do either, with a reasonable-inquiry certification if it pleads lack of knowledge about its own form. Rule 36.02 makes an admission conclusive. And Rule 36 has no numeric cap, unlike the 50-interrogatory limit under Rule 33.01(a).

**No. 51 does real work.** The language quoted above is from the **2014** policy. The operative form is whatever was delivered at the **October 22, 2023** renewal, and the project holds the renewal declarations rather than the renewal form itself. **RFP 35** (the complete policy as delivered at the 2023 renewal) is the document route and should now be treated as a priority; No. 51 is the admission route, and either one closes the gap.

## A-8. Payment and the contended amount — replaces withdrawn interrogatory item I-17

*Added September 12, 2026. Replaces the withdrawn interrogatory item **I-17**, which would have asked FIE to state under oath the amount it contends is owed and every amount it has paid.*

### Why this moved here

Answer ¶35 already alleges that net payment is **$0** and that none was due because the award did not exceed the deductible. That is a judicial admission and is binding. Two things are nonetheless worth pinning under Rule 36:

1. **Comprehensiveness.** ¶35's allegation is about *the awarded amount*. It does not say FIE paid nothing at all — for emergency repairs, temporary repairs, additional living expense, or directly to a contractor. A denial-of-benefits element should not rest on an inference from a narrower allegation.
2. **Form.** Rule 36.02 makes an admission **conclusively established** for the action unless withdrawn on motion. A pleading allegation has to be read, construed and argued from; an admission is simply a fact. For proving the § 604.18 predicate — that benefits were denied — the second is cleaner.

Neither point justified spending 2 of 50 interrogatories. Rule 36 has no numeric cap, so here they cost nothing.

### Proposed requests

> **REQUEST FOR ADMISSION NO. [52]:** Admit that You have made no payment of any kind to Plaintiffs under The Policy on account of the July 13, 2024 loss.
>
> **REQUEST FOR ADMISSION NO. [53]:** Admit that You have made no payment of any kind to any contractor, repair firm, or other third party for the repair or replacement of any part of The Property on account of the July 13, 2024 loss, including any payment for emergency or temporary repairs and any payment of additional living expense.
>
> **REQUEST FOR ADMISSION NO. [54]:** Admit that You contend no amount is owed to Plaintiffs under The Policy on account of the July 13, 2024 loss.
>
> **REQUEST FOR ADMISSION NO. [55]:** Admit that the application of the windstorm and hail deductible is the only reason You have made no payment on account of the two windows identified in The Appraisal Award.

**Why these work as admissions where the interrogatory did not.** No. 52 and No. 53 ask about **historical fact within FIE's own records** — FIE cannot answer them with a reservation of rights, and a lack-of-knowledge answer about its own payment ledger would require the Rule 36.01 reasonable-inquiry certification. No. 54 asks FIE to confirm the position it has already pleaded. No. 55 is a narrow defense lock that complements **A-6**: it forecloses FIE from later adding a coverage or exclusion argument against the two awarded windows on top of the deductible argument, which *Quade* already makes difficult for it.

**Two honest limits.** First, No. 54 and No. 55 speak as of the date of the response; neither prevents FIE from changing position later with an explanation, any more than the interrogatory would have. Their value is evidentiary, not preclusive — if the position shifts, the admission is the impeachment. Second, No. 55 may draw a qualified admission ("admitted as to payment; Defendant does not concede coverage"), which is itself informative and should be treated as the answer rather than as a discovery dispute.

## A-9. The Prieve engagement — replaces withdrawn interrogatory item I-18

*Added September 12, 2026. Replaces the withdrawn interrogatory item **I-18**, which would have asked FIE to state whether it contends the Prieve Report is expert material under Rule 26.02(e) or ordinary claim handling.*

### Why this moved here

I-18 was withdrawn because it asked a question FIE can defer twice over: Rule 26.02(e)(1)(A) is about whom FIE **expects to call at trial**, an election not due until April 17, 2027, and a "State whether You contend" question is a contention interrogatory subject to deferral under Rule 33.02.

But protection under Rule 26.02(d) and 26.02(e)(2) does not turn on designation. Both require that the material or the expert have been prepared or retained **"in anticipation of litigation or preparation for trial."** That is a historical fact about the engagement, and a request for admission reaches it directly: under Rule 36.01 FIE must admit, deny, or state in detail why it can do neither — *"we have not yet decided"* is not among the permitted answers, and a lack-of-knowledge response about its own vendor engagement requires the reasonable-inquiry certification.

### The factual ground

The November 4, 2024 denial letter (`EventDocuments/FarmersDenyLetter-11-4.pdf`, p. 1) states:

> "Our investigation found the condition of the window and components to be from wear/tear **as referrenced in the attached engineer report**."

The letter was "Delivered by email to: BILL_Y_LU@YAHOO.COM," and Prieve's Appendix A came with it (`EventDocuments/FarmersDenyLetter-11-4-AppendixA.pdf`). FIE attached its engineer's report to a claim decision and sent it to the insureds.

### Proposed requests

> **REQUEST FOR ADMISSION NO. [56]:** Admit that You engaged Prieve Engineering, LLC in connection with Your investigation, evaluation, and adjustment of The Claim.
>
> **REQUEST FOR ADMISSION NO. [57]:** Admit that You attached the Prieve Report to Your November 4, 2024 letter to Plaintiffs and delivered it to Plaintiffs by email on or about that date.
>
> **REQUEST FOR ADMISSION NO. [58]:** Admit that You relied on the Prieve Report as a basis for Your November 4, 2024 denial of The Claim.
>
> **REQUEST FOR ADMISSION NO. [59]:** Admit that You do not contend that the Prieve Report was prepared in anticipation of litigation or for trial within the meaning of Minn. R. Civ. P. 26.02(d).
>
> **REQUEST FOR ADMISSION NO. [60]:** Admit that Nathan Prieve was not retained or specially employed by You in anticipation of litigation or preparation for trial within the meaning of Minn. R. Civ. P. 26.02(e)(2).

### Why these work where the interrogatory did not

Nos. 56–58 are **historical facts within FIE's own records**, and Nos. 57 and 58 are close to self-proving from the denial letter itself. Nos. 59 and 60 are phrased as the **absence of a contention**, which is the form FIE cannot dodge: a party either does or does not presently contend a thing, and Rule 36.01 requires it to say which. Rule 36.02 then makes any admission **conclusively established**, so an admission to No. 59 or No. 60 forecloses the Rule 26.02(d)/(e)(2) objection to **RFP 5** before the fight starts.

**Who carries the load either way.** "The party objecting to the production of information has the burden of establishing that the sought-after information is immune from discovery." *In re Comm'r of Pub. Safety*, 735 N.W.2d 706, 712 (Minn. 2007). The standard is whether the document "can fairly be said to have been prepared or obtained **because of the prospect of litigation**." *City Pages v. State*, 655 N.W.2d 839, 846 (Minn. Ct. App. 2003). Materials generated in the regular course of business under standard procedures are not protected. *Brown v. St. Paul City Ry. Co.*, 241 Minn. 15, 62 N.W.2d 688 (1954).

**The honest counter.** Prieve was engaged **after** the September 3, 2024 denial and **after** Plaintiffs submitted rebuttal photographs on September 5, 8 and 10, 2024 — a sequence FIE can characterize as anticipating a dispute. Expect denials to Nos. 59 and 60 on that theory. A denial is still useful: it commits FIE to a position now, on its own burden, and it sits badly against No. 57 and No. 58. The answer to an insurer that re-investigates a challenged denial is that it is still adjusting the claim — and FIE's transmittal of the report to the insureds is the best evidence it treated the work that way.

**What is *not* here.** The date FIE first anticipated litigation is deliberately left out. It is the master key to every work-product objection across the RFP set, but it is worth an interrogatory only once FIE actually asserts the protection. It is earmarked in reserve at 1 in the interrogatory proposal (item **I-18**), triggered by FIE's privilege log or any withholding statement under RFP Instruction 4 or 9.

## A-10. Scope of replacement cost — replaces removed Interrogatory 7

*Added September 12, 2026. Implements interrogatory proposal item **I-21**: Interrogatory 7 was **removed from the live set** the same day and its four subparts are converted here, with the catch-all that item **I-11** would have added as a fifth.*

### Why the conversion is an upgrade, not a compromise

Every subpart of Interrogatory 7 was a yes/no proposition about a contention — the thing Rule 36 exists for. Three gains:

1. **Cost.** Four interrogatories (five with I-11) become zero. Rule 36 has no numeric cap.
2. **Default.** Under Rule 36.01 a request not answered within 30 days is **admitted**. An unanswered interrogatory just sits there until you move to compel.
3. **Effect.** Rule 36.02 makes an admission **conclusively established** for the action. An interrogatory answer is merely evidence that can be explained away at trial.

The requests are framed as what the replacement cost **includes**, so an admission is affirmatively useful and a denial commits FIE to a position it must then defend.

### Proposed requests

> **REQUEST FOR ADMISSION NO. [61]:** Admit that the replacement cost owed under The Policy for the covered windows includes the cost of detaching and resetting, or replacing, window blinds and other coverings mounted to or within those windows.
>
> **REQUEST FOR ADMISSION NO. [62]:** Admit that the replacement cost owed under The Policy for the covered windows includes the cost of repairing or replacing interior or exterior trim disturbed by the window replacement.
>
> **REQUEST FOR ADMISSION NO. [63]:** Admit that the replacement cost owed under The Policy for the covered windows includes the cost of exterior repaint necessitated by the window installation.
>
> **REQUEST FOR ADMISSION NO. [64]:** Admit that the replacement cost owed under The Policy for the covered windows includes the cost of remediating mold or water damage resulting from leakage through those windows.
>
> **REQUEST FOR ADMISSION NO. [65]:** Admit that You do not contend that any category of cost, other than those addressed in Requests for Admission Nos. 61 through 64, is excluded from or not included in the replacement cost owed for the covered windows.

**No. 65 is the catch-all**, in the "absence of a contention" form used at A-9. As written, it stops FIE from answering the first four and holding a fifth exclusion theory in reserve for summary judgment; Rule 26.05 then obliges FIE to supplement if one appears later.

### ⚠️ What the conversion gives up — and the earmark that covers it

Interrogatory 7 asked not only *whether* FIE contends each cost is excluded but for "the specific factual and contractual basis for each." **A request for admission cannot ask for that.** If FIE denies Nos. 61–64, you learn the position but not the reasoning.

Three ways back to it, in the order I would use them:

| Route | Cost | When |
|---|---|---|
| Rule 36.01 requires a denial to "fairly meet the substance of the requested admission" and to specify what is admitted where the rest is denied — a bare "denied" on a mixed proposition is itself objectionable | free | on receipt of the responses |
| The Rule 30.02(f) organizational deposition | free of interrogatory budget | after production |
| **One reserved interrogatory** — "state the factual and contractual basis for each denial of Requests for Admission Nos. 61 through 65" | **1** | only if FIE denies |

**Net budget effect: −4 today, −3 in the worst case.** Earmarked at 1 in the interrogatory proposal's reserve table.

## A-11. The *Quade* lock on the two awarded windows — replaces removed Interrogatory 14

*Added September 12, 2026. Implements interrogatory proposal item **I-22**. Interrogatory 14 asked FIE to state its position on whether the award binds it as to causation under* Quade v. Secura Ins., 814 N.W.2d 703 (Minn. 2012); *it was removed from the live set the same day. This belongs with the **A-6** defense-universe series and should sit next to it when the set is assembled.*

### Why an admission does this better than the interrogatory did

FIE's Answer already admits the **facts**: ¶¶31–32 admit the panel identified **hail** as the cause of loss for the two awarded windows, and ¶48, with Affirmative Defense #2, treats the award as **binding**. Under *Quade*, determining the "amount of loss" necessarily includes determining cause, so the panel's causation finding is within its authority and binds the insurer as to what it decided.

What FIE has never conceded is the **consequence** — that it may not now reassert wear-and-tear, marring, corrosion, cosmetic damage or any other exclusion against those two windows. Interrogatory 14 asked for FIE's legal position on that. A stated legal position binds nobody and can be abandoned at will; a Rule 36 admission is **conclusively established** under Rule 36.02. The interrogatory was also the item in the set most exposed to a Rule 33.02 deferral order, since it asked FIE to brief case law rather than state facts.

### Proposed request

> **REQUEST FOR ADMISSION NO. [66]:** Admit that You do not contend that any exclusion, limitation, or condition in The Policy bars or reduces recovery for the two windows identified in The Appraisal Award.

**Either answer is useful.** An admission closes those two windows to any further coverage defense and confines the fight to the units the panel never evaluated — the Count I lane. A denial is made in the teeth of FIE's own ¶¶31–32 and ¶48 admissions, and sets up the partial-summary-judgment motion already contemplated at item F of `../../../SummonsAndComplaint/AnswerFromFIE/FIE_Defense_Strategy_and_Anticipated_Motions.md`.

**Note the deliberate limit.** The request is confined to the two **awarded** windows. ## A-12. When the litigation hold issued — replaces removed Interrogatory 16

*Added September 12, 2026. Implements interrogatory proposal item **I-23**, which removed Interrogatory 16 ("State the date on which Your litigation hold concerning this matter was issued, its recipients, its scope, and each update to it") on the footing that RFP 14 covers the same ground.*

### The problem these requests solve

RFP 14 asks for "[d]ocuments sufficient to show" the hold's date, recipients, scope and updates — but it closes with: "Plaintiffs do not by this request seek the text of any communication from counsel." Litigation holds are commonly issued **by counsel**. FIE can therefore answer that the only responsive document is a privileged communication whose text Plaintiffs have disclaimed, and produce nothing.

The removed interrogatory did not have that weakness: it asked for the hold's date, recipients and scope as **facts**, and privilege protects the communication rather than the underlying circumstance that one was sent, when, and to whom. These requests restore that, in a device with no numeric cap and with Rule 36.02's conclusive effect.

### Proposed requests

> **REQUEST FOR ADMISSION NO. [67]:** Admit that, as of September 6, 2024, You had not issued any litigation hold, preservation notice, or document-preservation instruction directing the preservation of documents concerning The Claim.
>
> **REQUEST FOR ADMISSION NO. [68]:** Admit that, as of November 4, 2024, You had not issued any litigation hold, preservation notice, or document-preservation instruction directing the preservation of documents concerning The Claim.

### Why these two dates

They bracket the period in which the records at the centre of the case went missing. **September 6, 2024** is the date of Nicholas Brau's inspection — the inspection for which FIE has stated in writing that "the file does not contain a report." **November 4, 2024** is the second denial, transmitted with the Prieve report.

**Both answers are useful, which is the point of the framing.** An admission establishes that no preservation instruction was in place while Brau's notes existed and then ceased to exist — a fact that goes to culpability on any spoliation motion, and that FIE cannot later walk back, since Rule 36.02 makes it conclusive. A denial concedes that a hold **was** in place by that date, which is the date information Interrogatory 16 was asking for, obtained without spending a slot.

**Neither request seeks the hold's text, its author, or any advice.** They ask only whether a preservation instruction had issued by a stated date. FIE cannot answer "not applicable" or "privileged" without effectively asserting that the existence and timing of its own preservation efforts are privileged, which is a position it would have to defend under Rule 36.01's requirement to state in detail why it can neither admit nor deny.

**The reserve earmark stays.** If FIE's RFP 14 response produces nothing or invokes the counsel-communication carve-out, serve the removed interrogatory as a single second-set question — by then better targeted, because you will know what is missing. Earmarked at 1.
