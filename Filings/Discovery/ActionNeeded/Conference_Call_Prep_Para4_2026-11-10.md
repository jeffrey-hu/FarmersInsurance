### 3.4 Proportionality: Rules 1 and 26.02 (FIE's closing citation)

**What the two rules are (plain English).**
- **Minn. R. Civ. P. 1, "Scope of Rules."** The opening rule of the Minnesota Rules of Civil Procedure. Its first paragraph says the rules govern every civil case in Minnesota district courts (except the special proceedings listed in Rule 81) and must be applied to get a just, speedy and inexpensive result. Its second paragraph, added effective July 1, 2013 after the Minnesota Supreme Court's Civil Justice Reform Task Force, goes further than the federal rule it was modeled on: it makes proportionality a **shared duty of the court and both parties** for the whole case, not just discovery. In practice it is a guiding principle the court uses when managing a case (how much discovery, how many motions, what schedule). It does not by itself create a discovery objection; the operative limit is Rule 26.02(b).
- **Minn. R. Civ. P. 26.02, "Discovery Methods, Scope and Limits."** The rule that defines what discovery may reach and how it may be limited. Its key subdivision, **26.02(b)**, sets the scope: anything not privileged that is **relevant** to a claim or defense **and proportional** to the needs of the case. Information need not be admissible to be discoverable. Other parts of Rule 26.02 cover the discovery methods available, limits on frequency and extent (for example, duplicative requests, or information obtainable from a more convenient source), disclosure of insurance coverage, trial-preparation materials and work product, expert discovery (26.02(e)), and claims of privilege (26.02(f); the letter's relief item 4 relies on 26.02(f)(1), which requires a withholding party to describe what it withholds). Since 2013 the scope sentence has matched Fed. R. Civ. P. 26(b)(1).
- **How FIE uses them:** Rule 1 to frame Plaintiffs' discovery as costly and excessive overall; Rule 26.02(b) as the legal test, arguing the discovery is neither relevant (because of the award) nor proportional.
- **How Plaintiffs use them:** the same Rule 26.02(b) supplies "relevant to any party's claim or defense," which the letter already quotes in §5, and the proportionality factors favor Plaintiffs (table below). Rule 1 also binds FIE: refusing narrow, yes/no RFAs and pointing to 2,340 pages instead of citing records adds cost, not removes it.

**The rules (text).**
- **Rule 1** (second paragraph, added 2013): "It is the responsibility of the court and the parties to examine each civil action to assure that the process and the costs are proportionate to the amount in controversy and the complexity and importance of the issues." Factors: "needs of the case, amount in controversy, parties' resources, and complexity and importance of the issues at stake in the litigation."
- **Rule 26.02(b)** (scope of discovery): "Parties may obtain discovery regarding any nonprivileged matter that is relevant to any party's claim or defense and proportional to the needs of the case," considering the importance of the issues at stake, the amount in controversy, the parties' relative access to relevant information, the parties' resources, the importance of the discovery in resolving the issues, and whether the burden or expense of the proposed discovery outweighs its likely benefit. It is identical to Fed. R. Civ. P. 26(b)(1), so federal cases interpreting that rule are persuasive.
