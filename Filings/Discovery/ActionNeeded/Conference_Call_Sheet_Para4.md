## 4A. Proportionality (Rules 1 and 26.02)

**20-second version:**

> We agree proportionality applies, and it favors Plaintiffs. Defendant alone holds these records. What remains is 15 yes-or-no admissions, 7 interrogatories and specific documents, after we withdrew 82 RFAs. Defendant hasn't identified any specific burden. And proportionality doesn't excuse a withholding statement, a privilege log, or Bates citations. Those are what three of our four items ask for.

**Proportionality cuts against Defendant's own conduct:**
- **It does not excuse procedural duties.** Proportionality limits the *scope* of discovery. It does not excuse stating whether documents are withheld (Rule 34.02(c)), logging what is withheld (Rule 26.02(f)(1)), or identifying records by Bates number when answering by reference to records (Rule 33.03). Relief items 2 (Bates citations), 3 (withholding statements) and 4 (privilege log) rest on those duties.
- **Defendant's approach is what drives cost.** Refusing yes/no RFAs and pointing to 2,340 pages costs more than answering. Defendant can cite its own Bates numbers in minutes; Plaintiffs would have to search everything. Rule 1 binds Defendant too.

**If the Judge wants to trim:** offer the priority list in § 2 rather than leave the cuts to chance.
