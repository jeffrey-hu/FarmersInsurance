**2. The appraisal-award objection.**

Eleven of Defendant's 12 interrogatory answers and 46 of its 53 production responses object that Plaintiffs' claim "was resolved via the binding Appraisal Award." Whether, and how far, the award resolved the claim is the disputed merits question in this case. It is not a ground to withhold discovery, and no order staying or limiting discovery has been entered. The objection also does not fit the claims as pleaded:

- **Counts I–III** put the award's scope in issue. Defendant told the appraisers that "[p]olicy interpretation, coverage, and other disputes may not be addressed in appraisal" (FIE 1221). Its appraiser later wrote that "[t]he damage on the front windows in the dining room was from the prior claim" (FIE 1639).
- **Complaint ¶ 53 (Count I) and ¶ 69 (Count IV)** concern how Defendant handled the claim, mostly before Plaintiffs demanded appraisal on August 3, 2025. The award did not decide how the claim was handled.

Please withdraw the objection, or state for each response what is being withheld on its basis.

Rule 36 sets no limit on the number of requests. Several matters denied this way are recorded in Defendant's own production, for example RFA 85 (FIE 1660: "The file does not contain a report on the requested date"), RFAs 33–34 (FIE 1002) and RFA 35 (FIE 1014).
