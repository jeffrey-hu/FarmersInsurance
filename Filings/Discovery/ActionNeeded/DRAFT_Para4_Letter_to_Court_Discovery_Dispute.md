**The discovery and Plaintiffs' narrowing.** Plaintiffs served 53 requests for production on September 15, 2026, and 12 interrogatories and 141 requests for admission on September 20, 2026. When Defendant objected to the number of requests for admission, Plaintiffs withdrew 82 of them. Fifty-nine remain. Plaintiffs ask the Court to compel only the items listed in the relief below. They are holding the remaining requests until the Court rules on Defendant's pending motion.

**Defendant's responses.** Defendant objected to every interrogatory and every request for production, and to every request for admission after No. 25. Forty-six of its 53 production responses and 11 of its 12 interrogatory answers assert the same ground: that "Plaintiffs' claim was resolved via the binding Appraisal Award." It answered 14 of the 59 pending requests for admission and responded to the other 45 only "Pursuant to FIE's General Objections, deny." No production response states whether materials are withheld, as Minn. R. Civ. P. 34.02(c)(3) requires, and no privilege log has been served, although eleven responses assert privilege or work product. The claim file and claim notes have not been produced.

Defendant asserts the same objection as to its own pleading. Its Answer (¶ 14) alleges that Plaintiffs "adamantly advis[ed]" its inspector to examine only interior windows. Asked who made that statement, when, and what records it (Interrogatory No. 11; Request No. 42), Defendant objected on relevance and identified no person, date or document. Asked for the documents supporting its Second Affirmative Defense that the award resolved the claim (Request No. 38), Defendant objected that the request is irrelevant because the award resolved the claim.

**Why the discovery bears on Defendant's motion.** Whether the September 12, 2025 award resolved the claim depends on what the appraisal panel was asked to decide and what it decided. Defendant's production shows that this is disputed:

- On August 13, 2025, Defendant told both appraisers that "coverage … may not be addressed in appraisal," and asked that any award be "itemized … each item," based on an "itemized estimate" (FIE 1221–1222).
- Defendant's appraiser later wrote that "the damage on the front windows in the dining room was from the prior claim" (FIE 1639).
- The award lists two windows under one figure, identified only as "F1.7" and "B1.2."

Defendant has not produced its appraiser's "report" (Answer ¶ 36), the appraisal vendor's "closing docs" (FIE 1633), any document from the umpire, or any document identifying the windows designated "F1.7" and "B1.2." Its response to Request No. 39 states that such documents "have been produced."

**The stay request.** On October 23, 2026, Defendant amended its motion to seek, in the alternative, summary judgment under Rule 56, and it continues to ask that discovery be stayed. The motion will be heard January 6, 2027. Discovery closes March 15, 2027, and Plaintiffs' expert disclosures are due the same day. A stay until a decision, which may come up to 90 days after submission (Minn. Stat. § 546.27, subd. 1(a)), could extend past the close of discovery. Defendant should not obtain summary judgment on facts while withholding discovery of those facts. *See* Minn. R. Civ. P. 56.04.

   - serve a privilege log under Rule 26.02(f)(1);
3. Decide Defendant's request to stay discovery now. If the Court is inclined to stay any discovery, Plaintiffs ask that the stay exclude the items above and that each Scheduling Order deadline be extended by the length of the stay; and
4. If the matters are not resolved on the call, grant Plaintiffs leave to file a motion under Rules 36.01 and 37.01, to be heard on or before January 6, 2027.
