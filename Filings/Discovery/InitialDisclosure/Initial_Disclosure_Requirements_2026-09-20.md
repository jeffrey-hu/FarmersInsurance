## 2. Required content — what Rule 26.01(a)(1) actually demands

Verbatim, then what it means for this case.

### (A) Individuals

> "the name and, if known, the address and telephone number of each individual likely to have discoverable information — along with the subjects of that information — that the disclosing party may use to support its claims or defenses, unless the use would be solely for impeachment"

Four required elements per person: **name**, **address if known**, **telephone if known**, **subjects of that information**.

- The trigger is "**may use to support**." It is not "will call at trial," and it is not limited to friendly witnesses. An adverse witness whose testimony Plaintiffs affirmatively rely on — Brau on his dent observations, Grimm on the "Damaged – No" tagging, Tomes on his non-signature — is squarely inside the rule.
- The only carve-out is a witness used **solely** for impeachment. Anyone with a dual use is disclosed.
- "Subjects of that information" is **per individual**. A shared subject line covering several people does not answer the rule as to any of them.
- "If known" is a real limit, but it is measured against what Plaintiffs actually have. Contact details sitting in the project files are known.

### (B) Documents, ESI, and tangible things

> "a copy — or a description by category and location — of all documents, electronically stored information, and tangible things that the disclosing party has in its possession, custody, or control and may use to support its claims or defenses, unless the use would be solely for impeachment"

- **Production is optional.** "A copy — **or** a description by category and location." Either satisfies the rule.
- **Location is required** if the description route is taken. A single blanket location sentence is the thin end of compliance.
- "Tangible things" and "electronically stored information" reach beyond paper — audio recordings, native spreadsheets, photo sets, text-message threads.
- Same solely-for-impeachment carve-out as (A).

### (C) Damages computation

> "a computation of each category of damages claimed by the disclosing party — who must also make available for inspection and copying as under Rule 34 the documents or other evidentiary material, unless privileged or protected from disclosure, on which each computation is based, including materials bearing on the nature and extent of injuries suffered"

This subsection carries **two** obligations, and the second is easy to miss:

1. A computation **for each category** claimed; and
2. An affirmative duty to **make available for inspection and copying** the material each computation rests on.

What a "computation" means in practice — Minnesota's rule tracks Fed. R. Civ. P. 26(a)(1)(A)(iii) nearly word for word, and the federal case law is the best guide available:

- A **category label plus a number is not a computation.** Courts want the method: what was counted, at what unit price, with what multipliers and credits.
- **Lump sums have been held insufficient** where the party did not show how the figure was derived.
- **Pointing at a pile of documents is insufficient** — citing hundreds of pages without showing the arithmetic does not discharge the duty.
- **"Expert will address it later" is insufficient** as to categories computable now.
- The carve-out "**unless privileged or protected from disclosure**" is the textual hook for the work-product question about the analytical spreadsheets. Note what it does and does not do: it excuses producing *protected* backup material; it does **not** excuse the computation itself.

### (D) Insurance agreements

> "for inspection and copying as under Rule 34, any insurance agreement under which an insurance business may be liable to satisfy all or part of a possible judgment in the action or to indemnify or reimburse for payments made to satisfy the judgment."

Directed at judgments **against the disclosing party**. With no counterclaim pleaded, nothing is responsive for Plaintiffs, and the Farmers policy is the subject matter of the suit, not indemnity against a judgment. A short "none" answers it.

### The standard that governs all four — Rule 26.01(a)(5)

> "A party must make its initial disclosures based on the information then reasonably available to it. A party is not excused from making its disclosures because it has not fully investigated the case **or because it challenges the sufficiency of another party's disclosures or because another party has not made its disclosures.**"

Two practical consequences:

- "To be computed" and "to be determined" are not compliance for anything computable today. Where a figure genuinely is not available, the disclosure should state the **method**, the **basis**, and a **supplementation date** — that is a good-faith limit on present knowledge, which is what the rule contemplates.
- Plaintiffs' obligation is **independent**. It does not matter what FIE serves, or whether FIE serves at all, or whether its disclosures are adequate.

## 3. Format — what the document must look like

### 3.1 What the rules require

**Minn. R. Civ. P. 26.01(d) — Form of Disclosures:**

> "Unless the court orders otherwise, all disclosures under Rule 26.01 must be in writing, signed, and served."

Three requirements, no more: **written**, **signed**, **served**. There is no prescribed form, no verification, no notarization.

**Minn. R. Civ. P. 26.07(a) — Signing:**

> "Every disclosure under Rule 26.01(a) or 26.01(c) and every discovery request, response, or objection must be signed by at least one attorney of record in the attorney's own name — or by the party personally if self-represented — and must state the signer's address, e-mail address, and telephone number."

And the certification the signature carries, for a disclosure:

> "…with respect to a disclosure, it is complete and correct as of the time it is made"

Both Plaintiffs are parties and both are self-represented, so **both sign**, and each block must carry **address, e-mail address, and telephone number**. Note what the certification is and is not: it certifies completeness and correctness **as of the time made** — not that the investigation is finished.

**Minn. R. Civ. P. 5.04(b) — not filed:**

> "…disclosures under Rule 26, expert disclosures and reports, depositions upon oral examination and interrogatories, requests for documents, requests for admission, and answers and responses thereto shall not be filed unless authorized by court order or rule."

The disclosures are **served, never filed**. Rule 5.04(c)(3) separately authorizes the court administrator to reject an unauthorized discovery document, which is a second reason not to send it to the court.

**Is a certificate of service required?** Strictly, no. Read Rule 5.04(b) from the top:

> "All documents after the complaint required to be served upon a party, together with a certificate of service specifying the details of how and when service was accomplished and signed under oath or penalty of perjury by the person effecting service, **shall be filed with the court** within a reasonable time after service…"

The certificate is an attachment to a **filing**. The same subdivision then excepts Rule 26 disclosures from filing altogether. A document that is never filed never triggers the certificate requirement. **Include one anyway**: it is the contemporaneous proof of service, and a Rule 37.03(a) fight two years from now turns on being able to prove the date.

## 4. Service — who, how, when

**Who.** Counsel of record, not the party and not the registered agent. Kennedy Law Firm P.C. appeared July 28, 2026. Minn. R. Civ. P. 5.02(a): service "shall be made upon the attorney unless service upon the party is ordered by the court." Serve **kkennedy@kennedylf.com** and **mbaskfield@kennedylf.com**.

**How.** Email, PDF attachment, per the parties' September 14, 2026 agreement (`KeyDecisions.md` Decision 4). Authority: Minn. R. Civ. P. 5.02(b) and Minn. Gen. R. Prac. 14.03(d)(2) — where parties who are not E-Filing System users agree to electronic service, "service may be made in the agreed upon manner."

**When complete.** Gen. R. Prac. 14.03(e): service by an agreed electronic means is **complete upon transmission**. There is **no failed-delivery exception** — a bounce or a spam folder does not un-serve the document.

**The day count, stated precisely.** This is a deadline to *make* a disclosure by a date, not a period that runs *from* service, and the distinction matters:

- **Timeliness of Plaintiffs' service** depends only on transmitting on or before **September 25, 2026**. Rule 6.01(e)'s 5:00 p.m. provision adds a day to a *prescribed period* running from service; it does not shorten a fixed calendar deadline. Transmitting at 8:00 p.m. on 9/25 is still service on 9/25.
- **Serve well before 5:00 p.m. anyway.** It removes any argument, and it keeps the habit consistent with the discovery sets, where the 5:00 p.m. line genuinely moves FIE's response date.
- **Running the rule the wrong way round on an incoming deadline costs three days that do not exist.** The +3 days in Rule 6.01(e) is for **U.S. mail only**. Email adds nothing.
- Rule 6.01(a) rolls a deadline falling on a weekend or holiday to the next business day. September 25, 2026 is a **Friday** — no roll.

**Record it.** Add a row to `Filings/Discovery/Service_Log.md` with the exact transmission time, and keep the sent message with full headers.

## 5. What to know before serving

### 5.1 The sanction that makes this worth doing properly

**Minn. R. Civ. P. 37.03(a):**

> "If a party fails to provide information or identify a witness as required by Rule 26.01 or 26.05, the party is not allowed to use that information or witness to supply evidence on a motion, at a hearing, or at a trial, unless the failure was substantially justified or is harmless."

Exclusion is **automatic and self-executing** — no motion is needed to trigger it; a motion is needed to *escape* it. The court may additionally order payment of expenses and fees, inform the jury of the failure, or impose Rule 37.02 sanctions. A witness or a damages category left out today is presumptively unusable at summary judgment and at trial.

### 5.2 The continuing duty

**Rule 26.05** requires timely supplementation whenever a disclosure is learned to be "incomplete or incorrect" in a material respect. Two things follow:

- A stated supplementation date is a commitment in a document certified under Rule 26.07. **Do not promise a supplementation that has already been decided against** — an unkept commitment is worse than none.
- Calendar the triggers on the day of service, not later.

### 5.3 The expert dates are not what the earlier files assume

The September 8 Scheduling Order set, at ¶ 3(a), **Plaintiffs' expert disclosures and reports due March 15, 2027** — the same day discovery closes (¶ 2), and roughly **six months from now**. Defendant's are due April 17, 2027 and rebuttal May 14, 2027.

This supersedes the September 24, 2027 date that Plaintiffs had proposed and that the September 6 review and Proposed-Changes P-3C still reference. It matters here in two ways: the Reservation paragraph should not recite a date the Order did not adopt, and any category deferred to "after expert valuation" is being deferred into a window that is much shorter than the earlier files assume.

For completeness, the later disclosure obligations this triggers: **Rule 26.01(c)** pretrial disclosures are due at least **30 days before trial** (the trial block opens September 20, 2027), and Rule 26.05's expert-supplementation duty runs to the date those pretrial disclosures are due.

### 5.4 Independent obligation

Rule 26.01(a)(5) removes every reciprocity argument. Serve complete disclosures on September 25 regardless of what FIE does. If FIE's disclosures are thin, the remedy is a Rule 37 motion after a good-faith conferral — and under Scheduling Order ¶ 4, that route runs through a **three-page letter and a conference call with the Court before any formal motion**, not straight to a motion.

### 5.5 Posture

FIE's Answer pleads three boilerplate affirmative defenses and **no limitations defense**. A precise, fully sourced, on-time disclosure from pro se plaintiffs is worth more than it looks: the contested fights coming — Brau's missing inspection notes, the full 2020 Xactimate and photo log, the umpire's file — are credibility contests, and credibility is built before it is spent.

### 5.6 Two judgment calls the rule text frames but does not answer

- **Produce copies, or describe by category?** Rule 26.01(a)(1)(B) permits either. But note that (C) independently requires making the damages backup "available for inspection and copying." Describing under (B) while withholding under (C) is not an option.
- **Are the analytical spreadsheets disclosable?** The test is use, not label: if Plaintiffs **may use** them to support a claim — e.g. as Rule 1006 summaries — they are disclosable under (B), and (C)'s "unless privileged or protected" carve-out does not reach material a party intends to use affirmatively.

## 7. Pre-service checklist

**Content**

- [ ] Every individual carries name, address, telephone **and an individual subject line**
- [ ] Adverse and third-party witnesses included where their testimony will be used affirmatively
- [ ] First-name-only entries resolved, or the disclosure states expressly that the surname is unknown
- [ ] Section B describes **category and location**, and includes ESI and tangible things (audio, native spreadsheets, photo sets, texts)
- [ ] Every damages category shows its **method and arithmetic**, including deductible and credits
- [ ] No "to be determined" on anything computable today; where deferred, method + basis + supplementation date
- [ ] Backup material for each computation identified and available for inspection under Rule 34
- [ ] Section D answered

**Form**

- [ ] In writing, signed by **both** Plaintiffs, each block carrying address, e-mail and telephone (Rules 26.01(d), 26.07(a))
- [ ] Signature blocks **side by side**; page setup and indentation per the formatting standard
- [ ] Rule 26.07 certification paragraph present
- [ ] Certificate of service in the **canonical email form**, with § 358.116 declaration and date/county/state
- [ ] All drafter's notes, brackets and placeholders removed
- [ ] `.docx` rebuilt and the `.md` twin regenerated from it; **rendered to PDF and checked for blank pages**

**Service**

- [ ] PDF exported; both signature and certificate dates filled with the actual service date
- [ ] Sent to kkennedy@ and mbaskfield@kennedylf.com from bill_y_lu@yahoo.com, on or before **September 25, 2026**, before 5:00 p.m.
- [ ] Sent message retained with full headers; row added to `Service_Log.md` with the exact time
- [ ] **Not filed with the court**
- [ ] Rule 26.05 supplementation triggers calendared
