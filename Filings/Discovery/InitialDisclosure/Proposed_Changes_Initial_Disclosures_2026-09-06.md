### Why the table separates Defendant's employees from Defendant's vendors

Being **hired by** Farmers is not the same as being **represented by** Farmers' counsel, and the difference has two practical consequences.

**Who "through Defendant's counsel" is accurate for.** Minn. R. Prof. Conduct 4.2 bars a lawyer from communicating about the matter with a person known to be represented by another lawyer. Comment [7] extends that to an organization's constituents who supervise, direct, or regularly consult with the organization's lawyer, who can obligate the organization, or "whose act or omission in connection with the matter may be imputed to the organization." That describes Dudley, Brau, Monson, the three post-appraisal correspondents, and the Rule 30.02(f) designee — Farmers employees.

**It does not describe Hancock Claims Consultants or Prieve Engineering LLC.** Those are separate businesses that Farmers retained. Their people are not Farmers' constituents, and Farmers' counsel does not represent them unless counsel has actually appeared for them. Writing "through Defendant's counsel" for Grimm or Prieve would assert a relationship that may not exist.

**The consequence that actually bites is procedural.** A party's officers, directors, managing agents, and Rule 30.02(f) designees are deposed on **notice** under Rule 30. Everyone else is a **non-party**, reachable only by **subpoena under Rule 45**. Mislabeling Grimm or Prieve as party-side witnesses invites noticing a deposition that never happens because no subpoena issued. Plaintiffs' own deposition plans already have this right — `OffenseStrategies/Deposition/2.1Prieve_Nathan.md` and `3.2Grimm_Jayden.md` both record "Third-party (subpoena under Minn. R. Civ. P. 45)." The disclosure should match.

**Three related notes:**

- **Peter Pietila** is moved to the third-party group. His own agency address in Wayzata points to an independent agency rather than captive employment, and `3.1Pietila_Peter.md` records his status as "likely independent agent." Confirm the agency relationship in discovery; if he turns out to be a Farmers employee, this moves back.
- **Greg Tomes** is no longer listed as "through Defendant's counsel." `2.4Tomes_Greg.md` records him as "likely independent contractor; subpoena under Rule 45 (or party-deposition notice if Farmers stipulates)." His address needs supplementing.
- **Allen Banning** turns on whether he is *still* employed. Comment [7] to Rule 4.2 states expressly that "[c]onsent of the organization's lawyer is not required for communication with a **former** constituent." If Banning has left Farmers, he is contactable directly and is a Rule 45 witness. Worth establishing early — a former adjuster who is outside the no-contact rule is a materially different witness from a current one.

**A point specific to your posture.** Rule 4.2 binds *lawyers*. Comment [4] to the rule recognizes that "[p]arties to a matter may communicate directly with each other." As pro se parties you are not lawyers, so the no-contact rule constrains Farmers' counsel more than it constrains you. That said, contacting an adverse party's personnel directly is a decision worth discussing with an attorney before acting on — the analysis is not as simple as the rule's text, and mishandling it creates problems that are hard to undo.

### The neighbors come out — and so does the placeholder row

**Deleted from Section A entirely** (per your decision of September 6, 2026):

- The existing row reading "Neighboring homeowners in the immediate Eden Prairie neighborhood (approximately 14, from Plaintiffs' canvass) — identities and addresses to be supplemented"

**Why this is permissible.** Rule 26.01(a)(1)(A) reaches only individuals "that the disclosing party **may use to support its claims or defenses**." Plaintiffs are not required to identify witnesses they have decided not to call. Omission on a considered decision is what the rule contemplates; it is not a gap.

**Why it is also sound.** Complaint ¶ 20 offers four independent disproofs of the Prieve report's "most recent hail in Eden Prairie reported in 2020" premise. The canvass, subparagraph (c), is both the weakest and now the most redundant of the four:

| ¶ 20 | Proof | Status |
|---|---|---|
| (a) | National Weather Service / NOAA documentation of the July 13–14, 2024 storm | Documentary; no witness needed beyond a records custodian |
| (b) | Stormersite data showing hail near Eden Prairie in 2022, 2023, 2024 | Documentary |
| (c) | **Neighbor canvass — 14 of 19** | **Dropped as substantive proof** |
| (d) | Defendant's own September 12, 2025 appraisal award finding hail as the cause of loss | Binding; Defendant admits the award and the hail finding in its Answer ¶¶ 31–32, 48 |

Add the post-September 13, 2021 downspout dents — a dent on a component that did not exist before late 2021 — and hail at *this Property* after 2020 is established by Defendant's own documents. Testimony that hail struck *other* houses is a weaker version of a point already carried.

**The evidentiary problem it avoids.** A neighbor's statement that hail caused their damage is generally relayed from their own adjuster or contractor — hearsay, and lay opinion on causation under Minn. R. Evid. 701. The City permits themselves record only work type, address, contractor, and dates; they state **no cause** (`OffenseStrategies/KeyFacts/key-facts-excluding-legal-context.md` § 3 flags this expressly). The "Hail Damage: Yes" column is Plaintiffs' own canvass annotation, not a City field. Sponsoring fourteen — or even two — lay witnesses to prove a point already made by Defendant's own binding award trades a strong record for a contestable one.

**What is preserved, deliberately:**

- **The City of Eden Prairie records custodian stays in Section A**, and the permit records stay in Section B item 10. The permits remain objective public evidence that post-2020 window replacement occurred on the same street. What is dropped is homeowner testimony about *cause*, not the public record of the *work*.
- **The canvass document stays in Section B item 9** — for a different purpose. See **P-3A**.

**The risks, stated plainly:**

1. **Rule 37.03(a)** bars using an undisclosed witness. Mitigated, not eliminated: Rule 26.05 supplementation remains available up to the close of fact discovery (your proposed June 30, 2027). If Defendant's expert affirmatively testifies that no hail struck this neighborhood after 2020, that development is itself a substantial justification for adding the neighbors then.
2. **Complaint ¶ 20(c) still pleads the canvass.** The mismatch between the pleading and the disclosure is visible. It is not a defect — you may prove ¶ 20 through (a), (b) and (d) — but be ready to say so if asked.
3. **Omission does not conceal them.** If Defendant serves an interrogatory asking who has knowledge of hail in the neighborhood, you answer truthfully. This decision governs whether **you** may call them, not whether Defendant learns of them.

**Recommended handling: omit, and say nothing about it.** Rule 26.01 does not require a party to account for witnesses it is not calling, and a sentence such as "Plaintiffs do not presently intend to use neighbor testimony" only hands Defendant a quotable line. Silence preserves the Rule 26.05 path without conceding anything.

## P-5. Replace the introductory paragraph and the table

Replacement cost stays the primary measure. Actual cash value is added **in the alternative**, without conceding the measure.

### Proposed introductory paragraph

> Plaintiffs compute their damages by category as set forth below. The Policy provides replacement-cost coverage. Defendant has made no payment toward actual cash value. The Policy's period for claiming replacement-cost settlement runs "from the date of our first payment toward actual cash value," and has therefore not begun to run; Defendant's denial of the claim and its failure to pay any amount have also prevented Plaintiffs from repairing or replacing the damaged property. Plaintiffs accordingly state their computation at replacement cost value, and state actual cash value in the alternative and without conceding that recovery is limited to actual cash value. The computation is based on information reasonably available at this time and will be supplemented after expert valuation and the completion of discovery. The documents on which each computation is based are among those identified in Section B and are available for inspection and copying as under Minn. R. Civ. P. 34.
