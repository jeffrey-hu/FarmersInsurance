### 1.1 The $0 siding and debris lines ⚠️ *highest value in this pass*

**What FIE did.** Complaint ¶31 pleaded, in detail, that the award records "Hail" in the form's "Loss Cause" field **and in a per-item "Causation" column**, and that the panel listed a **"Siding & Paint"** line and a **"Debris"** line — *each likewise marked "Hail"* — at **$0 RCV and $0 ACV**.

FIE's answer admits the three appraisers' identities and that Roos and Foster signed. **On the causation column, the siding line, the debris line, and the $0 values it says only: "The Appraisal Award speaks for itself."** Not admitted, not denied.

**Why this is the biggest miss.** This is the single strongest structural fact in the case, and the project knows it. From `OffenseStrategies/AppraisalAwardStrategy/00_Overview.md`: a $0 award on an item the panel **expressly found hail-caused** is by definition an *amount or coverage* determination, not a causation finding. Under *Quade* the hail finding binds FIE on the siding; the $0 is a coverage call the Court decides de novo. It is pleaded at Complaint ¶31, ¶50, ¶57(j), and ¶61.

**And the RFA set does not mention siding once.** RFA 4 covers only *"hail as the cause of loss for the two windows it awarded."* A-1 through A-14 add forty-two more requests and none reaches the siding line, the debris line, or the causation column. Exhibit C (the award) is already attached and authenticated by RFA 34 — so these are pure, free, documentary admissions against a document FIE itself calls binding.

**Recommended — four requests:**

> Admit that The Appraisal Award contains a column captioned "Causation."
>
> Admit that The Appraisal Award marks the "Siding & Paint" line item "Hail" in the Causation column and states a Replacement Cost Value of $0 and an Actual Cash Value of $0 for that line item.
>
> Admit that The Appraisal Award marks the "Debris" line item "Hail" in the Causation column and states a Replacement Cost Value of $0 and an Actual Cash Value of $0 for that line item.
>
> Admit that The Appraisal Award does not state the actual value and the loss separately for siding repaint and for siding replace-and-remove, the two siding items identified in Plaintiffs' August 3, 2025 Demand for Appraisal.

**Why each answer helps.** FIE has pleaded that the award is binding and "speaks for itself" — it is in a poor position to deny what the document plainly shows, and Exhibit C ends the argument. An admission on the second request converts Complaint ¶57(j) and ¶61 from allegation to established fact, and it does so from FIE's own binding award. The fourth request is the Count III itemization argument applied to the siding, which the first pass's ¶33 interrogatory does not reach because that interrogatory is directed at the *windows*.

*Drafting note:* quote the award's captions exactly as they appear on Exhibit C before serving. A caption mismatch is how RFA 18 became deniable (see A-13).

### 1.2 FIE denied that hail damaged the residence — in the paragraph where it admitted the award found hail

**What FIE did.** The answer to ¶2, in sequence:

> "FIE **denies** that Plaintiffs' residence was damaged by hail on July 13, 2024. FIE **admits** that a binding appraisal award was entered on September 12, 2025 ('Appraisal Award'), which **identified hail as the cause of loss for two damaged windows**."

Two sentences apart, FIE denies hail damage to the home and admits a binding determination of hail damage to two of its windows. ¶32 repeats the admission; Affirmative Defense 2 makes the award's binding force FIE's own defense.

**What the sets do.** RFA 4 asks FIE to admit **what the award says** ("identified hail as the cause of loss"). Proposed Request 66 asks FIE to admit it claims no **exclusion** for those two windows. Neither asks FIE to admit **the fact** — that hail caused the damage. FIE's ¶2 denial is a denial of the fact, and nothing in three sets makes it defend that.

**Recommended — two requests:**

> Admit that hail caused direct physical damage to the two windows identified as "F1.7" and "B1.2" in The Appraisal Award.
>
> Admit that the date of loss for The Claim is July 13, 2024.

The first is the one that matters. FIE cannot deny it while asserting Affirmative Defense 2 without holding two positions at once in the same proceeding — and a Rule 36 denial, unlike a pleading denial, carries a Rule 37.03 fee consequence if the matter is later proved. The second closes a gap nobody has noticed: **FIE has never admitted the loss date**, having pleaded only the policy's effective dates everywhere the date appears.

### 1.3 ¶28 — FIE says it *did* respond to the July 11, 2025 email ⚠️

**What FIE did.** Complaint ¶28 alleges that Plaintiffs' same-day July 11, 2025 follow-up — confirming their understanding that the only applicable deadline was the two-year suit limitation — "drew no response or correction from Mr. Brau or anyone else at Farmers."

> **ANSWER:** "FIE admits that on July 10, 2025, Lu emailed FIE and that on July 11, 2025 Mr. Brau responded to Mr. Lu via email. … **FIE denies that it did not respond to Plaintiffs' July 11 email.**"

**Why it matters.** This is an affirmative denial of a negative — structurally the same find as the first pass's "September 4, 2024 correspondence," but stronger, because FIE volunteered it rather than misdating something. Either:

- a responsive communication exists that Plaintiffs have never seen — which would bear directly on the waiver/estoppel allegations of Complaint ¶29 and on whether FIE ever identified a deadline; or
- FIE is treating **Brau's July 11 reply to the July 10 email** as its response to the *July 11 follow-up*, which would be a denial that rests on conflating two emails one day apart.

Either answer is useful, and the second is useful in a way FIE will not enjoy explaining.

**Recommended — one request for production:**

> Every communication from You to Plaintiffs, and every claim-system entry, sent or created after 11:59 p.m. on July 11, 2025 and on or before August 3, 2025, responding to or concerning Plaintiffs' July 11, 2025 email about claim deadlines, together with all documents identifying its author and the date and manner of its transmission. **If You contend that a communication sent on or before July 11, 2025 constitutes Your response to Plaintiffs' July 11, 2025 email, identify that communication by date, time, author, and recipient. If no responsive communication exists, state so expressly.**

The identification clause is the working part. RFP 1 and RFP 20 would sweep the document in silently if it exists; only this makes FIE say which document it meant when it filed that denial.

### 1.4 ¶38 — the rear-garage window

**What FIE did.** Complaint ¶38 describes a two-sash fixed window on the rear elevation of the attached garage, obstructed by a shed, absent from all 217 photographs of Appendix A, and alleges on information and belief that **no one acting for FIE has ever inspected it** and that no record of it exists.

> **ANSWER:** "FIE is without sufficient information or knowledge to form an opinion on the truth of paragraph 38, and the same is therefore denied."

FIE pleaded no knowledge of **whether its own claim file contains a record of a window on the insured dwelling.** That plea is difficult to sustain and belongs on the §6 meet-and-confer list of the first pass.

**What the sets do.** Nothing. As set out in §0.2, Interrogatory 1 is limited by its own terms to windows Appendix A depicts, and this window's entire significance is that Appendix A depicts it nowhere. RFP 6 (all native Prieve photographs, not limited to Appendix A) would catch a photograph if one exists, but nothing asks the question the Complaint actually pleads.

**Recommended — two admissions and one request:**

> Admit that no photograph in the Prieve Report's Photo Appendix A depicts the window on the rear elevation of the attached garage located immediately south of the garage window depicted at photographs A-213 through A-217.
>
> Admit that Your claim file for The Claim contains no photograph, inspection record, measurement, or evaluation of that window.
>
> *(RFP)* All documents concerning any inspection, photograph, measurement, or evaluation, by You or by any person acting for You, of the window on the rear elevation of Plaintiffs' attached garage located immediately south of the window depicted at Prieve Photo Appendix A photographs A-213 through A-217, at any time from October 22, 2014 through the present. If no such document exists, state so expressly.

The second admission, if made, establishes Complaint ¶49(a)(ii) outright: an insured window that no one ever evaluated cannot have been within the panel's authority under *Quade*, whatever FIE says about the award's preclusive reach.

*Drafting note:* describe the window by its relationship to A-213 through A-217 rather than by compass direction. The Complaint's own text uses "rear elevation," "south of," and "north of" in ways that can be read against each other; anchoring to a photograph number FIE's own engineer assigned removes the ambiguity FIE would otherwise use to deny.

### 1.6 ¶21 — "incomplete and inaccurate" is a contention, not a denial

**What FIE did.** Complaint ¶21 pleads that the two denials rest on incompatible theories: September 3 says no exterior damage exists; November 4 acknowledges damage and blames aging.

> **ANSWER:** "FIE denies paragraph 21 **and alleges that paragraph 21 is incomplete and inaccurate in its description of the September 3, 2024 letter and the November 4, 2024 letter.**"

"And alleges" is FIE's own affirmative pleading. Like the ¶14 Hancock allegation that Interrogatory 16 was built to test, this is something FIE volunteered and must be able to support — and unlike ¶14, nothing in any set asks for the particulars.

An interrogatory would be the natural device, and the budget in §0.1 forecloses it. Two admissions get most of the value from the documents themselves, both of which are authenticated (Exhibits A and B, RFA 32 and 33):

> Admit that Your September 3, 2024 letter did not cite any exclusion, limitation, or other provision of The Policy as a basis for the claim outcome it communicated.
>
> Admit that Your November 4, 2024 letter states, "Our investigation found the condition of the window and components to be from wear/tear."

Together these are the incompatibility: one letter that found nothing to exclude, and one that excluded what the first letter said was not there. Whatever "incomplete and inaccurate" means, it has to survive both.

*If I-24 frees more room than expected,* the interrogatory version — "state each respect in which You contend paragraph 21 is incomplete or inaccurate" — is the better instrument and should displace the second-priority uses of that room.

### 1.7 Affirmative Defense 1 — the jurisdiction half is unpinned

Proposed Requests 41–47 (A-6) are a well-built defense-universe lock: limitations, contractual suit limitation, mitigation, setoff, conditions precedent, misrepresentation, and a catch-all. They cover every defense FIE *might* add.

**They do not cover the one FIE actually pleaded.** Affirmative Defense 1 asserts, in a single sentence, "there exists a **lack of jurisdiction over Defendant FIE** and Plaintiffs have failed to state a claim." Request 47's catch-all is expressly limited to defenses "other than the defenses asserted in Your Answer" — so it excludes this one by its own terms.

FIE is a licensed Minnesota insurer that issued a policy on a Minnesota home, admitted in ¶5 that it is "licensed to conduct business in Minnesota," and answered ¶¶6–8 with "conclusion of law, no response required." The defense is boilerplate. Pinning it is free and removes a motion.

**Recommended — two requests:**

> Admit that this Court has personal jurisdiction over You in this action.
>
> Admit that venue is proper in Hennepin County.

These pair naturally with the three Jie Hu standing requests in first-pass §4, which address the *other* half of Affirmative Defense 1. Serve them together as one block.

### 1.8 FIE will not call the September 3 letter a denial

**The pattern.** Across the whole Answer, FIE never once describes the September 3, 2024 letter as a denial. ¶18: "FIE admits that it sent **correspondence**." ¶53: "FIE's September 4, 2024 **claim outcome correspondence**." Contrast ¶20, where FIE freely admits it provided the Prieve report "as a basis for **its denial** of Plaintiffs claim" — FIE uses the word for November 4 and avoids it for September 3.

**But** ¶56 alleges FIE "has **denied coverage twice in writing**," and FIE's answer denies only the first sentence (justiciable controversy) and **"admits the remainder."** The first pass flagged ¶56 as the most quotable admission in the Answer; the connection to the studied avoidance is the part worth acting on.

**Recommended — one request:**

> Admit that Your September 3, 2024 letter communicated a denial of coverage for The Claim.

If admitted, the CFA count's opening chapter (Complaint ¶69(a)) has a denial to attach to. If denied, FIE has contradicted its own ¶56 admission on the record — and the September 3 letter, already Exhibit A, is in evidence to settle it.

A second, related admission is worth adding to the A-14 block, which already establishes that no engineer inspected before September 3:

> Admit that a justiciable controversy exists between You and Plaintiffs concerning coverage under The Policy for the July 13, 2024 loss.

FIE denied that sentence. It also demanded a jury trial on it.

## 2. ¶69 — a drafting error in a signed pleading, and why not to touch it

FIE's answer to Complaint ¶69, which catalogs the alleged CFA conduct in eight subparagraphs, reads:

> "FIE denies paragraph 69 **and alleges that the statements made in paragraphs 69(a) through (h) constitute 'prohibited conduct' under Minn. Stat. 325F.69, subd. 1.**"

As written, FIE affirmatively alleges that its own enumerated conduct **constitutes prohibited conduct under the Consumer Fraud Act.** A negation is plainly missing — the sentence was meant to say FIE denies that those statements constitute prohibited conduct, and the surrounding sentences ("FIE denies paragraphs 69(a) through (h) to the extent a response is required") confirm it.

**Recommendation: do not put this in a discovery request.** An RFA quoting it back costs nothing in the budget but everything in effect — it tells Kennedy Law Firm exactly what to fix, and a Rule 15.01 amendment or a Rule 36.02 withdrawal is routine and would almost certainly be allowed. The error is worth more sitting where it is, in a signed pleading under the Minn. Stat. § 549.211 acknowledgment, available for summary-judgment briefing or cross-examination if FIE ever argues its position on Count IV was carefully considered.

**Recorded here so that nobody "discovers" it later and drafts the request.**

## 4. Not a discovery item: Rule 9.03 and conditions precedent

Complaint ¶44 pleads performance of all conditions precedent. ¶47 repeats it. FIE's answers:

- **¶44:** admits three inspections, the sworn proof of loss, and the appraisal demand; pleads no knowledge of the remainder; denies the remainder "to the extent a response is required."
- **¶47:** "Paragraph 47 alleges a statement or conclusion of law to which no response is required in this Answer."

**Minn. R. Civ. P. 9.03** permits a general averment of performance but requires that "[a] denial of performance or occurrence shall be made **specifically and with particularity**." FIE's answer identifies no condition, no failure, and no facts. Proposed **Request 45** already asks FIE to admit it makes no such contention — which is the right discovery move and is already in the pipeline.

What is *not* in the pipeline is the pleading point: if FIE later raises a notice, proof-of-loss, or cooperation defense, Rule 9.03 and its own non-denial are the answer, and its ¶44 admissions (three inspections allowed, proof of loss submitted, appraisal demanded) supply the affirmative case. **Add to the SJ response playbook**, alongside the Rule 8.02 material in first-pass §6.
