**The definition as it stands:**

> **"Defendant," "Farmers," "FIE," "You," and "Your"** mean Fire Insurance Exchange, its attorney-in-fact Fire Underwriters Association, and each of their present and former agents, adjusters, employees, representatives, attorneys, third-party administrators, and any other person acting or purporting to act on their behalf.

## 1. Bottom line

The definition is **well sourced** — unlike the Photo Appendix A clause, its factual premise is documented on the face of Defendant's own policy. Its problem is not accuracy. It is that **one defined term is doing two incompatible jobs**, and the collision falls squarely on the twelve requests that matter most to Defendant's First Affirmative Defense.

- **Job 1 — scope.** For documents and knowledge, you want the term as broad as possible: everything held by anyone acting for the Exchange. The definition does this well.
- **Job 2 — identity.** For the entity questions, you need to know *which legal entity* did what. Here breadth is the enemy: a term that means "the Exchange and everyone acting for it" lets Defendant answer collectively and never name an entity.

**One fix carries most of the value** (§3.1): a single sentence defining the spelled-out name "Fire Insurance Exchange" to mean the Defendant entity alone. Without it, RFAs 5–16 — the entire block captioned *"Identity and capacity of the acting entity"* — can be read as asking about the group they were written to distinguish.

Five smaller items follow, then two findings that came out of reading the policy itself and are not about the definition at all (§5) — including one that looks like the cleanest answer to the First Affirmative Defense in the file.

## 2. What checks out

**The attorney-in-fact premise is documented, not assumed.**

- The policy's execution block reads: **"Fire Insurance Exchange / by Fire Underwriters Association / Attorney-in-Fact"** (`HomePolicy/2014_newBusiness_policy.pdf`, final page of the Next Generation Homeowners form).
- The Subscription Agreement Notice delivered with the policy states that the insured "appoint[s] Fire Underwriters Association (the 'Association') to act as the Attorney-in-Fact," and the 2023 renewal repeats it (`HomePolicy/2023_Renewal.pdf`).
- Farmers' own 2026 member communication for Fire Insurance Exchange confirms FUA remains the attorney-in-fact today, performing policy issuance and premium collection for a fee of 11.9% of premiums in 2025.

That matters because **Defendant did not admit the relationship.** Answer ¶5 admits only that "Defendant is a reciprocal insurance exchange licensed to conduct business in Minnesota" and dismisses the rest of the paragraph — the attorney-in-fact allegation, the Farmers Insurance trade name, the registered agent — as "statements and conclusions of law to which no response is required." The definition asserts what the Answer withholds. It is entitled to: the policy proves it. But see §3.2 — asserting it is not the same as making Defendant answer for FUA.

**Other things I checked and would not change:**

- **"Present and former"** is correct and load-bearing. Brau, Monson, Dudley, Miller and Bell may not all still be there.
- **"Person" is defined to include entities** in the Interrogatories and RFPs, so the closing catch-all reaches corporate affiliates — Farmers Group, Inc., a sister exchange, a claims-servicing affiliate — not merely individuals. This is what saves the definition from missing the entity that actually employs the claim staff. *(But see §3.3 — this is not true of the RFA set.)*
- **"Attorneys" in the definition** was already identified and cured in two of the three sets by Instruction 8. *(Prior finding — `RFP_First_Set_Review_2026-09-08.md` §3.1 and item I-30.)* §3.3 addresses the set where it was not.
- **A subject-matter limiter on the persons clause** — e.g. "acting on their behalf *in connection with The Policy, The Claim, or the appraisal*" — I considered and **rejected**. It would narrow the proportionality objection surface slightly, but it would also cut the corporate author of a training manual or claims-handling guideline out of "You," and RFPs 12, 13 and 51 depend on reaching exactly those people. Each request carries its own scope limits. Leave the persons clause open.

## 3. Issues, ranked

### 3.1 ⚠️ CRITICAL — "Fire Insurance Exchange" spelled out is not a defined term, and "FIE" means something else

RFAs 5 through 16 sit under the heading *"B. Identity and capacity of the acting entity,"* with a note tying them to the First Affirmative Defense. Every one of them deliberately uses the full name rather than a defined term:

> **RFA 5:** Admit that **Fire Insurance Exchange** issued and underwrote The Policy.
> **RFA 7:** Admit that Nicholas Brau was employed or engaged by **Fire Insurance Exchange** at the time of his September 6, 2024 inspection…
> **RFA 13:** Admit that **Fire Insurance Exchange** maintains the claim file for The Claim.
> **RFA 16:** Admit that Fire Underwriters Association acts as attorney-in-fact for **Fire Insurance Exchange** pursuant to a written instrument.

The drafting intent is unmistakable and correct: pin the acting entity by name, because that is the fact the joinder deadline turns on. **But the Definitions section defines "FIE" as the whole group** — the Exchange, its attorney-in-fact, and everyone acting for either. Defendant can argue, without straining, that "Fire Insurance Exchange" spelled out is simply the long form of the defined "FIE," and that RFA 7 therefore asks whether Brau was employed by *anyone in the Farmers organization* — which Defendant can admit while telling you nothing.

The same ambiguity runs through **Interrogatory 12** ("if You contend that any entity other than Fire Insurance Exchange should have been named") and **RFPs 52 and 53**, which ask for the entity that employed each person and the entity that appointed Pietila's agency.

**Fix — one sentence, appended to the definition, count effect 0:**

> **"Fire Insurance Exchange," used alone and spelled in full, means that entity only, and does not include its attorney-in-fact, any affiliate, or any other person within the definitions above.**

This costs nothing, changes no request, and converts twelve requests from arguable to airtight.

### 3.2 HIGH — nothing requires Defendant to say when it is answering only for itself

Defendant did not admit the attorney-in-fact relationship. The predictable response is a general objection — *"Defendant objects to the definition of 'You' to the extent it includes Fire Underwriters Association and other non-parties; Defendant responds only on its own behalf"* — followed by answers that look complete and are silently narrowed. You would not know which answers were affected.

The RFPs already solve the document version of this problem at **Instruction 10** (possession, custody or control; "identify the person who has it"). The information and admission versions are unprotected.

**Fix — add a parallel instruction to each set, count effect 0:**

> *Interrogatories / RFAs:* **Answering on behalf of others.** If You do not answer on behalf of every person within the definition of "You," state which persons You have excluded, and identify, as to each answer affected, the person who has the information withheld on that basis. Where an interrogatory or request asks which person or entity performed an act, made a decision, or sent a communication, identify the specific legal entity by name; attributing the act to "Farmers," "Defendant," or "the Exchange" collectively is not a complete answer.

That second sentence is the one that matters before **November 16, 2026**.

### 3.3 HIGH — the RFA set is missing two things the other two sets have

The three sets share the same definition, but not the same scaffolding around it.

| | Interrogatories | RFPs | **RFAs** |
|---|---|---|---|
| "Person" defined to include entities | ✅ | ✅ | ❌ **absent** |
| Counsel-of-record carve-out (Instruction 8) | ✅ | ✅ | ❌ **absent** |

**Consequence of the first.** The RFA definition of "You" ends with "any other **person** acting or purporting to act on their behalf" — and in that set "person" is undefined, so it carries its ordinary meaning of a natural person. Corporate affiliates, Hancock Claims Consultants and Prieve Engineering, LLC arguably fall outside "You" **in the RFA set only**, while being inside it in the other two. That is precisely backwards: RFAs 11, 12, 35 and 49 all turn on conduct by retained entities.

**Consequence of the second.** The RFA definition of "You" includes "attorneys" with nothing excluding Kennedy Law Firm. The practical exposure is smaller than it was for the RFPs — an admission request is not a document demand — but it is a free rhetorical opening of exactly the kind item I-30 was written to close, and Defendant's response can lead with it.

**Fix:** port the "Person" definition and a one-line counsel carve-out into the RFA set. Count effect 0 — neither is a request.

### 3.4 MEDIUM — "purporting to act" is a free qualification in the RFA set

Under Rule 36.01 a denial "must fairly meet the substance of the requested admission," and a party may qualify where good faith requires. A request whose subject is "You" — defined to include *persons who merely purport* to act for the Exchange — invites a qualified answer that is technically proper and practically useless: *"Defendant admits only as to itself; it can neither admit nor deny as to persons purporting to act on its behalf, whose conduct is unknown to it."*

"Purporting" earns its place in the Interrogatories and RFPs, where it captures a person Defendant may later disclaim. In a set where **any matter not properly answered is deemed admitted**, and where the goal is to force a clean yes or no, it hands back the very leverage Rule 36 provides.

**Fix (optional, RFA set only):** drop "or purporting" from the RFA definition. The other two sets keep it.

### 3.5 MEDIUM — RFA 21 asks the wrong person to be bound

> **RFA 21:** Admit that The Appraisal Award is binding upon **You** as to the matters it determined.

An appraisal award binds the parties to the appraisal. With "You" defined as the Exchange plus its attorney-in-fact plus every agent, adjuster, attorney and third-party administrator, the request as written asserts that the award binds a list of non-parties — and Defendant gets a clean, honest denial of a request that should have been unanswerable. Note that Defendant's **Second Affirmative Defense affirmatively alleges the award "is binding upon the parties"**: this is a request it has already conceded in its own pleading, and it should not be losable on a defined term.

**Fix:** *"Admit that The Appraisal Award is binding upon Defendant Fire Insurance Exchange as to the matters it determined."*

RFAs 9 and 10 have a milder version of the same mixed register — subject named in full, possessive left as "Your." They work, but *"the September 3, 2024 letter"* would be tighter, and the Letter Package is already a defined term.

### 3.6 LOW–MEDIUM — the discovery definition of "Farmers" does not match the Complaint's

Complaint ¶5 pleads: *"Defendant Fire Insurance Exchange (**'Defendant' or 'Farmers'**) is a reciprocal insurance exchange organized under California law…"* — so in the pleading, **"Farmers" means the Exchange alone**. In discovery, the same shorthand means the Exchange plus its attorney-in-fact plus everyone acting for either.

Nothing prohibits different definitions in different documents, and each set defines its own terms. But it is an argument handed over for free — *"Plaintiffs' own Complaint defines 'Farmers' as Fire Insurance Exchange; their discovery silently expands it to sweep in non-parties"* — and it costs nothing to avoid. If §3.1 is adopted, this largely resolves itself: the full name means the entity, the shorthand means the group, and the distinction becomes deliberate rather than accidental.

## 4. The one factual gap in the definition: there are two attorneys-in-fact in this file

The definition names **Fire Underwriters Association** as *the* attorney-in-fact. Defendant's own documents name two, for the same policy, in the same year.

| Document | Exchange named | Attorney-in-fact appointed | Compensation | "Acting since" |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **2014 Application for Insurance and Subscription Agreement** (form 31-7153 1-14) — *the instrument the insured signed* | **Farmers** Insurance Exchange | **Farmers** Underwriters Association | 20% of premium | 1928 |
| **2014 policy packet, "Subscription Agreement Notice"** — *binding "by payment of the policy premium"* | **Fire** Insurance Exchange | **Fire** Underwriters Association | 25% of premium | 1942 |
| **2023 Renewal** | Fire Insurance Exchange | Fire Underwriters Association | — | — |

Both 2014 documents bear **Policy No. 98055-49-65**, and the declarations on both read **"Underwritten By: Fire Insurance Exchange."** The application contains only the Farmers version; there is no Fire Insurance Exchange subscription agreement anywhere in the signed application.

This is not a drafting quibble. **General Condition 15 (Reciprocal provisions) provides that "[i]n any suit against us, service of process shall be upon the Attorney-in-Fact as shown in your Subscription Agreement."** The Subscription Agreement the insureds actually signed shows Farmers Underwriters Association. If the First Affirmative Defense — *"there exists a lack of jurisdiction over Defendant FIE"* — rests on service or on capacity, Defendant is relying on a provision that its own forms made ambiguous, against insureds who signed the version Defendant printed for them.

**Recommendations:**

1. **Add Farmers Underwriters Association to the definition**, in the alternative: *"…its attorney-in-fact Fire Underwriters Association (and, to the extent it acted as attorney-in-fact for The Policy, Farmers Underwriters Association)…"* Count effect 0. This forecloses an answer that disclaims knowledge because the wrong association was named.
2. **RFP 34 already requests** "all documents evidencing the attorney-in-fact relationship… including the subscription agreement and power of attorney applicable to Policy 98055-49-65." No change needed — but it is now the most important clause in that request, and worth watching on the meet-and-confer list.
3. **RFA 16 already asks** Defendant to admit FUA acts as attorney-in-fact pursuant to a written instrument. Consider a companion: *Admit that the Application for Insurance and Subscription Agreement for The Policy appoints Farmers Underwriters Association as attorney-in-fact.* It is proved by Defendant's own signed form.

## 5. Two findings from the policy that are not about the definition

Both came out of reading General Conditions 11 and 15 while checking the attorney-in-fact premise. Neither is presently in any set.

### 5.1 The policy says the Exchange may be sued in its own name

> **General Conditions Applying to the Entire Policy, ¶15 (Reciprocal provisions):** *"We may sue or be sued in our own name, as though we were an individual, if necessary to enforce any claims which arise under this policy."*

Defendant's First Affirmative Defense asserts a lack of jurisdiction over Fire Insurance Exchange. Defendant's own policy form says the Exchange may be sued in its own name. This is a document Defendant drafted, delivered, and charged premium for.

**Recommended RFA:** *Admit that The Policy provides, under General Conditions Applying to the Entire Policy, "We may sue or be sued in our own name, as though we were an individual, if necessary to enforce any claims which arise under this policy."*

A quoted-text admission of Defendant's own form is about as close to unrefusable as Rule 36 gets, and Minnesota places no numerical cap on requests for admission.

### 5.2 The policy contains a Minnesota choice-of-law clause

> **General Conditions ¶11 (What law will apply):** *"This policy is issued in accordance with the laws of the state in which the residence premises is located… The laws of the state where the residence premises is located shall govern any and all claims or disputes in any way related to this policy."*

Relevant to the same affirmative defense, and to any argument that California organization matters. Also worth pairing with Answer ¶5's single admission — that Defendant "is a reciprocal insurance exchange **licensed to conduct business in Minnesota**" — which Defendant volunteered.

Nothing in any of the three sets currently addresses licensure, transaction of business in Minnesota, or either of these provisions. Under Minn. Stat. § 71A.02, subd. 3, a reciprocal's attorney must file with the Commissioner an instrument consenting to service of process under Minn. Stat. § 45.028, subd. 2 — a filing that exists, is obtainable, and is inconsistent with a jurisdictional defense.
