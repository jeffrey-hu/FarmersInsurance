## Bottom line

This is a strong set. It is better targeted than most first sets: it goes after the three things that actually decide this case — **what the panel evaluated**, **what FIE's own file shows about the 2020 claim**, and **how the Prieve report got written** — and it does so with requests that are specific enough to enforce.

Three things keep it from being finished:

1. **It has a hole where the valuation case should be.** Every request is aimed at causation and claims handling. Not one asks for an estimate, a price list, a depreciation schedule, or the derivation of the $6,400 ACV. FIE can lose the causation fight window-by-window and still win the case by keeping covered ACV under $17,720 — and right now you have served nothing that would let you attack that number.
2. **Four requests seek non-party files (Prieve, Hancock, Roos, Tomes) as if FIE held them.** Rule 34 reaches only what is in FIE's possession, custody, or control. As written, these hand FIE a clean, non-frivolous objection to your four most important document sets. The fix is small: reframe them, and serve Rule 45 subpoenas in parallel.
3. **Four requests (9, 11, 12, 28, 33) are the ones FIE will build its protective-order motion around.** They are defensible — Count IV pleads institutional practice — but not in their current form.

Nothing here is fatal. The edits below are a couple of hours of work and they materially change how the first round of objections goes.

## 1. Timing and posture — you can serve these now

**You are past the discovery moratorium.** Minn. R. Civ. P. 26.04(a) bars discovery before the parties have conferred and prepared a plan under Rule 26.06(c). The parties conferred on **August 28, 2026**, and both sides submitted proposed plans (FIE's on September 1, yours on September 4). Rule 26.04(b) independently allows Rule 34 requests to be delivered more than 21 days after service of the summons, and deems them served at the conference. Either way, service now is proper — you do not have to wait for the Scheduling Order.

**Serving before the Scheduling Order issues is the right call**, for a reason specific to your case: your own proposed schedule sets substantial completion of document production at **February 26, 2027**. A set served now produces responses in mid-October 2026, which leaves room for one full meet-and-confer cycle and a motion to compel while there is still time to use what you get. It also gets FIE's scope answers on the record before the summary-judgment motion your strategy file (Motion A) expects.

**Two mechanical points to keep straight.** Under Minn. R. Civ. P. 34.02, FIE must serve a **written response within 30 days**, but "[t]he production must then be completed no later than the time for inspection specified in the request or another reasonable time specified in the response." So a 31st-day non-production is not automatically a violation if FIE's response specified a later reasonable date — what you police on day 31 is the *response*, not the documents. Second, Rule 34.02 requires that "[a]n objection must state whether any responsive materials are being withheld on the basis of that objection." That sentence is your best friend on a motion to compel, and Instruction 4 does not currently invoke it. Add it.

**Discovery is served, not filed.** The certificate of service correctly cites Minn. R. Civ. P. 5.04, which does exclude discovery requests and responses from filing. That is right as written.

## 3. Compliance and form — six fixes before you serve

### 3.1 The definition of "You" sweeps in litigation counsel

The definition of "Defendant," "Farmers," "FIE," "You," and "Your" includes "attorneys." Combine that with Request 1 ("every document ... and internal communication of any kind") and Request 29 ("[a]ll internal Farmers communications referring to The Appraisal Award ... through the date of production") and you have, on the face of the requests, demanded FIE's communications with Kennedy Law Firm about this lawsuit.

FIE will not produce them, correctly. What it will do is use the overreach as the framing for its whole response — and you will spend the first meet-and-confer defending something you never wanted.

**Fix.** Add an instruction: no request seeks documents protected by the attorney-client privilege or Minn. R. Civ. P. 26.02(d), and communications solely between FIE and its counsel of record in this action dated after July 8, 2026 need not be produced **or logged**. You give up nothing (you were never getting those), and you remove FIE's best rhetorical card while narrowing the privilege log to the period that matters — the claim-handling period, where Minnesota's work-product doctrine is unusually favorable to you (§5).

### 3.2 Four requests demand non-party files

Requests 4 (Hancock), 5 (Prieve), 16 (Roos), and 17 (Tomes) ask for "the complete file" of entities and individuals who are not parties. Rule 34.01 reaches documents "in the responding party's possession, custody, or control." FIE will answer that it does not have Prieve's field notes or the umpire's working file, and that answer will be true.

**Fix — and it is a two-part fix:**
- Reframe each as: *All documents constituting, reflecting, or relating to [X] that are in Your possession, custody, or control, including all documents You sent to or received from [X]. If You contend any responsive document is not within Your possession, custody, or control, identify the document and the person who has it.*
- Serve Rule 45 subpoenas in parallel. You already have `Shell_Subpoena_Duces_Tecum_to_Prieve.docx` drafted, and `05_AppraisalSpecific_Discovery_Deposition.md` already lists subpoena targets for Roos, Tomes, Foster, and Pure Construction. Serve them at the same time as this set, not after.

For **Request 16 specifically**, the reframing is not a concession — it is the point. If FIE answers that it holds no document from the umpire, that answer *is* your *Herll* / *Rymer* exhibit: a binding award with no methodology behind it. Ask the question in the form that produces a useful answer either way.

### 3.3 Instruction 1's time period contradicts your own requests

Instruction 1 sets the period at **January 1, 2020 through the present**. But Request 22 asks for the **2014 and 2015** claim inspection records, Request 24 asks for **drafting history** of endorsements that predates 2020, and the underwriting file you should be requesting (§6.A) goes back to policy inception on **10/22/2014**. The "unless a particular request specifies otherwise" carve-out probably saves Request 22, but "probably" is how you end up briefing it.

**Fix.** Change Instruction 1 to: *January 1, 2014 through the present as to The Policy, the underwriting file, and all prior claims at The Property; January 1, 2020 through the present as to all other requests, unless a particular request specifies otherwise.*

### 3.4 Interrogatory-shaped demands inside a document set

Instruction 6 requires FIE, for missing documents, to "identify it, describe its disposition (including the date, manner, and authorization of any destruction), and identify each person who has had custody of it." Request 3's second sentence and Request 14's list of attributes do the same thing. These ask for **narrative answers**, which Rule 34 does not compel — Rule 33 does.

This matters because the spoliation record around Brau's September 6, 2024 notes may be the most valuable thing in the case, and you do not want the answer to arrive as an objection about the wrong vehicle.

**Fix.** Keep the instruction (it is standard and it costs nothing), but make sure the substance is compelled through interrogatories. Interrogatory 10 already covers the search for Brau's records — good. You still have interrogatory capacity — but count it carefully before you spend it. Minn. R. Civ. P. 33.01 caps interrogatories at 50 and provides that "each subdivision of separate questions shall be counted as an interrogatory." Your first set is numbered 1–17, but Interrogatories 7 and 17 each carry four lettered subparts, and several others (5, 6, 9, 10, 11, 12) are compound. A strict count is closer to **23**, and FIE could argue higher. You are still well under the cap, so add one to the interrogatory set: *For each document responsive to any Request for Production that You contend no longer exists, identify the document, the date and manner of its destruction or loss, the person who authorized it, the retention policy under which it occurred, and each person who had custody of it.* That is one interrogatory doing the work of Instruction 6, with a rule behind it.

### 3.5 Place and manner of production

The preamble directs production "at 17756 George Moran Dr., Eden Prairie, Minnesota 55347." Rule 34.01 does require a reasonable time, place, and manner, so this is not wrong — but a claim file, an audit trail, and 217 native photographs are not going to arrive at your house, and specifying your home invites a pointless exchange about logistics.

**Fix.** Specify electronic production as the primary method (secure file transfer or encrypted media delivered by mail), with inspection at a mutually convenient location as the alternative. Keep Instruction 5's native-format-with-metadata demand — it matches paragraph 3 of your proposed discovery plan, and Request 6 depends on it. Note that if the Court does not adopt your ESI paragraph, the Rule 34.02 default is "a form or forms in which it is ordinarily maintained or in a reasonably usable form," which is weaker than what you asked for. That is an argument to make sure paragraph 3 of your plan gets into the Scheduling Order.

### 3.6 Two housekeeping items

- **Delete the bracketed note before Request 33.** The italic "[ Optional — delete this note before serving ... ]" paragraph is drafting scaffolding. Serving it tells FIE you already believe the request is overbroad, and it will be quoted back at you in the opposition brief.
- **Fill the blanks.** The Dated line, the telephone and email lines, and the certificate-of-service blanks. Your Discovery Plan V5 signature block has the phone numbers and emails for both plaintiffs — use the same ones so the record is consistent.

**One instruction to add:** *If You object to a request in part, produce all documents responsive to the unobjectionable portion, and state whether any responsive materials are being withheld on the basis of each objection, as Minn. R. Civ. P. 34.02 requires.*

## 5. Work product — FIE's blanket objection will be wrong, but know the limits of the point

FIE's first response will characterize much of the claim file as work product. There is a real argument against that in Minnesota, but it is narrower than it first looks, so state it precisely.

**Minnesota labels only opinion material "work product."** *Dennie v. Metropolitan Medical Center*, 387 N.W.2d 401, 406 (Minn. 1986): "'Work product' is defined as an attorney's mental impressions, trial strategy, and legal theories in preparing a case for trial. … However, materials prepared in anticipation of litigation that do not contain opinions, conclusions, legal theories, or mental impressions of counsel are not work product and are discoverable under Minn. R. Civ. P. 26.02[(d)]."

**But read the next sentence too.** *Dennie* continues: "Discovery of trial preparation materials requires of the party requesting them a showing of substantial need and inability to obtain the information by other means." So "not work product" is a **labeling** point, not a free-discovery point — non-opinion trial-preparation materials still require the substantial-need showing. Do not tell the Court that Minnesota is "narrower than federal law"; federal Rule 26(b)(3) has the same two levels under different names, and FIE will read *Dennie*'s next sentence back at you. The usable point is this: a blanket "the claim file is work product" objection misstates the standard, and FIE must separate opinion material (log it) from everything else (produce it, or make the substantial-need argument request by request).

**The stronger argument is the ordinary-course-of-business one.** "[E]ven though litigation is already in prospect, there is no work product immunity for documents prepared in the regular course of business rather than for purposes of litigation." *City Pages v. State*, 655 N.W.2d 839, 846 (Minn. Ct. App. 2003). Whether a document was prepared in anticipation of litigation is a **factual** determination. An adjuster's inspection notes, a vendor's inspection report, a claim-system entry, and a coverage evaluation exist because a claim came in — Requests 1, 3, 4, 20, 21, 30, and 32 all sit in that zone. (Note for briefing: *City Pages* is a Data Practices Act case about law-firm billing records, not an insurance-claim-file case. The rule it states is general; it is not Minnesota authority specifically on insurer claim files.)

**Employee witness statements are discoverable as of right.** Under Minn. R. Civ. P. 26.02(d) (formerly 26.02(3)), a statement of "a person who is not a party" is discoverable without the substantial-need showing — and the statement of a corporate defendant's employee who is not himself named as a party is a non-party statement. *Leer v. Chicago, M., St. P. & P. Ry.*, 308 N.W.2d 305, 307 (Minn. 1981); *Wiggin v. Apple Valley Medical Clinic, Ltd.*, 459 N.W.2d 918, 920 (Minn. 1990). Brau, Monson, Dudley, and Pietila are FIE employees, not parties. (*Wiggin* was a divided court and involved no privilege or work-product assertion — cite it for the non-party point only.)

**FIE bears the burden.** "[T]he party objecting to the production of information has the burden of establishing that the sought-after information is immune from discovery." *In re Comm'r of Pub. Safety*, 735 N.W.2d 706, 712 (Minn. 2007). (The objection there was proprietary/trade-secret rather than privilege, so cite it for the general burden allocation.) Combined with the Rule 26.02(f) log your Instruction 4 already invokes, that means FIE has to itemize what it withholds and why.

*(All five citations above were checked against Justia/CourtListener/FindLaw texts. Pull the reporters yourself before any of this goes into a brief.)*

## 7. Two statutory wrinkles that change how you use what you get

**§ 604.18, subd. 4(c) is FIE's best defense to the bad-faith claim, and it is already in play.** The statute provides: "An award of taxable costs under this section is not available in any claim that is resolved or confirmed by arbitration or appraisal." FIE's Answer and Affirmative Defense #2 assert precisely that the appraisal resolved this claim. That is not only a merits defense to Counts I–III — it is a jurisdictional-style bar to the bad-faith claim you have reserved. Interrogatory 13 already targets it, which is good drafting. Addition **F** above is the document half of the same attack, and it should be in this set rather than a later one, because you will need this record for the motion to amend, and your proposed Scheduling Order puts that deadline at November 1, 2027.

**§ 604.18, subd. 4(d) limits what you can do with Request 18 and with § 72A.201.** The statute makes four categories inadmissible "in any proceeding that seeks taxable costs under this section," including "(2) allegations involving, or results of, investigations, examinations, or administrative proceedings conducted by the Department of Commerce," "(3) administrative bulletins or other informal guidance published or disseminated by the Department of Commerce," and "(4) provisions under chapters 59A to 79A and rules adopted under those sections ... as standards of conduct."

Two consequences:

1. **Request 18 (Commerce Complaint ID 93547) is properly discoverable but has a ceiling.** Its value is as party admissions and inconsistent statements usable on Counts I and IV — what FIE told a regulator versus what it told you. Do not build the bad-faith presentation on it.
2. **Minn. Stat. § 72A.201 is chapter 72A, which falls inside "chapters 59A to 79A."** Your `FIE_Answer_Analysis` and `FIE_Defense_Strategy` files both lean on § 72A.201's claim-file documentation duty, and your Discovery Plan V5 subject list does too. That theory is fine as a *relevance* argument for why the audit trail and Brau's notes are discoverable, and fine as a spoliation argument under Rule 37. It is expressly **not** available as a standard of conduct in the §604.18 proceeding. Worth correcting in those files before the argument gets built into a brief.

Every one of those moves converts a future objection into a negotiation, and — if FIE refuses — into a much better record for a motion to compel.
7. **Calendar day 31.** Note the Rule 34.02 distinction from §1: what you enforce on day 31 is the written response and the Rule 34.02 statement of what is being withheld, not the documents themselves.
