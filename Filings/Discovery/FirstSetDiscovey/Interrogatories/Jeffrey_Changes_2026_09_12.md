## Change 1 — Interrogatory 7 rewritten in place (Brau's September 6, 2024 inspection records)

**New text:**

> **INTERROGATORY NO. 7:** State whether Nicholas Brau created any record of any kind of his September 6, 2024 inspection of The Property — including handwritten notes, sketches or drawings, measurements, photographs, voice memos, and claim-system or portal entries made on or after that date — and for each such record state its type, the date created, its present location and custodian, and, if it no longer exists or cannot be located, when and how it ceased to exist or became unlocatable and who authorized or caused that. Then state the basis for Your October 10, 2025 statement that "the file does not contain a report on the requested date," and identify the search performed to locate records of the inspection, the custodians and systems searched, the date of the search, and the person who performed it.

**Why.** The prior version asked only about the basis for FIE's October 10, 2025 "no report" statement and the search FIE performed. That let FIE answer truthfully — "we searched and found nothing" — without ever saying whether Brau wrote anything down, which is the fact a spoliation argument (Rule 37 sanctions or an adverse-inference instruction) actually needs. The Complaint at ¶16 alleges he took notes and drew windows with dimensions in a paper notebook in front of the family, so a sworn "he made no notes" is contradictable by eyewitnesses, and a sworn "he made notes and the file has none" establishes the record existed and is gone. The rewrite puts the existence question first and lists the record types, because "report" is a narrow word and an adjuster's field notes, sketches, and claim-system entries are not a "report." The duty-to-preserve element is already pleaded through Minn. Stat. § 72A.201, subd. 9(3), so no further interrogatory is needed for that. Count goes from 1 to 2.

## Change 2 — New Interrogatory 15 (what FIE did with the three post-denial evidence submissions)

**New text:**

> **INTERROGATORY NO. 15:** For each of the following submissions by Plaintiffs — (i) the January 31, 2025 response and its attachments concerning post-2020 storm activity and gutter replacement; (ii) the February 10, 2025 second response containing City of Eden Prairie permit records and the neighbor canvass; and (iii) the May 28, 2025 National Weather Service and Interactive Hail Maps material delivered through agent Peter Pietila — state whether any person acting for You reviewed the submission on its merits, and if so identify that person, the date of the review, the conclusion reached, and each claim-file note or other document recording it; and if no such review occurred, state so and the reason.

**Why.** The Consumer Fraud Act count and the implied-covenant claim both rest on FIE refusing to review evidence that its denial was factually wrong, and FIE's Answer (¶27) denies that it failed to engage. Nothing in the set previously made FIE say under oath what happened to those three packages after they arrived, which is the fact the count turns on and the fact a jury will understand without reading a single policy exclusion. Interrogatory 6 asks FIE to list communications imposing an "engineer's report only" rule, and FIE's Answer (¶24) already previews the reply that no such rule existed, so that question invites a premise fight rather than information. This one is keyed to three dated deliveries FIE admits receiving, so "we make no such contention" is unavailable. Every possible answer is useful: "no one reviewed them" is the CFA case in one sentence; "X reviewed them and concluded Y" gives a witness, a date, and a conclusion to test against the claim-file notes from RFP 1. Counts as 1 (the three submissions are particulars of one question).

## Change 3 — New Interrogatory 16 (FIE's "adamantly advising Hancock" allegation)

**New text:**

> **INTERROGATORY NO. 16:** With respect to the allegation in Your Answer to paragraph 14 of the Complaint that Plaintiffs "attempted to direct the claim-handling process by adamantly advising Hancock Claims Consultants that the only thing that needed to be inspected was the windows on the inside where they were leaking," state: each person who made and each person who received that advice, the date and manner of the communication, and each document in which it was recorded at the time; whether the scope of Hancock's August 29, 2024 inspection was in fact limited as a result and, if so, which components of The Property were not inspected on that account; and whether You contend that this alleged advice was a basis for the statement in Your September 3, 2024 letter that "[t]here were no other damages observed."

**Why.** This is the only affirmative factual allegation FIE volunteered anywhere in its Answer, and it is aimed squarely at excusing the September 3, 2024 denial's "no other damages observed" finding, which is the opening chapter of the CFA count (¶69(a)). If the allegation is true it blunts that paragraph; if it is false, FIE put an invented fact in a signed pleading to excuse its first denial, which strengthens the count considerably. Because FIE pleaded it in its own words, it cannot answer "no contention" or "speaks for itself," and a Rule 33.02 deferral request is weakest for a fact FIE necessarily had a basis for on the day it filed. The middle clause is the one that can collapse the story on its own evidence: the Complaint says Hancock's photo set tagged the exterior — window wraps, siding, garage, downspouts — "Damaged – No," and an inspector told to look only at interior windows does not photograph downspouts. RFP 42 already seeks the documents; this gets the sworn facts, and a mismatch between the two is itself worth having before Grimm and Dudley are deposed. Counts as 1.

## Change 4 — New Interrogatory 17 (date FIE first anticipated litigation)

**New text:**

> **INTERROGATORY NO. 17:** State the date on which You contend You first anticipated litigation concerning The Claim, identify the person who made that determination and the event or communication that prompted it, and identify each category of document created after that date that You contend is protected from discovery on that basis.

**Why.** The claim file is the core of the case, and FIE's main tool for withholding it will be work-product protection under Minn. R. Civ. P. 26.02(d), which covers only material prepared in anticipation of litigation. An insurer's ordinary job is investigating and deciding claims, and notes made in the course of that job are not protected, so the whole fight reduces to a date: when did FIE stop merely handling the claim and start preparing for a lawsuit. Left alone, FIE will assert the earliest plausible date (the November 19, 2024 Commerce complaint is the obvious candidate) and withhold everything after it behind a privilege log. Making FIE commit to a date under oath before it produces anything prevents that, and an early date is hard to defend against ten more months of routine claim handling through the September 2025 appraisal and the October 2025 correspondence. The third clause ties any later privilege-log withholding to the sworn date so post-denial adjuster notes cannot be hidden under a generic "work product" label. Counts as 1.
