#### F-1a. The reservation sentence (raised by Bill, 9/17/2026)

**Status:** ✅ Applied 9/17/2026 — deleted.

The sentence read: "Plaintiffs reserve the right to serve additional interrogatories **by stipulation or upon leave of court**." Read literally, that says a Second Set needs FIE's agreement or a court order. It does not.

- **Verified, Minn. R. Civ. P. 33.01(a):** a party may serve up to **50 interrogatories** on another party, cumulative across all sets, and needs the court's permission only to go past that number ("unless permitted to do so by the court upon motion, notice and a showing of good cause"). With about 37 served here, a Second Set within the cap needs nothing from anyone.
- **Verified, Minn. R. Civ. P. 29:** the parties may by stipulation "modify other procedures governing or limitations placed upon discovery," so a stipulation is one route **past** the cap. But an extension of the time to respond under Rules 33, 34 or 36 may be stipulated only with the court's approval if it would interfere with a completion, hearing or trial date — and this case has a **3/15/2027** discovery deadline.
- **Risk of leaving it in:** it is the only sentence FIE could cite to argue that Plaintiffs conceded a Second Set requires its agreement. Small risk, but there is no benefit on the other side of it: the reservation gave Plaintiffs nothing the rule does not already give.

**Decision (Bill, 9/17/2026): delete it.** Nothing replaces it. If a Second Set beyond 50 is ever needed, the route is a Rule 29 stipulation or a Rule 33.01(a) motion at that time, and the absence of a reservation here does not affect it.

Rule 33.01(a), verified text: "In computing the total number of interrogatories **each subdivision of separate questions** shall be counted as an interrogatory." My own count of the current set is **about 37 on a fair reading**, so "fewer than thirty-five" is wrong. No rule requires a certification, so **delete the paragraph.** Keep only the reservation sentence: "Plaintiffs reserve the right to serve additional interrogatories by stipulation or upon leave of court."

### F-2. Int. 10 and § 604.18, subd. 4(c)

**Status:** ✅ Applied 9/17/2026 to the `.md`. The text below is what was served-ready as of that date, followed by the reasoning for the per-item form.

Verified: the § 604.18 remedy is not available for claims "**resolved or confirmed** by arbitration or appraisal." Answer ¶¶2, 34, and 48 and Affirmative Defense 2 describe the award as "binding," which is the language of a *confirmed* claim. As drafted, FIE can truthfully answer "we do not contend it *resolved* the claim" and leave the "confirmed" theory unaddressed.

Two more problems:
- "Every window the award is **intended** to cover" asks about subjective intent, and the panel's intent is not FIE's to state.
- The interrogatory covers only windows, although the appraisal demand also covered siding (Answer ¶30).

**Text as applied (9/17/2026):**

> **INTERROGATORY NO. 10:** State each fact supporting any contention that The Claim was "resolved or confirmed by arbitration or appraisal" under Minn. Stat. § 604.18, subd. 4(c); and, for each item submitted to or addressed in the appraisal — including windows "F1.7" and "B1.2," "Siding & Paint," and each item in Plaintiffs' August 3, 2025 appraisal demand — state whether You contend The Appraisal Award determined its actual value and loss, and identify the document supporting that contention.

**Second look, 9/18/2026 (R-10).** Two changes after reading the award itself: **"Debris" added** to the enumerated items — the award's item column has three lines (Siding & Paint; Windows F1.7 B1.2; **Debris**), and Debris shows $0 RCV / $0 ACV with "Hail" in the Causation column, the same oddity as siding — and "and identify the document supporting that contention" trimmed to "with the document supporting that contention." Count unchanged. Final text:

> **INTERROGATORY NO. 7:** State each fact supporting any contention that The Claim was "resolved or confirmed by arbitration or appraisal" under Minn. Stat. § 604.18, subd. 4(c); and, for each item submitted to or addressed in the appraisal — including "Windows F1.7 B1.2," "Siding & Paint," "Debris," and each item in Plaintiffs' August 3, 2025 appraisal demand — state whether You contend The Appraisal Award determined its actual value and loss, with the document supporting that contention.

**Why the § 604.18 half is kept even though the claim is not pleaded.** Subd. 4(a) means the claim arrives by motion to amend; subd. 4(c) is the bar FIE will raise in opposition. Getting its facts now means briefing that motion knowing the theory, rather than meeting it for the first time in opposition. Expect a prematurity objection — Rule 33.02 answers it, and deferral takes a court order.

**Count.** Fair count **2**, the same as the old version, so F-2 costs nothing. Two questions: the facts behind the contention, and the per-item position. FIE's aggressive count is about **3**, one *lower* than the two-list alternative discussed below, because the statutory disjunction sits inside a quotation and there is a single enumeration rather than two lists.

#### Why "for each item … state whether," rather than two lists (Bill's question, 9/17/2026)

A two-list form ("identify each item the award determined, and each it did not") was considered and rejected:

1. **Who defines the universe.** Two lists let FIE write two short lists and leave the awkward items off both — the dining-room window, the rear-garage window, individual sashes, the siding line. The omission would not show on the page. The applied form names the universe from FIE's own documents (the August 3, 2025 demand, which Answer ¶30 admits covered windows, siding repaint and siding R&R; the award's own line items; the two awarded windows), so a missing item is an **incomplete answer under Minn. R. Civ. P. 37.01(c)**, which is treated as a failure to answer.
2. **Silence becomes a stated position.** Count III needs FIE on record that the award did **not** determine particular items. The per-item form produces that item by item. A categorical question invites the "the award speaks for itself" answer FIE used throughout its Answer.
3. **It makes the aggressive answer untenable.** If FIE claims the award determined everything, it must say so about the garage window no one inspected (RFAs 80–81) and about the "Siding & Paint" line that shows Hail with $0 (RFAs 31–32).
4. **Counting.** Rule 33.01(a) counts "each subdivision of separate questions." Two labeled lists read as two subdivisions; one enumeration with one required answer reads as one.
5. **Objection resistance.** A bounded, sourced list is harder to attack as overbroad than "every item of The Property."

**Format.** The wording permits — and invites — a table in response, which is the format Plaintiffs want.

**Residual risks.** (a) FIE may argue that a "for each" question multiplies by the number of items; no Minnesota authority interprets "for each" constructions, so the universe was kept short and anchored. (b) FIE may object that a § 604.18 question is premature because subd. 4(a) means the claim is not pleaded until a motion to amend. The answer is that Complaint ¶3 reserves it and FIE has already signaled the subd. 4(c) appraisal bar by calling the award "binding" (Answer ¶¶2, 48; Affirmative Defense 2). No text change; expect the objection.

### R-17. Int. 14 (old 17): anticipation of litigation and the hold date

**Status:** ✅ Applied 9/18/2026. Count **0** (stays 2 fair). Paired with new **RFA No. 204**.

**Text as applied:**

> **INTERROGATORY NO. 14:** State the date You contend You first anticipated litigation concerning The Claim, identifying who made that determination and what prompted it; and state the date any litigation hold or preservation instruction concerning The Claim was issued, and to whom it was directed.

**Why it is a pincer.** Rule 26.02(d) protects only what was prepared "in anticipation of litigation or for trial," so FIE's chosen date bounds every work-product objection it can make. **A late date** (e.g. the July 2026 suit) means nothing in the claim file is work product — the Prieve engagement, claim notes and internal correspondence are all discoverable, and RFAs 52–53 become near-automatic. **An early date** (e.g. autumn 2024) means the preservation duty attached then, while RFAs 150–151 ask FIE to admit it had issued **no** hold as of 9/6/2024 and 11/4/2024 — anticipating litigation with no hold, against Brau's unaccounted-for notes, is the spoliation argument in one line.

**The swap.** Out: "identify each category of document created after that date that You contend is protected" — Instruction 4 and Rule 26.02(f) already compel a privilege log describing what is withheld, so the clause paid for what the rules require. In: the **litigation-hold date**, which nothing else reliably produces — RFP 14 asks for "documents sufficient to show" it, and if no hold exists there are no documents; RFAs 150–151 fix only two dates in 2024, so a hold issued in 2025 would escape both.

**Dodges and answers.** Work-product objection to the date itself — the date is foundational, not protected content, and it is what makes the objection assessable under 26.02(f). "Legal conclusion" — Rule 33.02. "At various times" or "on advice of counsel" — Instruction 13 and Rule 37.01(c). "See the privilege log to be served" — Instruction 3 as amended (I-9) requires FIE to say the answer rests on incomplete work and to supplement. Answering only the first clause — same.

**Does the "no protective order at this time" email affect this? (Bill, 9/18/2026) — No.** A Rule 26.03 protective order governs confidentiality and use; Rule 26.02(d) governs what need not be produced. Declining one waives no work product. What it does affect is the **form** of the 10/15/2026 production: with no order and no designation framework, the manuals (RFPs 10, 31), vendor and engagement files (RFPs 4, 5) and other-claims data (RFPs 9, 28) arrive unrestricted and Jeffrey Hu may review them. ⚠️ The "at this time" reservation is most likely to be exercised exactly when those responses come due — expect a proposed stipulated order, possibly with a claw-back clause, and treat it as a ¶4 conferral rather than an October fight.

**Paired RFA (No. 204, new section AA):** "Admit that no document in Your claim file for The Claim created before July 8, 2026 was prepared in anticipation of litigation or for trial within the meaning of Minn. R. Civ. P. 26.02(d)." RFA 52 reaches only The Prieve Report; nothing reached the rest of the pre-suit file. An admission makes the whole pre-suit file discoverable; a denial forces a privilege log and must be squared with the date given here. July 8, 2026 is the service date — a line FIE itself has treated as significant.

### R-4. Int. 2 (old 4): the Prieve scoping question

**Status:** ✅ Applied 9/17/2026. Count **0** (fair stays 2; FIE's aggressive count drops from ~5 to ~3). Paired with new **RFA No. 201**.

**Text as applied:**

> **INTERROGATORY NO. 2:** State how the scope of Prieve Engineering's engagement came to be limited to the condition of the windows, identifying when and by whom that scope was set, whether Prieve asked to expand it and with what result, and, if no document records the reason, the reason and each person with knowledge of it.

**Why the interrogatory is kept.** The windows-only engagement is the spine of two theories: under § 604.18 prong one (*Peterson*), a denial resting on an engineer never asked to look at the siding, downspouts, flashing or doors is not a full investigation that weighs all the facts; under Rule 702, a report whose own scope omits parts of the loss it was used to deny has a completeness defect — the garage-window problem one level up. RFP 7 gets the documents; only an interrogatory gets an answer when no document exists, and only FIE or Prieve knows whether **Prieve asked to widen the scope and was refused**.

**What changed and why.**

- **"that Plaintiffs had reported as damaged" deleted.** What Plaintiffs had reported *before* the 10/9/2024 inspection is not nailed down in the record (the damage document was handed to Prieve that day; the siding items appear in the 2025 appraisal demand), so the clause was a deniable premise attached to the whole interrogatory.
- **"Your decision to scope … and to exclude" → "how the scope … came to be limited."** The old wording presupposed an affirmative decision, letting FIE answer that none was made and the assignment simply followed the reported window leaks. The new wording captures a decision or a default.
- **Four enumerated particulars folded** into one clause.
- **"whether Plaintiffs were informed of it" dropped** — provable by absence and free at deposition.
- The no-document fallback is kept in compressed form, because FIE's likely answer is that nothing records the reason.

**Paired RFA (No. 201, new section X):** "Admit that You did not retain Prieve Engineering, LLC to evaluate the condition of the siding at The Property." RFA 58 already fixes the engagement as windows "as relates to hail," yet **Conclusion 4 of the report opines on the siding** — and the appraisal award carries a "Siding & Paint / Hail" line. The admission locks the overreach instead of leaving it to argument.

## 4. Instructions and form

> **Rule check, 9/18/2026 (I-2).** **Rule 26.05(a)**, verified verbatim: a party who has responded to an interrogatory "must supplement or correct its disclosure or response: (1) **in a timely manner** if the party learns that in some material respect the disclosure or response is incomplete or incorrect, **and if the additional or corrective information has not otherwise been made known to the other parties** during the discovery process or in writing; or (2) as ordered by the court." "Seasonably" is the old wording. The rule's own exception (information otherwise made known) is deliberately **not** recited in the instruction — that is FIE's defense to a supplementation demand. Anchoring the first sentence to Rule 26.05 also keeps the incomplete-search clause (I-9) inside what a complete answer requires under Rule 33.01(d), rather than appearing to impose a duty beyond the rules (Rule 26.07). **Note for the expert stipulation, not for these instructions:** Rule 26.05(b) extends the supplementation duty for a reporting expert to both the report and the deposition testimony, with changes due by the Rule 26.01(c) pretrial disclosures.
>
> **Rule check, 9/18/2026 (I-1).** Verified from two independent fetches of the Revisor's text. **Rule 33.01(d):** answers are "stated fully in writing and shall be signed **under oath or penalty of perjury** by the party served" or, for an organization, "**by any officer or managing agent**," and a party "shall **restate the interrogatory** being answered immediately preceding the answer." The Revisor's rendering also carries the declaration language — "I declare under penalty of perjury that everything I have stated in this document is true and correct" — with the date, county and state of signing. **Minn. Stat. § 358.116** confirms that form substitutes for notarization and that a knowing false statement is perjury under § 609.48.
>
> ⚠️ **One clause to pull from the printed rule before quoting it in a motion** (Scheduling Order ¶13): "who shall furnish such information as is available" appeared in one rendering of 33.01(d) and not in the other's condensed version. Instruction 2 **paraphrases** it ("who must furnish the information available to Defendant"), so nothing turns on the exact wording at service.
>
> **Why the officer-or-managing-agent branch applies:** a reciprocal insurance exchange is an unincorporated association of subscribers, and FIE's own Answer admits it is "a reciprocal insurance exchange." Verification by an adjuster or by counsel is not what the rule requires, and an improperly verified answer is, in practice, no answer under Rule 37.01(c).
