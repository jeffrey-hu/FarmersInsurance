#### F-1a. The reservation sentence (raised by Bill, 9/17/2026)

**Status:** ✅ Applied 9/17/2026 — deleted.

The sentence read: "Plaintiffs reserve the right to serve additional interrogatories **by stipulation or upon leave of court**." Read literally, that says a Second Set needs FIE's agreement or a court order. It does not.

- **Verified, Minn. R. Civ. P. 33.01(a):** a party may serve up to **50 interrogatories** on another party, cumulative across all sets, and needs the court's permission only to go past that number ("unless permitted to do so by the court upon motion, notice and a showing of good cause"). With about 37 served here, a Second Set within the cap needs nothing from anyone.
- **Verified, Minn. R. Civ. P. 29:** the parties may by stipulation "modify other procedures governing or limitations placed upon discovery," so a stipulation is one route **past** the cap. But an extension of the time to respond under Rules 33, 34 or 36 may be stipulated only with the court's approval if it would interfere with a completion, hearing or trial date — and this case has a **3/15/2027** discovery deadline.
- **Risk of leaving it in:** it is the only sentence FIE could cite to argue that Plaintiffs conceded a Second Set requires its agreement. Small risk, but there is no benefit on the other side of it: the reservation gave Plaintiffs nothing the rule does not already give.

**Decision (Bill, 9/17/2026): delete it.** Nothing replaces it. If a Second Set beyond 50 is ever needed, the route is a Rule 29 stipulation or a Rule 33.01(a) motion at that time, and the absence of a reservation here does not affect it.

Rule 33.01(a), verified text: "In computing the total number of interrogatories **each subdivision of separate questions** shall be counted as an interrogatory." My own count of the current set is **about 37 on a fair reading**, so "fewer than thirty-five" is wrong. No rule requires a certification, so **delete the paragraph.** Keep only the reservation sentence: "Plaintiffs reserve the right to serve additional interrogatories by stipulation or upon leave of court."

### F-2. Int. 10 and § 604.18, subd. 4(c)

**Status:** ✅ Applied 9/17/2026 to the `.md`. The text below is what was served-ready as of that date, followed by the reasoning for the per-item form.

Verified: the § 604.18 remedy is not available for claims "**resolved or confirmed** by arbitration or appraisal." Answer ¶¶2, 34, and 48 and Affirmative Defense 2 describe the award as "binding," which is the language of a *confirmed* claim. As drafted, FIE can truthfully answer "we do not contend it *resolved* the claim" and leave the "confirmed" theory unaddressed.

Two more problems:
- "Every window the award is **intended** to cover" asks about subjective intent, and the panel's intent is not FIE's to state.
- The interrogatory covers only windows, although the appraisal demand also covered siding (Answer ¶30).

**Text as applied (9/17/2026):**

> **INTERROGATORY NO. 10:** State each fact supporting any contention that The Claim was "resolved or confirmed by arbitration or appraisal" under Minn. Stat. § 604.18, subd. 4(c); and, for each item submitted to or addressed in the appraisal — including windows "F1.7" and "B1.2," "Siding & Paint," and each item in Plaintiffs' August 3, 2025 appraisal demand — state whether You contend The Appraisal Award determined its actual value and loss, and identify the document supporting that contention.

**Count.** Fair count **2**, the same as the old version, so F-2 costs nothing. Two questions: the facts behind the contention, and the per-item position. FIE's aggressive count is about **3**, one *lower* than the two-list alternative discussed below, because the statutory disjunction sits inside a quotation and there is a single enumeration rather than two lists.

#### Why "for each item … state whether," rather than two lists (Bill's question, 9/17/2026)

A two-list form ("identify each item the award determined, and each it did not") was considered and rejected:

1. **Who defines the universe.** Two lists let FIE write two short lists and leave the awkward items off both — the dining-room window, the rear-garage window, individual sashes, the siding line. The omission would not show on the page. The applied form names the universe from FIE's own documents (the August 3, 2025 demand, which Answer ¶30 admits covered windows, siding repaint and siding R&R; the award's own line items; the two awarded windows), so a missing item is an **incomplete answer under Minn. R. Civ. P. 37.01(c)**, which is treated as a failure to answer.
2. **Silence becomes a stated position.** Count III needs FIE on record that the award did **not** determine particular items. The per-item form produces that item by item. A categorical question invites the "the award speaks for itself" answer FIE used throughout its Answer.
3. **It makes the aggressive answer untenable.** If FIE claims the award determined everything, it must say so about the garage window no one inspected (RFAs 80–81) and about the "Siding & Paint" line that shows Hail with $0 (RFAs 31–32).
4. **Counting.** Rule 33.01(a) counts "each subdivision of separate questions." Two labeled lists read as two subdivisions; one enumeration with one required answer reads as one.
5. **Objection resistance.** A bounded, sourced list is harder to attack as overbroad than "every item of The Property."

**Format.** The wording permits — and invites — a table in response, which is the format Plaintiffs want.

**Residual risks.** (a) FIE may argue that a "for each" question multiplies by the number of items; no Minnesota authority interprets "for each" constructions, so the universe was kept short and anchored. (b) FIE may object that a § 604.18 question is premature because subd. 4(a) means the claim is not pleaded until a motion to amend. The answer is that Complaint ¶3 reserves it and FIE has already signaled the subd. 4(c) appraisal bar by calling the award "binding" (Answer ¶¶2, 48; Affirmative Defense 2). No text change; expect the objection.

#### Why the defense is quoted verbatim (Bill's question, 9/17/2026)

Affirmative Defense 1 reads, in full: "Affirmatively alleges that, pursuant to Rule 12, Minn. R. Civ. P., there exists a lack of jurisdiction over Defendant FIE and Plaintiffs have failed to state a claim upon which relief can be granted." Both grounds sit in one sentence, so one quotation reaches the whole defense and no paraphrase can be disputed. Keeping "pursuant to Rule 12" pins FIE to Rule 12.02 grounds, which makes "state each fact" a fair question — a jurisdictional defense is fact-dependent, so "purely legal" is a weaker objection than it would be against the failure-to-state half standing alone. Quoting also matches the rest of the set (Ints. 7, 8, 16) and the RFA sections B, T and U, so FIE cannot read the two sets as describing different defenses.

#### Can FIE refuse as "defense strategy"? (Bill's question, 9/17/2026)

No. **Verified, Minn. R. Civ. P. 33.02:** a contention interrogatory "is not necessarily objectionable merely because its answer involves an opinion or contention that relates to fact or the application of law to fact, but **the court may order** that such an interrogatory need not be answered until after designated discovery has been completed" — so deferral takes a **court order on FIE's motion**; FIE cannot self-defer. Work product under Rule 26.02(d) covers documents and tangible things and the mental impressions in them, not the underlying facts; privilege covers communications, not facts. FIE's Answer also carries the § 549.211 acknowledgment. A proper answer may say that the failure-to-state half asserts no facts and is directed at the pleading — which is useful, because it forecloses a later factual version. If FIE stonewalls: Rule 33.01(c) requires grounds stated with particularity, Instruction 9 requires it to say whether anything is withheld, and Rule 37.01(c) treats an evasive or incomplete answer as a failure to answer. On the party question, a refusal is itself the good-cause showing for a Rule 111.04 motion to extend the 11/16 joinder deadline.

The 9/15 meet-and-confer letter, item 5 (answer due 10/9), is the fastest route but is not compellable. **The decisive point is timing:** RFAs served now are answered about 10/19, so a follow-up interrogatory served 10/20 comes back 11/19 — three days after the joinder deadline. There is no second chance before 11/16.

**Why these three names.** Brau (the 9/6/2024 inspection, the 11/4/2024 denial, the missing notes, the 10/29/2025 email) and Monson (the supervisor behind the engineer rule) are the two that drive the joinder call. Dudley was kept because he signed the 9/3/2024 letter and the email that delivered it — the representation behind Consumer Fraud subparagraph (a) — and because a **catastrophe** representative with a 913 area code is the name most likely to come back with a *different* entity, which is the fact that would prove the multi-entity structure. ### F-4. Int. 1: source wording, attribution, and the rear-garage window

**Status:** ✅ Applied 9/17/2026 — Int. 1 rewritten at **no count cost**; the rear-garage window held as **H-1**; Instruction 13 added (**I-8**).

Int. 1 is limited to windows "that the Prieve Report's Photo Appendix A depicts as dented." RFA 80 establishes that Appendix A contains **no** photograph of the two-sash fixed window on the rear (east) garage elevation, so Int. 1 excludes that window by its own terms. The Complaint (¶¶38, 49) reserves this window to the Court as never evaluated.

Two related gaps:
1. **"Depicts as dented" invites a quarrel about captions.** RFAs 83–102 identify dents by the sentences of the Prieve Report. Use both the report and the appendix.
2. **The "prior claim" attribution is not pinned down.** The Prieve Report assigns dents to "a prior hail claim" and to windows "already included and paid in prior claims," in the plural. The 2014 claim belonged to the previous owner, and the 2020 scope covered two casement units. This is the core of the scope fight (`08_DiningRoomWindow…` §2.2). Make FIE name the claim and the payment.

**Text as applied (9/17/2026)** — fair count **3**, unchanged, so F-4 costs nothing:

> **INTERROGATORY NO. 1:** For each window other than "F1.7" and "B1.2" at which The Prieve Report or Photo Appendix A records a dent, mark, scrape, or indentation, state the cause and approximate date You attribute to it, the facts supporting that attribution (including, for any condition attributed to a prior claim, that claim's number and the estimate line item You contend paid for it), any policy exclusion or limitation You contend applies, and any portion of The Appraisal Award You contend determined the question for that window.

**How the additions stayed free.** The prior-claim question rides inside the existing "facts supporting that attribution" as a parenthetical particular, and the date rides on the existing cause question ("the cause and approximate date You attribute to it"). Neither is separately posed, so the three questions are what they always were: attribution, exclusions, award reliance.

**Wording choices.**

- "depicts as dented, marked, or otherwise damaged" → "**records** a dent, mark, scrape, or indentation." FIE can argue a photo set "depicts" nothing as damaged; the report's sentences and the appendix captions are what record the conditions, and RFAs 83–102 are built on those sentences.
- **"Separately for each" was dropped.** "For each … state" already requires a per-window answer, and "separately for each" is the phrasing most likely to feed FIE's argument that the interrogatory multiplies by the number of windows. The anti-lumping work moved to **Instruction 13** (I-8), which costs no count and binds Ints. 1, 3, 5, 10 and 19 — and gives a cleaner Rule 37.01(c) argument if FIE answers collectively, because an express instruction was ignored.
- Trimmed: "of The Property" (defined), "designated … in The Appraisal Award" (RFAs 20–24 fix those designations), "and, if so, which" (the demand already asks which), and "or period" (Instruction 11 already makes dates approximate). About 100 words down to 85.

**Why the prior-claim clause matters.** The Prieve Report attributes dents to "a prior hail claim," in the plural, while FIE's own 2020 scope paid for exactly two casement units — the cap the dining-room analysis turns on (`08_DiningRoomWindow_PriorClaimExclusion.md` §2.2). RFAs 107–124 attack this from the other side, by asking FIE to admit the 2020 file records none of these dents, but a denial there never makes FIE say what it believes *does* record them. This clause does.

**Why the garage window came out — held as H-1.** Int. 1's template asks for the cause of a recorded condition. Applied to a window that no photograph shows and no one inspected, the honest answer is "nothing is recorded, so there is nothing to attribute" — a non-answer bought with a count. The facts that give that window its value (impeachment, expert reliability, reserved coverage under *Quade*) come from RFAs 80–81 and RFP 49, which are already served or ready. See the Held table in §0.

#### Why each change was made (Bill's questions, 9/17/2026)

- **"Each line item in the 2020 Xactimate estimate" was unanswerable.** The estimate has 32 line items covering the roof, chimney chase cover, scaffolding, gutters and an exterior door. Asking which window each covers invited an overbreadth objection to the one question that dismantles the prior-claim theory.
- **"The 2020 inspection" is a premise FIE can deny.** The estimate's "Date Inspected" field is blank; the file records no on-site inspection. What exists is the photograph log Prieve cites as "Inspection Photos dated October 13, 2020."
- **The package is not an "estimate."** It is the final claim document FIE made available through the claim website — a letter, a one-page recap worksheet, and the estimate. The definition therefore names no document type for the package as a whole, while using FIE's own label ("Estimate: YIBIAO_LU2," "a copy of the estimate," "Estimate/Invoice - (2)") for the third component only. It also says "provided to," not how it was delivered, and it absorbs the claim-number variant (the letter says 3013539499-1, the estimate says 3013539499-1-1).
- **No reference to the Building Loss Worksheet in the question.** The worksheet only summarizes completed and uncompleted work; the line items live in the estimate. Line 4 (scaffolding) was left out with it, so Int. 5 names the four items whose own descriptions refer to windows — the same four as RFA 126.
- **"Covers," not "paid for."** The worksheet shows the window work under Work Not Completed with depreciation withheld, so "paid for" invites the true but useless answer that nothing was paid for the windows. The value is which physical windows the 2020 scope included.
- **The argumentative last sentence was deleted** ("The correspondence … is known only to You and Your adjusters"). It was there to preempt a Rule 33.03 records-only answer, but Rule 33.03 requires the burden of deriving the answer to be substantially the same for both parties, which FIE cannot show for a mapping only its adjusters could make.

**Name changed 9/17/2026 (Bill's decision).** The term was "The 2020 Settlement Package" when first applied; it is now **"The February 18, 2022 Settlement Package."** Two names were considered and rejected: "The Settlement Notice" (that is the caption of the two-page letter only, so requests about line items could be read as reaching only the letter) and any name built on "created on or about," which invites an argument about system generation rather than the printed date. The delivery channel stays out of the definition — the document is available in the Documents section of FIE's online claim account, and that fact belongs in its own request, where a denial cannot take the definition down with it.

**Why the bad answer is still a good answer.** If FIE says the estimate does not record which physical windows the line items covered, its "already paid in a prior claim" attribution has no anchor — and Int. 1's prior-claim clause forces the same admission window by window.
