## I-19. Negotiate an expert-discovery stipulation with Kennedy ⚠️ *time-critical, and not obvious*

**This is not an edit to the document. It may be the most consequential item in this file.**

**Two Minnesota features combine badly here.**

*Expert depositions are not available as of right.* Minn. R. Civ. P. 26.02(e)(1)(A) gives you the expert **interrogatory**. Everything beyond it falls under 26.02(e)(1)(B): "Upon motion, the court may order further discovery by other means, subject to such restrictions as to scope and such provisions, pursuant to Rule 26.02(e)(3), concerning fees and expenses, as the court may deem appropriate." So deposing FIE's expert requires a motion, court-set scope, and — under (e)(3) — payment of "the expert a reasonable fee for time spent in responding to discovery." This is a real departure from federal practice, where you would simply notice the deposition.

*The Scheduling Order leaves no room to bring that motion.*

| Event | Date |
|---|---|
| All discovery closes | March 15, 2027 |
| **Defendant's expert disclosure due** | **April 17, 2027** |
| Non-dispositive motions must be **heard** by | May 14, 2027 |

FIE names its expert **a month after discovery closes**. To depose that expert you would have to move for leave, obtain a hearing date on Judge Anderson's calendar, and complete the deposition inside the 27 days between April 17 and May 14. That is not realistically achievable — Hennepin hearing dates are reserved months ahead.

**Proposed action.** Write to Kennedy Law Firm — well before March, ideally alongside service of these sets — proposing a written stipulation that:

1. each side may depose the other's retained testifying experts without motion under Rule 26.02(e)(1)(B);
2. those depositions may occur after the March 15, 2027 general discovery close, within a defined window keyed to the expert-disclosure dates; and
3. each side bears its own expert's fee for deposition time, or the parties adopt the (e)(3) default.

The stipulation is mutual — FIE needs to depose **your** engineer just as much, and your expert disclosure comes **first** (March 15). That symmetry is the reason Kennedy should say yes. If Kennedy refuses, you have a documented good-faith attempt supporting a Rule 16.02 motion to amend the schedule — which is a much harder motion after the Court's opening observation that your proposed deadlines ran "more than one year beyond the timeline for similar cases."

# VII. Budget review — what to remove from the First Set

*Added September 11, 2026, on a second pass focused solely on the interrogatory budget.*

## The structural point

**Interrogatories are the only capped discovery device in Minnesota.** Rule 33.01(a) allows "a total of 50 … upon any other party," cumulative across every set. Rule 34 (requests for production) and Rule 36 (requests for admission) contain **no numeric limit at all** — Rule 36.01 is silent on quantity, and Rule 34 has no cap.

That asymmetry should drive what stays in this set. An interrogatory is the right vehicle for three things only:

1. a **narrative explanation** only FIE can give ("state the basis for…", "describe the search performed…");
2. a **contention** you need pinned before depositions; and
3. a fact you want **sworn** by the party under Rule 33.01(d), because that is what makes it usable as a party admission on summary judgment.

Anything that is really a yes/no proposition belongs in an RFA. Anything answerable from documents belongs in an RFP. Six items in the current set fail that test.

## ⚠️ First, a counting risk that makes the budget worse than it looks

Interrogatories **3, 4, 5 and 6** are built as "for each X, state Y" — six exclusions, each non-window component, each 2020 line item. Counted by lettered subparts they are one each. Counted the way an adversary would argue Rule 33.01(a)'s "each subdivision of separate questions" applies, Interrogatory 3 alone could be pressed as six.

Two mitigations, both cheap:

- **State your counting method in the certification** (item I-4): "Plaintiffs count an interrogatory that calls for the same information as to each member of an identified set as a single interrogatory."
- **Do not compound the problem** by adding more "for each" constructions than the case requires.
