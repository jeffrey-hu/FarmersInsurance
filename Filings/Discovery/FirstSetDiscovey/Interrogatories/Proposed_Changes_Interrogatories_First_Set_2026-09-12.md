# I. Corrections

## Q-1. Interrogatory 2 — "the six policy exclusions" is wrong, and the exclusion/uninsured-type distinction is missing

**Current:**

> **INTERROGATORY NO. 2:** For each of **the six policy exclusions** cited in the November 4, 2024 denial letter, state the specific factual basis on which You contend that exclusion applies to The Claim, and identify the documents and witnesses on which You rely to support the application of that exclusion; and identify each person who decided to include that exclusion in the letter.

### Problem 1 — the count

The letter's "Coverage Outcome – Policy Provisions" attachment cites **ten numbered provisions across two differently-titled sections**:

| Section I.A — *Uninsured Types of Loss or Damage* | Section I.B — *Excluded Causes of Loss or Damage* |
|---|---|
| 4. Inherent Vice or Latent Defect | 4. Neglect or Lack of Maintenance or Failure to Make Repairs |
| 7. Wear and tear or mechanical breakdown | 5. Faulty, Inadequate, Defective or Incomplete Planning, Zoning, Maintenance, Repairs, Renovation, Manufacture or Construction |
| 8. Marring | 6. Inherent Vice or Latent Defect |
| 9. Corrosion, Deterioration, Decay or Rust | 8. Wear and Tear, Deterioration or Mechanical Breakdown |
| 14. Exceptions to Uninsured types of damage | 9. Corrosion, Deterioration, Decay or Rust |

Plus **Definition 16 (Marring)**, quoted in full.

"Six" is a fair count of *unique concepts* — wear and tear, marring, corrosion, inherent vice, neglect, faulty construction — but it is not what the letter cites. FIE can respond that the letter cites no such set of six, and that response is true.

### Problem 2 — the burden, which matters more

Section I.**A** is titled "Uninsured Types of Loss or Damage." Section I.**B** is "Excluded Causes of Loss or Damage." That is not a stylistic difference.

> "[T]he insurer bears the burden of establishing the applicability of an exclusion, which is strictly interpreted against the insurer," after which the burden shifts to the insured to prove an exception. *Travelers Indem. Co. v. Bloomington Steel & Supply Co.*, 718 N.W.2d 888 (Minn. 2006), citing *Amos v. Campbell*, 593 N.W.2d 263, 266 (Minn. App. 1999).

If marring and wear-and-tear are **exclusions**, FIE must prove they apply. If FIE characterises them instead as descriptions of what the policy never insured in the first place — a limit on the grant of coverage — the burden is Plaintiffs'. **Nothing in the current set makes FIE commit either way**, and calling all ten "exclusions" in the question invites FIE to correct the premise rather than answer it.

### Proposed

> **INTERROGATORY NO. 2:** For each policy provision cited in the November 4, 2024 denial letter and its "Coverage Outcome – Policy Provisions" attachment as a ground for denying The Claim, state: the provision by section and number; whether You contend it operates as an exclusion or as a limitation on the grant of coverage; the specific factual basis on which You contend it applies to The Claim; the documents and witnesses on which You rely to support its application; and the person who decided to include it in the letter.

**What changes.** The count disappears. The attachment is named, so FIE cannot answer only as to the letter's body. The exclusion-versus-coverage-limitation question is asked directly — and whichever way FIE answers, it is answering under oath and is stuck with it. And the answer is *useful either way*: "exclusion" concedes the burden; "limitation on the grant" is a position FIE must then defend against a policy that says loss is settled at replacement cost.

**Counts as:** 1 — unchanged. All of it is particulars about each cited provision, on the 1993 Advisory Committee pattern.

`APPROVE ☐   APPROVE AS EDITED ☐   REJECT ☐   HOLD ☐`

## Q-3. Interrogatory 1 ↔ Interrogatory 10 — the award-reliance overlap ⚠️ *needs your decision*

**Interrogatory 1** closes: *"…and, if You contend The Appraisal Award determined the question for that window, identify the portion of the award You rely on."*

**Interrogatory 10:** *"State each fact on which You base any contention that The Appraisal Award resolved The Claim within the meaning of Minn. Stat. § 604.18, subd. 4(c), and identify every window that the award is intended to cover and every window it is intended to exclude, with the document or communication supporting each identification."*

**These overlap, and the overlap is not deliberate.** It appeared yesterday: the award clause went into Interrogatory 1 as part of I-28 Option A, and Interrogatory 10 already covered the ground. FIE can answer No. 1's closing clause with "see response to Interrogatory 10" and be substantially right — which is exactly the "see our production" move that I-29 was written to stop elsewhere.

**Option A — keep both, and make the overlap do work.** Leave Interrogatory 1 alone and add to Interrogatory 10: *"If Your answer to this interrogatory is inconsistent in any respect with Your answer to Interrogatory 1, state which answer You intend to govern."* Cost: 0. This is the same technique used in Interrogatory 8's reconciliation clause.

**Option B — strike the clause from Interrogatory 1** and rely on Interrogatory 10 alone. Cost: **−1** (Interrogatory 1 returns to a single inquiry). Cleaner, and Interrogatory 10 is the better-drafted of the two on this point because it is anchored to the statute. The loss is the per-window framing: No. 10 asks which windows the award covers, not whether FIE relies on the award for *this* window's damage.

**My recommendation: Option B.** The per-window framing sounds valuable but is not, because the award is a two-window lump sum — there is no per-window content in it for FIE to point at. Interrogatory 10 already forces FIE to say which windows it says the award reached, and that is the whole of the available answer. Taking the clause out recovers 1 from a budget that has been getting more expensive, and removes a duplication FIE would otherwise get to name.

`OPTION A — KEEP BOTH, ADD RECONCILIATION ☐   OPTION B — STRIKE FROM NO. 1 ☐ ← recommended   KEEP AS IS ☐   HOLD ☐`

## Q-6. The certification ceiling is thirteen higher than the truth ⚠️ *needs your decision*

**Current:** *"…counting each subdivision of separate questions as an interrogatory, number **fewer than forty (40)** and do not exceed the fifty (50) interrogatories permitted without leave of court."*

Forty was chosen this morning when the set was mid-surgery and the count was moving. It has since settled at **27**, and would be **26** with Q-3 Option B, or **24** with I-24 as well.

**Why the gap is not harmless.** The certification is Plaintiffs' own sworn statement of the count. Certifying "fewer than forty" concedes that the set might fairly be read as 39 — and if FIE later argues the count on the "for each" constructions, Plaintiffs have already volunteered most of the distance. It also understates how much reserve remains for a second set.

**Three ways to write it, in the order I would choose:**

1. **State the number.** *"…number twenty-seven (27)…"* Most defensible, and the working that produces 27 is recorded in `../../README.md`. The cost is that a stated number is a target; if FIE argues one of the compound questions counts twice, the certification is wrong rather than merely tight.
2. **A tight ceiling.** *"…number fewer than thirty (30)…"* Keeps a three-question cushion against the counting risk, gives away nothing like thirteen.
3. **Leave forty.** Safe, but concedes room for no reason.

**My recommendation: option 2, "fewer than thirty (30)."** It is accurate today, survives the arguable count on Interrogatory 8's reconciliation clause, and does not invite a fight about whether the number is exactly right. **Revisit after I-24.**

`STATE 27 ☐   FEWER THAN 30 ☐ ← recommended   LEAVE 40 ☐   HOLD ☐`

The Prieve Report's ACTIVITIES list (p. 4 of 7) states that the evaluation included review of:

| Document | Requested anywhere? |

**The 2021 worksheet and the 2022 letter and estimate are the ones that matter.** They fall between The 2020 Claim and The Claim. Neither the Complaint nor any of the three discovery sets accounts for them, and a Farmers *letter and estimate* dated February 18, 2022 implies claim or valuation activity in a period the case currently treats as empty. If there was a 2021–2022 valuation of this property, it bears directly on FIE's prior-payment theory and on what the 2020 claim actually covered.

**The weather records matter for a different reason.** Prieve concluded that storm data does not document a July 13, 2024 event — a conclusion the family's NWS material contradicts. Which records Prieve consulted, and who supplied them, is the first question in taking that conclusion apart.

### Proposed — documentary first

**Add to the RFP set** (they are uncapped, and these are documents):

> **REQUEST:** Each document identified in the "ACTIVITIES" section of the Prieve Report as having been reviewed in connection with that evaluation, including the Farmers Insurance Photo Log dated September 11, 2014; the Farmers Insurance Estimates dated September 16, 2014 and July 15, 2015; the Building Loss Worksheet dated October 7, 2021; the Farmers Insurance Letter and Estimate dated February 18, 2022; the Window Condition Summary; and all weather records, together with all documents showing who provided each such document to Prieve Engineering and when.

> **REQUEST:** All documents concerning any inspection, estimate, valuation, or claim activity at The Property between January 1, 2021 and July 12, 2024, including the Building Loss Worksheet dated October 7, 2021 and the Farmers Insurance Letter and Estimate dated February 18, 2022.

**Optional interrogatory — 1**, only if you want the narrative under oath now rather than after production:

> **INTERROGATORY:** Describe the claim, inspection, estimate, or valuation activity at The Property reflected in the Building Loss Worksheet dated October 7, 2021 and the Farmers Insurance Letter and Estimate dated February 18, 2022, identifying the claim number if any, the persons involved, and any amount paid.

**My recommendation: take the two requests, hold the interrogatory.** The documents will explain themselves, and if they do not, the question is better aimed afterwards. But **do not let this sit** — it is the one thing in this review that could change the shape of the case rather than the polish of a document.

## Q-10. The September 3, 2024 denial's factual premise is not interrogated ⚠️ *needs your decision*

Interrogatory 2 reaches the **November 4** letter's provisions. Interrogatory 9 reaches supervisory review of both letters. Nothing reaches the September 3 letter's affirmative assertion that **"There were no other damages observed."**

That sentence is the hinge of the shifting-rationale argument. On September 3 FIE says nothing was damaged. On November 4 it concedes damage exists and attributes it to wear and tear. Brau inspected on **September 6 — three days after the first denial**. The two letters cannot both be right, and Minn. Stat. § 72A.201 subd. 5 makes inconsistent claim-handling practice actionable.

**Proposed:**

> **INTERROGATORY:** State the factual basis for the statement in Your September 3, 2024 letter that "[t]here were no other damages observed," identifying each inspection, photograph, or document on which that statement rested, each person who observed or reviewed the Property before the letter issued, and the date of each such observation or review.

**Counts as:** +1 — 27 → 28.

**Why it is worth a slot.** FIE cannot answer it with a denial, because the statement is its own. Either it names an inspection before September 3 — in which case Plaintiffs learn of an inspection not currently in the record — or it concedes the letter rested on nothing, three days before its adjuster attended and documented dents. Both answers are better than what is in the file now.

**Against it:** the budget is tighter than it was this morning, and RFP 30 (drafts and internal review of both denial letters) may produce the same ground documentarily.

`APPROVE ☐   APPROVE AS EDITED ☐   HOLD FOR SECOND SET ☐   REJECT ☐`

## Q-14. Interrogatory 13 — the Identify burden ⚠️ *needs your decision*

Interrogatory 13 asks FIE to identify **every person** with knowledge of any fact at issue. The Identify definition then demands, for each: full name, last known title or role, employer, last known business address, telephone number, and email address.

Applied literally to every person with any knowledge, that is the most likely proportionality target in the set, and an objection would delay the answer Plaintiffs actually need — the **names and subjects**, early enough to depose from.

**Proposed — add to Interrogatory 13:**

> As to persons no longer employed by You, their last known business contact information is sufficient; if You do not possess contact information for a person, identify the person by name, role, and dates of involvement and state that You do not possess it.

**Against it:** it volunteers a narrowing FIE has not asked for. The counter is that it removes the objection's foothold without giving up anything — Plaintiffs get names, roles and dates either way, and full contact details where they exist.

**Counts as:** 0.

`APPROVE ☐   REJECT — MAKE FIE OBJECT FIRST ☐   HOLD ☐`
