## 4. The two facts that should drive every ranking decision

Before ranking, two features of the record control what "value" means here.

### 4.1 The case is won or lost on SCOPE, because of the deductible

FIE's Answer ¶2 and ¶35 state its whole defense in one sentence: the appraisal award is **$12,800 RCV / $6,400 ACV**, the windstorm/hail deductible is **$17,720**, therefore *"no payment was due under the terms of the Policy."*

FIE pleaded only two affirmative defenses: (1) lack of jurisdiction over FIE / failure to state a claim; (2) **the September 12, 2025 Appraisal Award is binding**.

So the entire contest reduces to a single question: **can Plaintiffs establish covered loss beyond the two awarded windows sufficient to clear $17,720?** That requires defeating the preclusive effect of the award as to everything it did not itemize. Every interrogatory that expands scope past F1.7 and B1.2 is the case. Everything else — however satisfying — is secondary, and bad faith is downstream of winning this.

### 4.2 ⚠️ Three corrections to the project's legal premises

**(a) *Quade* holds that appraisers MAY determine causation — the opposite of the premise used in places in the file.** Verified: *"an appraiser's duty to determine the 'amount of loss' requires the appraiser to determine causation."* Do **not** argue the panel exceeded its authority by reaching causation. The usable half of *Quade* is the other one: *"Coverage questions, such as whether damage is excluded... are legal questions for the court,"* and *"appraisers... may not construe the policy."* **Consequence for this set: Interrogatory 2 (exclusions) is immune to the appraisal defense, and Interrogatory 1 (causation) is the most exposed to it.** That inverts their relative safety and is reflected in the ranking.

**(b) § 604.18, subd. 4(c) is a categorical BAR, not a clock.** Verified text, in full: *"An award of taxable costs under this section is not available in any claim that is **resolved or confirmed by arbitration or appraisal**."* There is no window, no trigger, no deadline. The file in places treats it as a timing provision. FIE's argument will not be "you are late" — it will be "**§ 604.18 is unavailable to you at all, because an appraisal happened**." That is a threat to the entire bad-faith track, and it is exactly what Interrogatory 10 is for. The answer is *Selective Ins. Co. of S.C. v. Sela*, No. 20-2029 (8th Cir. Aug. 30, 2021): *"The appraisal panel decided damages, but a jury resolved Sela's claim... Damages, as decided by appraisal, flowed from the resolution of the coverage issue but did not 'resolve' the claim."* Persuasive only — **no Minnesota state court has interpreted subd. 4(c)**, as *Sela* itself notes. Confirm the F.4th parallel cite before any filing.

**(c) § 72A.201 will not carry into the bad-faith motion.** Two independent problems. *Morris v. American Family Mut. Ins. Co.*, 386 N.W.2d 233 (Minn. 1986): *"a private person does not have a cause of action for a violation of the Unfair Claims Practices Act."* And § 604.18, subd. 4(d)(4) makes chapters 59A–79A inadmissible **as standards of conduct** in a § 604.18 proceeding. Complaint ¶41's reliance on § 72A.201, subd. 9(3) for the claim-file documentation duty therefore does not transfer to the § 604.18 motion. Reframe through *Peterson*'s reasonable-insurer standard instead — which reaches the same place, because *Peterson* holds the factfinder *"should consider the level of investigation a reasonable insurer would have conducted."* **Related trap: § 604.18, subd. 4(d)(2)–(3) make the November 19, 2024 Department of Commerce complaint and anything Commerce produced INADMISSIBLE in a § 604.18 proceeding.** Do not build any part of the bad-faith showing on it.

## 5. The ranking

Four axes, scored independently: **Deadline pressure** (is there a date that makes delay irreversible?) · **Case value** (does the answer move the $17,720 question, or a pleaded defense?) · **Answer resistance** (how easily can FIE evade — and therefore how much fight-time does it need?) · **Decay** (does the evidence get worse with time?).

One rule verified up front, because it changes the calculus for six of these: **Minn. R. Civ. P. 33.02 deferral requires a COURT ORDER.** Verified: a contention interrogatory *"is not necessarily objectionable merely because its answer involves an opinion or contention"*, but *"**the court may order** that such an interrogatory need not be answered until after designated discovery has been completed."* FIE **cannot** unilaterally defer. A response of "premature contention interrogatory; Defendant will respond at the close of discovery" is a **failure to answer** under Rule 37.01(c) (*"an evasive or incomplete disclosure, answer, or response is to be treated as a failure to... answer"*), and is motion-to-compel material. That materially raises the value of serving the contention interrogatories now rather than later.

### TIER 1 — Cannot wait. Deadline-locked or evidence-decaying.

**№ 12 — Entity identity and capacity.** *The only interrogatory in the set with a hard external deadline.*
FIE's **first affirmative defense** is *"there exists a lack of jurisdiction over Defendant FIE."* The Scheduling Order sets **joinder of parties at November 16, 2026 — 62 days out** — and sets no deadline to amend pleadings. If the wrong entity is named, joinder closes before any real production arrives. Served by mail September 16, answers land October 19: **28 days of margin**, barely enough to open the ¶4 process. Served October 1, there is no margin at all.
⚠️ **But an interrogatory is the wrong instrument.** See §6 — recast as RFAs. Rank 1 on urgency; restructure before serving.

**№ 7 — Whether Brau created any record of the September 6, 2024 inspection.** *Highest decay in the set.*
Claim-system audit logs, mobile-device data, and mail backups age out on retention schedules that are running right now. *Miller v. Lankow*, 801 N.W.2d 120 (Minn. 2011): *"The duty to preserve evidence exists not only after the formal commencement of litigation, but whenever a party knows or should know that litigation is reasonably foreseeable."* And bad faith is **not** required for a sanction — *Patton*/*Himes* focus on **prejudice**, with intent going to severity. FIE is also at maximum exposure right now, because it has two irreconcilable statements on the record (10/10/2025 *"the file does not contain a report on the requested date"* vs. Brau's 10/29/2025 *"all the information we can release has already been sent to you"*) and has not yet had to reconcile them under oath. The rewrite that put the *existence* question first — rather than only the basis for the "no report" statement — was the right call and is what makes this interrogatory work.
⚠️ **Caution before leaning on spoliation:** *Miller* runs both ways. If the windows have been repaired or replaced, Plaintiffs' own preservation duty attached when litigation became reasonably foreseeable, and discharge required advance notice to FIE sufficient to let it inspect. Confirm the windows' status before pressing this hard.

**№ 17 — The date FIE first anticipated litigation.** *A pincer that must be sprung before the privilege log exists.*
This is the best-designed interrogatory in the set and it is underrated in the file at a count of 1. It works whichever way FIE answers. An **early** date maximizes work-product protection under Rule 26.02(d) — and simultaneously fixes an early *Miller* preservation trigger, which converts every gap in the Brau record into spoliation exposure. A **late** date concedes that ten months of post-denial claim handling, the appraisal, and the October 2025 correspondence are all ordinary-course and fully discoverable. FIE cannot optimize for both.
**Timing is the whole point:** served now, FIE must fix the date *before* it builds a privilege log. Served after the log exists, the date gets reverse-engineered to fit whatever FIE wants to withhold. This is the clearest example in the set of an interrogatory whose value is destroyed by delay.

### TIER 2 — Highest case value. Serve in the same tranche, because these are the ones that will need compelling.

The ¶4 protocol takes four to eight weeks. These four will draw objections. That is precisely the argument for serving them first, not later.

**№ 18 — Award itemization.** ⚠️ ***The single highest-value interrogatory in the set, and the file does not rank it that way.***
It was added on September 14 as item AM-1 and is currently recorded mainly as a count problem. It is the hinge of the case. **§ 65A.01, subd. 3** (verified) requires the appraisers to *"appraise the loss, **stating separately actual value and loss to each item**; ... An award in writing, **so itemized**... shall determine the amount of actual value and loss."* Complaint ¶33 alleges the award does not do this. **FIE affirmatively denied it**: *"FIE denies that the Appraisal Award does not state actual value and loss separately to each item."* FIE has therefore already committed, in a signed pleading, to a position that the document on its face cannot support — a single lump $12,800 RCV against two undefined codes, "F1.7" and "B1.2."
This interrogatory makes FIE point to the itemization under oath (Rule 33.01(d): *"signed under oath or penalty of perjury... by any officer or managing agent, who shall furnish such information as is available"*). It cannot. And because affirmative defense 2 — *"the Appraisal Award is binding"* — is the only thing standing between Plaintiffs and the other ~25 windows, an award that is not itemized as § 65A.01 requires cannot preclude claims for items it never itemized. **That is the path past the $17,720 deductible.** It is binary, cheap at a count of 2, and unanswerable in FIE's favor.

**№ 2 — The six exclusions in the November 4, 2024 denial.** *Highest value per unit of objection risk.*
Under *Quade*, exclusions and policy construction are **for the court, not the appraisers** — so the "the appraisal decided it" objection does not reach this interrogatory at all. It is the one high-value question in the set that AD 2 cannot touch. It forces FIE to supply a specific factual basis for wear and tear, marring, corrosion, inherent vice, neglect, and faulty construction — six theories invoked against hail damage to windows — plus the supporting documents, the witnesses, and **the person who decided to include each one**. That last clause is the bad-faith hook: it produces a named decision-maker for deposition, and *Peterson*'s subjective prong asks whether the insurer *"knew, or recklessly disregarded information that would have allowed it to know, that it lacked an objectively reasonable basis."*
Also the single biggest count driver after 11 and 12 (FIE will call it 5). Worth every unit.

**№ 5 — The 2020 Xactimate line-item mapping.** *The arithmetic that kills the "prior claim" theory.*
Prieve attributes dents across ~25 windows to *"a prior hail claim."* Per Complaint ¶23, FIE's own 2020 scope approved **at most two casement window units**. The prior-claim basket cannot hold more than FIE's own file put in it — but the correspondence between 2020 line items and specific windows and sashes is not ascertainable from the estimate and is knowable only to FIE. The interrogatory says so expressly, which is good drafting: it pre-empts the **Rule 33.03** dodge, which requires that *"the burden of deriving or ascertaining the answer is **substantially the same**"* for both parties and that any specification be detailed enough *"to permit the interrogating party to locate and to identify, **as readily as can the party served**."* Expect FIE to try "see the estimate" anyway; beating that takes a full cycle, which is the argument for serving now.

**№ 1 — Per-window causation.** *The scope engine, and the most objection-exposed question in the set.*
This is what converts Photo Appendix A's ~25 dented windows into either covered loss or a stated FIE position. It will draw the heaviest burden objection **and**, post-*Quade*, a colorable "the appraisers determined causation" objection. Serve it first for exactly that reason — it needs the most runway. Note the drafting already anticipates the appraisal objection with its "if You contend The Appraisal Award determined the question for that window, identify the portion of the award You rely on" clause, which dovetails with № 18: FIE cannot point to award portions that do not exist.

## 6. ⚠️ The № 12 restructure — the most consequential recommendation here

Interrogatory 12 is the only question in this set tied to an irreversible deadline, and it is built on the wrong instrument.

**Verified: Minn. R. Civ. P. 36.01 imposes NO numerical limit on requests for admission.** There is nothing in Rule 36 parallel to Rule 33.01(a)'s cap. And the default is self-executing: *"The matter **is admitted** unless within 30 days after service of the request... the party to whom the request is directed serves... a written answer or objection."* Lack of knowledge is not an out: *"An answering party may not give lack of information or knowledge as a reason for failure to admit or deny unless the party states that a **reasonable inquiry** has been made."*

Set that against what an interrogatory gets you: it costs **7 of a capped 50**; FIE can object and, under Rule 33.01(c), *"answers to interrogatories to which objection has been made shall be **deferred until the objections are determined**"* — which self-defers the answer past November 16 with no motion required on FIE's part; and enforcing it means a ¶4 conference, a three-page letter, a conference call, and a motion — four to eight weeks that do not exist.

**Against a 62-day deadline, an uncapped request whose non-answer is an automatic admission beats a capped request whose objection is an automatic deferral.**

**Do this:**

1. ## 7. Smaller points, worth fixing while the document is open

- **Rule 33.01(d) has a Minnesota-specific requirement worth citing in the instructions:** *"A party shall **restate the interrogatory being answered immediately preceding the answer** to that interrogatory."* It is binding whether or not Plaintiffs ask, but quoting it in Instruction 2 makes non-compliance easy to call out in the first meet-and-confer instead of arguable.
