## Bottom line

This is a well-aimed set. It does the one thing interrogatories do better than any other discovery device: it forces FIE to commit, **in writing and under oath**, to positions it has so far kept vague. Interrogatories 2, 4, 6, 10 and 11 are the strongest — each targets a specific evasion in the record rather than a general subject.

Three things need attention before service:

1. **Two citations are wrong**, and both will be quoted back at you. Instruction 5 cites the wrong rule for the business-records option, and Interrogatory 13 cites the wrong subdivision of § 604.18 — the one paragraph in that statute you most need to get right.
2. **Nothing in this set asks who FIE actually is.** The Scheduling Order sets joinder at **November 16, 2026**. Entity identity and capacity is the one subject where a wrong answer is unfixable after that date, and it appears nowhere in the set.
3. **There is no expert interrogatory.** In Minnesota, Rule 26.02(e)(1)(A) makes the interrogatory the *primary* vehicle for expert discovery, and the Scheduling Order's ¶3 speaks in those terms ("expert interrogatories, disclosures, and reports"). Your expert disclosure is due March 15, 2027; FIE's follows on April 17.

You have used **23 of the 50** interrogatories Rule 33.01(a) allows, counting subparts. There is room for all of the additions below.

## 1. Posture and timing — what changed on September 8

The Scheduling Order entered **September 8, 2026** compresses everything and is the frame for this set:

| Event | Ordered |
|---|---|
| Rule 26.01(a) initial disclosures | September 25, 2026 |
| **Joinder of additional parties** | **November 16, 2026** |
| **All discovery complete** | **March 15, 2027** |
| Plaintiffs' expert disclosures and reports | March 15, 2027 |
| Defendant's expert disclosures | April 17, 2027 |
| Motions heard by | May 14, 2027 |
| Mediation complete | June 1, 2027 |
| Trial block | September 20 – October 22, 2027 |

**The response clock.** Minn. R. Civ. P. 33.01(b) requires answers within **30 days** after service. Service by U.S. Mail adds **3 days** under Rule 6.01(e), so answers are due **33 days from the date of mailing**, with a weekend or holiday ending date rolling to the next business day.

**The dispute clock is the one that actually binds.** Scheduling Order ¶4 requires the parties to confer, then serve and file a **correspondence of no more than three pages** requesting a conference call, before any discovery motion — and the Court "will not consider filings related to the dispute until the matter is considered via conference call." That sequence realistically consumes four to eight weeks. Against a March 15 close, any deficiency in FIE's answers has to be identified and escalated by **mid-January 2027**. Serve early; chase deficiencies immediately rather than accumulating them.

**Why this set matters more than the document requests.** Rule 33.01(d): "Answers to interrogatories shall be stated fully in writing and shall be signed under oath or penalty of perjury." Objections are signed by counsel; **answers are sworn by FIE**. That makes interrogatory answers usable as party admissions on summary judgment in a way that a document production never is. Reserve this device for the positions you most want locked before depositions — which is largely what the current set does.

## 2. Compliance — four defects, two of them citation errors

### 2.1 Instruction 5 cites the wrong rule ❌

**Current:** "If You elect to answer any interrogatory by producing business records under **Minn. R. Civ. P. 33.01(d)** …"

The business-records option is **Rule 33.03**. Rule 33.01(d) is something else entirely — it is the provision requiring that answers "be stated fully in writing and … signed under oath or penalty of perjury."

**Fix:** change the citation in Instruction 5 to **Rule 33.03**.

### 2.2 Instruction 2 is imprecise

**Current:** "…must be answered separately, fully, in writing, and under oath, and the answers must be signed by the person making them, pursuant to **Minn. R. Civ. P. 33.01**."

The requirement lives in **33.01(d)** specifically. Not wrong, but a bare "33.01" is the same looseness that produced 2.1. Make it 33.01(d).

### 2.3 Interrogatory 13 cites the wrong subdivision of § 604.18 ❌ — and this one matters

**Current:** "…any contention that The Appraisal Award resolved The Claim within the meaning of **Minn. Stat. § 604.18, subd. 4(b)**…"

Subdivision 4 breaks down as:

| ¶ | Content |
|---|---|
| **4(a)** | The complaint must not seek recovery under the section; a party may later move to amend |
| **4(b)** | Taxable costs are determined by the court in a proceeding **after** the fact-finder's determination, governed by Gen. R. Prac. 119 |
| **4(c)** | **"An award of taxable costs under this section is not available in any claim that is resolved or confirmed by arbitration or appraisal."** |

The appraisal bar — the thing Interrogatory 13 is aimed at — is **4(c)**. As written, the interrogatory asks FIE about the post-verdict procedural rule, which makes the question incoherent and gives FIE a clean basis to object that it does not understand what is being asked.

**Fix:** change to **subd. 4(c)**. This is the same class of error as the "page 7 of the Prieve Report" mis-citation corrected in the RFP set — worth a standing habit of pulling the subdivision before citing it.

### 2.4 The subpart certification understates the count

**Current closing:** "…the foregoing interrogatories number seventeen (17) and, including all **discrete subparts**, do not exceed the fifty (50) interrogatories permitted…"

Two problems. First, "discrete subparts" is the **federal** phrase. Minn. R. Civ. P. 33.01(a) says: "In computing the total number of interrogatories **each subdivision of separate questions** shall be counted as an interrogatory." Use Minnesota's language.

Second, the number invites an argument you do not need. Counting the lettered subparts in Interrogatories 7 and 17 gives **23**. On an aggressive reading — counting each separate "state / identify / describe" demand — it approaches **28**. Either way you are comfortably under 50, so say so accurately:

> Pursuant to Minn. R. Civ. P. 33.01(a), Plaintiffs state that the foregoing interrogatories, counting each subdivision of separate questions as an interrogatory, number fewer than thirty and do not exceed the fifty interrogatories permitted without leave of court.

### 2.5 Two smaller items

- **The "Identify" definition ends "…and to produce it."** Production is Rule 34. In an interrogatory set that clause is improper and hands FIE a free objection. Delete the production clause here; keep it in the RFPs.
- **Instruction 1's period is January 1, 2020.** The RFP set was extended back to **October 22, 2014** for the Policy, the underwriting file, and prior claims (item P-2). If you add the entity-identity and policy-interpretation interrogatories below, this set needs the same extension.

### 2.6 The package is now inconsistent

The RFP set carries **11 instructions** after the September 10 amendments. This set still has **7**. Three of the four new RFP instructions apply equally to interrogatories and should be carried over:

| RFP instruction | Why it belongs here |
|---|---|
| **8** — attorney-client / litigation-counsel carve-out | The definition of "You" includes "attorneys." Without the carve-out this set literally demands FIE's communications with Kennedy Law Firm, which gives FIE the same rhetorical opening the RFPs were amended to close. |
| **9** — partial objections; state whether anything is withheld | Rule 33.01 has no express counterpart to Rule 34.02's "must state whether any responsive materials are being withheld," which makes saying it yourself more valuable here, not less. |
| **11** — produce once, cross-referenced | Minor, but the sets overlap heavily. |

(Instruction 10, possession/custody/control, is a Rule 34 concept and does not need to carry over.)

## 3. Rule 33.02 — FIE can ask to defer your contention interrogatories

**Rule 33.02** permits the court to order that an interrogatory "need not be answered until after designated discovery has been completed, a pretrial conference has been held, or at another later time."

Interrogatories **2, 3, 13, 14 and 15** are contention interrogatories. Expect FIE to invoke Rule 33.02 and offer to answer them "at the close of discovery" — which, against a March 15, 2027 close and an April 23 non-dispositive motion deadline, would render them useless.

**The answer, and it is a strong one:** these are not premature. Each asks FIE to explain a position it has **already taken** on the record —

- Interrogatory 3 asks about six exclusions FIE itself cited in the November 4, 2024 denial letter;
- Interrogatory 13 asks about the appraisal defense FIE pleaded as its Second Affirmative Defense;
- Interrogatory 15 asks the factual basis of defenses FIE has already asserted;
- Interrogatory 2 asks the basis of a causation denial FIE made in its Answer.

A party that has pleaded a defense is not entitled to withhold its basis until discovery closes. Say that in the meet-and-confer, in writing, the first time it comes up — and if FIE presses, the ¶4 three-page letter is the vehicle.

## 6. Gaps — six additions, in priority order

### A. Entity identity and capacity ⚠️ *most urgent — joinder closes November 16, 2026*

Nothing in this set, the RFPs, or the RFAs asks who FIE actually is. FIE's **First Affirmative Defense** alleges lack of jurisdiction over it. Your own Discovery Plan V5 told the Court that "the documents bearing on the identity and capacity of the contracting entity — including the underwriting file and the attorney-in-fact documentation — are within Defendant's production," and used that to argue the amendment deadline should fall *after* substantial completion. **The Court did not adopt that.** Joinder closes November 16 — 66 days out — and a party you fail to join by then is a party you likely cannot add.

> **PROPOSED INTERROGATORY:** Identify, for each of the following, the specific legal entity that performed the act and the entity's state of organization and form: (a) the entity that issued and underwrote The Policy; (b) the entity that employed or engaged Nicholas Brau, Bradlee Monson, Christian Dudley, and Peter Pietila at the time of their involvement in The Claim; (c) the entity that made the coverage decisions communicated in the September 3, 2024 and November 4, 2024 letters; (d) the entity that retained Hancock Claims Consultants and Prieve Engineering, LLC; (e) the entity that maintains the claim file for The Claim and The 2020 Claim; and (f) the entity that appointed Greg Tomes as appraiser. For each, state the basis of the relationship between that entity and Fire Insurance Exchange, and identify the subscription agreement, power of attorney, or other instrument governing the attorney-in-fact relationship between Fire Insurance Exchange and Fire Underwriters Association.

Six subparts, so it counts as six against the 50. Worth every one. RFP 34 (the underwriting file, as amended) already seeks the documents; this seeks the sworn answer, which is what you need to decide about joinder.

### B. Expert interrogatory — Minnesota's actual expert-discovery vehicle

Minn. R. Civ. P. **26.02(e)(1)(A)**: "A party may through interrogatories require any other party to identify each person whom the other party expects to call as an expert witness at trial, to state the subject matter on which the expert is expected to testify, and to state the substance of the facts and opinions to which the expert is expected to testify and a summary of the grounds for each opinion."

Minnesota **did not** adopt the federal protections for draft expert reports or attorney-expert communications (Rule 26.02(e) retains the pre-2010 structure), and the Scheduling Order ¶3 speaks of "expert interrogatories." Ask the question now, in the form the rule provides, so the obligation is live and continuing under Rule 26.05 rather than something you have to chase in February 2027:

> **PROPOSED INTERROGATORY:** Identify each person You expect to call as an expert witness at trial, and for each state the subject matter on which the expert is expected to testify, the substance of the facts and opinions to which the expert is expected to testify, and a summary of the grounds for each opinion, pursuant to Minn. R. Civ. P. 26.02(e)(1)(A). State separately whether You expect to call Nathan Prieve or any person affiliated with Prieve Engineering, LLC or Hancock Claims Consultants as an expert witness.

The second sentence matters. Whether FIE designates Prieve as a *testifying expert* — as opposed to standing on his claim-handling report — changes your whole approach to the Prieve file and to a *Daubert* / Frye-Mack challenge. Make FIE answer it early.

### C. Persons with knowledge

A standard interrogatory, and its absence is conspicuous:

> **PROPOSED INTERROGATORY:** Identify each person known to You to have knowledge of the facts relating to The Claim, The 2020 Claim, the inspections of The Property, the appraisal, or the denial decisions, and state the subject matter of each person's knowledge.

Rule 26.01(a)(1)(A) disclosures reach only individuals FIE "may use to support" its defenses. This reaches everyone with knowledge — including people whose knowledge hurts FIE, which is the point, and which is how you find witnesses before the March 15 close.

### D. The ACV and depreciation calculation

RFP 33 (as amended) seeks the estimating documents. This seeks the sworn explanation:

> **PROPOSED INTERROGATORY:** State how the $6,400 Actual Cash Value figure in The Appraisal Award was calculated, identifying the replacement cost figure used, the depreciation method and rate applied, the age and condition assumptions for each component, and any allocation of that figure between the windows designated "F 1.7" and "B 1.2." State whether You contend any deduction beyond depreciation was applied, and if so its basis.

Your `FIE_Defense_Strategy` file identifies valuation as the way FIE wins even if it loses causation. Nothing in any of the three sets currently asks FIE to explain the number.

### E. Destroyed or unlocatable information

Previously proposed as item **P-33** in the RFP change file, still pending. It belongs here rather than in the RFPs, because it calls for a narrative answer:

> **PROPOSED INTERROGATORY:** For each document or item of information responsive to any discovery request in Plaintiffs' First Sets that You contend no longer exists or cannot be located, identify the document by type, date, author, and subject matter; state the date and manner of its destruction or loss; identify the person who authorized or caused it; identify the retention policy or schedule under which it occurred; and identify each person who had custody of it.

### F. What FIE contends is owed

> **PROPOSED INTERROGATORY:** State the total amount You contend is owed to Plaintiffs under The Policy for the July 13, 2024 loss, itemized by component, and state every amount You have paid on The Claim, identifying the date, payee, and component for each payment.

FIE has admitted paying $0. Making it state the contended amount **under oath** forecloses a later shift in position and gives a clean number to set against the $17,720 deductible.

**Capacity check.** Current 23, plus roughly 12 for A–F (A alone is 6). Total near 35 of the 50 permitted — comfortable, with room held back for a second set once FIE's answers land.

## 7. Sequencing

1. **Serve all three sets together**, so the 33-day clocks run in parallel and FIE cannot stage its objections.
2. **Add A and B before service if at all possible.** Entity identity is governed by a deadline the Court has already set and will not move casually; the expert interrogatory starts a continuing obligation running now rather than in February.
3. **Calendar day 33**, not day 30, and calendar it the day you mail.
4. **Read the answers against the clock in §1.** Any deficiency has to reach the ¶4 three-page-letter stage by **mid-January 2027** to be fixable before the March 15 close.
5. **Do not accept "we will answer contention interrogatories at the close of discovery."** See §3 — the answer is that FIE pleaded these defenses and must explain them now.
