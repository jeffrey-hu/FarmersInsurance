# Complaint Format Requirements — Minnesota State District Court

**Case:** Yibiao Lu v. Fire Insurance Exchange
**Court:** Hennepin County District Court (Fourth Judicial District), Minnesota
**Purpose:** Format and procedural requirements that govern the Complaint, for review *before* drafting. This is a requirements checklist, not the complaint itself.

> ⚠️ Not legal advice. These are the procedural/formatting rules as published by the Minnesota courts. A licensed Minnesota attorney should review before anything is served or filed, especially given the 7/13/2026 limitations deadline.

---

## 1. The big procedural quirk: in Minnesota you *serve* first, then file

Minnesota is a "hip-pocket service" state. Unlike federal court and most states:

- **A civil action is commenced when the Summons and Complaint are *served* on the defendant — not when they are filed with the court** (Minn. R. Civ. P. 3.01). Service is what stops the limitations clock.
- You then have **one year from the date of service to file the action with the court**, or it is "deemed dismissed with prejudice" (Minn. R. Civ. P. 5.04(a)) — unless the parties sign a stipulation to extend.
- Practical effect for this case: to beat the **7/13/2026** two-year contractual deadline, the Summons and Complaint must be *served on Fire Insurance Exchange on or before 7/13/2026*. Filing can follow within the year. **Do not assume filing alone preserves the claim — service is the deadline that matters.**

This means two documents must be prepared together: the **Summons** and the **Complaint**. The Complaint requirements are below.

---

## 2. Caption requirements (Minn. R. Civ. P. 10.01)

Every pleading's caption must contain:

1. **Name of the court** — "STATE OF MINNESOTA / DISTRICT COURT" with the **county** ("COUNTY OF HENNEPIN") and **judicial district** ("FOURTH JUDICIAL DISTRICT").
2. **Title of the action** — In the *Complaint specifically, the title must name ALL parties* (full names on both sides). (Other later pleadings may abbreviate to "first party on each side, et al.")
3. **Court file number** — included once assigned (blank on the initial complaint, since none is assigned until filing).
4. **Assigned judge's name** — only if/once a judge is assigned (blank initially).
5. **Case type designation in the upper right-hand corner** — must match the State Court Administrator's Case Type Index. **Decision (6/27/2026): use "Contract."** Minnesota's MNCIS structure files insurance-coverage suits under the Major Civil → Contract category (case type "Contract"); there is no separate "Insurance" case type, and the lawsuit's gravamen is Count I breach of the insurance contract. The catch-all "Civil Other/Misc." is reserved for cases that fit no defined type and is not used here. **A pleading can be stricken if the case type is omitted** (Rule 10.04), though it can be cured by promptly notifying the court administrator.
6. **Document title** below the caption — e.g., "COMPLAINT" (and "SUMMONS" on the companion document).

**Defendant styling (per KeyDecisions.md, Decision 2):**
> Fire Insurance Exchange, by and through its attorney-in-fact, Fire Underwriters Association (doing business as Farmers Insurance)

## 3. Body / form of pleadings (Minn. R. Civ. P. 8, 9, 10)

**Numbered paragraphs (Rule 10.02):** Every allegation must be in **numbered paragraphs**, each limited "as far as practicable" to a single set of circumstances. Paragraphs can be cross-referenced by number later.

**Separate counts (Rule 10.02):** Each claim founded on a separate transaction/occurrence should be stated in a **separate count** (e.g., "Count I — Breach of Contract," "Count II — Breach of the Implied Covenant of Good Faith and Fair Dealing," etc.) where separation aids clarity.

**Short and plain statement (Rule 8.01):** Each claim needs (a) a short and plain statement of the claim showing the pleader is entitled to relief, and (b) a demand for judgment for the relief sought. Notice pleading — detailed evidence is not required, but enough facts to give fair notice of each claim.

**Pleading special matters with particularity (Rule 9.02):** Any allegations of **fraud or mistake must be stated with particularity** (the who/what/when/where/how). Relevant if any misrepresentation-type theory is included.

**Exhibits (Rule 10.03):** A copy of any **written instrument attached as an exhibit becomes part of the pleading**. Attaching exhibits is *permissive*, not required — Minnesota is a notice-pleading state. **Decision (6/27/2026): file the complaint with NO exhibits at this time.** The policy, denial letters, and appraisal award will instead be identified, described, and quoted in numbered paragraphs in the body, and produced later in discovery. This keeps the operative documents (and any double-edged content, e.g., the Prieve report or the net-$0 award) out of the pleading and off the Rule 12 record.

**Adoption by reference (Rule 10.03):** Earlier paragraphs can be re-alleged/incorporated at the start of each count ("Plaintiff re-alleges paragraphs 1–X as if fully set forth herein").

## 4. The damages-amount rule (Minn. R. Civ. P. 8.01) — important and counterintuitive

Minnesota restricts stating a specific dollar figure in the demand:

- **For unliquidated damages exceeding $50,000, you do NOT state a specific dollar amount** — you state only that the amount exceeds $50,000 (mirrored in Minn. Stat. § 544.36). If the unliquidated demand is *under* $50,000, the amount is stated.
- **Liquidated / sum-certain amounts** (e.g., the specific unpaid policy benefit owed under the contract) *may* be pleaded as a specific number, since they are calculable from the policy.
- Practical approach for this case: the breach-of-contract demand for unpaid policy proceeds can state the sum certain owed; any open-ended/consequential damages should follow the "in excess of $50,000" convention rather than naming a figure.

## 5. The bad-faith claim CANNOT go in the initial complaint (Minn. Stat. § 604.18)

This is a critical drafting constraint and a change from KeyDecisions.md's framing of bad faith as the "primary claim":

- In **Minnesota state court**, a plaintiff is **prohibited from pleading a § 604.18 bad-faith claim (for taxable costs/insurer-bad-faith award) in the original complaint.**
- It must be added **later, by a separate motion to amend**, supported by **affidavits** showing a factual basis, and only after the court finds **prima facie evidence** in support (§ 604.18, subd. 4).
- **Implication:** The initial Complaint should plead **breach of contract** (and any other available common-law/statutory counts — e.g., breach of the implied covenant of good faith and fair dealing, declaratory judgment), but should **NOT include a § 604.18 count.** Reserve bad faith for a post-filing motion to amend.
- (Note: this restriction applies in *state* court. A federal court — Selective Ins. Co. v. Sela — has held the § 604.18 procedure does not bind federal pleadings. Since the decision is to file in state court, the state restriction controls.)

## 6. Physical / document formatting (Minn. Gen. R. Prac. 6; e-filing rules)

**If e-filed (mandatory for represented parties; the default in district court):** Must comply with the format in the *Minnesota District Court Registered User Guide for Electronic Filing* (filed as PDF through the eFS / Odyssey system).

**If filed on paper (Gen. R. Prac. 6):**
- **8½ × 11 inch paper** (Rule 6.02).
- **Double-spaced**, one side, legibly typed/printed on plain unglazed paper (Rule 6.01).
- **Top margin of at least one inch**, free of any text (Rule 6.01).
- **No backings or covers** (Rule 6.03); fasten with a single staple in the upper left, or clip at upper left.

**Restricted identifiers (Gen. R. Prac. 11):** Do **not** put full Social Security numbers, financial account numbers, or other restricted identifiers in the public pleading. Signing the pleading certifies compliance. Use a separate confidential "Restricted Identifiers" form if any are needed.

**Signature block (Minn. R. Civ. P. 11):** Must be signed. If self-represented (pro se), include name, address, telephone, and email. If by counsel, attorney name, MN attorney registration (license) number, firm, address, phone, email. The signature certifies a good-faith, non-frivolous filing.

**§ 549.211 Acknowledgment (required):** Minn. Stat. § 549.211, subd. 1 requires every pleading to "attach to and make a part of the pleading … a signed acknowledgment stating that the parties acknowledge that sanctions may be imposed under this section." Include an **ACKNOWLEDGMENT** section immediately before the signature block. **Decision (6/27/2026): use the language from Minnesota Judicial Branch Form CIV803 (Civil Complaint)**, adapted for two pro se plaintiffs, rather than a bare one-line recital. The CIV803 acknowledgment tracks the § 549.211, subd. 2 certifications (facts/law support; no improper purpose; not harassing/delaying/increasing cost; not a frivolous litigant; no order barring filing) and folds in the Rule 11 restricted-identifiers certification — the fuller, court-published form a self-represented filer should attest to.

**Page numbering / footer (not required):** Gen. R. Prac. 6 does not require page numbers or a footer in district-court pleadings (page numbering is required only in the appellate courts under Minn. R. Civ. App. P. 132). A plain centered page number is used here as a convenience.

**Verification:** A general civil complaint need not be verified (sworn) unless a specific statute/rule requires it. Optional but sometimes used.

## 7. Recommended structure of the Complaint (assembling the above)

1. **Caption** — court, county, judicial district; full party names; case type in upper-right; "COMPLAINT" title.
2. **Introduction / nature of the action** — one short paragraph.
3. **Parties** — numbered paragraphs identifying plaintiff(s) and the FIE defendant (with the attorney-in-fact styling; proactively address the "Farmers/Fire" naming discrepancy noted in KeyDecisions.md).
4. **Jurisdiction and venue** — Hennepin County District Court; property located in MN; amount/subject within district court jurisdiction.
5. **Factual allegations** — numbered, chronological (policy inception → 7/13/2024 storm → claim → denial → appraisal → post-appraisal non-payment), drawing on `key-facts-excluding-legal-context.md` and `ClaimTimeline.md`, each fact in its own numbered paragraph.
6. **Counts** — each in a separate count with re-alleged paragraphs:
   - Count I: Breach of Contract (failure to pay covered loss / honor the appraisal award).
   - Count II: Breach of the Implied Covenant of Good Faith and Fair Dealing (as available).
   - Count III (optional): Declaratory Judgment re: coverage obligations.
   - *(NOT § 604.18 bad faith — reserved for later motion to amend.)*
7. **Prayer for relief** — damages consistent with the Rule 8.01 amount rule, prejudgment interest, costs, and any other relief; reservation of right to seek § 604.18 amendment.
8. **Signature block** — per Rule 11.
9. **Exhibits** — *none attached at this time* (per 6/27/2026 decision). The policy, declarations, denial letters, and appraisal award are described and quoted in the body and reserved for discovery; the complaint relies on notice pleading rather than incorporated written instruments.

**Companion document:** A **Summons** must be prepared and served *with* the Complaint (it is the service of these together that commences the action).
