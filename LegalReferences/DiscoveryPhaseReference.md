**Court:** Hennepin County District Court, State of Minnesota
**Case type:** Contract (insurance coverage + bad faith)
**Filed:** July 8, 2026
**Governing rules:** Minnesota Rules of Civil Procedure (Rules 26–37) and Minnesota General Rules of Practice (Rule 111)

> **Anchor date — Defendant's Answer due: Monday, August 10, 2026** (extended by the Court).
> Most discovery deadlines below are counted from this date. Deadlines counted in days follow **Minn. R. Civ. P. 6.01**: do not count the anchor day, count every calendar day (weekends included), and if the last day falls on a Saturday, Sunday, or legal holiday, it rolls to the next business day.

## B. Discovery devices — what each side may use, and the limits

Written discovery may be served on FIE as early as **now** (the Summons & Complaint have already been served). Each device has its own limit and response clock:

| Device | Rule | Limit | Response deadline |
|--------|------|-------|-------------------|
| **Interrogatories** (written questions answered under oath) | 33 | **Max 50**, including all discrete subparts, without leave of court | **30 days** after they are served |
| **Requests for production of documents / ESI** (e.g., FIE's full claim file, adjuster notes, engineer reports, internal emails) | 34 | No fixed number (bounded by proportionality) | **30 days** after they are served |
| **Requests for admission** (admit/deny specific facts to narrow disputes) | 36 | No numerical cap | **30 days** after they are served — ⚠️ **anything not answered in 30 days is automatically deemed ADMITTED** |
| **Depositions** (sworn oral questioning of adjuster, corporate rep, experts) | 30 | **7 hours / one day per deponent** (presumptive); no fixed cap on the *number* of depositions in MN state court | Requires reasonable written notice |
| **Independent examination** | 35 | Rarely relevant here (property, not injury); deadline set by scheduling order if used | — |

**Response-clock notes:**
- The 30-day response clock runs from the date each specific request is *served* — it is rolling, not a single fixed calendar date. Calendar each set as it goes out or comes in.
- A **defendant** is not required to respond to discovery before **45 days after service of the Summons & Complaint** (Rules 33/34/36). Because FIE's response window is already open by August 2026, discovery the family serves now will run on the normal **30-day** clock.
- The family must calendar FIE's requests just as carefully — the **auto-admission trap** on Requests for Admission (Rule 36) cuts both ways.

**Number of SETS vs. the caps (confirmed Aug 2026 — so we don't re-research):**
- **Interrogatories (Rule 33.01):** the **50 cap is a cumulative TOTAL per party across ALL sets — not a per-set limit.** We may serve interrogatories in a first set, second set, etc., but the running total to a given defendant may not exceed 50 without (a) FIE's **written stipulation**, or (b) a **court order** on motion, notice, and a showing of **good cause**. **Each discrete subpart counts as a separate interrogatory** — tally subparts before serving (e.g., our Interrogatory No. 24 has subparts (a)–(d), which count as 4 toward the 50).
- **Requests for production (Rule 34):** **NO limit** on the number of requests or the number of sets. We can serve successive sets as the case develops, subject only to relevance/proportionality (Rule 26.02).
- **Requests for admission (Rule 36):** **NO numerical cap and NO set limit.**
- **The court can raise or lower any of these** limits by scheduling order or on motion (Rule 26.02(a)) — check the scheduling order once it issues.

**Governing standard for everything:** discovery must be relevant to a claim or defense **and proportional** to the needs of the case (Rule 26.02) — importance, amount in controversy, access to information, resources, and burden vs. benefit.

## C. Trial-driven deadlines (set once the Court fixes a trial date)

These are counted **backward from the trial date**, which is set in the scheduling order — so they are TBD until that order issues.

| Step | Rule | Deadline |
|------|------|----------|
| **Retained-expert written reports** (likely the heart of a hail/causation case) | 26.01(b) | **At least 90 days before trial** |
| **Rebuttal expert disclosures** | 26.01(b) | **30 days after** the opposing party's expert disclosure |
| **Discovery cutoff** — all discovery *completed* (not just served) | Gen. R. Prac. 111.02 | Date set in the scheduling order |
| **Pretrial disclosures** — final witness lists, deposition designations, exhibits | 26.03 | **30 days before trial** |
| **Objections to pretrial disclosures** | 26.03 | **Within 14 days** after they are served, or waived |

## D. Practice notes

1. **Meet-and-confer is a prerequisite to discovery motions.** Before filing any motion to compel or for sanctions, the family must actually confer with FIE's counsel and certify that it did (Rule 37; Gen. R. Prac. 115). Skipping this gets the motion denied.
2. **Serve first written discovery early.** Because the S&C are already served, the family can serve interrogatories and document requests on FIE right away to start the 30-day clock — no need to wait for the scheduling order.
3. **Priority targets for document requests:** FIE's complete claim file, all adjuster/Brau field notes and the disputed 9/6/2024 inspection record, the full Prieve Engineering file and communications, Hancock Claims materials, internal claim-handling notes, and the appraisal/umpire file — many of these are items FIE has previously said "do not exist," which the claim file production will test directly.
4. **Watch for the scheduling order by Oct 6, 2026.** It's the master timeline — once it issues, its discovery cutoff and trial date override the defaults here, and the trial-date-keyed deadlines in Section C can be filled in.
