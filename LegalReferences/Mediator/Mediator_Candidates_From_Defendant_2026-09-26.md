## 3. Charles J. Noel — decline

**Charles J. Noel & Associates, P.A., 2805 Dodd Rd., Suite 145, Eagan, MN 55121 — and listed as an attorney at Erstad & Riemer, P.A., Minneapolis**

He is, by every measure, an accomplished lawyer: admitted in Minnesota in **1977** (49 years), also Wisconsin, 50+ trials to verdict, 20+ appellate arguments including the Eighth Circuit, AV Preeminent for over 20 years, no disciplinary history in either state. None of that is in question.

**The problem is which side of this exact dispute he has spent his career on.**

- **Erstad & Riemer brands itself, in its own site title, as "Minnesota Workers' Compensation Lawyers | Minneapolis Insurance Defense Attorneys."** Its firm page speaks of taking input from "the client, the claims supervisor, or claims administrator responsible for the litigation" — the vocabulary of carrier-retained defense work.
- **His own firm biography** describes experience in "tort, contract, insurance-related, and commercial litigation," and specifically extensive experience in **"arson and fraud cases for insurers,"** plus **subrogation claims** and **insurance coverage matters**.
- **His Super Lawyers profile categorises his primary practice as "Personal Injury – General: Defense,"** with Insurance Coverage secondary.

Read that against your posture. You are a homeowner alleging your insurer wrongly denied a hail claim. The proposed neutral has built a career being hired by insurers — including to investigate policyholders for **arson and fraud**. Rule 114.13 subd. 2 defines a conflict as any past professional relationship "**likely to affect impartiality**" — not actual bias, and not bad faith. A career of carrier retentions is squarely the kind of relationship that standard is aimed at.

**Two further points:**

1. **He is not presented as a full-time neutral.** His firm bio says he "enjoys providing mediation services to other lawyers and their clients" — a practising trial lawyer who also mediates, not a dedicated neutral. That matters for both availability and detachment.
2. **One genuine point in his favour, for completeness:** his bio lists **appraisal** experience alongside trial, appellate, arbitration and mediation. Appraisal literacy is genuinely valuable given the *Quade* posture here. But that experience was accumulated on the carrier side, which is precisely the concern rather than an answer to it.

**You do not have to justify declining him.** Order ¶6 leaves selection to the parties' agreement, so you simply do not agree. Do not accuse him of bias — say the practice background is not a fit for a case of this shape and move on.

## 4. ⚠️ Neither name appears on the Rule 114 roster

> **⚠️ CORRECTION 9/26/2026 (later the same day).** A further search found a third neutral — Patrick R. Burns — who also publicly claims Rule 114 qualified-neutral status and also does not appear on the roster. Rule 114.12 subd. 2 lists neutrals who **apply** to be listed, so lapsed or unrenewed listings are ordinary and absence is **not** evidence of non-qualification. The inference drawn below was too strong. The practical step is unchanged — ask for current status in writing under Rule 114.13 subd. 3 — but this should not weigh against either candidate until they have been asked. See `Mediator_Reranked_Appraisal_and_Matching_2026-09-26.md` §4a.

I searched the Minnesota Judicial Branch ADR roster today, both the **Civil** and **Family** rosters, by surname.

| Search | Result |
|---|---|
| "Kramer" — Civil | **One** hit: Thomas G. Kramer, Granite Falls. Not Roger L. Kramer |
| "Kramer" — Family | Same single hit: Thomas G. Kramer |
| "Noel" / "Noe" — Civil | **Zero** results |
| "Noel" — Family | **Zero** results |
| *Control:* "Bartsh" — Civil | Full record returned, as expected — **the method works** |

I ran the control specifically because two absences in a row looked like a search error. It was not.

**What this does and does not mean.** Rule 114.04 provides that any individual providing ADR services "must **either** be a Qualified Neutral **or** be selected and agreed to by the parties." Since Order ¶6 leaves selection to the parties, an off-roster neutral is **permissible here if both sides agree**. So this is not disqualifying for either man.

But it costs you three things the criteria memo §I.3 flagged:

- the **Rule 114.12** training floor (30 classroom hours for civil facilitative work, plus continuing education);
- automatic application of the **Rule 114.13 Code of Ethics** and **ADR Ethics Board** jurisdiction — the enforcement backstop, including roster removal;
- the roster's **sanction asterisk**, which is the cheapest discipline check available and cannot be run on someone who isn't listed.

**There is also a discrepancy worth raising politely, not accusingly.** Kramer's National Academy of Distinguished Neutrals profile lists him as a "Minnesota Supreme Court Qualified Neutral under Rule 114," and Noel's firm bio calls him "a qualified ADR neutral." Neither appears on today's roster. The innocent explanations are ordinary — a lapsed listing, a renewal in process, or stale marketing copy. **Ask; don't allege.** The Rule 114.13 subd. 3 written statement of qualifications is the proper vehicle, and you are entitled to it from anyone serving for compensation.

**Cheap fix if you go off-roster.** Whichever neutral is chosen, put a line in the engagement agreement that the neutral agrees to conduct the process in accordance with the **Rule 114.13 Code of Ethics**. That restores by contract most of what roster status would have given you, and no reputable neutral will object. Worth doing even if they turn out to be listed.

## 5. What to send each candidate before agreeing

Same as the shortlist memo §6, with two additions for this situation:

1. **Written statement of qualifications** — Rule 114.13 subd. 3.
2. **Current Rule 114 roster status and category** — specifically whether listed as **Civil Facilitative/Hybrid**, and the HCI-equivalent confirmation in writing.
3. **Conflict disclosure** under Rule 114.13 subd. 2 naming: Fire Insurance Exchange; Fire Underwriters Association; Farmers Insurance and affiliates; **Kennedy Law Firm P.C.**, Kevin Kennedy and Mary Baskfield; **Prieve Engineering** and Nathan Prieve; **Hancock Claims Consultants**; adjuster Nicholas Brau; and the appraisal panel — Wade Roos, John Foster, Greg Tomes.
4. **Approximate split of work between policyholders and insurers**, over a career and in the last five years. *(New — and the single most informative question you can ask either of them.)*
5. **Experience with first-party property coverage and with appraisal-award disputes.**
6. **Fee schedule and draft engagement agreement** — hourly, minimum block, preparation, travel, cancellation.
7. **Experience mediating with a self-represented party.**
8. **Availability in the second half of May 2027.**

*(No conflict between Erstad & Riemer and Farmers turned up in public sources, and none between Kramer and any party — but absence of a public record is not a disclosure. Item 3 is how you actually find out.)*
