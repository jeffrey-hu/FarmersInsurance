## 1. Why these two criteria reshuffle the list

These are not general "insurance experience." They are two specific bodies of Minnesota law, and they are the two questions this case actually turns on.

**Appraisal.** The panel awarded two windows and $0 on both siding lines, marking every line "Hail" in the causation column. *Quade* preclusion reaches only items the panel **evaluated and awarded**; the other ~23 windows are reserved court questions (`../../OffenseStrategies/AppraisalAwardStrategy/03_LegalFramework.md`). A mediator who does not understand what an appraisal award does and does not decide cannot value this case.

**Matching.** *Cedar Bluff Townhome Condominium Ass'n v. American Family*, 849 N.W.2d 439 (Minn. 2014) held that "comparable material and quality" requires **a reasonable color match** between new and existing material — and, critically, that **the appraisal panel itself could interpret the policy's valuation provisions and decide that question.** An insurer-side commentary on that decision (Robins Kaplan's Scott G. Johnson) advises that "insurers should do all they can to avoid having an appraisal panel decide such issues." Your policy carries Endorsement **J6944** (Limited Matching of Undamaged Property), and the manufacturer, Semco, is defunct — which is what distinguishes *Noonan v. American Family* (8th Cir. 2019).

So the two criteria converge on one profile: **someone who has sat as an appraisal umpire and who knows construction "comparable material and quality" disputes.** That is a narrow population, and it changes who leads.

### 4a. ⚠️ A correction I owe you on the roster point

I searched today for "Burns" on both the Civil and Family rosters: **zero results** — even though his own profile says "Minnesota Supreme Court qualified neutral (Rule 114)."

That is now **four** professionals — Kramer, Noel, Burns, and Kramer's NADN listing — who publicly claim Rule 114 qualified-neutral status but do not appear on the published roster. Four independent claims is too many to be four coincidental overstatements.

The likelier explanation is administrative: Rule 114.12 subd. 2 provides that the State Court Administrator "shall review **applications** from individuals who **apply to be listed**," so public listing depends on applying and keeping the listing current. Lapsed or non-renewed listings are ordinary.

**So I was too strong in the 9/26 assessment of Defendant's candidates.** Absence from the roster is a reason to *ask*, not evidence of non-qualification. The practical consequence is unchanged — request current status in writing via the Rule 114.13 subd. 3 statement of qualifications — but the inference I drew was harsher than the evidence supports, and the earlier memo should be read with this correction in mind.

## 6. Questions that actually test for this

Add these to the §5 packet in `Mediator_Candidates_From_Defendant_2026-09-26.md`. They are deliberately hard to bluff.

1. **"Have you served as an appraisal umpire or as a party appraiser in a first-party property claim? How many times, and were you court-appointed or party-selected?"**
2. **"In your view, what does an appraisal award decide, and what does it leave to the court?"** *(Listen for the evaluated-and-awarded distinction — the* Quade *line. A neutral who thinks an award resolves everything will undervalue this case; one who thinks it resolves nothing will misread your leverage on the two awarded windows.)*
3. **"Are you familiar with *Cedar Bluff Townhome Ass'n v. American Family* and the matching requirement in 'comparable material and quality'?"** *(A blank response is disqualifying for the lead role.)*
4. **"Have you handled a dispute where the original material was discontinued or the manufacturer no longer exists?"** *(Semco is defunct — this is the J6944 and* Noonan *distinction.)*
5. **"Have you dealt with a limited-matching endorsement?"**
6. **"How do you approach a case where the panel awarded some items and was silent on others?"** *(The exact shape of this dispute.)*

Do **not** explain your theory of the case when asking. Ask the question, listen, write down the answer.
