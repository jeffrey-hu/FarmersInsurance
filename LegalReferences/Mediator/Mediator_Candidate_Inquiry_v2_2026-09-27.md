**Why the two changes**

- **"Mr. Thomson," not "Judge."** He is a practising attorney and has never been a judge. Using a title he does not hold is the kind of small error that reads as carelessness.
- **The added sentence is true and specific.** Construction and property insurance claims are genuinely why he is on the list. It signals you have done homework, which tends to get a faster reply from a senior lawyer, and it gives him an easy opening to say whether he thinks he is a fit. It stops short of discussing the merits.

## Why this version

The earlier draft read like an audit: five numbered demands, each with a rule citation, sent to a retired judge. Citing the rule that entitles you to something signals distrust before a relationship exists — and it was unnecessary, because **a neutral's standard response packet already contains everything those citations were chasing**: qualifications, fee schedule, engagement agreement, conflict check.

| Dropped | Why it is safe to drop |
|---|---|
| Rule 114.13 subd. 3 / subd. 2 / subd. 7(b) citations | The documents arrive anyway. The citations belong in your file, not in the ask |
| "Confirm your roster category" | You can check the roster yourself, and have |
| "This inquiry is administrative only" | Defensive; answers a worry the reader did not have |
| Appraisal and matching questions | Moved to the call, where *how* they answer is the information |
| The Code of Ethics undertaking | Raise at the engagement-agreement stage, not first contact |

The conflict list stays, but reframed — *"so you can run a conflict check"* — as help rather than interrogation. Neutrals need the full participant list; presenting it as a courtesy costs nothing and reads completely differently.

**What you still get:** availability, qualifications, fee schedule, engagement terms, and a conflict check. Everything that matters.

**Revised again 9/27:** the email no longer names a mediation date or a session length. Neither is ours to state — both are agreed between the parties, and if there is no agreement the Court decides. The email now gives only the Court's own outer deadline (June 1, 2027), which is a fact, and asks about availability in general terms. **Also reviewed after the insurer-/policyholder-side discussion: no change needed on that.** Which side a neutral comes from is answered by the bio they send, by a free case search, and on the call — none of which requires putting that question in a first-contact email.

## The call — ten minutes

Ask; don't put these in writing.

1. Have you mediated with a self-represented party? How do you handle it?
2. **If one side won't move, what do you do?**
3. How do you usually run a mediation — joint session, caucus, or both? How long do these normally take?
4. Over your career, and in the last five years, roughly what share of your work has been for policyholders versus insurers?
5. What does an appraisal award decide, and what does it leave to the court?
6. Are you familiar with *Cedar Bluff* and the matching requirement in "comparable material and quality"?
7. Have you dealt with a claim where the original material was discontinued?
8. Confirm the numbers: hourly rate, minimum block, preparation time, cancellation policy.

> **"Not off the top of my head — send me the award and I'll be up to speed before the session"** is a good answer to 5–7. That is what a neutral is supposed to say.

**Two things to listen for, beyond the answers:**

- **Question 2 is the one that matters most to you.** The risk for a self-represented party is not abstract pro-insurer leaning — it is a neutral who leans on the weaker, less-represented side because you are easier to move than a carrier with counsel and settlement authority. Listen for whether they describe pressing *both* sides.
- **Anyone who offers a view on the merits of your case during this call is telling you something.** A neutral should not be evaluating before hearing both sides.

Question 4 belongs here and **not** in the email — asked in writing at first contact it reads as an accusation, particularly to a retired judge; asked on a call it is an ordinary question they have heard many times. Most of it you can answer yourself first from their bio and a free case search — see `How_To_Tell_Which_Side_A_Neutral_Comes_From_2026-09-27.md`.

Do not describe your theory of the case, what it is worth, or what you would accept.

## Practical notes

- **Not ex parte.** No neutral is appointed; vetting before proposing is ordinary. Rule 114.10 applies once someone is serving.
- **Don't copy Baskfield yet.** It costs nothing substantively but reveals which candidates you are weighing before you have decided.
- **Attach nothing** — no complaint, no award, no correspondence.
- **Status:** Bartsh ✅ sent. Thomson and Knutson to follow — send both rather than waiting on Bartsh's reply. Two or three sets of rates and availability before the **October 13** exchange make that letter to Defendant far stronger than a list of bare names.

**Calendar:** 10/13 exchange · ~10/20 agreement · **10/27 notice to the Court** · **6/1/2027 mediation completed (Order ¶6)**.

*Late May 2027 remains our preferred timing — after rebuttal expert reports (5/14) — but that is a position to propose to Defendant, not something to state to a neutral as decided.*
