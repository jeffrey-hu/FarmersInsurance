## 1. Short answer: yes, it changes the result — and it largely restores the original list

I over-rotated. Reading "familiar with appraisal award" as "has sat as an appraisal umpire" turned a **screening question into a ranking criterion**, and that distorted the list in two ways:

- it promoted **Dean Thomson** on the strength of one credential rather than his overall fit;

## 2. The pool is 44 people, not 2

Re-ran the Rule 114 roster today (Civil / Mediation / Hennepin / Insurance) and filtered the results by experience tags:

| Filter | Count |
|---|---|
| Insurance + Mediation + Hennepin | **119** *(was 117 on 9/18 — the roster is live and has grown)* |
| …also tagged **Construction** | **67** |
| …also tagged **Complex Litigation** | **44** |

Requiring an umpire credential cut that to **two**. Requiring understanding leaves **44–67**.

That is the whole answer. Appraisal and matching are **two Minnesota cases** — *Quade* on what an award precludes, *Cedar Bluff* on "comparable material and quality" — not a subspecialty. Any competent civil neutral, and certainly any retired district judge, can be current on both after an hour with the opinions. **Judges read rules for a living; that is the job.**

**So treat this as pass/fail at the interview, not as a ranking factor.** What actually differentiates candidates is impartiality, locality, cost, and willingness to hold a late-May 2027 date.

## 4. Ask, don't rank

Keep these as **screening questions** — a candidate either satisfies you or doesn't:

1. "What does an appraisal award decide, and what does it leave to the court?" *(Listening for the evaluated-and-awarded distinction.)*
2. "Are you familiar with *Cedar Bluff* and the matching requirement in 'comparable material and quality'?"
3. "Have you handled a dispute where the original material was discontinued or the manufacturer no longer exists?"

A candidate who says *"not off the top of my head, but send me the award and the endorsement and I'll read up before the session"* has given a **good** answer. That is what a neutral is supposed to do. Rejecting that answer would cost you Bartsh — and neutrality is far harder to find than familiarity with two cases.

Do not explain your theory of the case when asking.
