# PART I — THE NEUTRAL MEDIATOR

## I.1 What the Order actually says

Scheduling Order ¶6, verbatim:

> "**MEDIATION:** This case is hereby referred to mediation with a neutral mediator to be selected by the parties. The parties must notify the Court in writing of the agreed-upon neutral and the date scheduled for mediation by October 27, 2026. The mediation costs will be shared equally by the parties. Mediation must be completed by June 1, 2027. The following people must attend the mediation: the attorneys who will try the case, the parties involved in the litigation, and any claims adjuster or other person who has authority to settle the case."

Four things follow directly from that paragraph, before any rule is consulted:

1. **The selection is the parties', not the Court's** — for now. The Court referred the case to mediation and left the choice of neutral to agreement. If there is no agreement, the fallback in Rule 114.04 takes over (§I.9).
2. **Two items must be in the 10/27 notice**: the neutral *and* the date. A notice naming a mediator with no date does not comply.
3. **Costs are split 50/50 by order.** Fee structure is therefore a direct out-of-pocket issue for Plaintiffs, not a Farmers problem. A $500/hr mediator with a 6-hour minimum and a cancellation fee is a real number to plan for.
4. **Farmers must bring a person with settlement authority.** ¶6 names "any claims adjuster or other person who has authority to settle the case." Rule 114.06 says the same thing independently (§I.8).

## I.2 Rule 114 applies to this case

Minn. Gen. R. Prac. 114.01(a): "All civil and family cases are subject to this rule except" a list of exclusions (medical malpractice under Minn. Stat. § 604.11, certain family matters, and cases the court finds inappropriate for ADR). A first-party property insurance action is not excluded. **Rule 114 governs.**

Rule 114.02 defines **mediation** as "[a] process in which a Neutral facilitates communication and negotiation to promote voluntary decision making by the parties to the dispute." Mediation sits in the **facilitative** category — not adjudicative, not evaluative. That classification drives the training requirement in §I.4 and the roster category to search in §I.10.

## I.3 Requirement #1 — the only hard eligibility rule

Minn. Gen. R. Prac. 114.04, verbatim:

> "Any individual providing ADR services under Rule 114 must either be a Qualified Neutral or be selected and agreed to by the parties."

**This is a disjunction, and it matters.** The rule permits either:

- **(a) a Qualified Neutral** — someone on the State Court Administrator's Rule 114.12 roster; or
- **(b) anyone at all, if both sides agree** — a retired judge not on the roster, an out-of-state coverage mediator, a JAMS/AAA panelist.

Because Order ¶6 tells the parties to select by agreement, route (b) is technically open here. **Recommendation: insist on route (a) anyway.** Choosing a roster Qualified Neutral imports, at no cost, the training floor of Rule 114.12, the Code of Ethics of Rule 114.13, and the ADR Ethics Board's enforcement jurisdiction. Choosing an off-roster person by agreement imports none of that automatically; whatever protection you want has to be written into the engagement agreement yourself. For a pro se plaintiff sitting across from an insurer's counsel, that is an unnecessary risk to accept.

## I.4 What "Qualified Neutral" actually guarantees — and the trap inside it

Minn. Gen. R. Prac. 114.12:

- **Subd. 2(a):** "The State Court Administrator shall establish rosters of Qualified Neutrals in the following categories: Civil (A) Civil Facilitative/Hybrid (B) Civil Adjudicative/Evaluative; (2) Family …"
- **Subd. 3(a):** "To become a Qualified Neutral, an applicant must have completed the certified training requirements provided in these rules."
- **Subd. 4(a)(1):** "All Qualified Neutrals providing facilitative or hybrid services, that include a mediation component in civil, non-family matters, must have received a minimum of **30 hours** of classroom training, with an emphasis on experiential learning." (The rule requires 15+ of those hours to be experiential.)
- **Continuing education:** facilitative/hybrid/evaluative neutrals must complete "18 hours of continuing education about alternative dispute resolution subjects within the 3-year period"; adjudicative neutrals, 9 hours.
- **Waiver:** an applicant may seek a waiver from the ADR Ethics Board where "an individual's training and experience clearly demonstrate exceptional competence." So a roster listing does not *always* mean the 30 hours were taken — it can mean a waiver was granted.

> **⚠️ The trap.** The **Civil Adjudicative/Evaluative** roster requires a minimum of only **6 hours** of training. A candidate can be a genuine, currently-listed "Rule 114 Qualified Neutral" on the strength of a six-hour course and never have taken the 30-hour mediation training. Many arbitrators and ENE evaluators are on that roster.
>
> **What to do:** do not accept "I'm a Rule 114 Qualified Neutral" as an answer. Ask, in writing, for the **specific roster category**, and confirm it reads **Civil Facilitative/Hybrid**. That is the category that matches the word "mediation" in ¶6.

## I.5 What the Code of Ethics entitles you to demand *before* you agree

Minn. Gen. R. Prac. 114.13 (Code of Ethics for Court-Annexed ADR Neutrals) is the most useful part of the rule set for selection, because several of its subdivisions create entitlements a party can exercise at the candidate stage.

| Subd. | What the rule says | What you can demand, now |
|---|---|---|
| **3 — Competence** | "No person shall serve as a Neutral unless they possess the qualifications and ability to fulfill the role that the Neutral has been requested or assigned to serve…"  **"No individual may act as a Neutral for compensation without providing the parties with a written statement of qualifications."** | A **written statement of qualifications** from every candidate. This is not a favor you are asking — it is a precondition to serving for pay. Ask both sides' candidates for it in the same email. |
| **2 — Conflicts of interest** | A conflict is "a direct or indirect financial or personal interest in the outcome of the proceeding or any existing or past financial, business, professional, family, or social relationship which is likely to affect impartiality…" Neutrals must disclose actual **and potential** conflicts; "[a]fter disclosure, the Neutral may serve, with the consent of the parties." | A **written conflict disclosure naming the specific entities** (§I.10). Note the rule's standard is *relationship likely to affect impartiality*, not *actual bias* — a past retention by Farmers' counsel is disclosable even if the candidate feels unaffected by it. |
| **1 — Impartiality** | "A Neutral shall conduct the dispute resolution process in an impartial manner and shall serve only in those matters in which the Neutral can remain impartial… If at any time the Neutral is unable to conduct the process in an impartial manner, the Neutral shall withdraw." | A continuing duty — it survives the 10/27 notice. If something surfaces in 2027, the withdrawal obligation is on the neutral. |
| **7(a) — Fees** | "A Neutral shall not enter into a fee agreement that is contingent upon the outcome of the alternative dispute resolution process." | Any candidate proposing a success fee is disqualified by rule. |
| **7(b) — Written agreement** | "In any civil or family court matter in which ADR is used, the Neutral shall enter into a signed written agreement for services with the parties either before or promptly after the commencement of the ADR process." The agreement must state the neutral's role, whether the process is binding, confidentiality, compensation terms, ethics compliance, and — for facilitative processes — that the neutral will not give legal advice. | Ask for the **draft engagement agreement before you agree to the candidate**, not on the morning of the session. Fee structure, minimum hours, cancellation policy, and travel charges are all in it, and they are all shared 50/50 under ¶6. |
| **7 (facilitative limits)** | Facilitative/evaluative neutrals "cannot draft court submissions, provide therapy/legal advice, or coerce settlement." | **Important for pro se parties.** Do not select a mediator on the expectation that they will tell you whether your case is good. They are barred from doing so. If you want a case evaluation, that is a different ADR process (ENE / non-binding advisory opinion under Rule 114.02), and it is not what ¶6 ordered. |
| **8 — Self-determination** | "[M]ediation is based on the principle of self-determination by the parties." | The rule-level answer to any pressure to settle. |
| **6 — Advertising** | "A Neutral shall refrain from promising specific results." | A candidate who pitches a settlement rate or predicts an outcome is signaling the wrong thing. |

**Enforcement is real.** The ADR Ethics Board takes written complaints, decides violations by **clear and convincing evidence**, and can impose sanctions "including but not limited to a public reprimand on the ADR webpage," notification to the appointing court and to professional licensing authorities, and **removal from the roster** (Rule 114.13, ADR Ethics Board Rules subds. 2–3).

**How to check a candidate (corrected 9/18/2026):** the **roster itself flags sanctioned neutrals** — its note reads, "Neutrals with an asterisk (*) by their name have been sanctioned by the Alternative Dispute Resolution Ethics Board for Ethics violations." Check the asterisk on the roster listing, which is republished continuously. The Board's public-reprimand PDFs (two lists, pre- and post-2023 amendments, most recent 2026) are then useful only to learn *what* a flagged neutral was sanctioned for. See `Mediator_Shortlist_and_Vetting_2026-09-18.md` §1 — none of the 117 candidates in the searched pool carries an asterisk.

## I.6 Fees

- **Rule 114.11:** "All fees of Neutral(s) for ADR services shall be fair and reasonable." The neutral "has the right to suspend services if not paid in accordance with the court order or agreement."
- **Order ¶6:** costs shared equally. So every hour of the neutral's rate is 50% a Plaintiffs' expense.
- **Rule 114.01(c)** — the inability-to-pay provision: "If a party qualifies for waiver of filing fees under Minnesota Statutes, section 563.01, or if the court determines on other grounds that the party is unable to pay for ADR services, and free or low-cost ADR services are not available, the court shall not require that party to participate in ADR."

  This is a rule to know about, not one to reach for casually. It is raised **with the Court**, it applies where free or low-cost services are unavailable, and here the Court has already referred the case and allocated costs. If mediator cost is a genuine obstacle, the time to say so is well before 10/27/2026 — and the practical first move is to select a candidate whose rate makes the question moot.

## I.7 Confidentiality — what protects candor at the session

- **Rule 114.07:** "Without the consent of all parties and an order of the court … no evidence from an ADR process … may be admitted in any later proceeding." Statements made in non-binding ADR are inadmissible and not discoverable.
- **Rule 114.08:** "Notes, records, impressions, opinions and recollections of the Neutral are confidential, and the Neutral shall not disclose them to the parties, the public, or any third person" absent agreement or legal requirement. "No record or recording of an ADR session may be made or disclosed without the agreement of all parties and the Neutral."
- **Rule 114.10:** during the process, the neutral may tell the court only whether the case went to ADR and whether it resolved, attendance failures, time requests, procedural issues, or that the process is unsuitable. Afterward, the neutral reports settlement status and, with consent, may discuss pending issues.

**Selection consequence:** a candidate who is vague about confidentiality, or who proposes to report substantively to the Court, is misreading the rule. Rule 114.13 subd. 4 requires the neutral to discuss confidentiality with the parties *before* beginning.

## I.8 Who must attend

Rule 114.06: "Unless the court has ordered otherwise, individuals with the authority to settle the case shall attend all evaluative, facilitative, and hybrid ADR sessions." The court "may impose sanctions" for violations.

Combined with Order ¶6, this is **two independent sources** requiring Farmers to produce a person with settlement authority in the room. Worth stating expressly in the 10/27 notice or the engagement agreement, so there is no argument in 2027 about a claims representative appearing by phone with a capped number.

## I.9 If the parties cannot agree — the fallback, and its 7-day clock

Rule 114.04:

> "If the parties cannot agree on an ADR process, the court shall order the parties to use a non-binding ADR process."
>
> "In the event that the parties are unable to agree on a Neutral, the court shall make the selection of a Qualified Neutral."
>
> "If the court selects a Qualified Neutral without the consent of all parties, any party may file a notice to remove the Qualified Neutral. Such notice must be filed with the court and served on the opposing party **within 7 days of notice of the court's appointment**."
>
> "After a party has once disqualified a Neutral as a matter of right, a substitute Neutral may be disqualified by the party only by making an affirmative showing of prejudice to the chief judge or his or her designee by motion filed **within 7 days** of notice of the court's appointment."

And Rule 114.04(d): "In all filed actions, the parties shall notify the court administrator of any agreed Rule 114 ADR process and the name and contact information for the selected Neutral. Upon appointment of a Neutral by the court, the court administrator shall provide a copy of the Order of Appointment to the Neutral."

**Three operational points:**

1. **Neither party can satisfy ¶6 alone.** ¶6 requires notice of an *agreed* neutral. If Farmers does not engage, the compliant filing is a **joint or unilateral notice of impasse** asking the Court to appoint a Qualified Neutral under 114.04 — filed *before* 10/27, not after.
2. **The removal right is one per party and it expires in 7 days.** If the Court appoints someone, calendar the 7-day date the moment the appointment notice arrives. A second challenge requires an affirmative showing of prejudice to the chief judge — a much harder standard.
3. **Notice goes to the court administrator** under 114.04(d), and to the Court in writing under ¶6. Do both; copy Baskfield per Order ¶12(a)(iii).

## I.10 Selection checklist — rule-required vs. prudent

**Required by rule or order (a candidate failing any of these is out):**

- [ ] Qualified Neutral on the Rule 114.12 roster **or** agreed to by both parties — Rule 114.04
- [ ] Roster category is **Civil Facilitative/Hybrid** (not merely Civil Adjudicative/Evaluative) — Rule 114.12 subd. 2(a), 4(a)(1)
- [ ] **Written statement of qualifications** provided — Rule 114.13 subd. 3
- [ ] Written disclosure of actual **and potential** conflicts — Rule 114.13 subd. 2
- [ ] **No contingent fee**; fees fair and reasonable — Rule 114.13 subd. 7(a); Rule 114.11
- [ ] Signed written services agreement with the required contents — Rule 114.13 subd. 7(b)
- [ ] Available for a session completed **on or before 6/1/2027**, with a date fixable by **10/27/2026** — Order ¶6

**Prudent for this case (not rule-imposed, but decisive):**

- [ ] **Conflict check by name.** Ask each candidate to state whether they have any past or present relationship with: Fire Insurance Exchange; Fire Underwriters Association; Farmers Insurance and its affiliates; **Kennedy Law Firm** and Katherine Baskfield; **Prieve Engineering** / Nathan Prieve; Hancock Claims Consultants; adjuster Nicholas Brau; and the appraisal panel members and umpire from the September 12, 2025 award. Rule 114.13 subd. 2 obliges disclosure — this list makes the obligation concrete.
- [ ] **First-party property / coverage experience.** This case turns on causation of hail damage to windows, the scope of an appraisal award, and a potential Minn. Stat. § 604.18 claim. A general civil mediator with no coverage background will spend paid hours learning the vocabulary.
- [ ] **Experience mediating with a self-represented party**, including how they handle the Rule 114.13 subd. 7 bar on giving legal advice without leaving the pro se side adrift.
- [ ] **Fee structure in writing:** hourly rate, minimum block, preparation time, travel, and the **cancellation policy** — all halved onto Plaintiffs by ¶6.
- [ ] **No asterisk next to the name on the roster listing** (the roster's own sanction flag); the Ethics Board reprimand PDFs only if an asterisk appears.
- [ ] **Willing to schedule the session late in the window** (see I.12).

Verify roster status at the Minnesota Judicial Branch ADR roster: `https://adrroster.courts.state.mn.us/` (select Civil). The State Court Administrator's ADR office is 651-297-7590 if the online listing is ambiguous about category.

## I.12 Timing — and why the mediation date matters as much as the mediator

The one clear win in the September 8 Order is sequencing: mediation must be completed by **6/1/2027**, which falls **after** Plaintiffs' expert reports (3/15/2027), Defendant's (4/17/2027), rebuttal reports (5/14/2027), and after all dispositive motions have been **heard** (5/14/2027). Farmers will arrive at mediation knowing whether its "the appraisal award is binding" defense survived.

**That advantage is only preserved if the date named on 10/27/2026 is late in the window.** A mediation scheduled in, say, January 2027 complies with ¶6 on its face and throws the sequencing away — Plaintiffs would mediate with no expert report, no production, and no ruling.

**Target a date in mid-to-late May 2027** (after 5/14, before 6/1), and say so in the proposal to Baskfield. Build in one fallback date inside the same window in case of a continuance.

# PART II — THE EXPERT

## II.1 There is no rule that lists expert credentials

No Minnesota rule says "a causation expert must hold X." The rules operate at three different moments, and the selection criteria are *derived* from what the expert will have to survive at each one:

| Moment | Rule | What it demands of the person you pick |
|---|---|---|
| **Admissibility at trial** | Minn. R. Evid. 702, 703, 705 | Qualification, helpfulness, **foundational reliability**, and a basis built on admissible data |
| **Disclosure** | Minn. R. Civ. P. 26.01(b); Order ¶3(a) | A signed written report by **3/15/2027** containing six specified categories — including a 10-year publication list and a **4-year testimony list** |
| **Discovery exposure** | Minn. R. Civ. P. 26.02(e), 26.05 | Deposition-readiness (only if obtained by motion or stipulation), a fee for responding, and no protection for drafts |

## II.2 Rule 702 — the qualification standard, verbatim

> "If scientific, technical, or other specialized knowledge will assist the trier of fact to understand the evidence or to determine a fact in issue, a witness qualified as an expert by **knowledge, skill, experience, training, or education**, may testify thereto in the form of an opinion or otherwise. **The opinion must have foundational reliability.** In addition, if the opinion or evidence involves **novel scientific theory**, the proponent must establish that the underlying scientific evidence is generally accepted in the relevant scientific community."

Three selection consequences:

1. **"Knowledge, skill, experience, training, or education" is disjunctive.** A licence is not the only route to qualification. But it is the cheapest route to *unchallenged* qualification, and an unchallenged qualification is worth a great deal to a pro se plaintiff who does not want to spend a motion hearing defending the witness instead of the opinion.
2. **Foundational reliability is about the opinion, not the résumé.** The question at the hearing will be whether the methodology — impact-force thresholds for extruded-aluminum and aluminum-clad wood windows, hail size and fall velocity at this address on 7/13/2024 — was applied reliably to the facts. **Pick someone who shows their arithmetic.** That is precisely the deficiency the repository already documents in the Prieve report (no impact-force calculation, no terminal-velocity calculation, no stated hail-size threshold). An expert of Plaintiffs' own with the same gap is an expert Farmers will move to exclude.
3. **Minnesota is not a Daubert state.** *Goeb v. Tharaldson*, 615 N.W.2d 800 (Minn. 2000); Frye-Mack from *State v. Mack*, 292 N.W.2d 764 (Minn. 1980) and *State v. Roman Nose*, 649 N.W.2d 815 (Minn. 2002). Frye-Mack bites only where the science is both **novel and scientific** — ordinary forensic engineering usually is neither. An expert who talks in Daubert terms is describing a different forum's test. (See `../Filings/Discovery/Expert/Expert_Discovery_and_Challenge_Research_2026-09-17.md` §2 and the naming problem in `../OffenseStrategies/Motions/Daubert/`.)

## II.3 Rule 703(b) — the Minnesota trap most out-of-state experts do not know

Minn. R. Evid. 703(b), verbatim:

> "Underlying expert data must be **independently admissible** in order to be received upon direct examination; provided that when good cause is shown in civil cases and the underlying data is particularly trustworthy, the court may admit the data under this rule for the limited purpose of showing the basis for the expert's opinion."

This is stricter than the federal rule and it is a **selection criterion, not just a trial problem.** An expert who builds a causation opinion on a proprietary commercial hail-verification database, an unauthenticated third-party report, or a vendor's summary may be able to *hold* the opinion but may not be able to put the supporting data in front of the jury on direct.

**Pick an expert who can anchor the opinion in records that come in on their own footing**: the NWS/NCEI storm data already in the Expert Record Packet (item 09), the inspection photographs and their metadata, Prieve's own report and Appendix A, the 2020 claim file, and the expert's own measurements from a site inspection.

**Ask the candidate directly:** *"For each input to your opinion, what is the source, and is it a record I can authenticate and offer in evidence?"* A good forensic expert answers that question easily. It also happens to be the question that separates a working expert from a report generator.

## II.4 Rule 26.01(b) — selection criteria that become discoverable

Order ¶3(a) requires Plaintiffs' "expert interrogatories, disclosures, and reports" by **3/15/2027**, under Minn. R. Civ. P. 26.01(b). The report "must be accompanied by a written report — prepared and signed by the witness" and must contain:

> (A) "a complete statement of all opinions the witness will express and the basis and reasons for them"
> (B) "the facts or data considered by the witness in forming them"
> (C) "any exhibits that will be used to summarize or support them"
> (D) "the witness's qualifications, including a list of all publications authored in the **previous 10 years**"
> (E) "a list of all other cases in which, during the **previous 4 years**, the witness testified as an expert at trial or by deposition"
> (F) "a statement of the compensation to be paid for the study and testimony in the case"

**Read (D), (E) and (F) as a vetting instruction.** Those three items will be handed to Farmers' counsel in March 2027 whatever they say. So obtain them **before retention**, in writing:

- **(E) is the important one.** A four-year testimony list that is overwhelmingly insurer-side is Farmers' cross-examination, written by your own witness and served by you. Ask for the list, then ask for the **plaintiff/insurer split** within it. (`Expert_Witness_Shortlist_and_Vetting.md` §6 already flags this for candidate A, who spent ~7 years at Haag Global — an insurer-side firm — until 2024. That history can be an asset on rebuttal; it is only a liability if it is discovered late.)
- **(D)** — publications are also impeachment material. A candidate with a paper taking a position inconsistent with the opinion in this case is a candidate to know about now.
- **(F)** — the compensation figure is disclosed. A rate that looks disproportionate to the claim is a cross-exam theme.

**Also (A) and (C):** the expert must be willing and able to write a **signed report** and to identify **exhibits**. Some excellent consultants will not write reports. Confirm in the engagement letter: a signed Rule 26.01(b)(2) report, delivered in draft by a date that leaves review time, final by 3/15/2027.

**Rule 26.05** adds a continuing duty to supplement both the report and any deposition testimony when it becomes materially incomplete or incorrect. Pick someone who will still be reachable and cooperative in 2027.

## II.5 Rule 26.02(e) — how exposed your expert will be

- **26.02(e)(1)(A):** the other side may, **by interrogatory**, require identification of each trial expert and a statement of the subject matter, the substance of the facts and opinions, and a summary of the grounds. Available as of right.
- **26.02(e)(1)(B):** any further expert discovery — including a **deposition** — is available only "[u]pon motion." **Minnesota has no federal-style right to depose an opposing expert.** This cuts both ways: Farmers cannot depose your expert as of right either, unless a stipulation or order says so. The pending expert-discovery stipulation (`Filings/Discovery/Expert_Discovery_Stipulation_Letter_to_Kennedy_2026-09-12.md`) is what will decide this, and it should be resolved before the expert is retained so the expert knows what they are signing up for.
- **26.02(e)(3):** the party seeking the discovery ordinarily pays the expert "a reasonable fee for time spent in responding to discovery." Budget both directions.
- **26.02(e)(2):** a **non-testifying** consulting expert is discoverable only on a showing of exceptional circumstances. If you want a consultant to evaluate the case before committing to a testifying expert, that is the protected slot — but it is a separate retention, documented as such from day one.

> **⚠️ No draft protection in Minnesota.** Minnesota has **not** adopted the federal work-product shields for draft expert reports and attorney–expert communications (Fed. R. Civ. P. 26(b)(4)(B)–(C)). Assume every email to and from your expert, and every draft, is discoverable. **Selection consequence:** choose an expert who forms opinions from the record rather than from your framing, and who writes their own report. Keep all instructions factual and in writing that you would be content to see read aloud.

## II.6 The schedule is a selection criterion

- **3/15/2027** is simultaneously the close of all discovery (¶2) and the due date for Plaintiffs' expert reports (¶3(a)). An expert cannot write from documents produced the same day.
- The practical consequence, already worked out in `../OfficialCourtDocuments/SchedulingOrder_Impact_Analysis_2026-09-10.md` §4.2: **Plaintiffs' real document-discovery deadline is December 2026 – early January 2027**, and the expert should be retained **in October 2026** so they can say what the report will require.
- **Ask every candidate for a written commitment to the 3/15/2027 report date and a draft date in February 2027**, plus their availability for a possible deposition after 3/15/2027 and for the mediation window (they will not attend, but their report will be the centerpiece).
- The **uninspected rear-garage window** is a reason to prefer a candidate who can perform a **site inspection** in Eden Prairie. That is first-hand data under Rule 703(b) rather than an opinion about someone else's photographs.

## II.7 Not a court rule, but a real disqualifier — licensure

Engineering causation opinions in Minnesota implicate the licensure statutes for the practice of professional engineering (Minn. Stat. ch. 326 / ch. 326A and the Board of AELSLAGID rules). A witness who is not a licensed PE in Minnesota (or licensable by comity) offering engineering opinions invites a fight that has nothing to do with the merits.

**This memo has not verified the licensure statutes against the primary source** — they are statutes, not court rules, and outside the question asked. Before retention, confirm (a) the candidate's PE licence number and status on the Minnesota licensing board's public lookup, and (b) for a meteorologist, the **CCM** certification. Then re-verify per Order ¶13.

**Fallback worth knowing:** Minn. R. Evid. 706 permits the **court** to appoint an expert of its own. It is rarely used and should not be planned around, but it exists if the expert-cost asymmetry between a pro se household and an insurer becomes acute.

## II.8 Selection checklist — rule-derived

**Derived from the rules (a failure here is a rule problem, not a preference):**

- [ ] Qualified under Rule 702 by knowledge, skill, experience, training **or** education — with credentials that will not themselves be litigated
- [ ] Methodology that shows quantitative work (impact force, terminal velocity, hail-size threshold) — Rule 702 foundational reliability
- [ ] Every input traceable to **independently admissible** data — Rule 703(b)
- [ ] Will prepare and **sign** a Rule 26.01(b)(2) report with contents (A)–(F), final by **3/15/2027**
- [ ] 10-year publication list obtained and reviewed **before** retention — 26.01(b)(2)(D)
- [ ] 4-year testimony list obtained and reviewed **before** retention, with the plaintiff/insurer split — 26.01(b)(2)(E)
- [ ] Compensation terms fixed and disclosable — 26.01(b)(2)(F); **no contingent or outcome-based fee**
- [ ] Available to supplement through trial — Rule 26.05
- [ ] Understands there is **no draft protection** in Minnesota — Rule 26.02(e) comparison

**Prudent (carried over and extended from `Expert_Witness_Shortlist_and_Vetting.md` §6):**

- [ ] Conflict check: no work for Fire Insurance Exchange / Fire Underwriters Association / Farmers, for **Kennedy Law Firm**, or with **Prieve Engineering**
- [ ] Able to perform a **site inspection**, including the never-inspected rear-garage window
- [ ] Covers both disciplines (forensic engineering **and** hail meteorology) or is paired with a second expert who does
- [ ] Balanced practice rather than a pure insurer-side or pure plaintiff-side history
- [ ] Written engagement letter: scope, report and draft dates, hourly rate, deposition rate, and a statement that all opinions are the expert's own
