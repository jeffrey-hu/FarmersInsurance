## 1. Method, and what the roster does and does not prove

Searched the Minnesota Judicial Branch ADR roster at `https://adrroster.courts.state.mn.us/` with three filters:

| Filter | Value | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Neutral Type | **Civil** | Not a family matter |
| Process | **Mediation** | This is the filter that returns the **Civil Facilitative/Hybrid** roster — the 30-hour training category, not the 6-hour adjudicative one (criteria memo §I.4) |
| County | **Hennepin** | Venue |
| Experience | **Insurance** | Self-reported area of experience |

**Result: 117 Qualified Neutrals.** Every name in this document is drawn from that list, so every one of them is confirmed **Civil Facilitative/Hybrid** — the category trap in §I.4 of the criteria memo is already cleared for all of them.

**Two verified findings worth recording:**

1. **The roster flags sanctioned neutrals directly.** Its note reads: "Neutrals with an asterisk (*) by their name have been sanctioned by the Alternative Dispute Resolution Ethics Board for Ethics violations." **None of the 117 carries an asterisk.** This is faster and more current than downloading the Ethics Board's reprimand PDFs — see §8, which corrects the companion memo on this point.
2. **No conflict appears on the face of the roster.** Searched all 117 entries for "Kennedy," "Farmers," "Baskfield," "Prieve," and "Hancock." **Zero matches.** No neutral in this pool is listed at Kennedy Law Firm or at any entity named in this case. That is a clean starting point — but it proves only what the listings say, not what the neutrals have done.

**What the roster expressly does not prove** — its own disclaimer, verbatim:

> "Neutrals on this roster are considered 'qualified' under Rule 114 of the Minnesota General Rules of Practice for the District Courts. Qualified neutrals are **not certified or licensed** in the field of ADR. **Areas of experience are supplied by the individual neutral, and have not been independently verified by the Minnesota Judicial Branch.** In choosing an ADR neutral, you should inquire about the qualifications and experience of the neutral."

So the "Insurance" tag is a self-declaration. It does **not** distinguish a first-party property coverage mediator from a personal-injury mediator who handles liability carriers — and for this case that distinction is the whole ballgame. That is what §§3–5 sort out.

## 2. What this case actually needs from a mediator

Four things, in priority order:

1. **First-party property coverage literacy.** Not liability, not personal injury. The dispute is what a homeowner's policy pays for hail damage to windows.
2. **Building-envelope / damage-causation fluency.** The fight is whether 7/13/2024 hail caused damage to aluminum-clad and extruded-aluminum windows, against an engineering report that says it did not. A mediator who has never looked at a causation report will spend paid hours getting oriented.
3. **Appraisal-award literacy.** Farmers' affirmative defense is that the September 12, 2025 appraisal award binds. A mediator who understands what an appraisal award does and does not resolve is worth a great deal here.
4. **No insurer-side tilt, and real comfort with a self-represented party.** Plaintiffs are pro se. Rule 114.13 subd. 7 bars the mediator from giving them legal advice, so the mediator's *process* discipline matters more than usual.

Cost is a fifth factor and not a small one — see §7.

## 7. Money — run this before you propose anyone

Order ¶6: "The mediation costs will be shared equally by the parties." Rule 114.11 requires fees to be "fair and reasonable," and Rule 114.13 subd. 7(b) requires a signed written services agreement.

**Almost no Minnesota neutral publishes a rate.** The one published figure found in this research is a useful anchor: a St. Paul neutral in this pool (Steven J. Kirsch, Larson King) lists **$585/hour** on a national directory. Assume Tier 1–2 candidates sit in roughly that range or above.

Working arithmetic for a single mediation day:

| Scenario | Rate | Hours (prep + session) | Total | **Plaintiffs' half** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tier 3 / junior neutral | $300 | 8 | $2,400 | **$1,200** |
| Mid-market | $450 | 8 | $3,600 | **$1,800** |
| Published anchor | $585 | 8 | $4,680 | **$2,340** |
| Senior / retired justice | $750 | 8 | $6,000 | **$3,000** |

Ask every candidate for four numbers, not one: **hourly rate, minimum block, preparation time charged, and the cancellation policy.** The cancellation policy is the one that bites — a May 2027 date held since October 2026 and then continued can carry a full-day charge, and half of it lands on Plaintiffs.

## 8. Correction to the companion memo

`Expert_and_Neutral_Selection_Criteria_2026-09-18.md` §I.5 and §I.10 say to check candidate names against the ADR Ethics Board's public reprimand PDFs. That still works, but the **roster itself is the better tool**: it marks sanctioned neutrals with an asterisk next to the name and is republished continuously (it read "Current as of 9/18/2026" today). Check the asterisk on the roster listing first; use the Ethics Board PDFs only to find out *what* a flagged neutral was sanctioned for.
